Less Decoder is More Encoder: Geometric Representation Learning from Novel View Synthesis

NeurIPS

Keerthi Kaashyap, Dennis Anthony, Akshay Krishnan, Nhi Ngoc Nguyen, Jeremy Collins, James Hays, Shreyas Kousik, Animesh Garg

AI Summary

Existing Novel View Synthesis (NVS) methods yield poor geometric representations due to spatially expressive decoders that dilute encoder capabilities and low-level pixel targets hindering feature learning. The paper proposes SNAP, a self-supervised encoder-decoder transformer that addresses this with a pose-conditioned local decoder and a latent-space reconstruction objective. SNAP achieves competitive performance against both geometry-supervised and self-supervised methods across five geometric tasks, demonstrating emergent viewpoint invariance and more graceful degradation under camera shifts, confirming that restricted decoder expressivity prevents suppression of transferable geometric structure.

EyeRobot 2.0: Active Gaze for Precise Manipulation without Wrist Cameras

arXiv

Kush Hari, Justin Kerr, Nidhya Shivakumar, Samarth Mahapatra, Carmelo Sferrazza, Jiahui Lei, Jitendra Malik, C. Karen Liu, Ken Goldberg, Angjoo Kanazawa

AI Summary

The core problem is enabling precise bimanual manipulation using only a single stereo camera without wrist cameras, which significantly degrades standard policy performance. EyeRobot 2.0 proposes "Active Visual Fixation" (AVF) where a stereo camera actively swivels to fixate on 3D points, foveally processing images, with gaze coordinated hierarchically by RL-trained policies. This method outperforms passive stereo by 40% in real-world tasks and 20% in simulation, closing the performance gap from removing wrist cameras and more than doubling the success of ego+wrist policies when occluded.

LESSER: Post-Training Data Selection with Output-Layer Gradients

arXiv

Lyuxin David Zhang, Eric Wong, Surbhi Goel, Anton Xue

AI Summary

Full-parameter gradient-based data selection for large language models is computationally intractable for extensive data pools due to the expensive backward pass required per sample. LESSER proposes using only output-layer gradients for data selection, which are significantly cheaper to compute via a forward pass. This method reduces feature-extraction FLOP costs by up to $9.7\times$ for SFT and $3.0\times$ for RL, while maintaining the downstream performance of full-gradient methods by selecting batches with similarly aligned gradients.

arXiv

Planning to Learn

arXiv

Ian Osband

AI Summary

Policy-gradient methods in classification are surprisingly outperformed by cross-entropy, despite exact gradients, due to their myopic focus on immediate gain rather than future learning potential. The authors propose "horizon loss," which interpolates between the patient, infinite-horizon view of cross-entropy and the zero-horizon exact policy gradient, adapting as training progresses. This method provably escapes optimization traps in a simple model and significantly improves top-1 accuracy over cross-entropy on MNIST and ImageNet datasets, with gains increasing under label noise.

Pivot-SD: Efficient Self-Distillation for Masked Diffusion Language Models

EMNLP

Seo Hyun Kim, Sunwoo Hong, Younwoo Choi, Chen-Hao Chao, Se-Young Yun, Rahul G. Krishnan

AI Summary

Masked diffusion language models (dLMs) face a credit-assignment problem where early, high-impact commitments during denoising significantly reduce uncertainty, but traditional post-training methods fail to utilize this signal. Pivot-SD introduces an efficient offline self-distillation framework that identifies these "pivots" using an information-gain metric and supervises only them, applying cross-entropy for successful trajectories and targeted unlikelihood for failed ones. This method significantly improves LLaDA-8B-Instruct on math and code benchmarks, outperforming full-sequence SFT and diffusion RL baselines with minimal training data.

MRVQ: One Resident Index for Dimension- and Rate-Elastic Vector Search

arXiv

Sean Culatana, Shang-En Huang, Kang Li

AI Summary

Dense retrieval services face high memory overhead when dynamically adapting to varying embedding dimensions and index bit rates. This paper proposes Matryoshka Residual Vector Quantization (MRVQ), a post-hoc residual quantizer whose single maximum-rate code can be truncated (by dropping residual stages or embedding coordinates) to flexibly serve all desired (dimension, rate) pairs. MRVQ significantly reduces memory consumption by 17.8-22.0x compared to separate quantizers and 1.89-2.02x versus a shared-model baseline, while achieving competitive quality that outperforms PQ, OPQ, and AdANNS-OPQ at matched code size. It represents a low-memory operating point for elastic retrieval.

PoCoFL: POlicy-COmpliant Federated Learning

arXiv

Dominik Roy George, Varesh Mishra, Aysajan Abidin

AI Summary

Federated Learning lacks guarantees for policy-compliant client contributions and correct aggregator processing, with existing verifiable FL systems being overly specialized. PoCoFL addresses this by separating FL type, policy semantics, and cryptographic realization, using non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs for clients to prove contribution compliance and for aggregators to prove correct processing based on policy. The framework was formally instantiated and demonstrated across four FL types, proving its versatility, practical feasibility, and ability to enforce complex policies while remaining network-topology agnostic.

On the Convergence of Success Conditioning for Policy Optimization

arXiv

Matthew Brun, Xu Andy Sun

AI Summary

Success conditioning, a common RL policy improvement strategy, lacks well-understood limiting behavior and convergence rates. This work rigorously analyzes the strategy, demonstrating its convergence to an optimal policy on a broad class of Markov decision processes (MDPs). It further derives specific convergence rates, showing $\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^p)$ iterations for discounted MDPs and $\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ iterations for single-period MDPs to achieve an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy.

arXiv

Yu Li, Guangfeng Cai, Long-Fei Li, Shuo Han, Shengtian Yang, Han Luo, Kaibing Yang, Lei Feng

AI Summary

Existing RL credit assignment methods for terminal agents fail to explicitly trace read-write dependencies between commands, leading to inefficient learning from irrelevant steps. This paper proposes Dependency-Aware Group Policy Optimization (DepGPO), which constructs a command dependency graph from execution traces, traces backward from verified resources, and assigns credit to relevant read-write operations to redistribute trajectory advantages. DepGPO demonstrably improves task performance and training stability on complex terminal tasks.

NeutronGym: Physics-Graded Neutron Instrument Design for LLM Agents

arXiv

Lijie Ding, Changwoo Do

AI Summary

The core problem is to objectively evaluate language model agents' ability to perform physics-based design, rather than just recall information, specifically for complex tasks like scientific instrument design, which lacks executable environments and undeniable grading. The proposed method introduces NeutronGym, the first executable environment for neutron instrument design, where agents build instruments with tools, McStas ray-traces them, and a non-LLM "level-resolved ladder" objectively grades designs on syntax, runtime, structure, and scientific merit. ACL

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the high inference latency of frontier models, which limits their use for real-time robot control despite strong few-shot reasoning. The paper proposes two complementary bridges: first, using a frontier model to autonomously generate demonstrations, augmented with corrective segments, for training a fast local policy, which a harness then uses for efficient execution. When a Correct Reward Is Not Enough: Diagnosing and Guiding PPO in an Analytically Solved Broker-Trader Game

arXiv

Siu Tung Wong, Carlo Campajola

AI Summary

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents, specifically PPO, struggle with accuracy and reliability in continuous-time financial optimal-control problems, even with analytically derived rewards, despite their actors' representational capacity. This study uses an analytically solved broker-trader game to diagnose PPO's limitations, comparing it against reference strategies and investigating policy adaptation from an analytical warm start. PPO achieved reference accuracy only in simplified scenarios; otherwise, its critics failed to reliably rank actions, and reward shaping did not improve performance. However, using the analytical policy as a warm start enabled PPO to effectively adapt to changing execution costs, demonstrating the diagnostic and guiding value of analytical solutions for RL.
