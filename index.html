Do You Miss the Nights We Spent Crafting Figures? Rethinking Teaser and Framework Figures in the Age of Generative AI

arXiv

Xiaoqi Zhao, Youwei Pang, Kailai Zhou

AI Summary

Generative AI introduces questions about explanatory clarity and purposeful visual expression in scientific figures. Researchers conducted a retrospective analysis and AI-assisted revisions of 371 motivation and framework figures from 200 papers, evaluated across six dimensions by a multimodal judge. AI revisions notably increased clarity but decreased aesthetic coherence, with greater gains for motivation figures. The study concludes that AI should assist exploration in researcher-led design, maintaining authors' responsibility for explanatory purpose, composition, and scientific relationships.

arXiv

Celeste De Schamphelaere, Juan J. Vazquez, Ari Spiesberger, Tomáš Gavenčiak, Gavin Leech, Euan Ong, Jiahai Feng

AI Summary

Interpreting language model residual stream directions by finding activating text is limited by corpus content. OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions

arXiv

Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, Chen Xu, Haruki Nishimura, Masha Itkina, Roberto Martín-Martín

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models frequently fail during deployment, and existing recovery methods require substantial, scenario-specific corrective data that poorly generalizes. OopsieRecover addresses this by inferring semantic intent from a handful of corrective demonstrations using abduction, encoding this knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs guide the policy away from undesirable behaviors. The method enables VLAs to overcome failures and generalize corrections across novel objects, scene variations, and related tasks with minimal data, eliminating the need for new corrective data per variation.

Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation

arXiv

Yixiao Wang, Peiyi Chen, Mustafa Mukadam, Shreekant Gayaka, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Son Tran

AI Summary

Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging, requiring both perceptual grounding and precise control. The Latent RL Prior addresses this by first pretraining a proprioception-conditioned action decoder with a latent task context using large-scale simulated RL. This decoder is then frozen, and a visual interface is learned to map images directly to the latent context using only action supervision from demonstrations. The method significantly outperforms fine-tuning baselines in low-data regimes, achieving full-data performance with 5-10x fewer demonstrations, and retains robust control knowledge evidenced by out-of-distribution generalization.

VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

AI Summary

Autoregressive vision-language models for physical AI generate visual plans one coordinate token at a time, leading to costly and slow richer predictions. VFlash introduces Flash Primitive Decoding, exploiting the known structure of visual primitives to jointly predict their coordinates in a single forward pass with a lightweight drafter. Furthermore, a world action model conditioned on VFlash plans demonstrates higher average task success rates (70.2% vs. 62.6% baseline).

Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Chenruo Liu, Zihan Wang, Yijun Dong, Qi Lei

AI Summary

This paper investigates the differing dynamics of LoRA updates between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). By tracking the singular spectrum and subspaces of LoRA updates, they found RL concentrates the spectrum and rotates subspaces faster than SFT, attributing these differences primarily to group-mean baseline subtraction. A quadratic model explains this as shifting the balance between coherent fitting and gradient noise. Based on these dynamics, they propose tailored LoRA updates for SFT (flattening full update spectrum) and RL (flattening updates outside the current LoRA span), which consistently yield improved performance on mathematical reasoning tasks.

T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

arXiv

Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen, Yingyan, Lin, Chaojian Li

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face high inference costs from processing redundant visual tokens and suffer from misallocated attention, reducing efficiency and robustness. The proposed T3R framework introduces a training-free, two-stage token refinement: segmentation-guided pruning removes spatially irrelevant visual patches, and saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates focus to action-critical regions. T3R removes 85% of visual tokens while matching baseline success rates, improves OOD robustness by up to 7%, and achieves up to 3.3x speedup with minimal overhead. These results highlight substantial accuracy-efficiency benefits, particularly in multi-camera VLA settings.

Anisotropy lifts topological protection of transport in pumped spin chains

arXiv

Mainak Pal

AI Summary

This paper investigates if momentum-space topological invariants guarantee quantized spin transport in anisotropic spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ XY chains. The authors map the system to an extended Rice-Mele-type fermionic model and employ both momentum-space Chern number calculations and real-space time evolution diagnostics. While momentum-space analysis predicts quantized pumping, real-space simulations reveal that spin transport is generally not quantized in the anisotropic regime, only surviving in the isotropic limit. This demonstrates that momentum-space topological invariants alone do not guarantee quantized transport when the transported quantity is not conserved, highlighting the importance of real-space diagnostics.

FedReason: Large Language Models as Weight-Space Meta-Aggregators for Heterogeneous Federated Learning

arXiv

Ali Mohammadi Ruzbahani, Abbas Yazdinejad, Hadis Karimipour

AI Summary

Federated learning faces challenges where fixed aggregation rules struggle with diverse or dynamic client heterogeneity. FedReason proposes using a Large Language Model (LLM) as a meta-aggregator, which periodically inspects client model weights via a server-side sandbox and optional proxy evaluations to generate a custom Python aggregation program. This method consistently outperformed FedAvg and intelligent baselines on label-skewed data and effectively excluded Byzantine clients early on. It achieved the strongest performance among server-side aggregation rules, demonstrating the LLM's capability to adapt aggregation strategies, with proxy feedback being crucial for its accuracy.

Module-Aware Optimization for Graph Neural Networks

NeurIPS

Guy Hadad, Haggai Roitman, Moshe Eliasof, Bracha Shapira

AI Summary

Matrix-aware optimizers for GNNs can suffer from update amplification due to the geometry imposed by graph-propagated features, a factor not considered by existing methods like Muon. To address this, GraphMuon adapts Muon's updates by performing orthogonalization and step normalization in a metric defined by the covariance structure of graph-propagated layer inputs. Theoretically, GraphMuon mitigates this graph-induced amplification, and empirically, it achieves significant training speedups (1.32x-2.42x validation-loss time-to-target over AdamW) across large-scale GNN benchmarks while maintaining competitive or superior predictive performance.

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing peer-prediction mechanisms fail to incentivize truthful compression of rich private information into coarser ordinal reports without ground truth.
