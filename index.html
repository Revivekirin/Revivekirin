$ω$-0: A Latent Predictive World Action Model for Concurrent Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Zhe Li, Zhenzhe Zhang, Yangyang Wei, Wenjie Zhang, Xichen Yuan, Peiyuan Zhi, Gen Li, Xinying Guo, Fengjie Gao, Jianfei Yang, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

Humanoid robots struggle with concurrent loco-manipulation for household tasks due to decomposed policies or limited world-action models. This paper introduces $ω$-0, a latent predictive whole-body world-action model that forecasts compact future observation embeddings, rather than videos, coupled with diffusion-based action generation to predict controller-compatible whole-body action latents from multi-modal inputs. Real-world experiments on 11 household tasks demonstrate $ω$-0's ability to produce smooth manipulate-while-moving behaviors, consistently outperforming various imitation learning, VLA, humanoid, and WAM baselines.

DyPES-VLA: Learning Shared Dynamics Priors and Embodiment-Specific Control for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

arXiv

Junfeng Li, Junjie He, Zhide Zhong, Yangyang Zheng, Pingyue Sheng, Jiayu Dong, Ruixin Li, Haodong Yan, Jiaguan Zhu, Tianran Zhang, Runze Yu, Wen Chen, Liuqing Yang, Yuxiang Gao, Haoang Li

AI Summary

The core problem is developing a single generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy for heterogeneous robot embodiments, which is hindered by underutilizing shared dynamics priors and requiring extensive manual action preprocessing. DyPES-VLA proposes to learn shared dynamics priors by training a VLM with a future-prediction objective on cross-embodiment data, capturing interaction-induced scene changes. These priors are then translated into executable controls via an embodiment-specific Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) action head, operating directly in each embodiment's native action space without manual pre-alignment. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulations and real-world tasks, reaching 98.0% success on LIBERO, 59.25% on RoboCasa-GR1, and 89.02% on RoboTwin 2.0.

AI Summary

arXiv

Chenghao Gu, Hanyang Yu, Jingbo Zhang, Haitao Lin, Wenyao Zhang, Jinghe Wang, Hanglei Jin, Shuzhao Xie, Jingyan Jiang, Zhi Wang

AI Summary

Existing robot world models suffer from limited action controllability and poor generalization to unseen environments, hindering robust manipulation. GeniWorld proposes an interactive world model that leverages pretrained video generative models and transforms numerical actions into visual representations via URDF rendering for spatially grounded control. GeniWorld achieves superior in-domain performance and robust zero-shot generalization to highly randomized, unseen environments, serving as a scalable policy evaluator and improving downstream policy performance.

RRC: Unlocking Generative Reward Models in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Ranking-Based Reward Construction

arXiv

Chenglong Wang, Ziming Zhu, Yifu Huo, Bei Li, Qiaozhi He, Yan Ding, Xiaoyang Hao, Yuxin Gao, Tianhua Zhou, Xiaojia Chang, Tongran Liu, Jingbo Zhu

AI Summary

Generative reward models (GRMs) are underutilized in LLM reinforcement learning (RL) despite strong ranking capabilities, due to a mismatch between their comparative nature and RL's scalar reward paradigm. To address this, RRC (Ranking-based Reward Construction) enables GRMs to provide effective RL signals by deriving rewards from relative preference rankings. RRC employs self-competitive ranking among sampled responses and anchor-guided ranking using a small set of reference responses. Experiments demonstrate RRC substantially improves RL training with GRMs across chat and reasoning benchmarks, consistently outperforming existing approaches.

Bias Analysis of L2 Speaking Assessment Systems Using Concept Activation Vectors

arXiv

Arya Labroo, Mengjie Qian, Kate Knill

AI Summary

The core problem is to detect and analyze bias from irrelevant speaker attributes (e.g., L1, age) in high-stakes, black-box transformer-based L2 speaking assessment systems. The proposed method extends Concept Activation Vectors (CAVs) to BERT and Whisper-based graders, differentiating between a concept being encoded in representations and its influence on scores, also exploring Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to improve CAV recoverability. Key results show that concept recoverability and sensitivity are architecture-dependent, and while SAEs improve linear recoverability, they can attenuate the original activation-space sensitivity. This underscores the importance of distinguishing concept encoding from its actual influence on predictions when auditing bias.

BaKron: Efficient Quantization with Kronecker-Factored Hessians

arXiv

Johann Birnick, Rayan Saab

AI Summary

The core problem is efficiently leveraging two-sided Kronecker-factored Hessians in neural network quantization to capture richer correlations, as direct application is computationally prohibitive. BaKron is introduced as an efficient solver that combines anti-diagonal parallelism with a recursive divide-and-conquer construction to address this challenge. For an $m \times n$ weight matrix, it reduces total work from $O(m^2n^2)$ to $O(mn(m+n))$ with $O(m+n)$ sequential steps, matching GPTQ's cubic scaling while exploiting richer curvature and offering modularity.

Nonlinear Compton scattering in a quantized pump field

arXiv

Kenan Qu

AI Summary

This paper addresses the limitations of non-quantized treatments of nonlinear Compton scattering by developing a fully quantized theory using exact quantum-Volkov states, incorporating pump depletion, back-action, and final-state correlations via Fock-state ladders. The method predicts discrete photon-transfer edges and a terminal spectral cutoff for finite Fock-state pumps. Key results show the squeezing angle of squeezed coherent light controls high-energy emission through photon-number fluctuations, and in the bright, weakly depleted regime, the theory reduces to a Wigner-function weighted-average of scattering probabilities.

The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

arXiv

Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu

AI Summary

Multimodal LLMs using active visual tools often exhibit marginal gains, high costs, and irrelevant operations, raising questions about whether returned visual evidence causally affects their answers. This work formulates visual tool-use as a causal graph and audits it through policy, trajectory, and step-level interventions, introducing "Visual Evidence Gain" to isolate observation contributions. They uncover "policy miscalibration" across models, characterized by "Calling Without Looking" (observations have no causal effect) and "Looking Without Planning" (informative but incoherent call schedules). This reveals an "illusion of visual tool-use," where aggregate accuracy gains do not translate to causal effectiveness across a broad range of model rollouts.

arXiv

Ke-Xia Jiang

AI Summary

The paper addresses the elusive physical origins of Lorentz and gauge symmetries, core to modern physics. It proposes a unified classical source: their isomorphic emergence from dynamical constraints on wave-packet excitations within a continuous elastic substrate medium (SM), without postulating fundamental symmetries or quantization. DASH: Divergence-Adaptive Supervision Horizons for On-Policy Self-Distillation of Reasoning Models

arXiv

ZhiYan Hou, Xinyu Tang, Hongyan An, Jianjin Zhang, Weizhen Wang, Yunyun Han, Gengsheng Li, Xiangzhao Hao, Haiyun Guo, Wenbin Hu, Jinqiao Wang, Yafeng Deng

AI Summary

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) for LLM reasoning suffers from underexploiting temporal structure, assigning equal weight to all local divergences regardless of their position or historical context in the teacher-student mismatch. To address this, DASH (Divergence-Adaptive Supervision Horizons) introduces adaptive propagation gates, derived from the gap between local distillation signals and the sequence-level mean, to control backward multi-step aggregation. This method dynamically adjusts token-level supervision weights based on how local divergences evolve during generation. Experiments show DASH consistently improves performance over vanilla OPSD across three mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model scales, with no additional computational cost.

arXiv

Hisham Khalil, Neil Fernandes, Thomas M. Kwok, Hsiu-Chin Lin, Yue Hu

AI Summary

The proposed Variable Impedance Diffusion Policy (VIDP) uses a Task-Parameterized Directionality-Aware Mixture Model (TP-DAMM) to extract physically consistent trajectory distributions from diverse demonstrations. ErgoSurf: Ergodic Control for the Coverage of Unknown Surfaces

arXiv

Stefan Schneyer, Timo Bachmann, Maged Iskandar, Korbinian Nottensteiner, Alin Albu-Schäffer, Freek Stulp, João Silvério

AI Summary

Robots performing contact tasks need to systematically cover unknown surfaces, but current ergodic control methods rely on unavailable prior surface geometry. This paper proposes an online ergodic control framework that simultaneously covers surfaces and reconstructs their unknown geometry by learning a Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS) model from intrinsic tactile sensing. Local surface approximations from tangent planes and heat-diffusion potential fields enable efficient online planning. Simulation and real-robot experiments validate the framework, demonstrating accurate simultaneous ergodic coverage and online surface geometry learning with reconstruction errors approaching ground truth.
