Knowing the Self, Understanding the World: A Dual-Cognition Benchmark for UAV Spatio-temporal Reasoning with MLLMs

arXiv

Like Liu, Zhengzheng Xu, Haitao He, Hongzhe Li, Shuchang Zhang, Dian Shao

AI Summary

Multimodal large language models lack sufficient exploration and benchmarks for the dual-cognition capability required in UAV scenarios, which involves reasoning about both the UAV's self-state and the external environment in multiview spatio-temporal contexts. To address this, the authors introduce **UAV-DualCog**, a new benchmark with image and video tasks designed to jointly evaluate self-state and environment-state reasoning, requiring precise spatial or temporal grounding, and built with an automated data generation pipeline. Evaluations reveal current MLLMs are unreliable in UAV dual cognition, struggling with self-state reasoning, viewpoint transformation, and spatio-temporal grounding. The benchmark is challenging for models but understandable to humans, and its training subset offers valuable structured supervision.

Searching Videos as Trees: Self-Correcting Agents for Grounded Long Video QA

arXiv

Ce Zhang, Ziyang Wang, Yulu Pan, Oluwatumininu Oguntola, Pranav Wagh, Qiyu Wu, Hiromi Wakaki, Mohit Bansal, Gedas Bertasius

AI Summary

Grounded long-video question answering (LVQA) agents often prematurely converge and cannot recover from early mistakes due to a lack of fine-to-coarse backtracking primitives. This paper proposes VideoTreeSearch (VTS), an agentic framework that casts grounded LVQA as iterative self-correcting search over an adaptive temporal tree constructed from visual scene boundaries. An agent navigates this tree using explicit `zoom_in`, `zoom_out`, `shift`, and `answer` operations, trained with synthesized trajectories that include deliberate detours and recovery. VTS significantly outperforms prior agentic methods on three Grounded LVQA benchmarks (e.g., +12.5 mIoU on CG-Bench) and transfers effectively to general long-video QA, with ablations confirming the criticality of self-correcting hierarchical search and explicit backtracking.

arXiv

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Motion Maps: An Open-Vocabulary, Uncertainty-Aware, Queryable Motion Attribute for 3D Scene Maps

arXiv

Dibyendu Ghosh, Ayushi Shakya

AI Summary

Existing open-vocabulary 3D maps lack the ability to answer language queries about how scene elements behave, assuming a static world. This paper introduces Vision-Language-Motion Maps (VLMM), an open-vocabulary, natural-language-queryable 3D map that assigns each element a fused motion attribute: a semantic movability prior combined with geometrically observed motion, along with per-element uncertainty. When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?

arXiv

Kai Ruan, Jinghao Lin, Zihe Huang, Ziqi Zhou, Qianshan Wei, Xuan Wang, Hao Sun

AI Summary

The core problem is the unclear value of the Muon optimizer for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training. The study proposes comparing vanilla Muon with AdamW in sparse-reward agentic RL on ALFWorld using a Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct model. arXiv

arXiv

AI Summary

arXiv

AI Summary

SAR ADCs face high switching energy from sequential MSB transitions and rigid conversion latency. This paper proposes a Kalman filter-assisted data-predictive SAR ADC that uses a recursive state estimator to predict the first four MSBs, enabling simultaneous parallel switching of MSB capacitors. arXiv

AI Summary

BayesContact is a Simulation-Based Inference framework that maintains a particle belief over object pose. Understanding Reasoning from Pretraining to Post-Training

arXiv

Jingyan Shen, Ang Li, Salman Rahman, Yifan Sun, Micah Goldblum, Matus Telgarsky, Pavel Izmailov

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the lack of understanding regarding how pretraining choices shape returns to RL post-training for LLM reasoning, and what RL fundamentally does to the model. To investigate, the authors use chess as a controlled testbed, pretraining models on human games, SFT on synthetic traces, and applying RL on puzzles, also validating with a math-domain LLM. They find that post-RL performance is well-predicted by pretraining loss, and the RL reward curve slope improves linearly with pretraining tokens. Critically, RL is shown to not just sharpen the SFT policy, but to surface nearly absent correct moves on hard puzzles.

Let the Body Follow: Coupled Egocentric Control for Whole-Body Robot Teleoperation

arXiv

Tsung-Chi Lin, Yichen Xie, Chien-Ming Huang

AI Summary

Whole-body robot teleoperation is difficult due to the complex coordination required across multiple robot components like the head, arms, torso, and base. This paper proposes "coupled egocentric control," a body-following teleoperation method where the robot's torso and base automatically respond to the operator's head and arm motions. For instance, head pitch adjusts torso height and end-effector workspace boundaries trigger base translation, reducing explicit commands. User studies demonstrate this approach improves object manipulation efficiency, reduces control effort and mental workload, and increases ease of use, learning, and user preference.

arXiv

AI Summary

When Model Merging Rivals Joint Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning: A Task-Vector Geometry Analysis

arXiv

S. Aaron McClendon

AI Summary

This paper addresses the underexplored direct comparison between model merging and joint multi-task training in reinforcement learning. By training and merging specialist RL agents (Qwen3-8B on AppWorld) against a jointly trained model, the authors demonstrate that merging rivals joint RL performance, with various merge variants yielding statistically indistinguishable results. This equivalence is attributed to specialist task vectors being near-orthogonal despite substantial parameter overlap, effectively causing sophisticated merging techniques to reduce to near-uniform averaging.
