Higher Berry curvature, second Chern numbers and magnetoelectric coupling in crystalline insulators

arXiv

Niclas Heinsdorf, Ken Shiozaki

AI Summary

The core problem is the quantized computation of higher topological invariants, specifically second Chern numbers, in lattice models. The proposed method rewrites a 4D Chern insulator model as 3D infinite chains, then computes its higher three-form Berry curvature using infinite matrix product states (iMPS). Key results show that the topological phase diagram derived from this higher Berry curvature (the DDKS number) precisely matches the known second Chern number phase diagram, demonstrating that higher Berry curvature can compute second Chern numbers in a quantized manner.

Learning Action Priors for Cross-embodiment Robot Manipulation

arXiv

Dong Jing, Tianqi Zhang, Jiaqi Liu, Jinman Zhao, Zelong Sun, Li Erran Li, Zhiwu Lu, Mingyu Ding

AI Summary

Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle to learn physical motion and cross-modal alignment, especially in cross-embodiment settings, because their action modules lack explicit motion priors. This paper proposes a two-stage framework that first pretrains a lightweight flow-matching-based action module solely on unconditioned action trajectories to learn temporal motion structure. This learned prior is then transferred to VLA training via decoder reuse and latent distillation, enabling end-to-end policy refinement. The approach achieves faster convergence, higher success rates, and stronger performance on data-scarce real-world cross-embodiment tasks, with scalability of action data further improving downstream VLA results.

ForceBand: Learning Forceful Manipulation with sEMG

arXiv

Botao He, Zhi Wang, Linna Kuang, Ishaan Ghosh, Jitendra Malik, Cornelia Fermuller, Tingfan Wu, Jiayuan Mao, Ruoshi Liu, Haozhi Qi, Yiannis Aloimonos

AI Summary

Human demonstrations for robot learning typically lack critical contact force information essential for force-sensitive manipulation. ForceBand, a low-cost wrist-worn sEMG system, addresses this by using human muscle activity to predict per-finger forces from sEMG and IMU signals, generating force-augmented demonstrations. This method achieves over 50% lower force prediction error than vision-based baselines. It also enables robots to achieve an 87% success rate on pick, squeeze, and place tasks requiring object-specific force control.

On-Policy Self-Distillation with Sampled Demonstrations Reduces Output Diversity

arXiv

Andrei Liviu Nicolicioiu, Mohammad Pezeshki, Aaron Courville

AI Summary

On-policy self-distillation, despite achieving strong pass@1 accuracy, significantly reduces output diversity and flattens pass@k curves. The paper theoretically shows this stems from compounding biases, where the teacher's feedback—conditioned on a sampled correct demonstration—amplifies existing probability gaps by tilting the base distribution towards dominant modes, unlike ideal reinforcement learning. Experiments on graph path-finding and science question-answering benchmarks confirm that self-distilled models exhibit substantially lower functional and semantic diversity, leading to failures in out-of-distribution settings requiring diverse strategies, even while matching or exceeding RL on average performance.

Neglected Free Lunch from Post-training: Progress Advantage for LLM Agents

arXiv

Changdae Oh, Wendi Li, Seongheon Park, Samuel Yeh, Tanwi Mallick, Sharon Li

AI Summary

Building process reward models for LLM agents is difficult due to complex, long-horizon interactions and stochastic environments, making scalable step-level evaluation challenging. This work proposes leveraging reinforcement learning post-training to derive an "implicit progress advantage," defined as the log-probability ratio between the RL-trained policy and its reference, which inherently recovers the optimal advantage function. This signal is annotation-free and a byproduct of standard RL training. The progress advantage consistently outperforms confidence baselines and dedicated trained reward models across multiple benchmarks and applications like test-time scaling, uncertainty quantification, and failure attribution, without requiring task-specific training.

Same Evidence, Different Answer: Auditing Order Sensitivity in Multimodal Large Language Models

arXiv

Akshay Paruchuri, Sanmi Koyejo, Ehsan Adeli

AI Summary

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit significant order sensitivity, providing inconsistent answers despite identical evidence due to input component shuffling, a critical reliability issue missed by standard benchmarks. The study introduces Facet-Probe, a five-facet audit applied to 18 frontier MLLMs, employing a Bayesian model to differentiate ordering noise from decoder stochasticity. All audited MLLMs displayed substantial order-sensitivity (24-50% flip rates, significantly exceeding decoder noise), with even the best models flipping 13.4% of the time, and prompt-level mitigations were found to be modality-specific and inadequate for general robustness.

All-electrical dephasing-protected spin qubits in altermagnets

arXiv

José Carlos Abadillo-Uriel, Andrea Maiani, Alberto Cortijo, Ramón Aguado, Rubén Seoane Souto

AI Summary

This paper addresses the need for scalable, all-electrical, dephasing-protected spin qubits. It proposes using altermagnetic semiconductors in gate-defined quantum dots, leveraging their intrinsic finite-momentum spin polarization for field-free qubit splitting. This method yields continuously tunable qubit frequencies via electrostatic gates, offers first-order insensitivity to electric field fluctuations for dephasing protection, and enables compatibility with superconducting resonators for high-fidelity readout. The platform supports a complete all-electrical gate set for both single-qubit control and two-qubit operations.

Deep Reinforcement Learning-Enhanced Event-Triggered Data-Driven Predictive Control for a 3D Cable-Driven Soft Robotic Arm

arXiv

Cheng Ouyang, Moeen Ul Islam, Kaixiang Zhang, Zhaojian Li, Xiaobo Tan, Dong Chen

AI Summary

Controlling soft robots with complex dynamics is computationally demanding for real-time model-free methods like DeePC, which continuously solve constrained optimizations. This paper proposes RL-ET-DeePC, an adaptive reinforcement learning framework where an RL policy determines when to invoke the DeePC optimizer, reducing unnecessary computations. Simulation results show up to a 66% reduction in optimization frequency with comparable tracking, while hardware experiments achieve a 34% reduction and consistent performance on a soft robotic arm.

arXiv

Han Bao, Bingyi Xia, Hanjing Ye, Yu Zhan, Hao Cheng, Baozhi Jia, Wenjun Xu, Jiankun Wang

AI Summary

This method integrates features into a DRL-compatible state embedding, achieving superior performance over baselines and enabling successful real-world vision-based crowd navigation.

RoboAtlas: Contextual Active SLAM

arXiv

Alexander Schperberg, Shivam K. Panda, Abraham P. Vinod, M. K. Jawed, Stefano Di Cairano

AI Summary

RoboAtlas addresses the challenge of adaptively balancing geometric exploration and semantic reasoning in Active SLAM. It proposes a contextual framework integrating a scalable 3D semantic mapping system (OpenRoboVox) with a multi-armed bandit to combine frontier exploration, global semantic-map reasoning, and egocentric VLM-based reasoning. The system achieved 100% task success in real-world large-scale environments (over 1800 m2) and state-of-the-art performance (90.6% SR with GPT-4o) on the GOAT-Bench "Val Unseen" benchmark. Notably, it outperformed all baselines using a much smaller VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), highlighting the importance of its semantic mapping for robust contextual Active SLAM.

FedReLa: Imbalanced Federated Learning via Re-Labeling

arXiv

Guangzheng Hu, Patricia Menéndez, Feng Liu, Mingming Gong, Guanghui Wang, Liuhua Peng

AI Summary

Federated learning suffers performance degradation due to coexisting global class imbalance and data heterogeneity, particularly when global class distribution is unknown and classes are severely absent across clients. FedReLa is proposed as a novel data-level approach that re-labels samples using a feature-dependent allocator to correct biased global decision boundaries without requiring knowledge of the global class distribution. This modular method significantly improves both minority and overall class accuracy on stepwise-imbalanced and long-tailed datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art techniques without additional communication overhead.

TriViewBench: Controlled Complexity Scaling for Multi-View Structural Reasoning in MLLMs

arXiv

Yu-Yang Chen, Lan-Zhe Guo

AI Summary

The core problem is the poor understanding of MLLM scalability under controlled structural complexity for multi-view structural reasoning. To address this, TriViewBench, a controlled three-view benchmark using synthetic 3D scenes with parameterized object count and occlusion, was introduced. Evaluation of 18 MLLMs revealed a consistent capability hierarchy and monotonic performance degradation with complexity, particularly severe for global recovery tasks. Error analysis identified distinct failure modes (occlusion blindness, cross-view identity confusion), and Chain-of-Thought prompting provided negligible benefit, suggesting the bottleneck lies in cross-view spatial representation rather than reasoning strategy.
