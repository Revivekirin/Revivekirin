Robot-Factored World Models via Robot Rendering

arXiv

Byungjun Kim, Taeksoo Kim, Hyunsoo Cha, Hanbyul Joo

AI Summary

Existing action-conditioned world models in robotics struggle to predict scene responses, as they either learn robot action realization or leak future interaction outcomes. The proposed robot-factored world models address this by decoupling action realization into a nominal trajectory via the robot's controller/kinematics and explicitly rendering this trajectory as robot geometry using its URDF. This method provides the world model with only visible robot geometry and scene context, allowing it to learn how objects respond to actions. Key results show this rendered interface outperforms vector-conditioned baselines, generalizes to unseen robot embodiments, and generates manipulation videos from human demonstrations.

SM4RT: Learning Structured Motion Geometry for 4D Reconstruction

arXiv

Shing Ho J. Lin, Wenzhao Zheng, Dong Zhuo, Yuqi Wu, Jie Zhou, Jiwen Lu

AI Summary

Monocular 4D reconstruction faces challenges in capturing the structured nature of physical motion, as most methods treat it as independent point-wise displacements instead of collective rigid-body kinematics. This paper proposes SM4RT, a Transformer-based method that represents scene dynamics using "Structure-of-Motion," decomposing motion into a compact set of SE(3) twist bases. Dense scene motion is recovered by assigning pixels to these shared bases, ensuring points on the same object share a common rigid-body trajectory. SM4RT jointly infers 3D geometry, world-coordinate motion, and scene kinematic structure from monocular video, achieving strong motion reconstruction while preserving geometric structure.

ICML

Kaixiong Gong, Xin Cai, Bin Lin, Hao Wang, Yunlong Lin, Mingzhe Zheng, Bohao Li, Jian-Wei Zhang, Miles Yang, Zhao Zhong, Liefeng Bo, Xiangyu Yue

AI Summary

Continuous unified multimodal models struggle with mismatched latent spaces for understanding (ViT) and generation (VAE), leading to optimization imbalance during joint training. The proposed Twins method creates a unified token space by channel-wise concatenating ViT and VAE features. Multiplexed storage and interaction of Rydberg spinwaves via the gradient echo memory protocol

arXiv

Bartosz Niewelt, Stanisław Kurzyna, Bartosz Kasza, Wojciech Wasilewski, Michał Parniak

AI Summary

Integration of collective Rydberg excitations with multiplexed quantum memories like GEM is challenged by rapid motional dephasing from large spinwave wavevectors. This work introduces a multi-photon addressing scheme utilizing two off-resonant driving fields at a magic angle to generate Rydberg excitations with near-zero momentum transfer and a closed wavevector loop, compatible with GEM. This method extends Rydberg spinwave lifetime almost tenfold, enabling the storage and manipulation of long-lived spinwaves in a multimode quantum memory. They demonstrated interaction-controlled attenuation and diffraction of retrieved optical signals, reestablishing Rydberg-GEM compatibility for multimode quantum memories with controllable long-range interactions.

Algebraic structure of Tiger codes

arXiv

Clément Poirson, Anthony Leverrier, Christophe Vuillot

AI Summary

Tiger codes, a family of multimode bosonic quantum codes unifying existing constructions, receive a rigorous algebraic treatment to understand their structure and logical operations. The proposed method starts from a kernel definition, proves that annihilation constraints admit a finite generating set, constructs an explicit orthonormal basis, and utilizes Fourier transforms to derive dual X- and Z-type descriptions. Key results include demonstrating that logical structure is governed by chain complex homology, extending the framework to non-linear number constraints, generalizing logical Pauli operators, and characterizing the logical action of non-Clifford gates via polynomial decomposition, relating polynomial degrees to the Clifford hierarchy for specific Tiger codes.

arXiv

Danyang Yan, Boyuan Wang, Jiaxun Liu, Boyuan Chen

AI Summary

Robots currently lack the ability to communicate through abstract visual projections like shadows, unlike humans, requiring a system that can reason about both bodily motion and its external visual transformation. This paper introduces a robotic system using a 21-degree-of-freedom soft-skinned hand and a learned, differentiable shadow self-model, which maps hand configurations to projected shadow appearance through task-agnostic self-exploration. Conformal Constraint Tightening for Chance-Constrained Motion Planning with Unknown Dynamics

arXiv

Shubham Natraj, Bruno Sinopoli, Yiannis Kantaros

AI Summary

Motion planning algorithms struggle to provide task-completion guarantees on real systems when true dynamics are unknown and only an approximate nominal model is available. This paper introduces a planner-agnostic constraint-tightening procedure that leverages conformal prediction to probabilistically bound nominal-to-true trajectory deviation. By tightening planning constraints using this bound, the method ensures that solving the tightened nominal problem provides a probabilistic task-completion guarantee on the true system. Empirical results demonstrate substantially improved task completion compared to nominal-model planning.

Local-Global Geometric Insights for Graph Neural Networks via Entropic Curvature

arXiv

Rachid Caich, Yassine Abbahaddou

AI Summary

Existing local graph curvature notions incompletely address GNN oversmoothing and oversquashing due to their failure to capture long-distance information propagation. This paper introduces Entropic Curvature, a global, transport-based curvature extending the Lott-Sturm-Villani framework to graphs, defining a tractable proxy called Weak Entropic Curvature. This theoretical framework yields a Poincare inequality for oversmoothing, a transport-entropy generalization bound, and an expansion paradox unifying oversmoothing and oversquashing as opposite ends of a single curvature spectrum. Practical mechanisms (E-Gate, ENT, MCR) derived from this theory demonstrate strong empirical performance on various node and graph classification benchmarks.

Uncertainty-Aware Fusion for Resilient Distributed Radar Sensing

arXiv

Christian Eckrich, Maik Pfefferkorn, Rolf Findeisen, Abdelhak M. Zoubir, Vahid Jamali

AI Summary

Distributed radar systems face increased target state estimation uncertainty due to communication link limitations like quantization distortion and latency when sharing measurements. To quantify this, the paper derives a Cramer-Rao lower bound (CRLB) for the fused target state by combining local Fisher information matrices, incorporating distortion bounds from rate-distortion theory, and modeling information aging. This framework reveals a fundamental tradeoff between update rate and quantization fidelity, governed by available channel capacity. Numerical simulations demonstrate this tradeoff, emphasizing the necessity of careful system parameter selection for maximum uncertainty reduction.
