PAC-MAN: Perception-Aware CBF-RL for Whole-Body Safety in Humanoid Dodgeball

ACL

Lizhi Yang, Junheng Li, Aaron D. Ames

AI Summary

The core problem is achieving whole-body safety and evasion for humanoid robots in dynamic projectile scenarios using only realistic onboard perception. The proposed method, PAC-MAN, is a perception-aware CBF-RL framework that couples control-barrier safety with onboard segmentation-masked depth from a head-mounted camera. Key results show that a fixed onboard camera is adequate for evasion, achieving performance near a state oracle, and a lightweight Link-CBF policy tolerates imperfect perception, succeeding on 95% of real-world throws on a Unitree G1.

Chimera: Designing and Chinchilla-Scaling Hybrid Visual Diffusion Transformers

arXiv

Chongjian Ge, Hanwen Jiang, Tianyu Wang, Jiuxiang Gu, Yiran Xu, Ziwen Chen, Shaoteng Liu, Jing Shi, Yicong Hong, Zefan Cai, Hailin Jin, Hao Tan

AI Summary

High-resolution, long-video, and multimodal visual generation faces prohibitive quadratic costs from full attention. Chimera, a hybrid visual diffusion transformer, addresses this with Kimi Delta Attention (O(N)), Multi-head Latent Attention, convolutions, and sparse MoE layers, scaled using the HeteroP scheme and Chinchilla-style laws. It achieves 7.3x compute efficiency over full-attention baselines and zero-shot extrapolates to 30-second videos from 5-second training clips with only 6.5% FID degradation, also providing compute-optimal scaling laws for image/video pretraining.

OSReward: Instituting Standardized Evaluation for Cross-Platform Computer-Use Reward Models

arXiv

Qiushi Sun, Kanzhi Cheng, Yian Wang, Bowen Yang, Hang Yan, Liheng Chen, Fangzhi Xu, Zichen Ding, Nuo Chen, Jialin Cao, Xingdong Gong, Zehao Li, Kaiming Jin, Xinfeng Yuan, Zhoumianze Liu, Jingyang Gong, Zhangyue Yin, Jiahui Gao, Zhiyong Wu, Tianbao Xie, Jianbing Zhang, Ben Kao, Lingpeng Kong

AI Summary

The core problem is the unexamined reliability of VLM judges used to evaluate Computer-Using Agents (CUAs) and provide reward signals, noting a systematic leniency bias and cost issues with current solutions. To address this, the authors introduce OSReward, a comprehensive, multi-stage human-annotated benchmark for evaluating VLM judges on diverse CUA trajectories, including hard cases and fine-grained metrics. They then train OS-Shepherd (9B and 35B) open reward models on OS-Shepherd-100K, an open corpus, achieving comparable reliability to commercial judges at 30-60% lower cost.

FA-RDP: A Frequency-Adaptive Reactive Diffusion Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation

arXiv

Lifeng Zhuo, Wendi Chen, Han Xue, Shirun Tang, Jun Lv, Cewu Lu, Chuan Wen

AI Summary

The core problem in contact-rich manipulation using diffusion policies is a fundamental tradeoff: fixed inference frequencies force a compromise between preserving pre-contact action multimodality and ensuring post-contact reactivity. FA-RDP addresses this with a frequency-adaptive approach, employing a shared multi-frequency visual-force Transformer that, guided by a learned multimodality indicator, dynamically selects between multi-step low-frequency sampling pre-contact and one-step high-frequency sampling post-contact. This is further enhanced by Manifold Consistency Distillation (MCD) for predicting actions on the robot action manifold. Experiments demonstrate FA-RDP achieves the highest success rates in contact-rich tasks while effectively preserving diverse pre-contact trajectory modes.

VAD: Attributing Visual Evidence for Target Reconstruction in Multimodal On-Policy Distillation

arXiv

Kangning Zhang, Yixing Li, Shuai Shao, Qingyao Li, Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, Jianghao Lin, Wenxiang Jiao, Yuan Lu, Weiwen Liu, Weinan Zhang, Yong Yu

AI Summary

Multimodal on-policy distillation's teacher corrections mix visual evidence with linguistic priors, making it difficult to isolate purely visual guidance for students. To address this, Visual Attribution Distillation (VAD) introduces a counterfactual target-reconstruction algorithm. VAD estimates the visually attributable part of a correction by evaluating a teacher with and without visual evidence, then projects the original correction onto this visual evidence proxy to reconstruct a clean, visually-aligned target for primary supervision. Across six fine-grained visual benchmarks, VAD outperforms direct distillation and visual-advantage weighting, demonstrating its ability to deliver task-relevant visual corrections.

MixFrag: Fragility-Guided Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization for Vision Transformers

arXiv

Md. Mehrab Hossain Opi, Robiul Islam Ryad, Md. Umar Faruk

AI Summary

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) for Vision Transformers (ViTs) inefficiently allocates uniform bit-widths, ignoring heterogeneous component sensitivity. MixFrag addresses this by estimating component-level quantization fragility via KL divergence and formulating bit allocation as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem for adaptive layer-wise precision. This method achieves competitive classification performance on ImageNet-1K and state-of-the-art results on COCO object detection/instance segmentation, improving performance by up to 9.6 AP in challenging settings.

$β$-OPSD: Deriving with Policy Optimization, Training with Self-Distillation

arXiv

Jiawei Xu, Minghui Liu, Juzheng Zhang, Tom Goldstein, Furong Huang

AI Summary

Vanilla On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) for reasoning language models is brittle due to its fixed formulation within a broader policy optimization framework. The authors propose $β$-OPSD, a generalization that introduces $β$ as a controllable regularization parameter; they derive its optimal policy as a geometric interpolation between a reference policy and a privileged teacher, which is then efficiently approximated via self-distillation by mixing token-level logits. $β$-OPSD consistently outperforms vanilla OPSD on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, significantly improving optimization stability and downstream reasoning performance without sacrificing efficiency.

arXiv

Jiacheng Tao, Qingyun Sun, Haonan Yuan, Ziwei Zhang, Jianxin Li

AI Summary

Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (MM-RAG) struggles with complex multi-hop reasoning, as existing graph methods face a dilemma between retrieval noise from fine-grained features and lost evidence from coarse representations. arXiv

SVR: Self-Verifying Refinement via Joint Verdict-Confidence Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Test-Time Compute

ACL

Hongyu Chen, Liang Lin, Guangrun Wang

AI Summary

The paper addresses the inefficiency of uniform test-time compute for language models and the reliance of refinement methods on external verifiers. It introduces Self-Verifying Refinement (SVR), an oracle-free multi-turn reinforcement learning framework where the model learns to adaptively allocate compute by generating solutions with self-verification verdicts and confidence scores, refining until self-verified as correct and confident. SVR achieves 0.563 accuracy on math reasoning benchmarks with only 2.99 average inference turns, outperforming strong baselines and oracle-guided references while using substantially fewer turns than fixed-budget inference.

Graph Neural Multilevel Preconditioners for Iterative Solvers

arXiv

Zechen Zhang, Rui Peng Li, Yousef Saad

AI Summary

Classical preconditioners like AMG lack robustness for indefinite or nonsymmetric sparse linear systems, and the utility of AMG-style hierarchies in GNN preconditioners remains underexplored.
