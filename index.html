CVPR

Agon: Competitive Cross-Model RL with Implicit Rival Grading of Reasoning

arXiv

Vladislav Beliaev

AI Summary

Standard reinforcement learning (RL) for reasoning models evaluates only final answers rather than the step-by-step reasoning process, which incentivizes verbosity over actual logical depth. To solve this, **Agon** introduces a competitive cross-model RL framework where two models alternate roles—one drafting a solution and the other reading it to solve—and are rewarded for out-reasoning each other without requiring manual process labels. This approach doubles the pass@1 performance of GRPO on the DeepMath hard split and yields consistent gains over traditional RL and Mixture-of-Agents baselines across competitive programming and diverse model families (including Qwen and Gemma).

RL Post-Training Builds Compositional Reasoning Strategies

ICML

Azwar Abdulsalam, Nishil Patel, Andrew Saxe

AI Summary

The core problem is whether RL post-training can build new, higher-level compositional reasoning strategies or merely amplifies existing primitive skills. The proposed method involves pretraining a Transformer on primitive symbol-rewrite chains in a rewrite-grammar environment, followed by RL post-training on a Trace-based reasoning task using only a binary final-answer reward. Key results show RL solves held-out problems largely unaddressed by the pretrained model, achieving this by reorganizing primitive competence into reusable sequential and parallel compositional procedures. RL's effectiveness stems from its selective exploration, focusing on valid, reusable structures rather than simply exploration volume.

Geometric Interpretation of Sum Photon Blockade

arXiv

Timur Khudaiberganov

AI Summary

This paper offers a geometric interpretation of the sum photon blockade effect in multimode quantum optical systems, representing the blockade condition as orthogonality within a Hilbert space. The core problem addressed is understanding and enabling robust, scalable non-classical light generation despite decoherence. A key method introduced is "dark-state typicality," showing that as the number of modes increases, the dark subspace expands to occupy a unit fraction of the Hilbert space. Key results include calculating the effect's practical feasibility and robustness against decoherence, particularly dephasing, and demonstrating how this approach transitions fragile mechanisms to macroscopically robust non-classical light, enabling the design of scalable quantum devices.
