Perception Before Supervision: Self-Contained Visual Distillation from Counterfactual Blind Spots

arXiv

Shravan Venkatraman, Omkar Thawakar, Ritesh Thawkar, Abdelrahman Shaker, Rao Muhammad Anwer

AI Summary

The core problem is that MLLM self-improvement typically relies on coarse scalar rewards or visual distillation methods requiring external annotations or stronger models for dense, token-level supervision. This paper introduces CVPD (Contrastive Counterfactual Visual Process Distillation), a fully self-contained framework for dense, on-policy, token-level visual self-distillation. CVPD identifies "visual blind spots" where a model encodes but fails to utilize perceptual information by using a three-gate Counterfactual Criterion to generate dense contrastive supervision directly from the model's own responses. On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, CVPD significantly outperformed six self-evolving baselines, including those using GPT-4o supervision, with gains of +3.60 on OCRBench and +3.38 on MMStar Fine-Grained Perception, without any regressions.

Multimodal Model Diffing for Feature Discovery and Control

ICML

Hunar Batra, Lachin Naghashyar, Ashkan Khakzar, Philip Torr, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Constantin Venhoff, Ronald Clark

AI Summary

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack identifiable, auditable, or controllable internal features responsible for their visual understanding, with existing interpretability methods failing to isolate multimodal-specific features or enable direct control. This paper introduces MMDiff, a multimodal model-diffing framework that trains multimodal Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to serve as feature-level interfaces for discovering and controlling MLLM behavior. MMDiff supports feature isolation by diffing base-LM and multimodal SAEs, task-specific causal feature detection, and feature-level control through removal or steering. Key results show MMDiff identifies sparse, causal features whose removal selectively degrades target behaviors (e.g., 12% spatial, 17% OCR, 24% safety attack reduction) without impacting VQA, and whose steering improves accuracy (+3.6% spatial, +1.8% OCR).

DistMoE: Private-data Rehearsal-free Routing in Mixture-of-Experts for Distributed Instruction Tuning

arXiv

Mainak Singha, Niccolò Biondi, Elisa Ricci, Subhankar Roy

AI Summary

Adapting MLLMs to diverse visual-language domains is challenging due to distributed, private data and the need for costly centralized training. DistMoE proposes a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) approach that augments public FFNs with client-specific private FFN experts in the language decoder to acquire domain knowledge. It introduces a public-anchored expert composition stage, updating only routers and lightweight private projection adapters with an isotropic regularization loss on mixed local/public data, enabling rehearsal-free cross-client composition. DistMoE achieves flexible expert reuse, effective domain adaptation, and competitive performance while preserving modular control over client-specific knowledge.

Consilience for Verifier-Free Test-Time Scaling

arXiv

Lecheng Kong, Like Hui, Haitao Mao, Jun Huan

AI Summary

Existing confidence-based verifier-free test-time scaling (VF-TTS) methods for LLMs fail on complex tasks by favoring confidently wrong answers due to insufficient exploration. The paper proposes "consilience," a novel selection framework that addresses this by evaluating the temporal asymmetry of confidence during reasoning. Consilience implements a combinatorial metric that actively penalizes high initial confidence (lack of exploration) while strictly demanding high final certainty. Extensive experiments on graduate-level mathematics and free-form code generation demonstrate that consilience effectively outperforms existing baselines.

RoSE: A Robotic Soft Esophagus for Endoprosthetic Stent Testing

arXiv

Dipankar Bhattacharya, Sherine Jesna V. A., Leo K. Cheng, Weiliang Xu

AI Summary

Incomplete endoprosthetic stent design and guidelines, due to limited clinical data, hinder understanding of stent migration and swallow efficacy impacts. To address this, a robotic soft esophagus (RoSE) was developed as an in vitro platform to test stents with differing radial stiffness, measuring radial force (RF), migration, and intra-bolus pressure signatures (IBPS) under varied peristalsis. Results showed higher RF from stiffer stents minimized migration, but significantly stiffer stents could decrease IBPS—especially with higher-concentration boluses—potentially reducing swallow efficacy and causing dysphagia if they buckle.

arXiv

AI Summary

Energy-Structured Latent World Models with Neural Time Fields for Physically Constistent Open-World Motion Planning

arXiv

Yapeng Liu, Yuanzhao Zhai, Bo Ding, Huaimin Wang, Lin Wang

AI Summary

Physically consistent motion planning in embodied AI is challenging because current latent world models learn unconstrained, implicit physics, leading to unreliable open-world navigation. We propose an Energy-Structured Latent World Model (ELWM) where the latent state explicitly carries energy and momentum for causal transitions, integrated into Physics-Conditioned Neural Time Fields (PC-NTF) for motion planning via the Eikonal equation. This method reduces 0.8-s motion-prediction NRMSE from 0.36 to 0.29 compared to generic latent models. Additionally, PC-NTF improves navigation success from 81.3% to 89.7% and cuts physical collision rates from 12.1% to 5.8%, demonstrating a clear path to safer, dynamically feasible motion planning.

ACL

On the Renormalization in Conformal Quantum Gravity

arXiv

Ioseph L. Buchbinder, Petr M. Lavrov, Thomas M. Sangy, Ilya L. Shapiro

AI Summary

The core problem is ensuring one-loop conformal invariant renormalizability in classically conformal, gauge-invariant theories in curved spacetime, despite non-conformal gauge-fixing and ghost terms. This paper extends prior BRST symmetry-based proofs to conformal quantum gravity, including its coupling to conformal matter fields. RynnValue: Scaling Robotic Value Foundation Models with Temporal Distance

arXiv

Dongchi Huang, Hongyin Zhang, Bohan Hou, Siteng Huang, Zhian Su, Hang Guo, Tong Lu, Zhaofeng Xu, Jiahao Tang, Jianfei Yang, Donglin Wang, Peixi Peng, Mingxiu Chen, Deli Zhao, Xin Li

AI Summary

General-purpose reward models are a bottleneck for scaling robot learning due to non-transferable supervision methods like preferences or normalized progress. RynnValue proposes a value foundation model that replaces these anchors with temporal distance—the directed cost-to-go from an observation to a language-specified goal, derived directly from timestamps. This enables scaling to over 7,000 hours of data without preference labels, augmented by robust temporal learning techniques. RynnValue achieves state-of-the-art performance (Kendall's tau_a 0.675) on RBM-EVAL-OOD, surpassing preference-supervised models, and significantly improves real-world policy success rates (e.g., 52.5% to 72.5% online) when converted to dense rewards.

arXiv

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards suffers from uninformative group-relative signals when rollouts are uniformly correct or wrong. SKALD (Skill-Anchored Latent Distillation) addresses this via an on-policy self-distillation framework where a student model learns from a teacher conditioned on abstract skill cards, internalizing the skill advantage into shared parameters without needing privileged input at test time. arXiv

ADOPD: Reference-Privileged On-Policy Distillation for MLLM-Based Industrial Anomaly Detection

arXiv

Jingtai He, Shiyuan Meng, Wenchao Meng, Qinmin Yang

AI Summary

Industrial anomaly detection (IAD) requires fine-grained analysis, and while MLLMs can leverage reference comparisons for improved accuracy, this method incurs significant inference-time costs; the core problem is internalizing these benefits into the model parameters. The proposed method, ADOPD, is a reference-privileged on-policy distillation framework where a reference-aware teacher supervises a query-only student. The teacher evaluates student rollouts with matched and mismatched references, using the matched-reference log-ratio for token-level learning direction and the likelihood gap for sequence-level weight calibration. ADOPD achieves 77.31% average accuracy on the MMAD benchmark under zero-shot inference, improving the Qwen3-VL-4B backbone by 6.14 points and outperforming its one-shot setting by 2.64 points.

RoboSeg: Online Part-Level Semantic Reconstruction for Robotic Manipulation via a Single Eye-in-Hand Camera

arXiv

Zhaochen Lan, Mengxiang Lin

AI Summary

Robotic manipulation necessitates identifying actionable object parts, not just categories, without relying on CAD models or pre-scanned meshes. RoboSeg proposes an online system that integrates VLM functional-part discovery, asynchronous RGB-D semantic reconstruction via an eye-in-hand camera, and task-oriented grasp generation. It uses separate threads for geometry (TSDF fusion) and semantics (SAM3 part masks), fusing projected masks via temporal voting into a part-labeled point cloud to guide grasp selection. RoboSeg achieved 83.4% mean part IoU and, in physical trials, consistently contacted the requested part in all 24 attempts, resulting in 21/24 successful tasks.
