OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments

arXiv

Xiang Fan, Yuheng Wang, Jiayuan Yan, Bohan Fang, Wisdom Oluchi Ikezogwo, Ainaz Eftekhar, Max Argus, Jason Ren, Jiafei Duan, Ranjay Krishna

AI Summary

Developing a single policy that effectively adapts manipulation strategies to diverse robot morphologies is a core challenge. OmniAct addresses this by conditioning a flow-matching policy on a kinematic graph representation of the robot's structure within a specific scene, trained across one million procedurally generated embodiments. This method achieves 63.0% success on held-out simulations (outperforming strong baselines and large models) and nearly twice the success rate on unseen real-world robots. OmniAct demonstrates learning distinct, morphology-specific manipulation strategies that leverage each robot's unique capabilities.

DiaCap: Jointly Learning Who Speaks When for Audiovisual Dialogue Captioning

arXiv

Binfei Ji, Zhiwen Cao, Anqi Yang, Prem Seetharaman, Shuo Yang, Ratheesh Kalarot, Yingyan Celine Lin, Mingze Xu

AI Summary

The paper addresses comprehensive audiovisual dialogue captioning, aiming to produce transcripts, timestamps, and visual speaker descriptions for every utterance by unifying utterance timing and audiovisual diarization. Their proposed method, DiaCap, is an audiovisual LLM trained in two stages: initial synthetic captioning for dense labeling, followed by reinforcement learning on human-corrected clips for fine-grained feedback. DiaCap matches Gemini 4 Argon in audiovisual diarization and significantly outperforms it in timestamp accuracy (76.59 vs. 42.76 TIME), also showing strong performance on multilingual/long videos and transferability to QA.

CoT-Proxy: Cross-Format Transfer for Distilling Long-Horizon Policies

arXiv

Dawid J. Kopiczko, Tijmen Blankevoort, Yuki M. Asano

AI Summary

The core problem is distilling long-horizon policies from strong, closed teacher models whose internal chain-of-thought (CoT) is hidden, preventing standard imitation learning. The proposed method distills policies solely from the teacher's observable actions, even with the student's reasoning block empty during training, enabling "cross-format transfer" where the student uses its own CoT at inference. The paper further introduces "CoT-proxies," short summaries of the teacher's reasoning, to enhance distillation. This approach significantly improved student task success, with Qwen3-4B on AppWorld rising from 7% to 48% when distilling from GPT-5.6 Luna using visible outputs and CoT-proxies.

Lost on Campus: Evaluating Embodied Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in the Wild

NeurIPS

Zehan Zheng, Yanyuan Chen, Deming Li, Yutao Tang, Jianwen Xie, Alan Yuille, Jieneng Chen, Cheng Peng

AI Summary

Embodied Spatial Reasoning (ESR) in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is inadequately evaluated, with existing benchmarks failing to capture real-world outdoor complexities. This paper proposes Lost on Campus, a benchmark utilizing large-scale real-world outdoor 3D environments reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting, featuring a unified reasoning-action framework that combines diagnostic QA and interactive navigation to assess six fine-grained ESR aspects. AI Summary

ROAM-Bench: Evaluating Embodied Active Spatial Intelligence through Evidence Verification

ACL

Xingjian Ran, Xiaoye Mo, Sihao Liu, Li Luo, Taiqiang Wu, Ji Zhao, Wenqi Shao, Hongyang Li, Jiaqi Wang, Bo Dai

AI Summary

Existing embodied spatial intelligence benchmarks inadequately verify if agents achieve correct outcomes by truly fulfilling task requirements. ROAM-Bench is introduced, featuring 250 diverse tasks and utilizing strong, independent checks (e.g., geometry, coverage) to robustly assess both task outcomes and requirement satisfaction under robot constraints. Human evaluators achieved 84% strict task success, with all correct outcomes satisfying these stringent checks, validating the benchmark's rigor. Frontier vision-language models exhibited two failure modes—correct outcomes without fulfilled requirements, and incorrect outcomes despite meeting stronger checks—highlighting the need for improved target observations and calibrated feedback.

iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction

arXiv

Vansh Kapoor, Max Sobol Mark, Sreyas Venkataraman, Max Simchowitz, Aviral Kumar

AI Summary

World-action models (WAMs) are computationally expensive due to explicit future scene generation, hindering real-time control while maintaining action-prediction quality. iWAM proposes to condition the action policy on informative intermediate features from a single, efficient video-model evaluation, rather than full future frame generation, by learning a bidirectional flow map to amortize future computation. This method recovers WAM's generalization benefits while substantially reducing inference costs across simulated and real-robot benchmarks.

arXiv

AI Summary

Robot policies trained on human demonstrations lack coverage of diverse low-level behaviors crucial for autonomous improvement and connecting challenging states. This paper proposes "midtraining" using $β$-Data, collected via adversarial teleoperation where a human perturbs the environment while an operator completes the task; trajectory segments are then relabeled and used to finetune a pretrained policy. Self-arresting vortex rearrangements in pinned spinning-down superfluids

arXiv

Anantharaman Sekharipuram Viswanathan, Dipankar Bhattacharya

AI Summary

The onset, progress, and termination of collective vortex migration in spinning-down, pinned superfluids are poorly understood. This work demonstrates that the initial vortex exodus occurs when a critical-angle vortex-free sector forms near the wall. Subsequently, this exodus weakens the boundary superfluid flow, thereby self-arresting further vortex exits. The resulting vortex depletion region has well-defined dimensions, determined by critical unpinning velocity, container size, and initial rotation rate, limiting angular momentum transfer.

Reinforcement Learning Can Be Recovered From Its Own Rollouts

arXiv

Jiayi Sheng, Jingwen Gu, Guanning Zeng, Zhaoyi Zhou, Stefano Viel, Qianqian Wang, Fahim Tajwar, Andrea Zanette

AI Summary

Traditional reinforcement learning discards model-generated rollouts after each update, with attempts to replay the original algorithm on archived rollouts leading to catastrophic collapse. This work proposes maximum likelihood (ML) on the archived rollouts as a remedy, explaining why direct replay fails. Surprisingly, replaying rollouts using ML in their generation order recovers the full performance of online RL, with a single ML update per batch closely tracking the entire learning curve. This suggests that much of RL's compute discovers an improved sampling distribution rather than solely learning from samples.

GREMLIN: Generating Random Environments for Meta-Learning with Interacting Networks

arXiv

Octave Gaspard

AI Summary

The core problem is whether randomly parameterized neural environments can supply useful experience for meta-learning a reinforcement learning algorithm. The proposed method, GREMLIN, generates sixteen neural environments combining bounded vector dynamics with random strategy and outcome rewards, used to train a teacher within DiscoRL's framework. Key results show that while DQN generally outperformed GREMLIN, the meta-learned rule significantly improved agent performance over an untrained teacher on all 26 games, demonstrating that generated experience can indeed improve a learning rule.

AIChipBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Automated AI Accelerator Design

arXiv

Guangyuan Wang, Chengran Li, Minchen Luo, Jichen Feng, Mengdi Wang, Yifan Zhang

AI Summary

LLM coding agents struggle to correctly implement AI accelerator hardware, such as LLM decoders in RTL, because state-dependent errors are difficult to detect with output-only testing. AIChipBench is proposed to benchmark agents on this task, evaluating their ability to design synthesizable LLM decoders against an integer reference and varying debugging feedback. Key results show that explicit state-mismatch feedback significantly improves agent success and efficiency over output-only feedback. However, many "accepted" designs fail under more rigorous final tests, underscoring the necessity for state-aware debugging tools and more comprehensive benchmarks for hardware-designing AI agents.

Shifter Quantization: Compact Representations for Native Integer Computation

arXiv

Yufeng Huang

AI Summary

Groupwise quantization's limitation in enabling full dot products for efficient GPU inference in LLMs is addressed by Shifter Quantization (SQ). SQ reconstructs native-width integer operands by appending and shifting shared bits, with a Shifter Integer (SHI) variant for INT4 kernels. SQ and SHI consistently outperform standard groupwise methods in both quality and inference speed across various bit widths. Key results include 2.71-3.77x higher 2-bit prefill throughput over llama.cpp and 1.43-2.47x higher 4-bit prefill throughput than NInfer, alongside substantial KL divergence reductions at all precisions compared to baselines.

arXiv

Erfei Cui, Shengjia Zhao, Yan Qin, Kaixuan Wang, Junming Chen, Ping Luo, Tianxing Chen

AI Summary

Existing embodied AI safety evaluations are insufficient, failing to adequately assess models' understanding of environmental hazards and their ability to make safe decisions. Can a Frontier LLM Repair a Quantized Network by Tool-Free Weight-Space Programming?

arXiv

Steffen Urban

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the significant accuracy degradation (from 81.4% to 60.4%) when a CNN is quantized to 4-bit using round-to-nearest. The proposed method involves providing frontier LLMs (Claude family) with network weights as text and tasking them to repair or perform 4-bit quantization without tools, data, or inference capabilities. A frontier LLM (Opus 5.5) successfully recovered most lost accuracy, achieving 77.95% by balancing rounding errors within kernels, 3.5 pp below the float network. When only given the float network, LLMs typically reverted to plain RTN, though one model reached 70.0% with mild clipping.

Training Lightweight Gaze Models without Target Gaze Annotation: What Makes a Good Teacher?

arXiv

Scott J. Lewis, Di Fu

AI Summary

The core problem is to train lightweight gaze target estimation models using scalable, model-generated labels, while identifying what constitutes a good teacher model for label generation *before* student training. The proposed method involves a controlled comparison of eleven different gaze-point label sources (teachers), keeping student models, training protocols, and datasets fixed, evaluating all students against human annotation. Key results demonstrate that imperfect model-generated labels can match or even outperform human-annotated supervision for lightweight students, superficial teacher competence can be misleading (e.g., LLMs copying inputs), and effective teachers can be reliably identified pre-training via validation label error or a small set of annotated clips.

MESSI: Multi-Expert Soccer Skill Integration for Physics-Based Character Control

arXiv

Xiaogang Peng, Jiaqi Zou, Lei Zhong, Sirui Xu, Huaizu Jiang

AI Summary

Physics-based soccer characters struggle with smooth transitions between diverse skills because sequential execution can leave the character and ball in unfamiliar states, causing individual skills to fail. The proposed MESSI framework first trains task-competent experts, distills them into a unified skill-conditioned Transformer, and then uses a shared, context-conditioned low-rank adapter. This adapter activates during skill switching to help the target skill handle unfamiliar entry states, with an exit mechanism returning control to the original student policy to preserve native skill performance. MESSI substantially improves skill transition success, increasing dribbling-to-shooting success from 19.8% to 61.8%.
