MolmoAct2: Action Reasoning Models for Real-world Deployment

arXiv

Haoquan Fang, Jiafei Duan, Donovan Clay, Sam Wang, Shuo Liu, Weikai Huang, Xiang Fan, Wei-Chuan Tsai, Shirui Chen, Yi Ru Wang, Shanli Xing, Jaemin Cho, Jae Sung Park, Ainaz Eftekhar, Peter Sushko, Karen Farley, Angad Wadhwa, Cole Harrison, Winson Han, Ying-Chun Lee, Eli VanderBilt, Rose Hendrix, Suveen Ellawela, Lucas Ngoo, Joyce Chai, Zhongzheng Ren, Ali Farhadi, Dieter Fox, Ranjay Krishna

AI Summary

Current VLA models for robots fall short on real-world deployment criteria due to being closed, hardware-intensive, high-latency, and unreliable. MolmoAct2 is introduced as an open action reasoning model featuring MolmoER, a specialized VLM backbone, an OpenFAST action tokenizer, new large-scale bimanual datasets, and MolmoThink for adaptive-depth reasoning to reduce latency. MolmoAct2 outperforms strong baselines (e.g., Pi-05) across 7 benchmarks, and MolmoER surpasses GPT-5 and Gemini Robotics ER-1.5 on 13 embodied-reasoning tasks. All models, training code, and data are open-sourced for practical deployment.

Enhancing RL Generalizability in Robotics through SHAP Analysis of Algorithms and Hyperparameters

arXiv

Lingxiao Kong, Cong Yang, Oya Deniz Beyan, Zeyd Boukhers

AI Summary

RL models exhibit poor generalization across environments due to high sensitivity to algorithm and hyperparameter configurations, whose specific contributions to the generalization gap are not quantitatively understood. The authors propose an explainable framework utilizing SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to quantify the impact of configurations on RL performance and generalization in robotics, enabling SHAP-guided configuration selection. This method reveals distinct and consistent configuration impact patterns across tasks, leading to improved RL generalizability and providing actionable insights for practitioners.

ACL

AI Summary

Trust, but Verify: Peeling Low-Bit Transformer Networks for Training Monitoring

arXiv

Arian Eamaz, Farhang Yeganegi, Mojtaba Soltanalian

AI Summary

Standard metrics fail to adequately monitor layer-wise optimization quality in deep neural networks, particularly expensive Transformers, leading to hidden inefficiencies and performance degradation. The paper proposes a layer-wise "peeling framework" where individual transformer layers are locally re-optimized against intermediate representations to create fine-grained, achievable baselines. This method reveals significant hidden optimization inefficiencies and under-optimized layers, even in quantized models, often surpassing the original model's performance. The proposed bounds consistently separate apparent convergence from effective optimality, highlighting optimization opportunities invisible to aggregate loss.

Reinforcement Learning for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems through Orchestration Traces

arXiv

Chenchen Zhang

AI Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of optimizing reinforcement learning for the *orchestration* of LLM-based multi-agent systems, encompassing work spawning, delegation, and communication, beyond individual agent actions. It proposes studying these systems through "orchestration traces," temporal interaction graphs that capture multi-agent interactions and decisions. This analysis identifies three technical axes: eight families of reward design (including orchestration-specific rewards), eight types of reward/credit signals, and five decomposed orchestration learning sub-decisions (noting a gap in RL for the stopping decision). The work synthesizes academic and industrial approaches, releasing a curated paper pool and a JSON schema for replayable traces.

arXiv

AI Summary

Automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems are brittle for dysarthric speech, and it's unclear if current audio-language models can effectively leverage additional clinical context to improve performance. Researchers created a benchmark using the SAP dataset to test if diagnosis labels, speech ratings, and clinical descriptions improve dysarthric ASR via prompting, then explored context-dependent LoRA fine-tuning. A decoupled diffusion planner that adapts to changing cost limits by using cost-conditioned generation for safety and reward gradients for performance

arXiv

Rufeng Chen, Zhaofan Zhang, Zhejiang Yang, Hechang Chen, Sihong Xie

AI Summary

Offline safe reinforcement learning policies struggle to adapt to varying safety budgets at deployment, as existing diffusion guidance often treats reward and constraint satisfaction as competing objectives. This paper proposes Safe Decoupled Guidance Diffusion (SDGD), which reinterprets adaptive safe trajectory generation as sampling from a constrained distribution. SDGD uses cost-conditioned classifier-free guidance to bias sampling toward safety limits and reward-gradient guidance for higher return, introducing Feasible Trajectory Relabeling (FTR) to prevent reward-induced cost drift. SDGD achieves superior safety compliance on the DSRL benchmark (94.7% of tasks) and the highest reward among safe methods on 21 tasks.

arXiv

AI Summary

ICML

AI Summary

Vision-language-action (VLA) models face poor real-world generalization due to the visual domain gap and limited diversity of inexpensive simulated training data. This paper introduces an efficient video augmentation framework that converts simulated VLA videos into realistic training videos while preserving task semantics and action trajectories. AI Summary

The core problem is enhancing the accuracy of NSCLC histological subtype differentiation (ADC vs. SCC) for personalized treatment, especially when expensive and radiation-intensive physical PET scans are unavailable. The proposed method utilizes "virtual scanning" where a 3D Pix2Pix GAN synthesizes pseudo-PET volumes from anatomical CT scans. Bolek: A Multimodal Language Model for Molecular Reasoning

arXiv

Frederic Grabowski, Jacek Szczerbiński, Maciej Jaśkowski, Kalina Jasińska-Kobus, Paweł Dąbrowski-Tumański, Tomasz Jetka, Bartosz Topolski

AI Summary

Molecular property models often lack auditable outputs, with classic predictors offering no rationale and language models providing weakly grounded explanations. To address this, Bolek is introduced as a compact multimodal language model that grounds natural-language reasoning in molecular structure by injecting a Morgan fingerprint embedding into an instruction-tuned text decoder. Bolek is fine-tuned on molecular alignment tasks and downstream reasoning using synthetic chains-of-thought anchored in molecular features. Key results show Bolek significantly outperforms baseline LLMs on classification tasks (mean ROC/PR AUC from 0.55 to 0.76) and provides more grounded, verifiable explanations, citing molecular descriptors with high accuracy and generalizing well to unseen tasks.

arXiv

AI Summary

Dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are bottlenecked by the frequent, temporally redundant execution of their expensive VLM backbone. Latent Bridge is proposed as a lightweight model that predicts VLM output deltas between timesteps, allowing the action head to operate on these predictions and reducing periodic calls to the VLM. This method generalizes across different VLA architectures (feature-space or KV-cache bridges) and achieves 95-100% performance retention across diverse manipulation benchmarks. It concurrently reduces VLM calls by 50-75%, yielding a 1.65-1.73x net per-episode speedup.

Visual Latents Know More Than They Say: Unsilencing Latent Reasoning in MLLMs

arXiv

Xin Zhang, Qiqi Tao, Jiawei Du, Moyun Liu, Joey Tianyi Zhou

AI Summary

Multimodal models suffer from "Silenced Visual Latents," where semantically enriched visual latents are systematically suppressed from contributing to final answers, as the autoregressive objective favors direct visual input. To address this, the paper proposes an inference-time optimization method that keeps backbone parameters frozen. This method involves a two-stage process: query-guided contrastive latent-visual alignment to warm up latents and improve semantic quality, followed by a confidence-progression reward to route predictions through latent reasoning. Experiments show that this inference-time latent optimization effectively unleashes the suppressed reasoning capacity of visual latents across various benchmarks and backbones without parameter updates.

Private Speech Classification without Collapse: Stabilized DP Training and Offline Distillation

arXiv

Yadi Wen, Tianxin Li, Enji Liang, Rong Du, Yue Fu

AI Summary

The core problem is strong-privacy (ε≤1) supervised speech classification on imbalanced tasks, where DP-SGD causes model collapse, hindering the distillation of an audio-only model from a potentially multimodal private teacher. The proposed method is a two-stage protocol: a DP-stabilized multimodal teacher is trained on private data, then an audio-only student is offline distilled using teacher outputs on a recording-disjoint auxiliary dataset. This approach, incorporating a DP-stabilizing acoustic front-end, adaptive loss reweighting, and privileged-modality dropout, addresses optimization instability, minority-class erosion, and modality mismatch to enable robust private speech classification.

Tensegrity crutches with compliance from a pre-stressed self-tensile module improve ground reaction force profiles, speed, effort, comfort, and perceived stability

arXiv

Jingxian Gu, Joanna Spyra, Andrew Walski, Lyla Elsaesser, Samuel Bierner, Dobromir Dotov

AI Summary

Rigid crutches cause secondary injury and limit sensory feedback, while spring-loaded designs offer compliance but may compromise stability. This paper proposes a biologically inspired tensegrity crutch featuring a pre-stressed, self-tensile compliant module to address these limitations. Both tensegrity and spring-loaded crutches reduced peak loading rates compared to rigid designs. However, the tensegrity crutch uniquely improved user effort, comfort, pain, and usability without reducing perceived stability or walking speed, unlike the spring-loaded alternative.
