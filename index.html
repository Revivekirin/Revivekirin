ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters

arXiv

Ruixi Yu, Dairu Liu, Xuchuan Chen, Zekun Qi, Zekai Li, Zhuo Cao, Luyang Niu, Yingsheng Zhang, Sikai Liang, Ke Fan, He Wang, Li Yi

AI Summary

Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies underutilize the potential of frontier-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robot control. To address this, ScaleVLA integrates a 552B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash VLM backbone with a trainable action DiT, allowing systematic comparison of VLM scales. This approach yielded superior sample efficiency (10 demonstrations outperform 50 from smaller backbones), enhanced generalization (45.1% LIBERO-PRO success), and improved real-world precise manipulation, establishing frontier VLMs as powerful foundations for efficient and generalizable robot policies.

ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization

arXiv

Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tongyan Wang, Xuyang Chen, Ruihua Shi, Zhichao Xu, Jiaxin Qin, Guoshenghui Zhao, Baijian Yang, Yingjie Victor Chen, Tan Yu, Weijie Zhao

AI Summary

Visual agents struggle with ultra-high-resolution images by relying on common sense rather than reading specific evidence, a limitation difficult to address with standard RL. This paper proposes Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on "contrast sets"—groups of images subtly varied to necessitate evidence-based reasoning. CrossRPO achieved 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench, outperforming GRPO, and their trained ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B agents demonstrated superior accuracy, surpassing several advanced proprietary models on most benchmarks.

FaceJev: Direct Visual Decisions for Face Forgery Detection

arXiv

Siran Peng, Tianshuo Zhang, Yibo Xue, Ajian Liu, Haoyuan Zhang, Weisong Zhao, Shuhuan Chen, Xiangyu Zhu, Zhen Lei

AI Summary

Face forgery detection methods often generalize poorly to unseen manipulations. This paper introduces FaceJev, an MLLM-based approach that makes direct visual decisions by answering eight independent questions about facial parts, inferring anomaly probabilities from native answer logits to classify a face as forged if any part is abnormal. It uses a new dataset, PartForensics, and optimizes part decisions with FS-GRPO. FaceJev significantly improves performance on unseen manipulations, outperforming the strongest baseline by 7.25 percentage points.

Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting with Physics-Indexed Specialist Memory: the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent

arXiv

Blas Eugenio Vicco Zarate, Miguel Hernán Vicco

AI Summary

Continual reinforcement learning struggles with catastrophic forgetting, requiring agents to adapt to new conditions while preserving performance on previous tasks. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) proposes to mitigate this by evolving small specialist neural networks without gradients, archiving them by detected dynamic regime, and retrieving them via Bayesian policy reuse. However, it adapted slower than PPO and had lower returns than SAC in other evaluated sequences.

SADL: On the Demand-Supply Geometry of Representation Collapse

arXiv

Junhan Sun, Yigui Feng, Mingzhe Liu, Haoyan Xu, Lei Ye, Ziqi Han, Hanru Bai, Yanzhao Zhang, Hao Zhao, Guofeng Zhang

AI Summary

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) suffer from representation collapse because the encoder quickly becomes dominated by a shared component that a projector cannot correct. The proposed SADL (Supply And Demand Loss) directly constrains the encoder by comparing the demand for representation differences (from the projector output) with the encoder's ability to supply them, applying a constraint only when supply is insufficient. SADL reduces the collapse rate from 50% to 0%, significantly increases encoder effective rank (3.4-6.4x), and dramatically lowers latent prediction error. It also improves control success (INTACT > 95%) and consistently enhances accuracy and stability in image classification across various datasets.

CertiSum: Bitwise-Identical MXFP4 Training Across Platforms and Parallelism Layouts

arXiv

Steve Rhyner, Yubo Gao, Stefan de Lasa, Sankeerth Durvasula, Nandita Vijaykumar

AI Summary

MXFP4 training is non-deterministic across GPU vendors and parallelism layouts, complicating debugging and audits. CertiSum proposes making MXFP4 training bitwise-invariant by leveraging exact native FP32 accumulation, certifying matrix multiplication results, and exact recomputation when needed, while maintaining consistent quantization blocks and partial sum merging. It successfully preserves training states bit-for-bit across AMD and NVIDIA GPUs and various parallelism layouts. Although introducing some latency, CertiSum can achieve speedups over BF16 and deterministic BF16.

Optimistic Verifiers Get Hacked: Reward Hacking Under an Exact Process Oracle

NeurIPS

Arunkumar V, Manoranjan Gandhudi, Gangadharan G. R., Arun Prakash, S. Senthilkumar

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards is vulnerable to reward hacking when proxy verifiers diverge from the true objective, a distance typically unmeasured. To study this, the authors created a testbed using executable authorization policies to provide an *exact* process-level reward oracle, then trained RL agents against seven realistically degraded verifiers while scoring against the true oracle. They discovered four distinct hacking phenotypes, most notably that "optimistic verifiers" are exploited by padding, where proxy performance improves but true faithfulness decreases. A model-free audit of the verifier can anticipate which hacking phenotype will emerge.

How Much Does Saliency Accuracy Matter for Saliency-Guided 360-degree Image Compression?

ACL

Sakib Ahmed, Sidi-Mohammed Senouci, Philippe Brunet, Guillaume Caron, Nevrez Imamoglu

AI Summary

The computational cost of accurate 360-degree saliency prediction hinders low-latency saliency-guided image compression, raising questions about its practical necessity. This study quantifies the impact of saliency accuracy on compression gain by evaluating diverse saliency predictors on a predictor-agnostic, ground-truth-trained learned codec and H.265/HEVC. Results show diminishing returns: a fast, lightweight predictor (52 fps) captures 65% of the ground-truth oracle's bit-rate saving, with significantly higher compute adding minimal or no further measurable benefit. This suggests lightweight predictors are a practical choice for latency-constrained 360-degree streaming.

Procedure without Judgment: What Small Language Models Learn under an Exact Verifier

ACL

Arunkumar V, Manoranjan Gandhudi, Gangadharan G. R., Arun Prakash, S. Senthilkumar

AI Summary

The core problem is generating minimal and exact explanations for authorization policy decisions. The proposed method involves evaluating and training Language Models (LLMs) against an exact verifier that provides ground truth and allows models to query the policy. Frontier models achieve around 50% exactness, which improves to 75% with a minimality prompt. While a fine-tuned 4B model reaches 92% on trained policies, it significantly loses minimality (48%) and generalization on unseen policies, where prompted frontier models perform better, and reinforcement learning did not improve performance.

arXiv

Itamar Mishani, Mukund Krishnakumar, Oren Salzman, Maxim Likhachev

AI Summary

Long-horizon manipulation planning is computationally expensive due to the vast search space and costly evaluations of parameterized skills using high-fidelity simulators. Lazy-MOSAIC addresses this by introducing a lazy planning framework that searches over hypothesized skill outcomes, employing a cheap learned skill-effect world model to predict skill feasibility and resulting states. arXiv

AI Summary

Multi-bubble blowup for the energy supercritical Schrödinger equation

arXiv

Xuanying Li

AI Summary

This article establishes the existence of finite-time multi-bubble Type II blowup solutions for the focusing energy supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger equation, concentrating at multiple distinct points. The authors construct solutions for specific parameters ($K \geq 2$ distinct points, $L \geq 2$, sufficient dimension) exhibiting a precise asymptotic form: $\psi(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac1p}(t)\sum Q(\lambda(t)(x-x_j))+\zeta^\ast(x)+o(1)$, where $\lambda(t)=t^{-\frac12-\nu}$ defines the blowup rate. The proof combines matched asymptotic construction of approximate solutions with high-order energy estimates and a compactness argument, critically eliminating a self-similar oscillatory branch via a quantized blowup rate choice.

Out of Sight, Into Memory: Compact Visual Latents Retain Local Evidence in Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing

arXiv

Meng Tang, Siyuan Du, Yuxin Liu, Shuaishuai Cao, Di Wang, Haonan Guo

AI Summary

Multimodal LLMs face a challenge in UHR remote sensing QA, where global views obscure local details and full crops are context-heavy, questioning if compact latents can retain fine-grained information. This paper proposes encoding high-resolution crops into short sequences of continuous visual latent memory tokens for frozen backbones. A 64-token memory achieved 7.6- to 9.9-fold compression, improving accuracy by 2.5 to 4.8 percentage points over global images and recovering most high-resolution crop gains. Crucially, prediction accuracy depended significantly on the encoded regional evidence, establishing that compact visual latents effectively retain useful local information.
