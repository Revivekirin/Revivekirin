UrbanGround: From Local Perception to Spatial Agency in a Real-Scale City

arXiv

Tianjie Ju, Zheng Wu, Yueqing Sun, Yuhan Cui, Bobo Li, Shengqiong Wu, Pengzhou Cheng, Haodong Zhao, Zongru Wu, Xinbei Ma, Doris Zhang, Kunling Li, Mong-Li Lee, Wynne Hsu, Hao Fei, Qi Gu, Gongshen Liu, Zhuosheng Zhang

AI Summary

The core problem is whether Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can translate local street-view perception into reliable, sustained action and navigation within a complex, real-scale city. To investigate this, UrbanGround is proposed, a novel sandbox featuring a physically constrained, 3D replica of Hong Kong built from geospatial data, enabling closed-loop first-person interaction and testing of scene grounding, navigation, and robust movement. Experiments show MLLMs possess useful visual recognition and short-range spatial reasoning, but struggle with reliable orientation, pedestrian-aware movement, and fail significantly in extended exploration where local abilities do not compose into sustained goal-directed behavior.

TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization

arXiv

Aozhe Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Jianze Wang, Shangke Lv, Ying Liu, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Hua Yang, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen

AI Summary

Existing post-training methods for LLMs, such as RL and OPSD, require ground-truth labels, precluding test-time training; naive pseudo-labeling is fragile due to error propagation. TTPO proposes an asymmetric objective that distills agreeing rollouts via OPSD while penalizing disagreeing rollouts with Grouped RL, further refined by token-level selection for both branches. Without any labels, TTPO matches label-supervised OPSD on five competition-level benchmarks, notably improving Qwen3-1.7B by 7.2% in TTT, and demonstrates strong cross-task generalization.

Boosting LLM Exploration via Weak-Model Guidance in RLVR

arXiv

Xingyu Shen, Huishuai Zhang, Peng Li, Yinchun Wang, Dongyan Zhao

AI Summary

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) improves LLM reasoning but causes policy entropy collapse, narrowing reasoning coverage and degrading pass@$k$ performance for larger $k$. This work proposes a weak-model guidance approach where a target LLM completes reasoning trajectories initiated by a smaller, weaker LLM. These unfamiliar prefixes disrupt over-confidence, encouraging exploration of distinct reasoning paths. This method consistently outperforms vanilla RLVR, showing increasingly pronounced gains as $k$ scales up, substantially expanding reasoning coverage and mitigating entropy collapse without complex modifications.

Scaling Graph Neural Networks for Friend Recommendation: Multi-Hash User Embeddings and Temporal Neighbor Sampling

arXiv

Maksim Utushkin, Andrei Ovsiannikov, Alexander D'yakonov

AI Summary

The core problem is scaling Graph Neural Networks for friend recommendation on massive production social graphs due to prohibitive memory for user ID embeddings and inefficient temporal neighbor sampling. The proposed method is an end-to-end GNN ranking system leveraging multi-hash ID embeddings and temporal neighbor sampling implemented with timestamp-sorted CSR storage and binary search. This approach reduced ID embedding table size by over 98% and temporal sampling cost from $O(deg(v))$ to $O(\log(deg(v)))$. In production, it increased friend additions by 16% and unique friend adders by 11.5% over a strong baseline.

Consolidating RLVR Capabilities Across Domains: A Deep Dive into Fusion Paradigms

arXiv

Siye Wu, Kai Yang, Yuchen Cai, Xin Xu, Peng-Yuan Wang, Jiaxuan Wang, Jiashun Liu, Jiafei Lyu, Yangkun Chen, Saiyong Yang, Yanghua Xiao

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) struggles to consolidate multiple domain-specific capabilities into large language models, often requiring separate experts and an unclear strategy for their fusion. This paper compares three fusion paradigms—Merge (combining expert task vectors), Mix RL (pooling datasets), and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD)—using shared experts, data, and a multi-domain benchmark suite. While average performance differences are small, individual benchmark gaps are significant and linked to cross-domain relations in task-vector geometry. Each method exhibits distinct training dynamics and constraints, all improving single-sample accuracy without losing held-out capabilities, leading to practical guidelines for their selection.

arXiv

AI Summary

CLAP: Cross-Embodiment Video World Models are Zero-Shot Physical Simulators

arXiv

Kechen Liu, Ola Shorinwa

AI Summary

Current action-conditioned video models are limited to single robot embodiments, preventing them from utilizing diverse video data due to disparate and often absent action representations across agents. CLAP addresses this by introducing a cross-embodiment video generation framework that reconciles action spaces using end-effector poses, language, and latent actions, employing a curriculum-based learning recipe. This approach first learns physical priors from unlabeled data, then grounds them in end-effector actions for zero-shot deployment. CLAP approaches or surpasses state-of-the-art single-embodiment models, delivering a comprehensive suite of world models applicable across diverse action types and robot morphologies.

FlashVLA: Streaming Action Decoding for Fast and Asynchronous VLA Inference

arXiv

Zekai Li, Jiaming Tang, Zhijian Liu

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are bottlenecked by high inference latency and unstable asynchronous execution, especially for iterative flow-matching-based action decoding. FlashVLA introduces a streaming action decoding framework using a multi-noise-level streaming action buffer and chunk-wise causal attention to produce one executable action chunk per inference step. This design, combined with a chunk-wise autoregressive formulation, ensures action continuity for smooth asynchronous execution. FlashVLA substantially improves inference speed and maintains strong task performance, achieving $\geq$30Hz control frequency with smooth asynchronous inference in real-world deployment.

arXiv

AI Summary

A Finite Sample Analysis for Quantile Temporal Difference Learning in Distributional Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Zijie Cheng, Xiang Li, Yang Peng, Zhihua Zhang

AI Summary

This paper provides a global finite-sample guarantee for synchronous Quantile Temporal Difference (QTD) learning in tabular distributional reinforcement learning. The method involves a two-stage analysis: first, a global comparison argument using $W_\infty$ contraction brings iterates into a local neighborhood; then, a local linearization of the QTD mean field combined with a variance-sensitive martingale analysis is applied. Key results show the leading last-iterate fluctuation is $\widetilde O\bigl(T^{-a/2}/\sqrt{1-γ}\bigr)$ with no polynomial dependence on the number of quantiles. However, the deterministic transient and burn-in can still depend on the smallest Bellman-target density, distinguishing local stochastic fluctuation from global sample complexity.

ICML

Hai-tao Yu, Nan Min, Zheng Fang, Hongyu Zhan, Yusen Tan, Yuhan Wang, Jun Xia

AI Summary

Directly concatenating heterogeneous multimodal spectroscopic signals for molecular structure inference causes performance degradation due to pronounced heterogeneity and multimodal imbalance. arXiv

Su Wang, Yaochen Li, Min Yang, Jiaohao Nie, Chang Liu, Yuehu Liu

AI Summary
