Skill-Space Shooting for Autonomous Robot Policy Improvement

arXiv

Zihang Rui, Renhao Wang, Haoxu Huang, Yang Gao

AI Summary

Robots struggle to autonomously improve from failures and convert agentic system behaviors into learnable, scalable policy corrections without extensive human demonstration. The proposed "skill-space shooting" method addresses this by using foundation models to guide the exploration of corrective actions through reusable "skills," translating successful trials into policy improvements. Real-world experiments demonstrate repeated autonomous policy improvement and the ability to share skills across tasks, enabling scalable and generalizable learning.

Imagine3D-LLM: Teaching MLLMs to Imagine 3D Scenes Before Answering

NeurIPS

Jaewoo Jung, Hyeonseo Yu, Honggyu An, Jisang Han, Mungyeom Kim, Minkyeong Jeon, Heeseong Shin, Wonjun Moon, Federico Tombari, Daniel Barath, Marc Pollefeys, Seungryong Kim, Sunghwan Hong

AI Summary

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with coherent 3D understanding from multi-view images due to difficulties integrating evidence across viewpoints. Imagine3D-LLM proposes teaching MLLMs to "imagine" a compact 3D Gaussian Splatting representation by appending learnable summary tokens, which are decoded and supervised by a photometric reconstruction loss. This 3D imagination process is jointly trained with next-token prediction, propagating 3D-aware signals throughout the model. As a result, Imagine3D-LLM consistently outperforms prior approaches across multiple spatial reasoning and 3D understanding benchmarks.

AI Summary

Robotic in-context learning (ICL) faces a core problem of prompt ambiguity, where visual demonstrations convey too much information, making the robot's learning target unclear. Counterfactual Video Generation Enables Scalable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Zihan Wang, Zhen Wu, Pieter Abbeel, Rocky Duan, Jitendra Malik, Carmelo Sferrazza, C. Karen Liu, Guanya Shi, Angjoo Kanazawa

AI Summary

Collecting diverse, high-quality real-world videos for training humanoid loco-manipulation skills via visual imitation poses a significant scaling barrier. The proposed PRISM framework addresses this by generating hundreds of diverse "counterfactual" human-object interaction videos from a few real exemplars using video-to-video generation, then converting these into physically plausible simulated trajectories. This enables training a single policy that generalizes to novel objects within categories, allowing a real humanoid to perform pick-up, carry, and drop tasks on various unseen objects using only onboard depth, without real-world fine-tuning.

STEPQuant: When and Where Errors Matter in Delta-Rule Recurrent State Quantization

arXiv

Bingchen Yao, Haobo Xu, Haokun Lin, Yichen Wu, Ziyu Guo, Renrui Zhang, Zhichao Lu, Zhenan Sun, Ying Wei

AI Summary

Recurrent states in linear attention models are a memory bottleneck, but direct quantization causes severe accuracy degradation due to propagating errors that vary temporally and spatially. STEPQuant is proposed as a spatial-temporal post-training quantization framework that allocates precision based on error magnitude, memory lifetime, and jointly fits scales according to state distributions and key-row impact. It achieves FP32-state accuracy with a 6-bit budget and outperforms uniform INT8 at 4-bit. Integrated, 6-bit STEPQuant offers over 5x recurrent-state compression and reduces total serving memory by up to 68.7%.

LeapQuant: Efficient Linear Attention with Accurate Recurrent State Quantization

arXiv

Yi Pan, Haocheng Xi, Kan Zhu, Xingyang Li, Yibo Wu, Mayank Mishra, Hongtao Zhang, William X. Zheng, Baris Kasikci, Song Han, Kurt Keutzer, Rishabh Iyer, Ion Stoica

AI Summary

Linear attention in LLMs suffers from a recurrent state bottleneck due to repeated reading/updating, with direct quantization degrading quality from error accumulation and outliers. LeapQuant proposes a training-free method using per-window quantization, updating the 8-bit state only once per window while buffering high-precision updates internally. It further reduces quantization error by treating state outliers as high-precision Compensator Tokens and smoothing the residual. This achieves near-lossless performance with FP32 accuracy, yielding 2.05-3.70x kernel-level and 1.47x end-to-end inference speedups.

Rho: A Foundation for Efficiently Adaptable VLA Models

arXiv

Rho Team, Simran Bagaria, Daphne Chen, Dean Fortier, Jianlong Fu, Michael Harrison, Tess Hellebrekers, Neel Joshi, Andrey Kolobov, Dalton Moore, Galen Mullins, Michael Murray, Eduardo Salinas, Reuben Tan

AI Summary

General-purpose physical AI models struggle with efficient, data-light adaptation to new tasks and varying robot embodiments while maintaining broad visual-linguistic capabilities and precise control. This paper introduces Rho, a family of open-weights VLA models for bimanual manipulation, employing an action-expert architecture with embodiment midtraining and a novel internal latent policy for online adaptation. Rho variants demonstrate superior or matching performance against existing VLAs across multiple embodiments and tasks, with embodiment midtraining significantly improving downstream adaptation. Furthermore, its online adaptation mechanism enables handling out-of-distribution scenarios with as few as 15 corrective episodes, positioning Rho as a robust and adaptable foundation for robotic manipulation.

Rethinking Representations for World-Action Modeling

arXiv

Haoyi Jiang, Liu Liu, Xinjiang Wang, Zhihao Sun, Zequn Chen, Sen Wang, Xinjie Wang, Xia Chen, Jingfeng Yao, Weiheng Zhao, Shanglin Yuan, Zhizhong Su, Wei Sui, Wenyu Liu, Xinggang Wang

AI Summary

World-action models face the core problem of designing effective representation spaces for robot policy learning, as neither reconstruction fidelity nor pre-trained perceptual features alone guarantee success. The proposed method, ReWAM, is a representation-centric world-action model leveraging pre-trained DINO features, enhanced by Feature Calibration, a Temporal Representation Bottleneck for compact states, and Action-Grounded Representation Shaping to allow the policy to sculpt representation encoding. Key results show ReWAM achieves 93.6% success on RoboTwin 2.0 without generative video pre-training, and an average score of 12.29 with 8.28% success on RoboDojo using 600 hours of embodied data.

LongLive-Plug: Once-for-All Distillation for Video Generation

arXiv

Shuai Yang, Luozhou Wang, Wei Huang, ZhiFei Chen, Bohan Zhang, Xiao Fu, Qianli Ma, Chen-Hsuan Lin, Weian Mao, Bryan Chu, Song Han, Yukang Chen

AI Summary

Specialized video diffusion model distillation is inefficiently repeated for each new model. LongLive-Plug proposes a once-for-all distillation framework that learns reusable capabilities (e.g., single-pass CFG, few-step sampling, long-context error correction) as LoRAs on a base model for training-free, plug-and-play deployment. These LoRAs are reusable across 54 downstream models from various backbone families and tasks, even with architecture changes, enabling efficient, per-backbone distillation and retaining key controls like CFG.

AdviSD: Learning to Advise Frontier LLMs via Targeted Multi-Turn Self-Distillation

arXiv

Rishabh Agrawal, Hejie Cui, Shasha Li, Shanchan Wu, Sercan Ö. Arık

AI Summary

The core problem is effectively learning from feedback for language model advisors, as plausible corrections that don't immediately alter executor behavior can paradoxically limit future learning if not selectively processed. AdviSD proposes a method pairing outcome-based reinforcement learning with selective self-distillation, where decisions for supervision are chosen based on the magnitude of change in executor response when advice is applied versus not. AdviSD significantly outperforms baselines like advisor-GRPO on BFCL-v3 and EnvScaler, demonstrating improved generalization, transferability across different LLM executors, and validating its selective supervision rule.

Multi-Agent Flow Matching with Decoupled Generative Guidance

arXiv

Ruoyu Lin, Magnus Egerstedt, Fabio Pasqualetti

AI Summary

Generative models lack formal guarantees for satisfying hard constraints, especially in multi-agent systems where requirements are coupled but agents must determine guidance independently. This paper introduces DeGG-Flow, a multi-agent flow matching framework that employs a control-affine dynamical system to develop decoupled generative guidance conditions for both shared and private coupled requirements. Group-graded Ore extensions and graded simplicity

arXiv

Yassine Ait Mohamed

AI Summary

This paper addresses the characterization of graded simplicity for Ore extensions $R[t;\sigma,\delta]$ of rings graded by an arbitrary group $\Gamma$, noting that classical necessary conditions are insufficient. The authors investigate conditions for extending the grading and analyze the structure of graded ideals. They find that a grading extends if $\sigma$ and $\delta$ are compatible, and show a graded-simple Ore extension (with $R$ graded-simple) is characterized by the absence of homogeneous normal elements monic in $t$. For Ore extensions of group algebras, graded simplicity is linked to the nonvanishing of an $H^1$ class and forces the group to be metabelian in characteristic zero.

WUSH-KV: KV Cache Quantization with Data-Adaptive Transforms

arXiv

Jiale Chen, Vage Egiazarian, Eldar Kurtić, Torsten Hoefler, Dan Alistarh

AI Summary

To mitigate the severe memory and bandwidth bottlenecks of KV caches during long-context LLM inference, the authors propose WUSH-KV, a low-bit quantization framework that applies data-adaptive transforms derived from second-order statistics to key and value states. The method folds the value transform into model weights, applies the key transform post-RoPE, and pairs them with clipped quantizers to minimize layerwise reconstruction error. Consequently, WUSH-KV achieves lower end-to-end perplexity than competing transforms and matches or outperforms the OSCAR baseline at 2-bit quantization across multiple models and downstream tasks.

VideoLoop: Looped Working Memory Against Semantic Thrashing in Long-Form Video Agents

arXiv

Jinfa Huang, Jianming Xu, Jingyang Lin, Zhengyuan Yang, Jiebo Luo

AI Summary

Multimodal agents for long-form video reasoning suffer from "semantic thrashing," where append-only working memories continuously accumulate noise and degrade attention to critical evidence. To resolve this, the authors introduce VideoLoop, an agent framework featuring coupled reasoning loops where an inner loop retrieves past artifacts and actively rewrites a bounded working memory after each outer-loop step. Experiments show that VideoLoop effectively mitigates memory degradation, improving four LVLM backbones by an average of 4.2% on VideoMME (long) and achieving up to 88.3% accuracy with Gemini 3.1 Pro.

GA-EIRFS: A Geometry-Augmented Repeat-Factor Sampling Method for Long-Tailed LiDAR 3D Object Detection

arXiv

Taufiq Ahmed, Constantino Álvarez Casado, Daniel Herrera Castro, Sasan Sharifipour, Abhishek Kumar, Miguel Bordallo López

AI Summary

Current long-tailed LiDAR 3D object detection methods rely strictly on class frequencies, overlooking critical differences in geometric observability and supervision quality across sparse classes. To solve this, the authors propose GA-EIRFS, a detector-agnostic frame-sampling strategy that modulates frequency-based repeat factors using a geometric score derived from point count, surface-normal entropy, and surface coverage. Without altering model architectures or inference, GA-EIRFS consistently improves mAP and NDS on nuScenes across multiple detectors—such as boosting CenterPoint bicycle AP from 0.306 to 0.359—demonstrating that geometric evidence effectively amplifies frequency-driven sampling.
