HarmoWAM: Harmonizing Generalizable and Precise Manipulation via Adaptive World Action Models

arXiv

Qiuxuan Feng, Jiale Yu, Jiaming Liu, Yueru Jia, Zhuangzhe Wu, Hao Chen, Zezhong Qian, Shuo Gu, Peng Jia, Siwei Ma, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

Existing World Action Models (WAMs) exhibit a fundamental trade-off between generalizable long-range transit (Imagine-then-Execute) and precise, temporally coherent manipulation (Joint Modeling). HarmoWAM proposes an end-to-end WAM that unifies predictive and reactive control using a world model, conditioning a predictive expert for generalizable transit and a reactive expert for precision, adaptively coordinated by a Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism. This allows the system to leverage physical priors for both control types and expand the exploration space. HarmoWAM achieves strong zero-shot generalization on training-unseen real-world robotic tasks, significantly outperforming prior state-of-the-art VLA models and WAMs by 33% and 29%.

Power Reinforcement Post-Training of Text-to-Image Models with Super-Linear Advantage Shaping

arXiv

Haoyuan Sun, Jing Wang, Yuxin Song, Yu Lu, Bo Fang, Yifu Luo, Jun Yin, Pengyu Zeng, Miao Zhang, Tiantian Zhang, Xueqian Wang, Shijian Lu

AI Summary

Existing reinforcement learning post-training methods for text-to-image models are prone to reward hacking and miscalibration from normalization, limiting genuine performance improvements. This work proposes Super-Linear Advantage Shaping (SLAS), which uses an information geometry perspective to introduce a non-linear, advantage-dependent geometric structure to reshape the policy space, amplifying high-advantage signals and suppressing noise. SLAS consistently surpasses baselines like DanceGRPO, achieving faster training, improved out-of-domain performance, enhanced robustness, and mitigated reward hacking while preserving generative fidelity.

Variational Inference for Lévy Process-Driven SDEs via Neural Tilting

arXiv

Yaman Kindap, Manfred Opper, Benjamin Dupuis, Umut Simsekli, Tolga Birdal

AI Summary

Bayesian inference for Lévy process-driven SDEs is intractable, as existing Monte Carlo methods lack scalability and neural variational inference (VI) relies on Gaussian assumptions, failing to capture jumps and heavy tails. This paper introduces a neural exponential tilting framework for VI in Lévy-driven SDEs, constructing a flexible variational family by exponentially reweighting the Lévy measure using neural networks, which preserves jump structure and enables tractable inference via quadratic parametrization and specialized estimators. The method accurately captures jump dynamics and yields reliable posterior inference in regimes where Gaussian-based VI approaches fail, on both synthetic and real-world datasets.

PriorVLA: Prior-Preserving Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

arXiv

Xinyu Guo, Bin Xie, Wei Chai, Xianchi Deng, Tiancai Wang, Zhengxing Wu, Xingyu Chen

AI Summary

Full fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for downstream tasks often degrades broad pretrained priors towards narrow training distributions. PriorVLA proposes a novel framework to preserve these priors by using a frozen Prior Expert and training an Adaptation Expert, integrating scene and motor priors via Expert Queries while updating only 25% of parameters compared to full fine-tuning. PriorVLA demonstrates superior performance over full fine-tuning and SOTA VLA baselines across various benchmarks and real-world tasks, especially in out-of-distribution and few-shot settings.

Dynamic Skill Lifecycle Management for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Junhao Shen, Teng Zhang, Xiaoyan Zhao, Hong Cheng

AI Summary

Existing methods for LLM agents using external skills are limited by assuming skills either persistently accumulate or are fully internalized, failing to account for a dynamic, non-monotonic optimal skill set. SLIM proposes a dynamic Skill Lifecycle Management framework that treats the active external skill set as an optimization variable, updating it through retaining high-value, retiring negligible, and expanding the skill bank based on marginal contribution estimates. Optimal and Scalable MAPF via Multi-Marginal Optimal Transport and Schrödinger Bridges

ICML

Usman A. Khan, Joseph W. Durham

AI Summary

This paper addresses anonymous multi-agent path finding (MAPF) on a graph. It proposes recasting MAPF as a multi-marginal optimal transport (MMOT) problem with a Markovian structure, reducible to a polynomial-sized linear program (LP) that yields optimal, non-overlapping integral paths. For scalability, a Schrödinger bridge formulation is introduced, providing an entropic regularization solvable via Sinkhorn-type iterations to generate a fractional transport. This fractional solution then serves as a template for a reduced LP, achieving near-optimal, integral transports with significantly reduced complexity.

arXiv

Richie Yeung, Aleks Kissinger, Rob Cornish

AI Summary

The core problem is synthesizing Clifford quantum circuits by finding elementary gate sequences that reduce a given symplectic matrix representation to the identity. CapVector: Learning Transferable Capability Vectors in Parametric Space for Vision-Language-Action Models

arXiv

Wenxuan Song, Han Zhao, Fuhao Li, Ziyang Zhou, Xi Wang, Jing Lyu, Pengxiang Ding, Yan Wang, Donglin Wang, Haoang Li

AI Summary

Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face challenges in effective performance improvement and adaptation cost reduction during standard finetuning, while advanced auxiliary-objective methods incur significant computational overhead. CapVector proposes to decouple auxiliary-objective finetuning into general capability enhancement and task-specific fitting in the parameter space, deriving "capability vectors" from the parameter difference between two distinctively trained models. These vectors are merged with pretrained parameters to create a capability-enhanced meta model, which, with lightweight orthogonal regularization, achieves comparable performance to auxiliary finetuning with reduced overhead. The method demonstrates effectiveness, versatility across models, and out-of-the-box generalization to novel environments and embodiments.

ACL

Compute Where it Counts: Self Optimizing Language Models

ICML

Yash Akhauri, Mohamed S. Abdelfattah

AI Summary

LLM inference inefficiently applies uniform computation per token despite varying token difficulty, leading to over- or under-computation. Self-Optimizing Language Models (SOL) address this by coupling a frozen LLM with a lightweight policy network that dynamically allocates per-token compute, controlling attention sparsity, MLP pruning, and activation quantization. This policy is trained with group-relative policy optimization to balance quality and a target budget. SOL achieves a superior quality-efficiency trade-off, outperforming static allocation and random search, and improving MMLU accuracy by up to 7.3%.

CADBench: A Multimodal Benchmark for AI-Assisted CAD Program Generation

arXiv

Anna C. Doris, Jacob Thomas Sony, Ghadi Nehme, Era Syla, Amin Heyrani Nobari, Faez Ahmed

AI Summary

Progress in AI-assisted CAD program generation from visual or 3D inputs is hampered by fragmented and inconsistent evaluation methodologies. To address this, CADBench is introduced as a unified multimodal benchmark comprising 18,000 diverse evaluation samples across five input modalities and six metrics covering geometric fidelity, executability, and program compactness. Benchmarking eleven systems revealed specialized mesh-to-CAD models significantly outperform general vision-language models, which still struggle with reliable CAD program reconstruction. The benchmark further highlighted that quality degrades with geometric complexity, specialized models are brittle under modality shifts, and model rankings vary based on the chosen metric.

BenchCAD: A Comprehensive, Industry-Standard Benchmark for Programmatic CAD

arXiv

Haozhe Zhang, Kaichen Liu, Miaomiao Chen, Lei Li, Shaojie Yang, Cheng Peng, Hanjie Chen

AI Summary

Current multimodal models struggle to generate executable industrial CAD programs that accurately reflect complex 3D structures and engineering parameters. BenchCAD addresses this by providing 17,900 execution-verified CadQuery programs across 106 industrial part families to evaluate models on perception, parametric abstraction, and code synthesis. Results show that while frontier models can recover coarse geometry, they fail to produce faithful parametric designs, frequently oversimplify complex CAD operations, and exhibit limited generalization to unseen parts.
