Coding Agents with an Obstacle-Aware Harness for Safe Robot Manipulation

ACL

Bingxin Xu, Yuzhang Shang, Zhen Dong, Emilio Ferrara

AI Summary

Coding agents for robot manipulation consistently fail to ensure safety by colliding with obstacles, as their planning prioritizes task completion over explicit safety constraints despite perceiving and being instructed about obstacles. To address this, the paper introduces SafeHarness, an obstacle-aware framework comprising two components: route planning that grounds objects as bounding boxes for path verification and replanning, and contact execution that selects obstacle-avoiding contact positions. SafeHarness achieves 71.9% task success and 87.5% collision avoidance, surpassing the previous SOTA by 6.5% and 27.0% respectively, and outperforming the baseline agent by 2.3x and 1.5x.

Workspace Models: Lightweight Robotic Memory via Saliency-Driven Supervision

arXiv

Nitish Dashora, Douglas Chen, Idan Shenfeld, John Marangola, Pulkit Agrawal, Max Simchowitz

AI Summary

Conditioning robotic policies on long histories causes spurious correlations, while using real-time vision-language models (VLMs) to filter task-salient information is computationally prohibitive during deployment. To resolve this, the authors introduce the "workspace token," a lightweight latent memory trained by distilling VLM-identified salient historical features via a set-reconstruction decoder loss exclusively at train time. Across both simulated and physical robot experiments, workspace tokens act as drop-in observation replacements that eliminate test-time VLM calls while outperforming baseline memory approaches on complex manipulation tasks.

FAMOS: Feed-Forward 3D Articulation Modeling from Sparse Observations

arXiv

Kevin Qu, Tao Sun, Massimiliano Viola, Liyuan Zhu, Zhizhuo Zhou, Sayan Deb Sarkar, Konrad Schindler, Iro Armeni

AI Summary

Modeling articulated objects from sparse, partial monocular views is challenging, particularly for feed-forward methods that rely on single views and learned shape priors. FAMOS proposes a feed-forward model that predicts movable-part segmentation and joint parameters from a sparse set of partial point clouds. It employs a Multi-state Articulation Transformer with alternating attention to aggregate cues across observations, an observed articulation span objective, and a procedural data generator for training. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over both feed-forward and optimization-based baselines on multiple datasets.

Score Centering Stabilizes Off-policy Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Martin Marek, Max Ryabinin

AI Summary

Large language model reinforcement learning is destabilized by training-inference mismatch (TIM), which creates an accumulating persistent bias ("drift") between engines. To resolve this, the authors propose "score centering," an additive correction term that stabilizes off-policy RL by directly canceling this drift. Across models ranging from 0.6B to 30B parameters, score centering matches or exceeds importance-sampling baselines under quantization and can be combined with importance sampling to deliver superior performance in staleness settings.

PosteriorBench: From Point Estimates to Posterior Matching in Evaluating Generative Inverse Solvers

arXiv

Jiachen Yao, Zi-Siang Hsu, Xi Deng, Aditi Gupta, Xin Ju, Sally M Benson, Gege Wen, Anima Anandkumar

AI Summary

Current generative inverse solver evaluations are insufficient for ill-posed problems, as they focus on single point estimates rather than capturing the full posterior distribution and its associated uncertainty. To address this, PosteriorBench is introduced as a benchmark evaluating distributional accuracy, utilizing high-fidelity reference posteriors from MCMC/rejection sampling across four physics problems, alongside a five-metric suite for comprehensive assessment. Experiments reveal significant distribution-matching gaps in existing solvers, while highlighting neural operators' resolution robustness and the critical role of guidance weights and generation noise for accurate posterior-variance calibration.

arXiv

Xin Chen, Sen Chen, Yujuan Ding, Jian Liu, Guoqing Wang, Wei Ye, Heng Tao Shen, Yi Bin

AI Summary

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies rely on fixed action chunking horizons that fail to adapt to varying control precision and feedback demands across different task stages. To address this, the authors propose GeoAAC, a training-free method for flow-based VLAs that dynamically adjusts the action execution horizon based on prediction reliability inferred from the geometry of Flow Matching denoising trajectories. arXiv

Hanchu Zhou, Brendan Lynch, Raman Goyal, Dechen Gao, Begum Kasap, Boqi Zhao, Junshan Zhang

AI Summary

Existing tactile World Action Models (WAMs) for contact-rich robot control are limited by large-scale generative backbones, hindering inference efficiency and flexible deployment. Agile-WAM proposes an agile architecture that encodes visual and tactile observations into a shared latent space, using a direct vision-tactile-to-action flow-matching process with multi-horizon multimodal prediction to account for their differing timescales. arXiv

AI Summary

End-to-end driving policies struggle with compounding errors and safety risks in closed-loop deployment due to distribution shifts, and existing closed-loop post-training methods are prohibitively costly for sensor-based models. OPTED proposes decoupling RL from post-training by first training a render-free, privileged teacher using RL on vectorized inputs, which then provides supervision to the pre-trained, sensor-based student during closed-loop fine-tuning. MoWAM: Explicit Future Motion Prediction for Efficient World Action Models

arXiv

Jiayu Wang, Bin Zhu, Yue Yu, Jingjing Chen

AI Summary

World Action Models (WAMs) face a core problem of high computational overhead from explicit future video generation at inference, or reduced robustness if future dynamics are only implicitly encoded. MoWAM proposes an efficient solution by replacing future video generation with explicit future *motion* prediction, modeling structured robot motion as a compact abstraction via a Mixture-of-Transformer architecture and enabling efficient inference-time candidate selection with a motion-aware verifier.
