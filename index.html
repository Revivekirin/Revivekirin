Learning to Stop without Learning to Stop: Self-Supervised Confidence Training Improves Reasoning Efficiency

arXiv

Parsa Hosseini, Akasha Tigalappanavara, Sumit Nawathe, Chenrui Fan, Sourya Basu, Genta Indra Winata, Anirban Das, Soheil Feizi, Nima Chitsazan

AI Summary

Reasoning models suffer from computationally expensive inference due to overly long reasoning traces. This paper proposes a self-supervised confidence training method where models learn to predict their answer confidence at intermediate reasoning steps, without explicit optimization for length or stopping. Despite using standard inference, this fine-tuning reduces generated tokens by up to 25% at matched accuracy across diverse models and benchmarks. This suggests efficient reasoning can emerge as a downstream consequence of learning metacognitive confidence signals.

Learning Robot Policies from Sparse Success Signals via STL-Guided Stein Variational Policy Gradient

arXiv

Hongrui Zheng, Cristian Ioan Vasile, Antonio Loquercio, Rahul Mangharam

AI Summary

Learning robot policies for complex tasks with sparse success signals, involving coordinated actions and precise physical interactions, is challenging as traditional reward shaping often fails to ensure global task completion. The paper proposes Signal Temporal Logic-guided Stein Variational Policy Gradient (STL-SVPG), a population-based method using smooth STL robustness as a trajectory-level objective to assign credit to actions based on their impact on the complete task specification. STL-SVPG achieved the highest mean success rate among compared methods on five of six quadcopter and manipulator benchmarks, with simulation-trained policies successfully transferring temporal and contact task behaviors to the real world.

Generate, Track, Improve: Perceptive Multi-Skill Humanoid Locomotion with RL-Fine-Tuned Motion Generators

arXiv

Zachary Olkin, William D. Compton, Aaron D. Ames

AI Summary

Humanoids require robust, perceptive, multi-skill locomotion controllers for dynamic navigation across diverse terrains. This work presents a two-layer architecture: a perceptive motion generator, which plans whole-body trajectories from raw depth images, and an RL-trained tracking policy that follows them, both initialized with dynamically optimized human data. A novel, sample-efficient off-policy RL fine-tuning loop iteratively improves the motion generator's terrain consistency using a structured search and advantage-weighted regression. This method increased successful terrain traversals by up to 25% and skill selection by up to 80%, enabling a single policy to execute complex locomotion skills on a real humanoid outdoors using only raw depth input.

Strategically Diverse Sampling for Self-Training

arXiv

Alexander Gurung, Esmeralda S. Whitammer, Mirella Lapata

AI Summary

LLM self-training data typically overrepresents a model's existing strategies by sampling IID responses filtered for correctness, hindering robust improvement by lacking "strategic diversity." This paper proposes two methods, GROOT (hierarchical sampling) and adapted Verbalized Sampling, to generate self-training data with substantively diverse problem-solving approaches. Models trained on this strategically diverse data significantly outperform IID-trained counterparts on difficult tasks and serve as better initializations, with diverse but incorrect traces from a smaller model even surpassing IID distillation from a much larger teacher. These results show diversity of approaches can be more critical for self-training than correctness or teacher scale.

Generalization behavior of OPTQ and the role of regularization

arXiv

Erin George, Rayan Saab

AI Summary

This paper investigates the generalization behavior of OPTQ weight quantization for large neural networks, focusing on the role of regularization. The proposed method involves studying OPTQ and stochastic OPTQ by deriving bounds for the expected squared quantization error on test points. Key results include relating generalization error to calibration error, bounding stochastic OPTQ's error independently of the calibration set for suitable distributions, and demonstrating the critical role of the regularization term $\lambda$. Based on these insights, a new and experimentally favorable recommendation for choosing $\lambda$ is provided.

EAServe: Encode-Aware Disaggregated Serving for Multimodal Large Language Models

arXiv

Kunxiong Zhu, Zhihao Shu, Hangyu Zheng, Minghai Qin, Miao Yin, Gagan Agrawal, Wei Niu

AI Summary

Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) introduce an "Encode" stage to the Prefill-Decode pipeline, creating an EPD pipeline that suffers from severe Encode GPU underutilization and resource imbalance, starving downstream workers. EAServe addresses this by repositioning Encode as the EPD pipeline's control point, managing work entry, prefill execution, and GPU sharing through a runtime that employs load-adaptive micro-batching, rate-controlled offload, and dynamic SM partitioning. A co-designed configuration layer optimizes GPU allocation, encode batch size, and offload ratio. This approach achieves up to 4.3x higher goodput than NVIDIA Dynamo and 1.7x higher than vLLM, sustains more balanced and higher GPU utilization across the EPD pipeline, and reaches near-optimal configurations faster.

HySTAR: Anchored Hypergraphs for Stable Credit Assignment in Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Xinglong Luo, Yuding Zhang, Yuheng Kuang, Shuxuan Yuan, Zhenni Zeng, Weiqiang Zhu, Zhenhai Ji, Zhengning Wang

AI Summary

The core problem in cooperative MARL is "structural target drift," where existing critics inconsistently map agents/coalitions to value components, hindering stable credit assignment. HySTAR is proposed as a MAPPO-based framework that separates adaptive representation learning from a temporally consistent high-order value-decomposition basis, anchoring an overlapping sparse hypergraph and using a spatiotemporal encoder. Experiments on SMAC, GRF, Traffic Junction, and MPE demonstrate HySTAR consistently outperforms MAPPO-style, value-factorization, and dynamic-grouping baselines, achieving significant performance gains and faster convergence.

Scaffold: Support Graph Theory Based Sparsification for Graph Neural Networks

arXiv

Siddhartha Shankar Das, Sai Karthik Navuluru, S M Ferdous, Ryan A. Rossi, Baris Coskunuzer, Lakshman Tamil, Edoardo Serra, Alex Pothen, Robert Rallo, Mahantesh M Halappanavar

AI Summary

Dense graphs make Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) computationally and memory intensive, while indiscriminate sparsification distorts communication and degrades performance. Scaffold is an unsupervised, topology-based graph sparsification framework derived from support graph theory, which jointly controls dilation (rerouting path length) and congestion (rerouting path concentration) to preserve short communication paths and avoid structural bottlenecks. Across 19 benchmarks, Scaffold achieves the best aggregate rank among evaluated methods. Using only 10%-50% of edges, it recovers or closely approaches full-graph GNN performance, reducing memory by over half and decreasing end-to-end training time.

Depth-resolved elastic property tomography using higher-order shear-horizontal modes

arXiv

Peter Huthwaite

AI Summary

Conventional ultrasonic methods dilute weak, localized elastic stiffness changes, making early material degradation difficult to resolve with depth. This paper introduces DEPTH, a novel tomography method utilizing mode-to-mode scattering among higher-order shear-horizontal guided waves and a Born formulation to reconstruct depth-resolved elastic property variations within a plate. Simulations demonstrate excellent through-thickness resolution (FWHM 0.84-1.03 mm) and successful recovery of compact targets at correct depths, even with single-surface excitation and reception. This provides the first demonstration of depth-resolved elastic-property tomography from multimode guided-wave transmission with surface-access measurements.

Different Corruptions, Different Signals: Uncertainty and Loss in Federated Data Quality

arXiv

Bradley Scott, Zeqi Luo, Edmond S. L. Ho

AI Summary

The paper investigates whether input-conditional uncertainty and prediction-label loss equally expose input-level versus label-level data corruption in federated learning (FL). It compares these two signals—uncertainty (via aleatoric variance, MC dropout, entropy) and prediction-label loss—for detecting additive image noise (input corruption) and persistent random label flips (label corruption) on non-IID FL datasets. Key results show prediction-label loss is a strong detector for label flips (AUC 0.85-0.95), while uncertainty estimators stay at chance; conversely, expected-entropy uncertainty performs better for image noise (AUC ~0.67) than loss (~0.64). This highlights that robust FL data quality assessment requires matching the detection signal to the specific corruption type.
