CityRAG: Stepping Into a City via Spatially-Grounded Video Generation

arXiv

Gene Chou, Charles Herrmann, Kyle Genova, Boyang Deng, Songyou Peng, Bharath Hariharan, Jason Y. Zhang, Noah Snavely, Philipp Henzler

AI Summary

The core problem is generating 3D-consistent, spatially-grounded, navigable simulations of real locations, as existing video generative models struggle to reconstruct the real world under arbitrary conditions for applications like autonomous driving. The proposed method, CityRAG, is a video generative model that leverages large corpora of geo-registered data for scene grounding and uses temporally unaligned training data to semantically disentangle the underlying scene from transient attributes. Key results demonstrate CityRAG can generate minutes-long, physically grounded video sequences, maintain consistent weather/lighting over thousands of frames, achieve loop closure, and reconstruct real-world geography through complex trajectories.

Safe Continual Reinforcement Learning in Non-stationary Environments

arXiv

Austin Coursey, Abel Diaz-Gonzalez, Marcos Quinones-Grueiro, Gautam Biswas

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning (RL) struggles with safe adaptation in non-stationary real-world environments because existing continual RL and safe RL methods do not adequately address their intersection: maintaining safety throughout adaptation. This work systematically investigates safe continual RL by introducing three benchmark environments and evaluating various approaches, including regularization-based strategies. Empirical results reveal a fundamental tension between maintaining safety constraints and preventing catastrophic forgetting, with existing methods generally failing to achieve both objectives simultaneously, although regularization partially mitigates this trade-off.

UniT: Toward a Unified Physical Language for Human-to-Humanoid Policy Learning and World Modeling

arXiv

Boyu Chen, Yi Chen, Lu Qiu, Jerry Bai, Yuying Ge, Yixiao Ge

AI Summary

Scaling humanoid foundation models is bottlenecked by scarce robotic data and the challenge of transferring abundant human data due to kinematic mismatches. UniT (Unified Latent Action Tokenizer via Visual Anchoring) proposes a unified physical language through a tri-branch cross-reconstruction, grounding heterogeneous kinematics to universal visual consequences and creating a shared, discrete latent space of embodiment-agnostic physical intents. For policy learning (VLA-UniT), this achieves state-of-the-art data efficiency and robust zero-shot generalization across simulations and real-world humanoid deployments. For world modeling (WM-UniT), it enables direct human-to-humanoid action transfer and enhanced action controllability for humanoid video generation by aligning cross-embodiment dynamics.

FASTER: Value-Guided Sampling for Fast RL

arXiv

Perry Dong, Alexander Swerdlow, Dorsa Sadigh, Chelsea Finn

AI Summary

The core problem is the prohibitive computational cost of test-time action sampling in high-performing reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly those using diffusion policies. FASTER proposes to solve this by modeling the multi-candidate denoising and selection process as a Markov Decision Process, learning a policy and value function to progressively filter action candidates early. This lightweight method consistently improves underlying policies and achieves top performance on challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks. Furthermore, when applied to a pretrained VLA, FASTER maintains performance while substantially reducing training and inference compute.

FB-NLL: A Feature-Based Approach to Tackle Noisy Labels in Personalized Federated Learning

arXiv

Abdulmoneam Ali, Ahmed Arafa

AI Summary

Existing Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) methods that cluster users based on learning dynamics are vulnerable to noisy labels, degrading personalization performance. FB-NLL proposes a feature-centric framework that decouples user clustering from training dynamics, performing a one-shot, label-agnostic grouping based on the spectral structure of local feature covariances. It then integrates a feature-consistency-based strategy to detect and correct noisy labels within these clusters by leveraging directional alignment in the learned feature space. Experiments demonstrate FB-NLL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in average accuracy and stability across diverse datasets and noise regimes.

AI Summary

Existing robot world models often overfit to irrelevant RGB video details, reducing generalization and leading to fragile control policies. Learning Hybrid-Control Policies for High-Precision In-Contact Manipulation Under Uncertainty

arXiv

Hunter L. Brown, Geoffrey Hollinger, Stefan Lee

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning (RL) pose-based policies struggle with high-precision in-contact manipulation, like delicate connector insertion, due to limited explicit force control, risking damage. This work proposes learning hybrid position-force control policies that dynamically select control modes, introducing Mode-Aware Training for Contact Handling (MATCH) to improve learning efficiency by mirroring mode selection behavior. MATCH substantially outperforms pose-control policies in fragile peg-in-hole tasks under uncertainty, achieving up to 10% higher success rates and 5x fewer peg breaks. It also demonstrates robust sim-to-real transfer, succeeding twice as often in high noise and applying 30% less force than impedance policies.

Multi-Cycle Spatio-Temporal Adaptation in Human-Robot Teaming

arXiv

Alex Cuellar, Michael Hagenow, Julie Shah

AI Summary

Optimizing human-robot plans in multi-cycle domains is challenging because existing methods isolate task-level scheduling and motion-level adaptation, failing to jointly account for individualized human spatial and temporal behaviors. AI Summary

AI Summary

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) acting as autonomous agents demonstrate insufficient ability to proactively address safety hazards, with existing evaluations primarily focusing on disembodied hazard recognition rather than active risk mitigation. The paper introduces SafetyALFRED, an embodied agent benchmark augmenting ALFRED with real-world kitchen hazards, to evaluate MLLMs on both hazard recognition and active risk mitigation through embodied planning. Lyapunov-Certified Direct Switching Theory for Q-Learning

arXiv

Donghwan Lee

AI Summary

This paper analyzes constant-stepsize Q-learning by representing its error as a direct stochastic switching system, modeling the Bellman maximization error as a stochastic policy. This approach reveals the intrinsic drift rate of Q-learning is the Joint Spectral Radius (JSR) of this switching family, which can be strictly tighter than standard rates. Utilizing a JSR-induced Lyapunov function, the authors derive a finite-time final-iterate bound and provide a computable quadratic-certificate version.

Multi-modal Reasoning with LLMs for Visual Semantic Arithmetic

arXiv

Chuou Xu, Liya Ji, Qifeng Chen

AI Summary

Large language models (LLMs) struggle with visual semantic arithmetic, inferring semantic relationships from images (e.g., "king" - "man" + "woman" with visual inputs), due to modality gaps and difficulty extracting concise concepts, which is crucial for robotics. To address this, the paper formulates two novel tasks and constructs the Image-Relation-Pair Dataset (IRPD) for benchmarking. They propose Semantic Arithmetic Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (SAri-RFT), which post-trains large vision-language models (LVLMs) using a verifiable function and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). SAri-RFT achieves state-of-the-art results on both IRPD and the real-world Visual7W-Telling dataset, significantly advancing cross-modal relational reasoning for domestic robots.
