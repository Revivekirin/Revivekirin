Multimodal Flow: Unified Flow Modeling of Language and Vision in Embedding Spaces

arXiv

Hongyuan Tao, Xinggang Wang, Lianghui Zhu, Yongkang Li, Yunchao Wei, Bin Feng, Shaoyu Chen, Qian Zhang, Chang Huang, Kai Yu

AI Summary

Existing unified multimodal models either introduce visual quantization bottlenecks or require modality-dependent objectives, leaving fully continuous unified modeling underexplored. Multimodal Flow proposes a unified continuous generative model that organizes language and vision into ordered continuous "hyperchunks" and learns a shared vector field over them using Flow Matching. Pretraining Multimodal Flow consistently improves performance across scales, achieving competitive results on various multimodal benchmarks with significantly less pretraining data. It further outperforms representative hybrid and discrete models under matched conditions, establishing a new fully continuous paradigm for unified multimodal modeling.

Ranking-Aware Prompt Optimization for Multimodal Clinical Diagnosis

arXiv

Tian Xia, Minghao Liu, Yiqing Liang, Laixi Shi, Jiayun Wang

AI Summary

MLLMs for clinical diagnosis struggle with class-imbalanced data, making traditional accuracy-based prompt optimization ineffective and clinically misleading; thus, AUROC optimization is critical. This paper introduces Ranking-PE, a pair-level Pareto prompt evolution method that directly optimizes empirical AUROC by replacing accuracy's instance-level correctness with pairwise-ordering comparisons throughout the prompt search. Ranking-PE significantly outperforms accuracy-based prompt evolution, boosting AUROC by up to +16.2 percentage points on MLLMs for multimodal clinical diagnosis.

Semifactual Credit-Augmented Policy Optimization

arXiv

Junshu Pan, Zhizhang Fu, Shulin Huang, Yiran Ding, Zifan Cheng, Wenqi Shao, Qiaosheng Zhang, Yue Zhang

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trained LLMs are sensitive to task-irrelevant prompt features, a limitation exacerbated by methods like GRPO which assign uniform credit to all tokens, potentially reinforcing spurious dependencies. This paper proposes Semifactual Credit-Augmented Policy Optimization (SCAPO), a causally inspired method that uses semifactual interventions to measure token probability drift and incorporates normalized stability scores to reduce advantages for unstable tokens during early training. SCAPO significantly improves reasoning accuracy, outperforming GRPO by 5.63 and 4.17 percentage points on AIME 2024-2026 for Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-1.7B-Base respectively, achieving superior results across most mathematics and all evaluated out-of-distribution benchmarks.

arXiv

Jiahao Zhang, Yeying Fan, Moitreya Chatterjee, Suhas Lohit, Bernhard Egger, Tim K. Marks, Anoop Cherian, Stephen Gould

AI Summary

This paper investigates whether pretrained general-purpose agents can perform 3D object assembly through visual interaction without specific fine-tuning. To address this, the authors introduce AssemblyWorld, an interactive 3D environment where agents manipulate parts based on rendered views, and AssemblyWorldBench, a benchmark of 100 diverse assembly tasks. arXiv

Quantization through Dissipation and the Optical Quantum Hall Effect

arXiv

Zhenisbek Tagay, Ahmed Abouelkomsan, Yugo Onishi, Adbhut Gupta, Loren Pfeiffer, Liang Fu, N. P. Armitage

AI Summary

The paper explores an additional route to understanding the exact quantization in the integer quantum Hall effect, focusing on its origin in finite-frequency dissipative bulk electrodynamics. Using high-precision terahertz Faraday rotation and numerics, the authors show that cyclotron resonance alone does not produce quantized plateaus. Quantization is recovered only by including a faint, low-frequency, topologically enforced dissipative contribution from impurity states, thereby tying the DC Hall conductivity to the finite-frequency optical response.

Compression Footprints as Security Signals for Model-Poisoning Defense in Federated Learning

arXiv

Sachi Shome, William Eiers

AI Summary

Existing Federated Learning (FL) model-poisoning defenses often overlook lossy compression as a potential security signal, treating it only as an error source. This work proposes using "compression footprints"—low-dimensional statistics induced by lossy compressors—to differentiate honest and malicious updates. Their method, CRAFT, is a server-side robust aggregation technique leveraging these footprints to detect and suppress poisoned updates without additional communication or client-side metadata. CRAFT, particularly with error-bounded compressors, effectively suppresses malicious influence, consistently achieving competitive or best accuracy against various poisoning attacks and outperforming multiple baselines under challenging conditions.

DynaHarness: A Dynamic Physical Harness for Self-Evolving Robot Agents

arXiv

Haoyuan Deng, Jiebin Liu, Tengxiao Zhang, Langning Yan, Hongye Cao, Ziwei Wang

AI Summary

Long-horizon robot manipulation faces challenges in coordinating slow semantic reasoning with fast physical execution, as current failure signals provide limited guidance for system revisions. DynaHarness proposes a dynamic physical harness that couples semantic reasoning and physical governance through a shared execution contract, using a "slow brain" for capability proposals and a "fast brain" for grounding, monitoring, and replanning. This system attributes execution failures to specific components, enabling targeted capability revisions and a self-evolution loop via regression checks. On LIBERO-Pro, DynaHarness achieves 75.2% success on new states compared to 17.5% for a frozen policy, and 74.0% with full dynamic execution versus 63.9% with nominal one-step replanning.

GPU-Accelerated Path-Dependent Marginal Information Gain for Autonomous Exploration

arXiv

João Félix Mendes, Rodrigo Ventura, Meysam Basiri

AI Summary

Autonomous exploration planners face the challenge of efficiently computing path-dependent marginal information gain, as current methods assume viewpoint independence due to the computational cost of volumetric raycasting. This work proposes a GPU-accelerated method that uses depth buffers to represent previous observations, projecting candidate rays into ancestor buffers to identify and exclude observation overlap, processing the planning tree and candidates in parallel. The method achieves marginal gain computation within 5-10% of exact values with substantial speed-ups (up to 118x). It consistently reduced the time to 95% coverage in simulations and real-world experiments by up to 30%, also leading to earlier exploration termination.

Belief-Aware Multi-Agent Path Finding under Map Uncertainty

ACL

Viraj Parimi, Shao-Hung Chan, Han Zhang, Jingkai Chen, Brian Williams

AI Summary

Classical Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) assumes known maps, but real-world environments have spatially correlated uncertainties that prior methods fail to leverage, leading to inefficient pathfinding. This paper proposes Multi-Agent Gaussian belief Inference for Coordination (MAGIC), a framework that online updates a shared traversability belief using a Gaussian Markov Random Field and Gaussian Belief Propagation. MAGIC then uses this inferred belief to construct detour-aware costs for standard MAPF planners. Experiments show MAGIC reduces the executed sum of costs on 96.3% of instances compared to existing approaches, proving its applicability to large-scale MAPF with up to 800 agents.

ACL

AI Summary

arXiv

Hurum Maksora Tohfa, Matthew McQuinn

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the costly storage and slow sharing of enormous cosmological datasets from simulations and sky surveys, where 32-bit float precision is often excessive given scientific accuracy needs. The proposed method evaluates the scientific compressor SZ3 and a novel neural compressor utilizing an autoregressive transformer to learn quantization bin probabilities for fixed-quantization lossy compression.
