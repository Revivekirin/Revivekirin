arXiv

arXiv

AI Summary

Aquatic self-reconfigurable robots face the challenge of safely forming desired shapes while avoiding inter-agent collisions. This paper proposes a hybrid framework combining distributed Model Predictive Control (MPC) for coordinated trajectory generation and distributed Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for real-time collision avoidance. Higher Superspace Supergravity and its IR-Completions

arXiv

Grigorios Giotopoulos, Hisham Sati, Urs Schreiber

AI Summary

This paper rigorously re-examines the concept of higher-dimensional supergravity (SuGra) being on-shell via super-Bianchi identities and develops a framework for its infrared (IR) completions. The proposed method involves interpreting super-Bianchi identities within differential nonabelian cohomology theories, allowing for IR-completion through electromagnetic flux quantization. Key results include proving that 11D SuGra solutions are equivalent to $\mathfrak{l}S^4$-Bianchi identities on super C-field flux, and deriving analogous equivalences for 6D M5-branes and dimensionally reduced 10D IIA/9D SuGra. This framework enables consistent IR-completions of 11D SuGra in 4-Cohomotopy, 10D IIA SuGra in twisted unstable K-theory, and M5-branes in twisted relative Cohomotopy.

Real Quantum Field Theory, J-Quantization, and Standard Model

arXiv

I. Aref'eva, I. Volovich

AI Summary

This paper introduces Real Quantum Field Theory (RQFT) to formulate quantum field theory entirely using real numbers, by replacing the imaginary unit 'i' with a real 2x2 matrix 'J' (J^2=-1) and developing a corresponding J-calculus. It successfully constructs RQFT for a scalar field, demonstrating that physical observables and predictions remain identical to ordinary QFT, with the scattering operator becoming orthogonal and symplectic. Furthermore, the Standard Model admits this purely real formulation, exhibiting ortho-symplectic symmetry. Breaking this J-symmetry is proposed as a marker for potential new physics beyond the Standard Model.

arXiv

Md Ridwan Hossain Talukder, Roshan Dhakal, Elizabeth Phillips, Gregory J. Stein

AI Summary

Standard multi-robot task planners in persistent shared environments are myopic and inconsiderate, optimizing tasks in isolation and accumulating future costs across lengthy sequences. The proposed "courteous anticipatory planning" selects plans that jointly minimize immediate cost and aggregated expected future cost across all robots, estimated by independent, learned per-robot estimators to maintain modularity. Angular Momentum Quantization of a Charge-flux Composite: Quantum Electrodynamic Approach

arXiv

Kicheon Kang

AI Summary

The paper addresses a conceptual discrepancy where semiclassically derived fractional angular momentum for two-dimensional charge-flux composites conflicts with the strict integer/half-integer quantization demanded by fundamental symmetries for isolated systems. Using a full quantum electrodynamic (QED) approach combined with Noether's theorem, the authors demonstrate that the interaction between charge and flux generates an intrinsic, gauge-invariant angular momentum. This interaction angular momentum exactly compensates for the fractional kinetic angular momentum, ensuring the composite's net angular momentum is strictly integer or half-integer quantized. The formalism clarifies that conventional fractional spin corresponds to the expectation value of the kinetic angular momentum within the perturbed QED ground state.

DINS-IO: Learned Inertial Odometry via Differentiable INS Consistency

arXiv

Hao Qiao, Yan Wang, Jian Kuang, Xiaoji Niu

AI Summary

The core problem is that learned inertial odometry training requires costly, hard-to-acquire dense position ground truth. DINS-IO proposes a self-supervised method that learns directly from IMU streams by leveraging the differentiable strapdown INS velocity recursion as a consistency prior in a sliding-window least-squares loss. A high-frequency network predicts dense body-frame velocity, which is then metrically calibrated using low-rank adaptation (LoRA) with a small fraction of labeled trajectories. This approach matches or surpasses fully supervised baselines on standard benchmarks using significantly fewer labels.
