Uncovering Understanding-Generation Synergy in Native Unified Multimodal Models: From Representation, Task to System

arXiv

Penghao Wu, Haiwen Diao, Weichen Fan, Lewei Lu, Dahua Lin, Ziwei Liu

AI Summary

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) aim to jointly perform visual understanding and generation, but it's unclear if these objectives genuinely synergize, compete, or merely coexist. This study investigates their relationship at the representation, task, and system levels in a native, non-pretrained setting. It found that understanding and generation offer mutual benefits at the representation level, but forcing them through the same computation path causes one to dominate, which a task-decoupled architecture can mitigate. Positive bidirectional transfer occurs when tasks share knowledge, and an end-to-end UMM outperforms pipeline approaches on complex tasks, demonstrating that appropriate specialization, shared knowledge, and end-to-end optimization enable true synergy.

Singular Weak-Field Thermodynamics of 2D Superconductors

arXiv

Guopeng Xu, Chunli Huang

AI Summary

Unlike 3D materials, 2D superconductors possess an anomalous size-dependent lower critical field for vortex formation, which this study investigates using an analytically tractable microscopic many-body model coupled to 3D Maxwell electrodynamics to account for screening. The authors find that the field required to create the first vortex decreases with sample area $\mathcal{A}$, scaling as $\mathcal{A}^{-1}\ln(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}_0)$ under weak screening and $\mathcal{A}^{-1/2}$ under strong screening. This reveals a singular zero-field thermodynamic limit in the $(1/\mathcal{A}, B)$ plane, where any system carrying a fixed finite magnetic flux ultimately converges to a uniform, vortex-free ground state.

Facet-0: A Robotic Foundation Model for Contact-Rich Precise Manipulation

arXiv

Haoyuan Deng, Haichao Liu, Wenkai Guo, Yuan Ling, Zaijia Yang, Yuanjiang Xue, Haosheng Sun, Liangzi Wang, Ziwei Wang

AI Summary

To address the challenge of sub-millimeter precision and compliant interaction in contact-rich robotic assembly, the authors introduce Facet-0, a robotic foundation model that jointly predicts executable actions and anticipated contact wrench profiles using flow matching and reinforcement learning post-training. Trained on the 1,000-hour ManuFacet-1K dataset, the system achieves an 82% mean success rate on five sub-millimeter computer-assembly tasks compared to 15% for the strongest baseline, while maintaining 0.5 mm placement accuracy and 50 ms command latency.

StudentSim: Training LLM-based Student Simulators

arXiv

Ke Yang, Chenglong Wang, Michel Galley, Chandan Singh, Jeevana Priya Inala, ChengXiang Zhai, Jianfeng Gao

AI Summary

Existing student simulators fail to simultaneously mirror individual learner competence and respond realistically to instructional guidance, hindering the data-driven training of adaptive AI tutors. To address this, the authors introduce **StudentSim**, a framework that leverages pooled training followed by per-student specialization to create simulators that capture individual behaviors while updating under tutor guidance, alongside the **StudentSimEval** benchmark. Across chess, ESL writing, and mathematics, StudentSim outperforms strong baselines (including GPT-5.4) in behavioral fidelity and guidance responsiveness, successfully serving as a reward model to train personalized, expert-preferred AI tutors via reinforcement learning.

The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally

NeurIPS

Jundong Hu, Shekar Ramachandran

AI Summary

Post-training quantization (PTQ) causes uneven accuracy loss in LLMs, creating uncertainty over how to best allocate a limited additional precision budget between selective layer protection and global adjustments. To investigate this, the authors used causal mixed-precision interventions across nine open-weight models to map quantization damage and evaluate common heuristics for layer recovery. The results demonstrate that quantization damage is predominantly diffuse, and allocating additional precision globally via finer quantization granularity consistently outperforms locally repairing critical layers by 21 to 52 points.
