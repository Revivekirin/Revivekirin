SeeQ: Training Generalist Value Functions for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

arXiv

Saksham Singh, Zheyuan Hu, Max Sobol Mark, Jeffrey Yu, Zackory Erickson, Aviral Kumar

AI Summary

Generalist robot policies struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks due to the long credit-assignment and difficult Bellman backups associated with sparse task-level rewards. SeeQ (Subtask-elicited Q-functions) addresses this by learning Q-values for the currently active subtask, leveraging subtask annotations from offline data to shorten the prediction horizon and enable effective TD learning. Crucially, its architecture autoregressively predicts the active subtask in natural language before value estimation, eliminating the need for test-time annotations. This method substantially improves best-of-N policy steering across four real-world manipulation tasks on two bimanual robot platforms.

AI Summary

The core problem is unifying UI grounding, multi-step navigation across diverse digital environments (mobile, desktop, web), and visual tool use, which are typically handled by domain-specific agents. LIMBO: Learning and Internalizing Model-Free Barrier Objectives for Agile and Safe Whole-Body Control

ACL

Jake Gonzales, Arturo Flores Alvarez, Yu-Ming Chen, Aaron D. Ames, Lillian J. Ratliff, Manikantan Nambi

AI Summary

Designing and reusing safety certificates for high-dimensional, nonlinear whole-body control is challenging. LIMBO addresses this by synthesizing a state-action control barrier function (Q-CBF) from black-box transitions over residual actions around a base controller, then distilling this learned safety into a task policy using risk-guided boundary sampling. This enables robust whole-body control for complex tasks (e.g., humanoid dodgeball and obstacle avoidance), facilitates diverse safety strategies, and allows deployment on hardware without online safety filtering.

AI Summary

$λ$-Controlled GRPO: Turning Flow-Matching Ratio Instability into a Budgeted Resource

arXiv

Yufeng Wang, Parivesh Priye, Meeshawn Marathe, Ramit Pahwa

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning alignment of flow-matching image generators faces instability due to systematic drift and dispersion of importance ratios across denoising steps, termed "path variance," previously addressed by ad-hoc stabilizers. The proposed $λ$-Controlled GRPO method quantifies this path variance using the sampler's Gaussian transition kernel and leverages it to calibrate importance-ratio behavior and allocate gradient effort across steps, reframing instability as a budgetable resource. This approach significantly improves text accuracy and preference rewards over empirical stabilizers on a text-to-image model, while successfully keeping late-step path variance within its intended budget.

arXiv

AI Summary

Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving suffer from early perception being agnostic to downstream reasoning and navigation goals, processing visual inputs without prioritizing cues informed by prior decisions. Optical Mode Sorting with a Programmable Diffractive Neural Network

arXiv

Qian Zhang, Shiyue Chen, Juergen W Czarske

AI Summary

Programmable diffractive optical processors struggle with a performance gap between simulation and experiment due to real-world optical aberrations and nonideal responses, making dynamic compensation difficult. This paper proposes a hybrid optimization framework that combines high-dimensional numerical design of optical transformations with low-dimensional hardware-in-the-loop calibration. Utilizing stochastic parallel gradient descent to optimize a truncated Zernike basis (19 coefficients per layer) directly on the physical system, it compensates for experimental distortions without retraining the full network. Applied to sorting six multimode fiber modes, this method achieved a 3.26 dB SNR improvement and a 0.04 decrease in amplitude error, demonstrating a scalable approach for adaptive spatial-mode processors.

SkelWAM: A Skeleton-Guided World-Action Model for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

arXiv

Pengjun Niu, Yujia Xie, Rui Peng, Hang Zhao, Ke Liu

AI Summary

The core problem is reusing manipulation experience across vastly different robot embodiments due to varying visual appearance, action dimensionality, and body configurations, ideally in a zero-shot manner. SkelWAM proposes a skeleton-guided world-action model that uses a shared 25-D canonical state, comprising arm centerline geometry, TCP pose, and parallel-jaw commands, for both perception and action. A transformer predicts these canonical skeleton action chunks, which are then translated into embodiment-specific controls by constrained decoders. The method achieves 43.3% success on the new LIBERO-Cross10 benchmark, outperforming baselines by 36.2 percentage points, and demonstrates real-world zero-shot transfer to a continuum robot.

CARF: Contrastive Attraction-Repulsion of Failure-Guided Flow Matching

arXiv

Shuqi Zhao, Bang Du, Cheng-En Wu, Yichen Xie, Yixiao Wang, Masayoshi Tomizuka

AI Summary

Robot learning from demonstrations often includes failed trajectories, but existing methods overlook "failure-critical behaviors" that directly lead to task failure. CARF proposes a contrastive attraction-repulsion framework using a progress-based importance scorer to identify progressive versus failure-critical segments in imperfect data. These scores guide a unified flow-matching objective, attracting the policy to progressive behaviors and repelling it from failure-critical ones. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over baselines in simulation and real-world scenarios, validating the proposed scoring and attraction-repulsion mechanisms.

Automata-Theoretic Verification of Interval Markov Decision Processes

arXiv

Sarvin Bahmani, Soumyajit Paul, Sven Schewe, Sadegh Soudjani, Ashutosh Trivedi

AI Summary

This paper tackles the automata-theoretic verification of Interval Markov Decision Processes (IMDPs), which model stochastic systems with uncertain transition probabilities, against ω-regular temporal specifications. It proposes extending classical automata-theoretic techniques, revealing a key distinction: "stable" IMDPs reduce to ordinary MDP analysis using good-for-MDP automata, while "unstable" IMDPs require a game-like approach with good-for-games automata. Algorithms are developed based on these insights, providing probabilistic guarantees for IMDPs learned from sampled data, enabling principled verification under model uncertainty.

arXiv

AI Summary

When Should a Failing Robot Ask? Initiating Corrective Human-Robot Dialogue from Audited Sensor Evidence

arXiv

Eshika Pathak, Leela Krishna

AI Summary

A core problem is determining when a failing robot should self-diagnose, consult other sensors, or interrupt a human, based on sensor evidence and diagnosis reliability. Researchers built a simulated benchmark with known failure causes to measure sensor informativeness (e.g., force vs. camera data) and tested six vision-language models (VLMs) on their diagnostic abilities and asking behavior. Key results show force data is highly diagnostic (0.99) while images are poor (<0.55), VLMs' asking rates are highly sensitive to prompt wording rather than evidence or confidence, and while VLMs improved with textual sensor data, they failed to follow an optimal asking policy. However, asking a human significantly improved overall reliability (0.70-0.81), highlighting that asking should be tied to measured accuracy and costs, not model confidence.

MAAP: Multi-Agent Active Perception for Collaborative Manipulation

arXiv

Bruno N. Y. Chen, Li Kang, Heng Zhou, Xiufeng Song, Zhemeng Zhang, Jiahua Ma, Yiran Qin

AI Summary

Multi-agent manipulation underutilizes inherent wrist camera observations for active perception, often relying on dedicated sensing and achieving suboptimal task success. This paper introduces MAAP (Multi-Agent Active Perception), where every arm is dual-purpose for manipulation and sensing, paired with RAIL (Role-Aware Imitation Learning), a controller conditioning actions on predicted arm roles. In simulation, MAAP alone increased success from 56.5% (fixed camera) to 70.0% (all active views), while MAAP+RAIL achieved 79.2%, notably boosting a three-arm task from 47% to 82%. On a real dual-arm robot, MAAP+RAIL successfully completed 14 of 20 placement trials, outperforming a fixed-view baseline (0/20).

Quantized Transport of Gap Solitons in the Harper-Hofstadter Model

arXiv

Oliver Ashfield, Henry Davenport, Tom Sheppard, David G. Reid, Holly A. J. Middleton-Spencer, A. C. Berceanu, Frank Schindler, Iacopo Carusotto, Hannah M. Price

AI Summary

This work addresses the challenge of robustly controlling nonlinear wavepacket transport by investigating the dynamics of gap solitons in the Harper-Hofstadter model with cubic nonlinearity under an external force. The authors demonstrate that solitons positioned deep within the band gap travel with a robust, quantized velocity that is perpendicular and linearly proportional to the applied force, preserving their spatial profile over long timescales. In contrast to solitons near the band edge that deform and delocalize, these deep-gap solitons enable arbitrary, force-tunable steering of nonlinear wavepackets in cold-atom and optical platforms.

Beyond Kinematics: Benchmarking Simulation Fidelity for Muscle-Driven Imitation Learning

arXiv

Ayah G. Ahmad, Claire E. Borden, Maegan Tucker

AI Summary

Although motion-imitation reinforcement learning pipelines successfully reproduce human kinematics, it remains unclear whether they accurately capture the underlying neuromuscular mechanics critical for designing robotic assistive devices. To evaluate this, the authors systematically benchmarked a physiologically detailed simulator (SCONE/HyFyDy) against a computationally efficient one (MuJoCo/MyoSim) using paired human motion-capture and electromyography (EMG) data. While both frameworks achieved comparable kinematic accuracy, HyFyDy demonstrated substantially higher physiological fidelity, aligning far more closely with experimental muscle activations than MuJoCo (activation RMSE of 0.164 vs. 0.344).
