RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering

arXiv

Tuna Han Salih Meral, Kaan Oktay, Alec Helbling, Atakan Kara, Duen Horng Chau, Pinar Yanardag

AI Summary

RL post-training significantly improves diffusion transformers, with the underlying assumption that it learns complex, input-dependent transformations for prompt-dependent tasks like text rendering. This paper proposes a simpler method: fixed "steering vectors" added to frozen model block outputs, which are input-independent and fitted to match the velocity predictions of public RL-tuned checkpoints. These vectors, with 65x fewer parameters, recover 90.0-111.9% of the reward gains from full RL-tuned models. The key finding is that a constant, input-independent shift largely explains the benefits of RL post-training, essentially steering the pretrained generator towards what it can already compute.

AnyVIO: Feed-Forward Streaming Visual-Inertial Odometry Across Platforms

arXiv

Omoruyi Atekha, Marcus Greiff, Ray Zhang, Luca Carlone

AI Summary

Existing geometric foundation models (GFMs) for camera motion lack metric scale, and integrating inertial data typically reintroduces complex tuning or growing optimization costs for visual-inertial odometry (VIO). AnyVIO proposes a real-time, feed-forward streaming VIO solution that combines a streaming GFM with an Inertial Foundation Model (IFM), using a learned fusion head to blend their metric motion estimates in a single pass. This approach reduces metric position error by 36% while achieving a 70ms latency per pose, demonstrating accuracy across diverse datasets and robust deployment on real vehicles in challenging conditions.

One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning

arXiv

Yuxin Jiang, Wenqiang Sun, Jiaqi Li, Yuchao Gu, Lvmin Zhang, Ivor Tsang, Mike Zheng Shou

AI Summary

Latent action learning from videos struggles with learning underlying dynamics from scratch. The proposed method, O$^{2}$LaF, repurposes a single pretrained video generator (e.g., Video DiT) as a prior for both inverse dynamics (transition encoder) and forward dynamics (action-conditioned generator), optimized end-to-end. This approach learns transferable actions from unlabeled videos, achieving new state-of-the-art in few-shot action probing and enabling stronger zero-shot action transfer across diverse visual contexts and embodiments.

Fractional Charge at Dislocations from Band-Inversion Cancellation

arXiv

Vladimir Juričić, Rodrigo Soto-Garrido

AI Summary

A bulk topological probe is lacking for crystals where band inversions with opposite Chern numbers cancel, obscuring the underlying topology. The authors propose that dislocations in inversion-symmetric two-band crystals with cancelling band inversions bind a half-integer charge, revealing the bulk polarization. This fractional dislocation charge is robust, insensitive to core perturbations, and persists even after the in-gap core state is removed, providing a detectable bulk topological signature distinct from Chern number effects.

AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control

arXiv

Yiyang Du, Yuxuan Kuang, Qitao Zhao, Yixuan Wang, Zhanqiu Guo, Yi Xu, Xin Li, Wenbin He, Liu Ren, Chenyan Xiong

AI Summary

Existing benchmarks for frontier agents controlling robots directly via low-level actions provide only episode-level success rates, obscuring *how* or *where* they succeed or fail. To address this, the paper introduces AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework where the policy agent states a local intent for each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode. Action-level analysis revealed promising capabilities like closed-loop recovery and zero-shot manipulation, but also limitations such as physical execution bottlenecks (e.g., grasping); an open-source critic was also validated, achieving 74.24% failure-mode accuracy for efficient analysis.

Local velocimetry for atomic superfluids: from Riemann rarefaction waves to quantized vortices

arXiv

Yansheng Zhang, Yi Jiang, Feiyang Wang, Louis Depaepe, J. Q. Matthew Chia, Christoph Eigen, Gehrig Carlse, Zoran Hadzibabic

AI Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of local velocimetry in two-dimensional atomic superfluids. The proposed method utilizes matter-wave interferometry with Raman-Bragg beam splitters to map the local phase gradient of the superfluid's wavefunction to interference fringe displacement, thereby reconstructing the 2D velocity field. Key results include the direct observation of self-similar velocity evolution in 1D Riemann rarefaction flows and the precise mapping of 2D velocity fields around quantized vortices, explicitly demonstrating velocity circulation quantization.

A Tutorial on Complementarity-Based Contact Models for Robotics

arXiv

Onur Beker, A. René Geist, Michael Posa, Marc Toussaint, Justin Carpentier, Georg Martius

AI Summary

The core problem is the lack of a self-contained, pedagogical treatment for robotics on complex complementarity-based contact models used in rigid-body dynamics simulation. This tutorial bridges rigid-body dynamics, conic optimization, and frictional contact, explaining how complementarity conditions encode non-penetration and frictional dissipation without prescribed contact modes. It distinguishes formulations, analyzes solution existence/uniqueness, compares solver strategies (first-order, Newton, regularization), and discusses trade-offs, enabling robotics researchers to interpret simulations and develop robust contact dynamics routines.

Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher

ACL

Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, Yuxin Chen

AI Summary

Acquiring all features for an instance is often costly, and existing Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) methods train policies using sub-optimal self-constructed utility targets. This paper proposes OracleImitator, which explicitly constructs an oracle teacher at training time that ranks features by their effect on downstream prediction error. OracleImitator then distills this oracle ranking into a deployable policy using soft-target imitation. The method matches or outperforms existing AFA approaches, recovering 90% of attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost.

PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Kai Lu, Chenyang Ma, Ioannis Havoutis

AI Summary

Long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation tasks challenge VLA models due to tightly coupled temporal structure, skills, and precision. PGX addresses this by offloading temporal and precision control to classical, training-free components, allowing VLA models to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. It employs a three-layer decomposition: a predicate-based state machine for task progression, VLA skills for active manipulation, and world-coordinate locking for spatial consistency. Experiments on simulated and real humanoid robots demonstrate PGX improves stage progression and task success rates.

Probing the "Conscious" Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models

arXiv

Jiahao Huang, Jun Wang

AI Summary

Vision-language models' internal concept representations remain opaque, hindering understanding of their processing in terms of information selection and availability. This paper introduces the Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens), a multimodal Jacobian lens, which maps intermediate VLM states to a shared semantic space to study concept accessibility and allows internal concept interventions. Key results show that concept information is readable, selectively accessible as tasks require, and actively participates in downstream computation and reasoning. These patterns of accessibility and selective access, whose development can be tracked via novel J-space metrics, are consistent across diverse VLM families and sizes.

Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs

arXiv

Ziqing Yang, Yixin Wu, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang

AI Summary

Multimodal image editing systems often have safety mechanisms that fail to jointly consider risks from both input images and text prompts, leading to critical safety gaps. To analyze this, the authors introduce EditSafetyBench, a benchmark comprising 5,940 image-instruction pairs across 11 risk categories, used to probe four advanced editing systems. Results reveal a systematic text-vision safety mismatch: systems readily refuse unsafe text prompts but frequently allow editing of unsafe images with neutral instructions. This highlights the urgent need for safety guardrails that reason over both visual and textual modalities.

arXiv

Yuanshuo Zhang, Ying Zhang, Ziying Wang, Fanping Sui

AI Summary

AI data centers' bursty and regime-dependent load patterns present significant challenges for reliable energy management, necessitating accurate short-term GPU power forecasting. This paper proposes a thermal-guided ensemble reinforcement learning framework where a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent adaptively weights LSTM, TCN, and Transformer predictors. Grid Cells as Spectral Successor Features: A Reusable Basis for Continual Reinforcement Learning

NeurIPS

Élodie Côté-Gauthier, Doina Precup, Isabeau Prémont-Schwarz

AI Summary

Continual reinforcement learning faces challenges in adapting to task changes while efficiently reusing environmental knowledge, especially regarding the choice of base features for successor features. This paper proposes constructing these base features from eigenvectors of a fixed reference policy's transition matrix, inspired by entorhinal grid cells. This approach allows long-term return to be predicted from the same features as immediate reward via a closed-form linear combination, eliminating the need for successor-feature networks or replay buffers. Agents built this way achieve near-maximal return across task changes in gridworlds, outperforming baselines like SimpleSF and DQN which suffer significant performance drops.
