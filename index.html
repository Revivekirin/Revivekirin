AutoDex: An Automated Real-World System for Dexterous Grasping Data Collection

arXiv

Mingi Choi, Gunhee Kim, Jisoo Kim, Taeksoo Kim, Taeyun Ha, Jongbin Lim, Hanbyul Joo

AI Summary

Real-world dexterous grasping data is difficult to acquire at scale due to the slowness/bias of teleoperation and the lack of physical validity in simulation. AutoDex is an automated real-world data collection system that closes the loop by performing perception under severe occlusion, collision-monitored execution, success labeling, and active object resetting without human intervention. The system collected 3,593 grasp trials across 100 objects, demonstrating a 4.8x throughput improvement over teleoperation. Grasps retrieved from the AutoDex-validated database achieved a 76% success rate, significantly outperforming simulation-only validation (34%).

LIBERO-Safety: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Physical and Semantic Safety in Vision-Language-Action Models

arXiv

Rongxu Cui, Zongzheng Zhang, Jingrui Pang, Haohan Chi, Jinbang Guo, Saining Zhang, Shaoxuan Xie, Xin Jin, Yao Mu, Jiaolong Yang, Guocai Yao, Xianyuan Zhan, Ya-Qin Zhang, Hao Zhao

AI Summary

To address the unverified operational safety of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, the authors introduce LIBERO-Safety, a parametric benchmark featuring a novel keypose-driven data generation pipeline that curated 19,664 collision-free demonstrations. Systematic evaluation of ten VLA and embodied foundation models reveals a critical trade-off between generalization and safety. Specifically, while high-diversity training improves safety, overall task success remains bottlenecked by sub-optimal trajectory synthesis and semantic misalignment.

LaST-HD: Learning Latent Physical Reasoning from Scalable Human Data for Robot Manipulation

arXiv

Jiaming Liu, Yinxi Wang, Chenyang Gu, Siyuan Qian, Xiangju Mi, Hao Chen, Jiawei Chen, Qingpo Wuwu, Xiaoqi Li, Nuowei Han, Yiming Zhang, Xuheng Zhang, Yang Yue, Yeqing Yang, Lei Wang, Peng Jia, Hao Tang, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

To address the challenge of physical dynamics misalignment when transferring human demonstrations to robot manipulation, the authors propose LaST-HD, a learning paradigm that aligns cross-embodiment trajectories in a shared latent forward-dynamics space. Guided by an auxiliary action-conditioned world model and supported by a low-cost motion-capture glove (OOL Glove) for data collection, LaST-HD enables robots to internalize shared physical reasoning. Consequently, this approach significantly improves generalization to novel objects and environments, achieving over 90% task accuracy with only 20 minutes of human correction data.

CoorDex: Coordinating Body and Hand Priors for Continuous Dexterous Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Sikai Li, Shuning Li, Zhenyu Wei, Yunchao Yao, Chenran Li, Mingyu Ding

AI Summary

**Core Problem:** Traditional humanoid loco-manipulation is limited by disjointed "stop-and-go" execution and low-DoF end effectors, preventing fluid, high-dimensional dexterous manipulation while in motion. **Proposed Method:** The authors present **CoorDex**, a learning pipeline that distills simulated body and hand demonstrations into separate, proprioception-conditioned latent priors and coordinates them via a downstream residual reinforcement learning policy to preserve natural movement while ensuring precise contact control. **Key Results:** Implemented on a Unitree G1 humanoid with a 20-DoF hand, CoorDex successfully achieves continuous, contact-rich tasks like on-the-move bottle grasping and fridge opening, significantly outperforming joint-space and monolithic baseline methods.

AIR: Adaptive Interleaved Reasoning with Code in MLLMs

arXiv

Cong Han, Xiaohan Lan, Haibo Qiu, Yujie Zhong

AI Summary

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with complex numerical computation during interleaved reasoning because their current tool-use approaches rely primarily on visual-only heuristics. To resolve this, the authors introduce AIR, a framework that trains MLLMs via reinforcement learning (RL) on code-augmented tasks using a cold-start data pipeline, RL filtering, and an adaptive tool-invocation strategy with a group-constrained reward function. This approach improves average benchmark performance by 6.1 percentage points (pp), boosts interleaved reasoning accuracy by 9.9 pp, and achieves a tool-use success rate of over 95%.

IMAGIN-4D: Image-Guided Controllable Interaction Generation

arXiv

Sai Kumar Dwivedi, Federica Bogo, Buğra Tekin, Chenhongyi Yang, Nadine Bertsch, Tomas Hodan, Michael J. Black, Dimitrios Tzionas, Shreyas Hampali

AI Summary

On the Limits of Prompt-Conditioned Language Models as General-Purpose Learners

arXiv

David Mguni, Julian Ma, Jun Wang

AI Summary

This paper addresses the fundamental limitation of prompt-conditioned Large Language Models (LLMs) acting as general-purpose solvers due to the capacity-constrained nature of natural language. By modeling user-system interactions as a bilevel *cheap-talk* game and deriving PAC-Bayes bounds, the authors analyze the limitations of prompt-based task inference and execution under alignment constraints. Their analysis proves that LLMs face irreducible error floors—an "expressivity floor" when task complexity exceeds communication capacity, and an "objective-misalignment floor" from safety constraints—demonstrating that prompting alone cannot achieve universal problem-solving.

A Reduced Order Model for Emergent Mechanics in Woven Systems

arXiv

Anvay A. Pradhan, Evgueni T. Filipov, Talia Y. Moore

AI Summary

Existing models for woven structures struggle to balance computational efficiency with the detailed representation of complex, emergent mechanical behaviors. To bridge this gap, the authors propose a reduced-order model that represents individual weaver interactions using a node system and four physically interpretable stiffness elements capturing axial deformation, in-plane uncrimping, inter-weaver shear, and frictional slip. Calibrated to within 5% of empirical data, the model successfully simulates complex, non-continuum phenomena beyond the reach of traditional approaches, including crimp interchange, progressive weaver pullout, and programmable mechanical anisotropy.

Flatness Preserves Instruction Following in Vision-Language-Action Models

arXiv

Haochen Zhang, Yonatan Bisk

AI Summary

Finetuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on limited robot data degrades pretrained representations, causing "instruction blindness" where models ignore language cues due to a sharp loss landscape. To resolve this, the authors propose flatness-preserving optimization by applying Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) during downstream finetuning. This simple approach improves instruction following by over 60% across simulation and real-world benchmarks without requiring additional data or architectural modifications.

Pose Anything Anywhere:Model-free Object Poses from Arbitrary References

arXiv

Hongli Xu, Jiaqi Hu, Junwen Huang, Boyang Zhong, Peter KT Yu, Nassir Navab, Benjamin Busam, Slobodan Ilic

AI Summary

Existing model-free 6D object pose estimation methods struggle with occlusion and large viewpoint changes due to their reliance on limited pairwise single-anchor matching. To address this, the authors propose PANY, a unified framework that uses a multi-view transformer backbone to learn view-consistent geometry from sparse, pose-free RGB/RGB-D reference views, aggregating them via pose-graph canonical registration to handle severe viewpoint variations. PANY achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing model-free baselines by improving pose accuracy by +12% on YCB-V and over +20% on LM-O.

RECALL: Recovery Experience Collection for Active Lifelong Learning in Vision-Language-Action Models

arXiv

Ulas Berk Karli, Tesca Fitzgerald

AI Summary

**Core Problem:** Fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models via passive imitation learning is highly inefficient, wasting demonstrator effort on redundant task phases and requiring the robot to fail before triggering data collection. **Proposed Method:** The authors propose an active, continual learning paradigm that utilizes uncertainty-guided data collection to target states requiring recovery supervision, combined with continual learning techniques (such as replay-based data mixing and elastic weight consolidation) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. **Key Results:** While uncertainty-guided active data collection significantly improves fine-tuning efficiency compared to passive methods, training solely on this targeted recovery data causes catastrophic forgetting, highlighting a critical trade-off between model plasticity for new behaviors and the retention of previously learned skills.
