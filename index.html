ParVL: Parallel Scaling and Expandable Compute Allocation for Multimodal LLMs

arXiv

Yang Yang, Qinyu Zhao, Mouxiang Chen, Xiaohui Li, Lixin Gu, Wenhai Wang, Hongjie Zhang, Wenwei Zhang

AI Summary

Existing MLLM scaling strategies suffer from high memory/latency overhead and rigid computation allocation between Vision Transformer and Large Language Model components. ParVL addresses this by proposing a parallel scaling framework that reuses ViT and LLM backbone parameters across multiple vision and language branches, employing branch-specific prefix parameters for flexible compute allocation. This method improves multimodal performance compared to single-branch baselines and reveals that the optimal vision-language computation allocation varies by task.

TurnSight: Turn-Level Hindsight Self-Distillation for Tool-Integrated Reasoning

arXiv

Changle Qu, Sunhao Dai, Hengyi Cai, Yuqi Zhou, Xinran Chen, Simon, Jun Xu

AI Summary

Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) struggles with limited fine-grained credit assignment in trajectory-level RL and existing self-distillation methods rely on non-visited state contexts or token-level signals. TurnSight proposes a turn-level hindsight self-distillation framework that derives supervision directly from execution-conditioned hindsight, constructing multiple hindsight views with different lookahead horizons. It selects reliable supervision through cross-horizon directional agreement, which is then normalized across sibling rollouts to adaptively modulate RL advantages. Extensive experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate the framework's effectiveness.

Latent Reward Registers for Diffusion Preference Alignment

arXiv

Yuanshen Guan, Zipeng Feng, Zhiwei Xiong, Peiqin Sun

AI Summary

Sparse terminal rewards in diffusion model preference alignment present a severe temporal credit-assignment challenge across multi-step denoising. This paper proposes Latent Reward Registers (LRR), learnable tokens prepended to a frozen Diffusion Transformer, to estimate dense, differentiable rewards directly from intermediate noisy latents. Low-Dimensional High-Leverage Subspace Optimization: Beyond Full-Parameter Coupled Training for Neural Network Quantization

arXiv

Peng Xia, Junbiao Pang, Zheng Huang

AI Summary

Low-bit quantization severely degrades accuracy on compact networks due to monolithic full-parameter training that ignores parameter subspace heterogeneity. This work identifies normalization affine parameters as a low-dimensional, high-leverage subspace for quantization robustness, proposing Normalization Affine Preconditioning (NAP). NAP either fine-tunes only affine parameters for Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) or introduces an alternating QAT-NAP schema to decouple feature learning and numerical calibration. Experiments show NAP recovers collapsed low-bit quantization, consistently boosts PTQ, and outperforms full-parameter Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) with negligible tuning cost.

ATLAS: Learning to Recommend Across Unseen Domains

arXiv

Pervez Shaik, Prosenjit Biswas, Abhinav Thorat, Ravi Kolla, Niranjan Pedanekar

AI Summary

Recommender systems currently struggle with domain generalization, requiring retraining or adaptation to operate on unseen domains. The proposed method, ATLAS, is a multi-source domain generalization framework that learns a shared, domain-invariant user-item representation from multiple heterogeneous source domains for zero-shot recommendation. ATLAS leverages Gromov-Wasserstein alignment for user relations, an adversarial objective for item representations, and residual vector quantization to suppress domain-specific variation. Key results show ATLAS outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on most unseen domains, achieving an average relative HitRate gain of 24%, with performance improving significantly with increased source domain diversity.

arXiv

Junyi Luo, Xinting Jiang, Tai-Hao Wen, Ruichen Qi, Minxing Chu, Hongyi Wu, Gregory Kielian, Ben Laurie, Qirui Zhang, Quan Cheng, Dennis Sylvester, Mehdi Saligane

AI Summary

Low-bit LLM inference using MXFP4 suffers substantial accuracy loss due to fixed quantization formats that fail to capture heterogeneity across blocks and operands. This paper introduces AdaMX (Adaptive Microscaling), a heterogeneity-aware format and accelerator that adaptively selects precision-recovery schemes per block and representation per operand without increasing equivalent bit width. Implemented in a 22nm prototype, AdaMX removes 83% of MXFP4 accuracy loss on LLMs (3B-70B) and up to 96% of FP16 accuracy on multimodal models, with only about 1% additional system energy.

Quantization Effects on Biomedical LLM Reliability

arXiv

Anton Rasmussen, Hong Qin

AI Summary

The core problem is that LLM classification reliability and calibration, especially under quantization, are highly sensitive to rarely-treated experimental variables like prompt templates and probability scoring rules. This study evaluated three Mistral-7B variants (FP16, INT8, INT4) on biomedical sentence classification using four prompt templates and different log-likelihood scoring rules. Key findings show that the probability extraction protocol dominates apparent calibration, with scoring rule changes reversing model rankings, and prompt template choice leading to significant accuracy differences (7-24 percentage points). INT8 quantization generally had minor impacts (1-2 percentage points) on specialized models' accuracy/F1 but larger effects on the base model.

arXiv

AI Summary

UHP Detection: LVLMs have their Unique Hallucination Pattern in the Consistency Space

arXiv

Amir Mohammad Ezzati, Kiyan Rezaee, Bardiya Kariminia, Mohamad Amin Yousefi, Asal Mohammadjafari Mamaqani, Behrad Samimi, Mohammad Hossein Rohban

AI Summary

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are prone to hallucination, which existing black-box detection methods inadequately address by relying on a single consistency metric to characterize diverse uncertainty. The paper proposes Unique Hallucination Pattern (UHP) Detection, a black-box framework that models hallucination as a structured uncertainty pattern by intersecting perturbation modality (image/text) and logical polarity (statement/negation) to form four complementary consistency groups. Features derived from these groups train a classifier. UHP Detection consistently outperforms prior black-box and white-box baselines (up to +18.72% AUC-ROC, +20.07% AUC-PR), demonstrating that each consistency group contributes complementary information and that the learned pattern generalizes across datasets, reflecting a model-specific hallucination behavior.
