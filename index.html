Heralding probability optimization for nonclassical light generated by photon counting measurements on multimode Gaussian states

arXiv

Jaromír Fiurášek

AI Summary

How Fast Should a Model Commit to Supervision? Training Reasoning Models on the Tsallis Loss Continuum

arXiv

Chu-Cheng Lin, Eugene Ie

AI Summary

Reasoning models struggle with cold-start stalling when trained with output-level RL from verifiable rewards (RLVR) at low initial success probabilities. The authors propose a Tsallis $q$-logarithm loss family $J_Q$ that interpolates between RLVR ($q=0$) and log-marginal-likelihood ($q=1$) via a gradient amplification mechanism to accelerate cold-start escape. They develop two Monte Carlo estimators, Gradient-Amplified RL (GARL) and Posterior-Attenuated Fine-Tuning (PAFT), to make this intractable loss practical. Experiments on FinQA, HotPotQA, and MuSiQue show GARL ($q=0.75$) effectively mitigates cold-start stalling where GRPO fails, and PAFT ($q=0.75$) provides stable training and superior performance in warm-start settings, particularly on HotPotQA.

arXiv

Dominik Żurek, Kamil Faber, Marcin Pietron, Paweł Gajewski, Roberto Corizzo

AI Summary

Variational Neural Belief Parameterizations for Robust Dexterous Grasping under Multimodal Uncertainty

arXiv

Clinton Enwerem, Shreya Kalyanaraman, John S. Baras, Calin Belta

AI Summary

Robust dexterous grasping under multimodal uncertainty is difficult, as existing risk-sensitive methods (e.g., particle filters) are inefficient and make high-variance approximations for tail risk (CVaR). This paper proposes a variational inference formulation for grasp acquisition, representing beliefs with a differentiable Gaussian mixture model that enables pathwise gradients through a differentiable CVaR surrogate. The method significantly improves robust grasp success and reduces planning time tenfold in simulation, while achieving faster execution, higher grasp quality, and more accurate risk calibration on a physical robot compared to baselines.

Three Models of RLHF Annotation: Extension, Evidence, and Authority

arXiv

Steve Coyne

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the unexamined normative role of human judgments in preference-based alignment methods like RLHF. The paper proposes three conceptual models for this role: extension (annotators reflect designers' judgments), evidence (annotators provide factual information), and authority (annotators represent a broader population). Key results include demonstrating how these models implicitly operate in existing literature, identifying failure modes from conflating them, and recommending that RLHF pipelines decompose annotation into separable dimensions, tailoring each to the most appropriate model.

arXiv

Pengcheng Fang, Hongli Chen, Xiaohao Cai

AI Summary

The core problem addresses the unclear role of future-prediction branches in world action models, questioning if future information acts merely as a regularizer when removed at inference, and how current-only policies can fully leverage its benefits. The paper proposes Privileged Foresight Distillation (PFD), which formulates privileged foresight as an action-denoising residual correction and distills this residual from a training-time teacher (with future access) into a small adapter for a current-only student policy. Semi-Markov Reinforcement Learning for City-Scale EV Ride-Hailing with Feasibility-Guaranteed Actions

arXiv

An Nguyen, Hoang Nguyen, Phuong Le, Hung Pham, Cuong Do, Laurent El Ghaoui

AI Summary

This paper addresses the city-scale control of electric-vehicle (EV) ride-hailing fleets, managing dispatch, repositioning, and charging decisions under uncertain demand and travel times, while respecting strict charger and feeder limits. The proposed method formulates this as a semi-Markov decision process and uses a robust Soft Actor-Critic (PD-RSAC) agent, optimized against a Wasserstein-1 ambiguity set to handle uncertainty. Critically, to guarantee physically feasible actions, high-level policy intentions are projected through a time-limited rolling Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) at every decision step. Experiments on an NYC taxi data simulator show PD-RSAC achieved a net profit of \$1.22M, significantly outperforming baselines (\$.58M-\$.70M), while maintaining zero feeder-limit violations.
