MemoryWAM: Efficient World Action Modeling with Persistent Memory

arXiv

Sizhe Yang, Juncheng Mu, Tianming Wei, Chenhao Lu, Xiaofan Li, Linning Xu, Zhengrong Xue, Zhecheng Yuan, Dahua Lin, Jiangmiao Pang, Huazhe Xu

AI Summary

Existing World Action Models (WAMs) face a trade-off: efficient methods struggle with non-Markovian tasks due to bounded memory, while long-history methods incur high computational costs. MemoryWAM is proposed, using a hybrid memory design that combines recent frames, event-boundary anchor frames, and compact gist tokens, along with a tailored attention mechanism for efficient context retrieval. This approach enables MemoryWAM to outperform strong baselines on long-horizon, memory-dependent manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, while maintaining favorable computational efficiency.

From Efficiency to Leakage -- Privacy Backdoor in Federated Language Model Fine-Tuning

arXiv

Shanghao Shi, Chaoyu Zhang, Heng Jin, Yang Xiao, Yevgeniy Vorobeychik, William Yeoh, Ning Zhang, Y. Thomas Hou, Wenjing Lou

AI Summary

The core problem is that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) in federated learning (FL) for language models is vulnerable to a "privacy backdoor," allowing a malicious server to memorize client training data without degrading model utility. The proposed method, NeuroImprint, exploits this by corrupting PEFT adapters, assigning a dedicated, single-update memorization neuron to each training sample to prevent update collisions and mixing. This design allows for the analytical inversion of isolated per-sample updates to reconstruct text embeddings and token sequences. Key results show NeuroImprint reconstructs 59% to 79% of all fine-tuning samples with high semantic fidelity across multiple large language models and diverse datasets.

arXiv

The Token Is a Group Element: On Lie-Algebra Attention over Matrix Lie Groups

arXiv

Przemyslaw Musialski

AI Summary

Existing attention mechanisms struggle to process bare matrix Lie group elements as tokens, often relying on complex representation theory, learned kernels, or failing for non-compact affine groups, with vector-token methods breaking invariance. This paper introduces Lie-Algebra Attention, where tokens are group elements and attention scores are derived directly from the negative squared algebra norm of their relative poses, forming a closed-form, intrinsic proximity kernel. This method successfully applies to diverse matrix Lie groups, including challenging affine groups, achieving competitive or superior performance to learned kernels with 50-80x fewer parameters and maintaining invariance where baseline methods fail significantly.

arXiv

AI Summary

The paper addresses the limitation of KV-cache-only LLM serving for low-latency, small-batch, on-device physical-AI, which requires managing the complete execution state for frequent branching, resets, and re-entry. StylisticBias: A Few Human Visual Cues Drive Most Social Biases in MLLMs

ICML

Shaghayegh Kolli, Timo Cavelius, Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Samantha Dalal, Jana Diesner

AI Summary

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit social biases driven by visual cues, but which specific cues cause these biases remains unclear due to confounding identity with appearance. arXiv

Solène Debuysère, Nicolas Trouvé, Nathan Letheule, Elise Colin, Georgia Channing

AI Summary

Multimodal foundation models for synthetic aperture radar (SAR) are limited by the lack of large-scale, very-high-resolution (VHR), complex-valued SAR-optical-text datasets. The SARLO-80 dataset addresses this by standardizing VHR complex SAR data to an 80cm slant-range grid, aligning high-resolution optical imagery to this native SAR geometry, and generating multi-variant natural language captions. The dataset, including fixed splits and code, is publicly available to enable reproducible multimodal SAR research.

ACL

AI Summary

ARC: Adaptive Robust Joint State and Covariance Estimation

arXiv

Alexandre Hadji-Thomas, Andrew Stirling, James R. Forbes

AI Summary

Classical state estimators are unreliable due to outliers and non-Gaussian noise, while existing robust or joint estimators fail to simultaneously estimate state and covariance accurately under these conditions. This paper proposes ARC, a self-tuning joint state and covariance estimator built on a unified Block-Coordinate Descent framework combining a norm-aware adaptive robust loss, an Iteratively Reweighted Least-Squares state update, and a Minimum Weighted Covariance Determinant covariance estimator. ARC consistently recovers the true inlier measurement covariance and matches or exceeds baseline state estimation accuracy in simulations and real-world experiments, notably without requiring any manual parameter tuning.

arXiv

AI Summary

LIT-GS proposes a LiDAR-inertial-thermal Gaussian Splatting framework that explicitly injects LiDAR-derived plane geometry as a constraint.
