UniClawBench: A Universal Benchmark for Proactive Agents on Real-World Tasks

arXiv

Zhekai Chen, Chengqi Duan, Kaiyue Sun, Bohao Li, Yuqing Wang, Manyuan Zhang, Xihui Liu

AI Summary

Existing benchmarks for proactive agents struggle with real-world dynamic environments, single-turn evaluations, and diagnosing failures due to mixed capabilities. To address this, UniClawBench is introduced as the first capability-driven benchmark, featuring 400 real-world tasks across five foundational capabilities, evaluated in live Docker containers with step-by-step checkpoints and a closed-loop multi-turn feedback strategy. Evaluations with state-of-the-art models and frameworks reveal that both base model capabilities and agent framework designs jointly determine performance in real-world scenarios.

Decoupling Limit of Quiver Theories and the Angular Spectra of Extreme C-metrics

arXiv

Peng Yang, Kilar Zhang

AI Summary

The paper investigates the analytical evaluation of the angular eigenvalue problem for the extreme charged C-metric, where the governing differential equation degenerates into a Confluent Extended Heun Equation. To address this, a decoupling limit is formulated within the dual four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=2$, $\mathrm{SU(2)}\times \mathrm{SU(2)}$ linear quiver gauge theory, deriving parameter dictionaries and renormalized Matone relations. Utilizing an instanton counting method based on regular boundary conditions, an algebraic quantization condition is established. This method yields angular eigenvalues that are consistent with numerical results.

ContactMimic: Humanoid Object Interaction via Contact Control

arXiv

Xinyao Li, Xialin He, Runpei Dong, Saurabh Gupta

AI Summary

Keypoint tracking alone is insufficient for robust object interaction, often failing to ensure meaningful physical contact despite correct robot poses. CONTACTMIMIC addresses this by learning to track explicit part-level binary contact commands alongside keypoint trajectories, utilizing contact-following rewards and a trajectory augmentation scheme to decouple contact behavior from geometry. Learning Adaptive Solvers for Distributed Factor Graph Optimization on Matrix Lie Groups

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing distributed geometric optimization solvers for robotics are often limited to rigid body pose graphs and require brittle hand-tuning for large-scale problems. The Illusion of Equivalency: Statistical Characterization of Quantization Effects in LLMs

arXiv

Baha Rababah, Cuneyt Gurcan Akcora, Carson K. Leung

AI Summary

Current quantization evaluation for LLMs relies on accuracy and perplexity, which fail to capture significant behavioral changes induced by quantization. This paper introduces "correctness agreement," a decision-level metric measuring the overlap in correct predictions between base and quantized models, and analyzes quantization as a structural operator on attention weights. Results show behavioral divergence under moderate quantization even when task performance is preserved, revealing non-linear breakpoints at low bit-widths and increased sensitivity in query and key projections.

Latent Memory Palace: Reasoning for Control as Autoregressive Variational Inference

arXiv

Chuning Zhu, Eva Xu, Jose Barreiros, Krishnan Srinivasan, Paarth Shah, Abhishek Gupta

AI Summary

Transferring adaptive reasoning from language models to continuous control policies is challenging due to language's lack of granularity for spatial understanding and precise motions. The authors propose Latent Memory Palace (LMP), framing reasoning as variational inference with an autoregressive latent distribution, optimized via a novel latent-space reinforcement learning technique. Additionally, the framework yields LMP-$\texttt{tok}$, a variable-length action tokenizer that significantly improves downstream autoregressive policies.

MPFlow: Learning Budgeted Max-Flow Optimization on the Lightning Network with Deep Graph Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Harrison Rush, Vincent Davis, Simone Antonelli, Vikash Singh, Jesse Shrader, Emanuele Rossi

AI Summary

The core problem is optimizing liquidity placement in the Bitcoin Lightning Network, specifically selecting budget-constrained channels to open to maximize routing capacity, measured by $s$-$t$ max-flow. The proposed method, MPFlow, is a deep graph reinforcement learning agent that uses a message-passing policy network with PPO and action masking, trained under a hub-exclusion curriculum for capacity-aware placement. MPFlow consistently outperforms strong heuristic baselines on the max-flow objective. It has been successfully deployed in production, making thousands of channel-open decisions and allocating 267.3 BTC across 30 managed nodes.

arXiv

Ethan Leung, Elias Lumer, Corey Feld, Austin Huber, Vamse Kumar Subbiah, Kevin Paul

AI Summary

The study addresses the trustworthiness and bias of LLM judges acting as reward models for evaluating citation quality (source relevance and factual support) in deep-research systems. Researchers benchmarked 8 diverse LLM judges against human gold labels on an adversarial long-form dataset of 1,248 citation rubric decisions. They found that cheaper models, like GPT-5-mini, are competitive for source relevance (F1 0.908), and judges are statistically indistinguishable on factual support. arXiv

Shilin Ou, Yifan Xu, Luyao Zhang

AI Summary

Evaluating trustworthy economic agents in decentralized energy markets is challenging, as autonomous agents can exploit invalid physical data and create market instability. This paper proposes SolarChain-Eval, a physics-constrained benchmark formulated as a Markov Decision Process, which assesses agent policies across multiple dimensions including market utility, physical safety, and auditability, featuring an LLM-based Planner/Auditor. EdgeRefine: Privacy-Utility Balance for Graphs via Jaccard Sampling under Edge Differential Privacy

arXiv

Wenxiu Ding, Muzhi Liu, Zheng Yan, Mingjun Wang, Yifan Zhao, Qiao Liu

AI Summary

The core problem is maintaining privacy-utility balance for Graph Neural Networks under edge-level differential privacy, where standard noise injection severely degrades graph utility. The proposed method, EdgeRefine, uses adaptive edge refinement: it estimates edge-existence probabilities via Jaccard similarity, ranks edges, and then separately samples true and false edges based on these probabilities and the privacy budget. Key results show EdgeRefine achieves accuracy comparable to noise-free baselines, significantly outperforming other privacy-preserving methods (e.g., 17.8-19.7% higher node classification accuracy), with only ~5% degradation in graph classification, while maintaining strong resilience against reconstruction attacks.

Secure Decentralized Federated Learning via Gossip and Virtual Voting

arXiv

Amirhossein Taherpour, Xiaodong Wang

AI Summary

Decentralized federated learning (DFL) using gossip often lacks provenance finality and resilience to Byzantine or lazy participants, while ledger-assisted methods reintroduce central coordination costs. This paper proposes gspDAG-FL, a secure DFL framework that achieves consensus and finality over unique model-origin tuples by deriving it from the same gossip history via DAG reconstruction and Hashgraph-style virtual voting among full nodes. Resilience is improved through combined payload, accepted-proof, and private semantic validation. Experiments demonstrate gspDAG-FL achieves learning quality comparable to validation-based ledger FL, reduces coordination bottlenecks, improves throughput, and maintains high invalid-origin detection under mixed adversarial participation.

Multi-Modal, Multi-Environment Machine Teaching for Robust Reward Learning

arXiv

Ali Larian, Qian Lin, Chang Zong Wu, Daniel S. Brown

AI Summary

Current Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods struggle to learn reward functions that generalize across diverse operational environments, often overfitting due to reliance on single-environment, demonstration-only feedback. It Takes Few to TANGO: A Quantized Distributed Model for Binaural Speech Enhancement

arXiv

Zahra Benslimane, Pierre Chouteau, Martyna Poreba, Fabrice Auzanneau, Michal Szczepanski, Fabian Chersi, Romain Serizel

AI Summary

Neural network-based multichannel speech enhancement systems are limited on resource-constrained devices by high computational and memory demands. This paper addresses this by investigating low-precision quantization (post-training and quantization-aware training) for TANGO, a hybrid binaural speech enhancement system, and simplifying it into MN-TANGO. The analysis shows that downstream spatial filtering compensates for quantization errors in mask estimates. Leveraging this robustness, the most compact MN-TANGO achieves significantly reduced model size (0.177 MB) and complexity (4.65 MMAC/s) with INT8 quantization, maintaining comparable performance.
