Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations

arXiv

Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov

AI Summary

Learning whole-body mobile manipulation policies from human demonstrations is challenging due to inherent embodiment differences. Pixel-UMI addresses this with Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR) to generate collision-aware robot trajectories, and Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM) to mitigate viewpoint mismatches. It further incorporates DAgger for refining contact-rich interactions, achieving 87.0% average task progress across five diverse and challenging whole-body manipulation tasks.

Pixel: A Co-Designed Full-Stack Robot System for Learning General-Purpose Mobile Manipulation

arXiv

Tony Tao, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Deepak Pathak

Pixel proposes a co-designed, full-stack system integrating low-cost hardware, diverse human/robot data collection interfaces, a unified whole-body controller, and a common policy learning pipeline, enhanced by a hierarchical learning stack for complex tasks. This system successfully demonstrates complex mobile manipulation behaviors requiring dexterity, active perception, navigation, and whole-body coordination.

EgoActionBench: Benchmarking Dense Action Captioning for Egocentric Human Videos

arXiv

Yujiao Shen, Jiahe Song, Haitian Li, Jingkang Yang, Ziwei Liu

AI Summary

Existing benchmarks for egocentric action understanding lack fine-grained action atomicity, temporal precision, and hand-action association. To address this, the authors introduce EgoActionBench, a new benchmark containing 41,132 human-refined annotations of temporally localized atomic actions linked to specific hands, alongside a novel comprehensive evaluation protocol. Benchmarking 20 baseline models reveals significant current limitations, though their fine-tuned EgoAction models achieve substantial performance gains (15.9 and 23.2 points on EgoActionScore), outperforming all open-source baselines and highlighting the gap in embodied intelligence.

Not All Reasoning Generalizes: A Case Study on Learning Generalizable Reasoning Strategies in RLVR

arXiv

Ziqi Pang, Yu-Xiong Wang

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning exhibits a subtle failure: models learn correct in-distribution strategies but revert to ineffective brute-force enumeration under out-of-distribution (OOD) shifts, causing sharp accuracy drops. This paper proposes an indirect intervention: co-training with bijective inversion tasks. This approach surprisingly restores the correct reasoning strategy and substantially improves OOD accuracy, despite these tasks not being inherently harder or closer to the OOD evaluation. The findings suggest outcome-only RLVR can implicitly shape and credit effective reasoning processes for better generalization.

FADE: Factored Ability--Difficulty Filtering for Online RLVR Scheduling

arXiv

Tomas Duncun, Jonah Ellison, Mateo Ruiz, Hanae Fujita, Ingrid Solberg

AI Summary

Existing adaptive schedulers for RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) are inefficient because their single-metric pass-rate trackers conflate fast-changing policy competence with slow-changing prompt difficulty. The paper proposes FADE (Factored Ability--Difficulty Estimation), a bilinear state-space filter that disentangles these two timescales by modeling prompt success logits as an interaction between a K-dimensional skill loading, a fast-drifting policy ability vector, and a slow-shrinking prompt difficulty. FADE efficiently updates policy ability posteriors, enabling accurate utility estimation for informative rollouts. Evaluated across multiple benchmarks, models, and scheduling paradigms, FADE consistently improves downstream accuracy and calibration with negligible computational cost.

D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models

arXiv

Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li, Gavin Angeloff, Ankit Goyal, Jesse Zhang, Dieter Fox, Fabio Ramos, Anqi Li, Abhishek Gupta

AI Summary

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for zero-shot robotic task progress estimation suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions and failing to capture nuanced task completion. The proposed D&A (Describe & Assess) method decouples progress estimation into an objective textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment using explicit rubrics, compelling the VLM to define absolute success externally. This prompt-based approach mitigates confirmation bias, provides fine-grained partial progress metrics, accurately penalizes failures, and generalizes across tasks and embodiments, matching or surpassing post-trained reward models for efficient reinforcement learning.

Reviso: Not All Visual Evidence Deserves Equal Attention in Video Captioning

arXiv

Tengfei Liu, Yang Shi, Yuran Wang, Yuqi Tang, Yizhuo Jia, Zhuoran Zhang, Qixun Wang, Shujie Li, Xuanyu Zhu, Xinyu Liu, Minghui Guo, Shixuan Liu, Haotian Wang, Yuanxing Zhang, Pengfei Wan

AI Summary

Existing MLLMs for video captioning often overemphasize minor details while missing essential context due to treating all visual evidence equally. Reviso, an agentic framework, addresses this by learning *what* to observe and *how* to gather relevant visual evidence through a hierarchical Context--Focus--Points (CFP) observation plan, guiding goal-directed active perception and selective revisiting of video segments. This strategy is learned via a verification-guided pipeline and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with rewards for caption quality, plan alignment, and observation cost. Reviso outperforms open-source baselines on four video captioning benchmarks, producing captions that better support downstream video question answering.

DynaSolver: A Neural Operator for Autoregressive 4D Flow Prediction with Dynamic Boundaries

arXiv

Rui Qian, Shibo Dai, Mengjun Lin, Yuk-Kit Hou, Saining Zhang, Bohan Li, Junhao Chen, Hao Zhao, Chaojian Li

AI Summary

Neural operators typically fail at dynamic-boundary 4D Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) prediction for unsteady flows with continuously moving geometries. To address this, the authors propose DynaSolver, a neural operator that explicitly conditions on boundary evolution and causal flow history, employing an efficient physical-token formulation and Replay-Augmented Parallel Training. Evaluated on the new RoboCFD-4D benchmark, DynaSolver achieves state-of-the-art full-rollout accuracy, demonstrating significantly reduced autoregressive error accumulation compared to other learned CFD surrogates.

FedReady: Bounded Agents for Federation Formation

arXiv

Ziyue Xu, Holger R. Roth, Chester Chen, Peter Cnudde

AI Summary

Federated learning (FL) currently requires laborious, recurring manual federation formation—selecting eligible sites and preparing their data and task code, which is complicated by privacy constraints. This paper introduces FedReady, a system that automates this process using bounded LLM agents to propose shared contracts, local adapters, and task code, with a runtime verifying artifacts after site discovery and local eligibility checks. Across 39 simulated retinal sites, FedReady achieved perfect recall in site discovery and eliminated all false positives in eligibility verification. It successfully prepared 45,575 task-record instances for four 100-round FL jobs and showed negligible performance difference (max survival-curve difference 0.001133) compared to manual preparation in a patient cohort analysis, establishing the feasibility of automated FL data preparation.

Seeing Through the Illusion: Test-Time Scaling for World Action Models

arXiv

Cheikh Ahmed, Mahdi Mostajabdaveh, Milad Yazdani, Zirui Zhou

AI Summary

World action models (WAMs) often produce actions and imagined futures that lack consistency, meaning the generated action might not lead to its predicted outcome. To mitigate this, WISE (World Action Inference Scaling through Evaluation) is proposed as a pre-execution selector that scores multiple sampled action-future rollouts based on action validity, inverse-dynamics consistency, and instruction-conditioned task progress, executing the highest-scoring candidate. WISE significantly boosts closed-loop success rates on RoboLab (6.0% to 24.0%) and LIBERO (75.1% to 78.3%), outperforming prior selectors, with task progress emerging as the most crucial scoring factor.

PlantVLM-Edge: Quantized Vision-Language Models for On-Device Crop Health Diagnosis

arXiv

Gaurab Wagle, Bishal Adhikari, Jiajia Li, Xiaolei Huang, Dong Chen

AI Summary

Timely and accurate crop health diagnosis is challenged by the need for expert knowledge and the computational demands of deploying large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on resource-constrained edge devices. This paper introduces PlantVLM-Edge, a quantized VLM framework that integrates agricultural domain adaptation and post-training quantization to enable conversational crop health diagnosis directly on edge devices, removing cloud dependency. The framework achieves minimal quality loss with 8-bit quantization and retains 98.6% of full-precision quality with 4-bit activation-aware weight quantization (AWQ), reducing the 2B backbone weight memory by 49%. This enables compact models (2.01 GiB) to deliver rapid diagnostic responses (0.78s for 64 tokens), proving the practicality of on-device crop health assessment.
