Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots

arXiv

Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao, Zekai Li, Xingyang Li, Yuyang Zhao, Song Bian, Zhijian Liu, Song Han

AI Summary

Open-weight robot-use agents (RUAs) currently lack the capabilities of frontier proprietary models for general robot control. This paper proposes training an open-weight 27B reasoning model to natively use real robots via reinforcement learning (RURL) entirely in simulation, using a custom Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) to generate end-effector poses. The simulated training successfully generalizes to real-world robots, unseen tasks, and novel instructions, achieving 60% success on a real dual-arm robot and significantly outperforming demonstration-tuned models in task robustness, all while retaining general reasoning abilities.

Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Tony H. Zhang, Connor Mattson, Daniel S. Brown

AI Summary

Accelerating Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) with demonstrations is challenging due to collection difficulties and limited imitation policy quality. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), which extends single-agent IBRL to MARL using Round-Robin Behavior Cloning for initial imitation policies. During training, centralized critics select actions from an ensemble of RL and imitation policy proposals, with a gated penalty regularizing RL policies toward imitation where beneficial. IBMARL significantly outperforms demonstration-based baselines, surpassing imitation policies faster and more consistently across cooperative MARL benchmarks, even exceeding demonstrator performance and showing robust gains with real human demonstrations.

arXiv

AI Summary

ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL

arXiv

Zelin He, Siyu Zhu, Jacob Helwig, Yujian Liu, Li An, Rohit Jain, Shiyu Chang, Alborz Geramifard

AI Summary

Agentic reinforcement learning typically relies on a fixed, hand-written harness, which is suboptimal given that effective harnesses should dynamically evolve with the model and task. ModHarness-RL addresses this by recasting the harness as learnable programs—specifically, a Compression program for context assembly and a Steering program for guidance—that are learned from data and evolve during RL training. These programs are proposed from recent trajectories, verified, and replayed to adapt to shifting model behavior. Key results show that this dynamic, learned harness approach improves held-out performance over fixed-harness baselines across diverse tasks like CodeSearch, ALFWorld, and BIRD SQL.

arXiv

Dhruv Dixit, Khushi Patel, Congcong Xu, Min Song

AI Summary

Small language models struggle with mathematical reasoning tasks that require decomposition, exact computation, and tool use. ARCHER introduces a two-stage framework: first, it compiles free-form rationales into executable, tool-grounded action traces for policy supervision, and second, it refines this policy via asymmetric hierarchical reinforcement learning with grouped trajectory rewards. When Will You Hand It Over? A Hidden Start Delay Makes Self-Play Handover Robust to Unfamiliar Partners

arXiv

Mohammad Khoshnazar, Mike Degany, Deyuan Qu, Qing Yang, Matyas Szanto

AI Summary

Self-play trained robot receivers fail to generalize effectively to unfamiliar partners in object handover, specifically because they learn a fixed rendezvous due to their trained partner immediately initiating the offer. The proposed solution is Hidden Start-Delay Randomisation (HSD), which randomly delays the learned partner's offer during self-play, unobserved by the receiver. This method dramatically improves handover success with unseen scripted partners from 31% to 80%, maintains robustness across diverse novel conditions, and reduces high-force contact.

The State Is Not the Bottleneck: A Paged-State Gated-DeltaNet Tile on a Tri-Mode Multicast Mesh, Bit-Exact from RTL to Silicon

arXiv

Cong Xu, Ravi Sankar

AI Summary

This paper investigates whether paging recurrent states to external memory creates a decoding bottleneck for linear-attention models on memory-constrained edge FPGAs. To test this, the authors implemented a bit-exact hardware tile on an Agilex 5 FPGA running a layer of a 369M-parameter hybrid Gated DeltaNet using a multicast mesh of tri-mode processing elements. Results show that state paging accounts for only 13% of layer execution time and 11.2% of memory traffic, whereas weight matrix-vector multiplications consume 62% of the time, demonstrating that weight delivery—not state paging—is the primary decoding bottleneck.

arXiv

Tao Hu, Ling-Xiang Zhang, Da-Wei Zhou

AI Summary

Multimodal continual instruction tuning with MoE models suffers from severe cross-task redundancy and linear parameter growth due to separate experts for each task, along with catastrophic forgetting. CoPre-MoE addresses this by learning task-specific MoE and then employing a shared-and-private expert architecture. Topological Hybridization Gaps from Molecular Orbitals Coupled to Dirac Fermions

arXiv

Julián Castro, Jorge I. Facio, Verónica Vildosola

AI Summary

To control the topological Hall response of topological-insulator surfaces beyond conventional half-quantization, this work proposes hybridizing surface Dirac fermions with a flat band of chiral molecular orbitals. Spin-momentum locking drives the hybridization to wind around the band-crossing ring, opening a robust topological gap that shifts the surface Hall conductivity to $j e^2/h$, where $j$ is the orbital's total angular momentum. First-principles calculations of a nitric oxide monolayer on $\text{Bi}_2\text{Se}_3$ validate this approach, predicting a $\sim 20\text{ meV}$ hybridization gap and a quantized surface Hall conductivity of $\frac{3}{2}e^2/h$.

WeatherDreamer: Learning a World Model from Multimodal Earth Observations

arXiv

Kate Duffy, Elise Penn, Tristan Ballard, August Posch, Thomas J. Vandal

AI Summary

Machine learning weather prediction typically depends on separate, computationally expensive data assimilation (DA) systems or fails to recursively propagate atmospheric states across sparse observations. To resolve this, WeatherDreamer introduces an end-to-end world model trained directly on 18 multimodal observing systems without reanalysis, unifying hourly DA filtering and forecasting within a single learned latent-state cycle. Generating hourly global analyses in about 1.5 minutes on one GPU, WeatherDreamer outperforms NOAA's GEFS ensemble out to 12 hours for surface variables, provides better calibration than both GEFS and ECMWF, and infers unobserved quantities like precipitation with operational-grade skill.
