Splat2Act: From Captured Scenes to Personalized VLA Manipulation

ACL

Xiaomeng Ye, Krishna Jaganathan, Gyubeum Lim, Gunjan Chhablani, Muhammad Zubair Irshad, Zsolt Kira

AI Summary

Generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policies fail in new user environments without adaptation, and collecting real demonstrations is prohibitively expensive. Splat2Act proposes an automated real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that personalizes VLA policies by creating photorealistic digital twins of building-scale indoor environments from phone scans, automatically discovering receptacles, and generating diverse pickup demonstrations using a simulation oracle. Applied to 101 scanned environments, the pipeline yielded over 68,000 demonstrations, enabling a personalized VLA policy to achieve 62.5% aggregate pickup success in target scenes and 63.9% on a real robot. This demonstrates the potential of automated real-to-sim-to-real training for VLA policy personalization using only simulation-oracle data.

Noise Space Learning for VLA Adaptation

arXiv

Junjie Lu, Chuheng Zhang, Yucen Wang, Yuhua Jiang, Xinyao Qin, Min Xu, Li Zhao

AI Summary

Adapting pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models to specific tasks is challenging due to limited robot interaction, computation, and safety constraints. Noise Space Learning (NSL) addresses this by adapting flow-matching-based VLAs through learning a context-conditioned noise distribution while keeping the VLA fixed, integrating with various learning algorithms (PPO, SAC, AWR). Theoretically, NSL enables efficient adaptation, which is empirically validated across multiple benchmarks and real-robot manipulation tasks. The study develops effective NSL variants and provides practical recommendations for choosing them under different resource and deployment regimes.

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback

arXiv

Yucheng Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Shusheng Yang, Jiakang Chen, Serena Yeung-Levy

AI Summary

Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (MMDiTs) leverage image-to-text (I2T) feedback, where the evolving image updates internal text representations, but this feedback is found to preserve image errors rather than correcting them. To address this, the paper proposes Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG) and its in-place variant (iSFG), which suppress the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens it, or by directly suppressing it and strengthening text-to-image conditioning. These methods significantly improve generation alignment and preference-model scores across various image and video generation backbones, exemplified by SFG raising GenEval on FLUX from 0.6661 to 0.7479 and iSFG adding +1.33 HPSv2.1 on SD3.5M at zero extra NFE.

Chronos Policy: Learning Robot Policies on Physical Time

arXiv

Changhao Wang, Jaden Clark, Shuran Song

AI Summary

Robot policies often fail under variable observation rates, delays, or inference latency due to their implicit handling of physical time. Chronos Policy addresses this by explicitly conditioning policies on observation capture and desired action execution times, primarily through time-aware Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) within a diffusion transformer and temporal randomization during training. This method improves performance on dynamic and multisensory tasks where timing is critical, and enhances robustness to timing variations on quasi-static tasks across simulation and real-world experiments.

Goal-Oriented Bundle Management and Flow Control for Deep-Space Communications via Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Sajjad Baghaeea, Aimin Lib, Shanmugarajan Balasubramanian, Elif Uysal

AI Summary

Deep-space communications suffer from intermittent connectivity and limited relay storage, where conventional bundle schedulers fail to optimize mission-critical utility or ensure fairness across heterogeneous sources. This paper proposes a Reinforcement Learning framework, using masked Lagrangian Proximal Policy Optimization for a Constrained Semi-Markov Decision Process, to manage relay-side bundles and flow control. Simulations demonstrate improved age-based utility and inter-flow fairness compared to baseline schedulers.

Beyond the Snowflakes: Disentangling Weather Effects from Confounding Meta-Factors in LiDAR Object Detection

arXiv

Yiming Yang, Jeremy P. Bos

AI Summary

LiDAR object detection performance is known to degrade in adverse weather, but this paper found a counter-intuitive improvement in detection accuracy with increased snow intensity. Through granular evaluation and ablation studies, they attribute this to a dominant increase in object point count caused by snow accumulation on vehicle surfaces, which outweighs the negative impact of atmospheric noise. This implies that broad weather-severity evaluations can be misleading, and accurate assessment requires decoupling atmospheric noise from object-surface accumulation effects.

Tracking the Invisible: Self-Supervised Wheel Track Detection with Thermal-RGB Fusion

arXiv

Yiming Yang, Jeremy P. Bos

AI Summary

State-of-the-art drivable area detection is inadequate for snow-covered roads, where accurately following existing wheel tracks is crucial for safety and traction. This paper proposes a low-cost, self-supervised approach that uses synchronized RGB-I (Thermal-RGB) imagery to detect these critical wheel tracks. The method automatically generates training labels by projecting future wheel trajectories from GNSS data, which then trains a neural network designed for challenging, white-out conditions. Experimental results demonstrate its effectiveness in visually monotonous and featureless adverse environments, providing a robust and cost-effective solution for winter autonomy.

Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization via Zeroth-Order Residual Tracking

ACL

El Mahdi Chayti, Alireza Mirrokni, Martin Jaggi

AI Summary

The paper addresses optimization problems where only a partial gradient is directly accessible, requiring the remaining black-box component to be inferred from function evaluations in settings like performative prediction and bilevel optimization. It introduces Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization (PISO), which combines the known gradient with a running estimate of the residual, centering zeroth-order observations on this estimate to make probing variance depend on tracking error rather than residual magnitude. PISO achieves convergence guarantees for smooth nonconvex objectives dependent on residual variation, not magnitude, attains leading function-evaluation complexities of \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-6})\) and \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-5})\), and empirically outperforms baselines across several challenging settings.

HorizonNav: Geometry-Guided Video Diffusion for Anticipatory 3D Robot Navigation

arXiv

Hajira Amjad, Valerii Serpiva, Viacheslav Liubimov, Didar Seyidov, Dzmitry Tsetserukou

AI Summary

HorizonNav addresses the challenge of creating geometrically consistent 3D environment reconstructions from single RGB images and text prompts for anticipatory robot navigation, as standard video diffusion alone lacks such precision. The proposed method utilizes a video diffusion model to generate future observations, guided by a vision-language model, and crucially employs a geometry-guided depth alignment stage. This stage combines estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to enforce multi-view geometric consistency on dense depth predictions. Key results show increased planning-feasible scenes (13/19 to 15/19) and successful RRT trials (62/90 to 75/90), effective collision recovery for generated trajectories, and a mean reconstruction error of approximately 0.30m.

Mapless, Stabilizing, and Anti-Stuck Flipper Control for Articulated Tracked Robots

ACL

Valentýn Číhala, Pavel Sarga, Martin Pecka, Tomáš Svoboda, Karel Zimmermann

AI Summary

Existing flipper controllers rely on elevation maps that are susceptible to corruption from odometry drift, particularly for occluded terrain, causing robots to get stuck. This paper introduces Stabilizing Anti-Stuck (SAS), a mapless controller combining body roll/pitch stabilization with an anti-stuck mechanism. SAS utilizes IMU, flipper joint angles, and a non-accumulated motion cue, thereby avoiding odometry-integration drift. It achieves traversal quality comparable to state-of-the-art map-based controllers and uniquely navigates hidden obstacles that cause map-based systems to fail.

arXiv

AI Summary

Dream to Remember: Continual Steering of Generalist Robot Policies with World Models

arXiv

Bahey Tharwat, Iman Nematollahi, Marvin Alles, Xingyuan Zhang, Akshay L Chandra, Abhinav Valada

AI Summary

Generalist robot policies suffer from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially learning new tasks, whether via fine-tuning or steering modules. Dream to Remember (D2R) addresses this by continually steering a frozen generalist policy, using a frozen pretrained world model to rehearse prior tasks through imagined rollouts, thereby anchoring the steering module without a replay buffer. D2R significantly outperforms fine-tuning on CALVIN, raising average sequential task success from 63.7% to 74.8%, and effectively learns new tasks on LIBERO-90 and a real Franka robot while retaining prior skills, sometimes entirely in imagination.

arXiv

Moumita Mukherjee, Avijit Banerjee, Anton Koval, George Nikolakopoulo

AI Summary

LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO) suffers severe reliability degradation from airborne obscurants like smoke, unlike radar. This paper proposes a one-class temporal learning framework, using an LSTM autoencoder trained on nominal data and diverse degradation-sensitive features, to detect LiDAR degradation. SimVTA: Simulation-Grounded Video-Trajectory Alignment for Driving World Action Models

arXiv

Changhui Jing, Haochen Tian, Hongchen Li, Tianyu Li, Caojun Wang, Zihao Zhang, Yanhao Liu, Chengen Xie, Bailin Li, Hao Zhao, Zhenjie Yang

AI Summary

Driving world action models lack visual supervision for alternative trajectories during post-training, as logs only provide videos for recorded paths, limiting planning improvements. SimVTA addresses this by sampling candidate trajectories, using a real-world simulator and 3DGS to render future videos for high-reward candidates, and prioritizing supervision for higher-error futures among them. It then optimizes trajectory planning with GRPO and updates shared representations with a flow-matching loss on these generated videos. SimVTA improves PDMS from 86.2 to 91.3 on NAVSIM v1, achieves 88.2 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, and an average HD-Score of 38.8 on HUGSIM, demonstrating the efficacy of simulation-grounded visual supervision.

ReplaySSM: Cache SSM Inputs, Not State

arXiv

Ze-Wei Liou, Harris Nover, Amey Naik, Andrii Skliar, Igor Shovkun, Ido Shahaf, Tri Dao

AI Summary

Hybrid State-Space Models suffer from performance bottlenecks due to eager, per-step reading/writing of recurrent state, inefficient speculative decoding, and accumulating quantization errors. ReplaySSM addresses this by unrolling the recurrence, representing state as a periodic checkpoint and recent input buffer, separating output computation from checkpoint updates. This avoids frequent state writes, enables cheap speculative rollback, and allows distinct precision checkpoints for output vs. recurrent updates. Implemented in vLLM, ReplaySSM accelerates standard decoding by 1.20-1.47x and achieves ~1.8x speculative decoding throughput, while also mitigating accuracy losses from quantization errors.

MARGoS: A Configuration-Driven Platform for Reproducible MARL Experiments in ARGoS

arXiv

Tom Philipp Backert, Mohammad D. Soorati, Javad Ghofrani

AI Summary

MARL experiments in ARGoS-based swarm robotics suffer from limited reproducibility and reuse due to the manual and fragile management of diverse experiment configurations and parameters. To solve this, MARGoS is proposed as a lightweight, configuration-driven platform that elevates these experiment-defining inputs to first-class, platform-managed artifacts. MARGoS ensures reproducibility via configuration hashing, seed propagation, environment fingerprinting, and exportable experiment bundles. Evaluations confirm that MARGoS significantly improves the reuse, reproducibility, and hand-off of ARGoS+MARL experiments.
