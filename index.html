HANDOFF: Humanoid Agentic Task-Space Whole-Body Control via Distilled Complementary Teachers

arXiv

Lizhi Yang, Junheng Li, Nehar Poddar, Yiling Hou, Gio Huh, Robert Griffin, Georgia Gkioxari, Aaron Ames

AI Summary

The core problem is that existing humanoid whole-body controllers require dense kinematic references, making them difficult for task planners to synthesize from high-level semantics. HANDOFF proposes a compact, explicit task-space interface for whole-body control, distilled from three complementary specialist teachers (motion tracking, locomotion, and fall-recovery) into a mixture-of-experts student via KL distillation. On the Unitree G1, HANDOFF matches state-of-the-art velocity tracking, offers one of the largest robust manipulation workspaces, and enables natural-language-driven task execution without task-specific data or controller fine-tuning.

TempoVLA: Learning Speed-Controllable Vision-Language-Action Policies

arXiv

Dong Jing, Jingchen Nie, Tianqi Zhang, Jiaqi Liu, Huaxiu Yao, Zhiwu Lu, Mingyu Ding

AI Summary

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models operate at a single fixed speed, failing to dynamically adjust execution for varying task risks (e.g., fast transit, slow contact). TempoVLA proposes explicit speed control via two components: Variable-Speed Trajectory Augmentation (VSTA) to re-time demonstrations, and a model-side conditioning mechanism to feed the target speed to the policy. This enables flexible, bi-directional speed control in simulation and real-world tasks, with VSTA also boosting default performance. When integrated with a large multimodal model, TempoVLA further achieves dynamic, risk-aware speed adjustments.

arXiv

Chenming Zhu, Jingli Lin, Yilin Long, Peizhou Cao, Tai Wang, Jiangmiao Pang, Xihui Liu

AI Summary

Vision-Language Models often struggle with robust spatial reasoning, failing to infer unobserved layouts or maintain cross-view consistency from limited egocentric observations. The authors propose Astra, an agentic spatial reasoning framework that empowers VLMs with "action-conditioned visual imagination." RREDCoT: Segment-Level Reward Redistribution for Reasoning Models

arXiv

Mykyta Ielanskyi, Kajetan Schweighofer, Lukas Aichberger, Sepp Hochreiter

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning for reasoning models suffers from high variance due to delayed rewards at the end of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces, while traditional credit assignment methods like Monte Carlo sampling are computationally prohibitive. To address this, the authors propose RREDCoT (Reward REDistribution for Chain of Thoughts), a method that leverages the model itself to approximate segment-level reward redistribution without requiring additional generation. Results demonstrate that RREDCoT offers superior credit assignment compared to Monte Carlo sampling and alternative attribution methods while remaining computationally efficient.

Superconducting triode effect in a quantum-dot Josephson junction with a biased top gate

arXiv

Yu-Hang Li, Xiaan Du, Hua Jiang, X. C. Xie

AI Summary

The core problem is that conventional superconducting diode effects require breaking both time-reversal and parity symmetries. This paper proposes a superconducting triode effect using an asymmetric quantum-dot Josephson junction with a biased top gate, which breaks parity symmetry while preserving time-reversal symmetry. The key results show this setup yields a strong, continuously tunable non-reciprocal supercurrent, achieving ideal unidirectional current via the top gate. Furthermore, it exhibits highly asymmetric Shapiro steps under radio-frequency radiation, realizing fully quantized supercurrent rectification.

RiskFlow: Fast and Faithful Safety-Critical Traffic Scenario Generation

arXiv

Qi Lan, Yining Tang, Yu Shen, Yi Zhou, Yuhao Wei, Jie Li, Guofa Li

AI Summary

Existing diffusion-based methods for safety-critical traffic scenario generation are computationally expensive and produce unrealistic motion artifacts due to iterative denoising and accumulated errors. RiskFlow proposes a novel closed-loop framework that formulates future trajectory generation as transport in the action space, learning an average velocity field to transform Gaussian action sequences into commands in a single forward pass with output-space guidance. Experiments show RiskFlow achieves a strong adversariality-realism trade-off, consistently improving realism while maintaining competitive safety-critical generation capabilities, and substantially reducing inference time compared to baselines.

Double Preconditioning (DoPr): Optimization for Test-Time Performance, not Validation Loss

arXiv

Thomas T. Zhang, Alok Shah, Yifei Zhang, Vincent Zhang, Nikolai Matni, Max Simchowitz

AI Summary

Deep learning models deployed through self-rolling predictions suffer from test-time feedback (TTF), where error accumulates, leading to a mismatch between training loss and downstream performance. This paper proposes Double Preconditioning (DoPr), a novel optimization paradigm that combines gradient-wise and activation-wise preconditioning to mitigate this error accumulation. DoPr demonstrably increases downstream model performance across various TTF settings, notably achieving these gains even without consistent improvements in validation loss.

Smooth horizons from topology change in canonical quantum gravity

arXiv

Venkatesa Chandrasekaran

AI Summary

This paper resolves the firewall paradox in JT gravity by incorporating topology change into canonical quantum gravity and introducing boost edge modes, where the firewall is realized as a one-sided boost or relative phase between Hawking partners. At Page time, topology change generates two interior branches: one carrying the firewall and one being a zero mode of the boost generator. Gravitational constraints then eliminate the firewall branch. On the remaining branch, the horizon vacuum measurement and early radiation purity measurement are identified as the same Dirac observable, ensuring a smooth horizon.

Quantum Geometry from Area Fluctuations

arXiv

Jerzy Kowalski-Glikman, Ludovic Varrin

AI Summary

The paper seeks statistical evidence for discrete quanta of geometry by constructing an analogue to Einstein's black-body energy fluctuation argument within causal-diamond geometry. It quantizes the Poisson algebra of averaged fields over a stretched horizon and computes fluctuations of the averaged area density in a thermal state. In the null limit, the resulting boundary area operator's thermal fluctuation formula contains a linear term characteristic of independent microscopic constituents. This linear term is interpreted as a statistical signature of discrete geometry quanta, providing bottom-up evidence for quantum area degrees of freedom.

Waypoints Matter: A Systematic Study for Sampling-Based Trajectory Planning

arXiv

Josep M. Barbera, Antonio Artuñedo, Jorge Villagra

AI Summary

This study addresses the unexplored impact of waypoint placement on the performance and reliability of sampling-based trajectory planners in autonomous driving. The authors systematically evaluate three waypoint placement strategies—uniform spacing, an augmented Ramer-Douglas-Peucker ($RDP^*$) variant, and a novel curvature-conditioned allocation—across various road complexities. The results reveal that nominal inter-waypoint spacing is the primary driver of planner reliability, with well-tuned uniform sampling matching or outperforming complex geometry-aware strategies except on highly curved roads where the curvature variant offers a minor advantage.
