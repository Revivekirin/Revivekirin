AutoScaffold: AI-Guided Scaffolding for Reinforcement Learning of Language Models

arXiv

Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu, Jiangweizhi Peng, Shiyang Li, Sujay Bhatt, Sihan Zeng, Mingyi Hong

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) often underutilizes rich training experience, and existing scaffolding methods are human-designed and domain-specific. AutoScaffold is an AI-for-AI framework where a frozen teacher AI adaptively generates two types of scaffolds—shared strategies and individual hints—by analyzing student training experience and diagnosing bottlenecks, which are then removed before evaluation. This framework significantly outperforms RL baselines across diverse domains, including ALFWorld (+7.2% success), search-based QA (+4.0% exact match), and GPU kernel generation (+5.0% correct faster kernels). On mathematical reasoning, it achieves comparable accuracy to QuestA using one-third the training steps.

Emergent Biological Capabilities in a Foundation Model for Molecular Interactions

NeurIPS

Adi Mashiach

AI Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of developing foundation models for multimodal biology that exhibit emergent capabilities without structural data or task-specific supervision. It proposes Bonbon, a foundation model trained solely on 3.2 trillion tokens of paired protein-ligand sequences. Bonbon demonstrates three zero-shot emergent capabilities—functional mechanism, binding site localization, and bond type discrimination—which generalize to out-of-distribution complexes and emerge sharply at larger model scales. Furthermore, it achieves state-of-the-art binding/affinity prediction orders of magnitude faster than existing methods and shows a 61.5% hit rate in prospective wet-lab validation, yielding novel inhibitors for disease targets.

Is Your Robot's Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies

arXiv

Chubin Zhang, Bo An

AI Summary

Memory-augmented VLA policies that sample memory by time suffer from "phantom progress," mistaking null events for task completion due to a "temporal shortcut" that equates elapsed time with progress. This leads to a drastic reduction in task success, as the robot stops early. The failure is rooted in how memory is indexed, where frames of null events still occupy time-sampled slots. While training with injected pauses fails, indexing memory by task-relevant events rather than clock ticks effectively recovers significant performance without retraining.

A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets

arXiv

Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dylan J. Foster

AI Summary

Policy optimization in language model post-training suffers from "coverage collapse," where algorithms like REINFORCE can drive optimal response probabilities to near zero, stalling convergence. The paper proposes repeatedly resetting the KL reference to the most recent policy, formalized within Approximate Policy Mirror Descent (APMD), to mitigate this issue. This method provides the first global convergence guarantees for stylized PPO/GRPO and squared-loss algorithms, demonstrating they are "coverage-preserving" and can learn near-optimal policies efficiently.

OnlyAct: Extracting Action Representations from Foundation Models for World Models

arXiv

Runjia Li, Minghao Chen, Junyu Xie, Philip Torr, Andrea Vedaldi, Tomas Jakab

AI Summary

Video world models are limited by scarce and fragmented action supervision, and existing latent action learning methods face generalization issues and scene leakage. This paper proposes OnlyAct, which extracts action representations by adapting pretrained foundation model embeddings (V-JEPA 2) using a label-free contrastive framework. This method pulls together frame pairs with identical motion in different scenes while pushing apart pairs with different motion in the same scene, avoiding reconstruction bottlenecks. OnlyAct yields substantially more expressive and scene-invariant action representations, enabling world models to transfer actions to novel scenes more faithfully across diverse benchmarks.

Precision on Demand: Escalating Weight Bits at Uncertain Decoding Steps in a Small Local Model

arXiv

Mohammed Nayeem Khan, Logesh L, Aliyas Ahmad Misri

AI Summary

Local decoding typically fixes weight precision, leading to potential inefficiency; this paper proposes dynamic precision allocation by storing 8-bit weights but initially decoding with 4-bit, escalating to 8-bit only when low-precision output indicates uncertainty (entropy/margin). Simulations show this approach accurately predicts `argmax` flips (AUC 0.93), catching 78-94% of flips with a 15% escalation budget. In live problems, a 16% escalation rate achieved 62.7% accuracy at 0.87x all-high weight bytes, though the statistical significance of this performance relative to fixed-precision baselines was not definitively established.

Bits or permutations? Spending a fixed inference budget on weight precision or on option-order averaging in small local language models

arXiv

Mohammed Nayeem Khan, Logesh L, Aliyas Ahmad Misri

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is how to optimally allocate a fixed inference budget between weight precision (quantization) and answer-option reordering (averaging) for small local language models. Researchers simulated various weight precisions (3-8 bits vs. fp16) and multiple answer-option reorderings on Qwen3 models, evaluating their impact on accuracy and "answer-slot skew." Key results indicate 6-bit weights retain accuracy, while 4-bit weights significantly increase skew, but averaging four option orders substantially recovers performance. The optimal strategy is to use 6-bit weights and allocate any remaining budget towards averaging up to four option reorderings.

Uncertainty-Aware Hybrid Offline Reinforcement Learning with Online Action Selection Enabled by Sparse Gaussian-Process Critics

arXiv

Marco Chan, Christos Evripidou, Liqiu Dong, Benoit Chachuat, Mehmet Mercangöz

AI Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of operator-driven industrial process control by examining the Hybrid Offline Learning and Online Optimisation (HOFLON) method, specifically its adversarial-autoencoder (AAE) penalty for unfamiliar states. It proposes replacing HOFLON's tree-ensemble critic and AAE with an uncertainty-aware sparse variational Gaussian-process (sGP) critic, which selects actions by maximizing an uncertainty-adjusted return ($μ - κσ$). Experimental results demonstrate that removing the AAE or setting $κ=0$ led to significant failures, whereas the sGP critic with $κ=3$ prevented all failures and achieved superior median and lowest returns compared to the original HOFLON on a polymerisation reactor benchmark.

arXiv

Guoqiang Liang, Yiqi Lin, Yanzhe Chen, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Mike Zheng Shou

AI Summary

Integrating low-dimensional robot signals into pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) for World Action Models (WAMs) is inefficient due to conventional spatial encodings misaligning with the VDM's prior. The paper proposes MetaWAM, which unifies all robot signals into a single "meta-frame" by packing each signal into disjoint frequency-domain channel bands, then lifting them via an inverse DCT, thereby aligning the latent interface with the VDM's prior. From Prediction to Action: Learning Physical Context for Deformable Linear Object Manipulation

arXiv

Chengxiao Dong, Alessio Caporali, Hongyu Lan, Gianluca Palli

AI Summary

This paper addresses the gap between predictive accuracy and decision utility in deformable linear object (DLO) manipulation by introducing Geometry-Pointer-Flow (GPF), a policy architecture that decouples grasp-location selection from motion generation. The base geometry-only GPF cuts executed shape error by 32.8% compared to a DLO diffusion baseline while using 18.7 times fewer parameters and achieving nearly 193 times faster inference. Crucially, while estimated physical parameters improve forward state prediction, learned interaction history uniquely optimizes closed-loop control—reducing executed error by 9.9% and enabling the strongest zero-shot hardware transfer—demonstrating that physical representations must be evaluated by the actions they enable rather than predictive fidelity alone.
