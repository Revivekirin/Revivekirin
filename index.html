Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding

arXiv

Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang, Shengyi Qian, Shang-Wen Li, Mike Lewis, Marjan Ghazvininejad, Luke Zettlemoyer, Lucas Beyer, Mengye Ren, Jakob Verbeek, Saining Xie, Hu Xu

AI Summary

The optimal design for visual encoding in multimodal language models, concerning visual priors, dedicated capacity, and placement, is debated due to confounding factors in current models. This study disentangled these choices by pretraining all models from scratch under controlled conditions and evaluating them on VQA and language benchmarks. Key results show a separate visual prior is unnecessary, but dedicated vision capacity is crucial for visual understanding. Furthermore, this capacity can be effectively placed *within* early backbone layers rather than exclusively pre-fusion, potentially lowering latency.

ProtoVis-Nav: Prototypical Visual Imagination for Visual-Spatial Aligned UAV Navigation

arXiv

Guangyuan Sui, Yunzhong Hou, Chi Harold Liu

AI Summary

Vision language navigation (VLN) for UAVs is challenged by small 3D motion prediction errors and existing methods' inability to imagine execution outcomes for accurate waypoint prediction. ProtoVis-Nav addresses this by forcing the model to imagine an outcome-associated local visual representation for the next UAV movement. It achieves this by predicting feature prototypes and combining them to estimate the visual feature of the target region, using this imagined context to generate actions. Extensive experiments demonstrate substantial improvements over existing baselines.

Internalizing Investment Mandates: A Neurosymbolic RL Approach to Portfolio Allocation

arXiv

Zeynep Yıldız, Satchit Chatterji, Ercan Atam, Erman Acar

AI Summary

Deep reinforcement learning typically fails to enforce complex, continuous-action investment mandates in portfolio allocation, while existing neurosymbolic methods are limited to discrete action spaces. This paper proposes a PLPG-inspired continuous neurosymbolic RL approach that estimates and integrates the probability of an action satisfying the mandate directly into the training objective using reparameterized Monte Carlo sampling, enabling the agent to learn compliance internally. The method significantly reduces mandate violations from 96-98% (unconstrained RL) and 33.4% (baseline) to 4.8% on a six-rule, regime-conditional mandate, demonstrating effective internalization of symbolic rules by a continuous-action policy.

Learning When Diffusion Language Models Should Regenerate

arXiv

Minhae Oh, Jeonghye Kim, Jinwoo Koo, Yeongoon Kim, Jimin Seo, Minsik Seol, Jungwoo Lee

AI Summary

Diffusion language models lack effective mechanisms to know when and what to revise during remasking, often failing to correct errors without comprehensive contextual revision. To address this, the authors propose DiVR, a verifier trained on the generator's own trajectory correctness, which predicts if a partial sequence will lead to a correct answer. DiVR then decides when to regenerate a block and which alternative to retain. This method improved a LLaDA-8B-Base generator's average accuracy from 35.3% to 43.4% on five reasoning benchmarks at lower computational cost than search baselines, while also generalizing to other generators.

Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards

NeurIPS

Jiacheng Xu, Shuo He, Fuxiang Zhang, Chaojie Wang, Bo An

AI Summary

Standard Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) fails when only negative examples are reliably verifiable ("one-sided verifiability"), leading to false positives and unreliable policy updates. To address this, they propose Negative-Only Policy Optimization (NOPO), which treats verified positives as unlabeled and updates the policy solely by suppressing trusted negative examples using a reference-relative suppression loss and a group-level adapter. NOPO consistently improves pass@k over baselines on code, logic, and math benchmarks across various verifiers, demonstrating effectiveness and robustness under inference-time scaling and transfer.

Global Fit Inference for LISA with samsara++

arXiv

Lorenzo Valbusa Dall'Armi, Gabriele Astorino, Riccardo Buscicchio, Angelo Ricciardone, Walter Del Pozzo

AI Summary

LISA observations require a Global Fit (GF) analysis to disentangle an unknown number of overlapping gravitational-wave sources, their parameters, and instrumental noise. This paper presents samsara++, a C++/CUDA continuous-time MCMC sampler, which addresses this trans-dimensional problem using birth-death processes for source count inference, mutation moves for parameter estimation, and efficiency optimizations like parallel tempering. Benchmarks on simulated LISA data with up to 16,435 binaries (131,480 parameters) demonstrate its computational feasibility for source reconstruction and catalog generation. A stress test involving 16,435 binaries completed 15 million iterations in 16 hours with 3GB RAM, establishing a foundation for comprehensive LISA global fit.

Structure-Preserving Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Dynamics on Arbitrary Time Scales

arXiv

Billur Kaymakçalan, Behiye İlayda Selçuk

AI Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of accurately modeling leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons on arbitrary, non-uniform Hilger time scales. It proposes a formulation using Hilger calculus, employing an inverse cylinder transformation to construct the delta-dynamic equation. This method exactly restricts the classical continuous-time LIF trajectory to the chosen time scale, preserving its passive membrane physics, unlike direct transcriptions. The approach yields a robust firing time recursion $t_{n+1}=Π_{\mathbb{T}}^{+}(t_n+T_c)$ after the first spike, independent of time scale periodicity or homogeneity. For discrete time, it provides an explicit lattice-quantized inter-spike interval law that converges to the continuous-time value.

The de Haas--van Alphen effect in a two-dimensional nematic Fermi fluid

arXiv

Luís E. Leite Macêdo, Daniel G. Barci

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the complex interplay between quantum oscillations and spontaneous nematic order in the Fermi surface of metals. Researchers used a uniform mean-field approximation in a two-dimensional interacting electron model subjected to a perpendicular magnetic field. Key results show that Landau quantization can both induce and suppress nematic order, creating alternating isotropic and nematic regions. Conversely, the evolving nematic order significantly modifies de Haas--van Alphen oscillations, with frequency changes at fixed chemical potential and preserved leading Onsager frequency (but altered cyclotron mass) at fixed particle density.

Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning

arXiv

Benjamin A. Christie, Tianyu Qiu, Amy H. Choi, David Fridovich-Keil, Dylan P. Losey

AI Summary

Imitation learning policies often fail to extrapolate robustly under state perturbations because existing regularizers penalize sensitivity at isolated states rather than enforcing consistent responses across neighboring states. To resolve this, the authors propose Inner Product Regularization (InneR), a plug-and-play method that aligns the null spaces of policy-induced state inner products across clusters of nearby states to ensure locally consistent reactions to perturbations. In evaluations, InneR significantly improves policy robustness against irrelevant dimensions and distribution shifts, notably boosting the average real-world robot manipulation success rate of a diffusion policy from 18% to 57% without extra training data.

arXiv

Kuang Shi, Qingqi Zhao, Teng Man, Sergio Andres Galindo-Torres, Yimin Lu

AI Summary

Reentrant Nematicity from Landau Quantization near a Pomeranchuk Instability

arXiv

Luís E. Leite Macêdo, Daniel G. Barci

AI Summary

This study investigates how Landau quantization can induce electronic nematic order in an otherwise isotropic two-dimensional Fermi liquid near a quadrupolar Pomeranchuk instability. Using a mean-field theoretical framework, the authors demonstrate that tuning the magnetic field drives reentrant transitions between alternating nematic and isotropic phases through continuous or first-order transitions. Furthermore, this field-induced nematicity produces distinct signatures in de Haas–van Alphen oscillations, characterized by substantial frequency shifts at fixed chemical potential and modulated cyclotron masses and harmonic amplitudes at fixed particle density.
