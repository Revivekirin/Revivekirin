InSight: Self-Guided Skill Acquisition via Steerable VLAs

arXiv

Maggie Wang, Lars Osterberg, Stephen Tian, Ola Shorinwa, Jiajun Wu, Mac Schwager

AI Summary

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are limited by the skills in their training data, preventing autonomous acquisition of novel manipulation capabilities. InSight proposes a framework that enables VLA primitive-action steerability through an automated demonstration segmentation pipeline and a VLM-guided data flywheel. This system autonomously identifies, attempts, labels, and integrates missing primitives into the VLA training set. InSight successfully learns novel manipulation skills in simulation and real-world without human demonstrations, demonstrating that primitive steerability is a practical foundation for continual skill acquisition and task composition.

FLUX3D: High-Fidelity 3D Gaussian Generation with Diffusion-Aligned Sparse Representation

arXiv

Haorui Ji, Weizhe Liu, Hongdong Li, Hengkai Guo

AI Summary

Current sparse voxel image-to-3DGS methods struggle to preserve high-frequency visual details due to issues in 2D feature selection and cross-modal alignment between dense 2D image tokens and sparse 3D voxel latents. FLUX3D proposes Diffusion-Aligned Structured Latents (DA-SLAT) for improved 3DGS reconstruction fidelity and a sparse-structure-aware diffusion framework integrating Sparse-structure Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (SMDiT) and Modal-Aware Rotary Positional Embedding (MARoPE) for geometry-agnostic 2D-3D alignment. This method substantially improves appearance fidelity and significantly outperforms all state-of-the-art methods in generating high-quality 3DGS assets.

MANGO: Automated Multi-Agent Test Oracle Generation for Vision-Language-Action Models

ACL

Pablo Valle, Shaukat Ali, Aitor Arrieta, Lionel Briand

AI Summary

Existing test oracles for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robotic models are manually constructed, environment-specific, and restricted to end-state assessments, which limits scalability and fault localization. To address this, the authors propose MANGO, a multi-agent framework (featuring Generator, Assessor, and Judge agents) that automatically generates fine-grained, simulator-grounded executable oracles from natural-language task descriptions. Evaluated on the LIBERO_10 and RoboCasa benchmarks, MANGO achieves failure detection performance comparable to manual symbolic oracles while successfully localizing intermediate faults and providing rich diagnostic feedback.

Vision-Language Model Reasoning for Contextual Semantic Mapping in Intralogistics

arXiv

Marvin Rüdt, Hao Pang, Constantin Enke, Zäzilia Seibold, Kai Furmans

AI Summary

Intralogistics robots currently lack semantic and contextual understanding of objects beyond basic geometric maps. This paper proposes a contextual semantic mapping pipeline integrating SLAM, SAM, instance clustering, and multi-view VLM reasoning to infer object class and contextual properties like movability in a zero-shot, open-vocabulary manner. The pipeline achieved 98.93% mIoU for semantic classification and 89.17% mAcc for object movability estimation. VLM reasoning was identified as the primary bottleneck for contextual understanding, while instance clustering limited panoptic performance.

Pocket-SLAM: Rendering-Area-Aware Pruning for Memory-Efficient 3DGS-SLAM

arXiv

Leshu Li, Jie Peng, Yang Zhao

AI Summary

3DGS-SLAM faces a critical limitation in large-scale scenes: continuously increasing memory consumption due to Gaussian point accumulation, hindering its applicability. Pocket-SLAM proposes a rendering-area-aware pruning strategy that selectively removes Gaussians based on their contribution to the effective rendering area, directly addressing memory redundancy. This method achieves over 60% memory reduction and more than 2 times FPS improvement on EuRoC and KITTI datasets, outperforming existing approaches while preserving localization and mapping accuracy.

Compact Object-Level Representations with Open-Vocabulary Understanding for Indoor Visual Relocalization

arXiv

Zhaopeng Cui, Jiarui Hu, Jingbo Liu, Boming Zhao, Xiyue Guo, Boyin Feng, Haocheng Peng, Yujun Shen, Hujun Bao, Guofeng Zhang

AI Summary

Prior indoor visual relocalization methods suffer from low-level vision schemes, lacking scene semantics and interpretability. This paper introduces OpenReLoc, a system that organizes rich object information and leverages foundation models for multi-modal, open-vocabulary 2D-3D object matching. It further employs object-oriented reference frames with a Distance-IoU (DIOU) selection strategy and a dual-path 2D Iterative Closest Pixel loss for robust pose estimation. OpenReLoc achieves superior relocalization recall and accuracy across various datasets.

UniDrive: A Unified Vision-Language and Grounding Framework for Interpretable Risk Understanding in Autonomous Driving

arXiv

Xiaowei Gao, Pengxiang Li, Yitai Cheng, Ruihan Xu, James Haworth, Stephen Law, Yun Ye

AI Summary

Existing autonomous driving scene understanding models struggle with a fundamental trade-off between temporal reasoning and spatial precision, leading to missed hazards or ungrounded explanations. UniDrive proposes a unified framework combining a multi-frame temporal reasoning branch with a high-resolution perception branch, fused via gated cross-attention, to jointly generate natural-language risk descriptions and grounded bounding boxes. This approach outperforms representative baselines in both captioning and risk-object grounding, demonstrating superior small-object localization, zero-shot generalization, and human-rated interpretability.

Material identification using laboratory X-ray beam tracking: quantitativeness and signal-to-noise ratio requirements

arXiv

Sumera Rehman, Ashkan Ajeer, Connor Darling, Marco Endrizzi, Alessandro Olivo, Silvia Cipiccia

AI Summary

The core problem is the need for non-destructive, simultaneous structural and elemental material identification in a standard laboratory setting, circumventing reliance on synchrotron facilities. The proposed method utilizes monochromatic X-ray beam tracking (XBT) with a laboratory X-ray source to simultaneously access quantitative absorption and phase properties for material discrimination, specifically investigating the impact of signal-to-noise ratio. Key results demonstrate the successful identification of Ag, Fe, and Cu test samples, confirming XBT's capability for elemental identification in a laboratory environment.
