LIBERO-PeRM: Benchmarking Personalized Robotic Manipulation

NeurIPS

Zhixu Li, Keqian Tang, Litian Gong, Jingyu Yao, Wenqian Zhang, Tianze Xu, Zehao Wang, Yuping Wang, Junge Zhang, Jiachen Li

AI Summary

Personalized robotic manipulation lacks a controlled benchmark to evaluate preference satisfaction independently from task completion. This paper introduces LIBERO-PeRM, a benchmark with a controlled formulation and taxonomy for language-expressible preferences (motion, skill, user-profile) in simulation, featuring five task suites and paired demonstrations. Experiments show current VLA policies struggle with zero-shot personalization, though SFT makes preferences learnable. While mixed training improves no-preference retention and contextual prompt performance, it can weaken motion-preference learning, and preference generalization or conflict handling remain difficult.

Value as Memory: Online Value-Guided Post-training for General Robot Policies

arXiv

Wenye Yu, Yijin Chen, Kaiyi Xiong, Wenbo Han, Wendi Chen, Yang Jin, Han Xue, Jun Lv, Cewu Lu, Chuan Wen

AI Summary

Generalist robot policy post-training faces challenges from partial observability and delayed feedback from offline methods. Value as Memory (VaM) addresses this through value caching, which conditions actions on recent value predictions as task-progress memory, and online real-world reinforcement learning that combines diverse data for timely, sample-efficient adaptation. VaM achieved a $29.4\%$ success-rate gain with value caching and a $32.8\%$ gain with online autonomous learning, performing $8\times$ more policy iterations with over $2\times$ faster gradient steps compared to offline training.

T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models

arXiv

Chenyuan Zhou, Yining Hong, Huang Huang, Yunfan Jiang, Wenlong Huang, David Held, Vitor Campagnolo Guizilini, Yue Wang, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Leonidas Guibas, Yejin Choi

AI Summary

Robotic world models struggle with accurate predictions due to unobservable environmental factors like friction or rules. T2-Z addresses this by augmenting a world model with a compact, learnable environment latent, which is optimized at test time using minimal interactions to adapt the model to novel environments. This approach significantly reduces object prediction error (15.20 to 7.00) and improves action success (33.73% to 72.96%) across diverse robotic tasks, while efficiently inferring structured environmental representations.

Perceive, Predict, and Plan: a Unified World Action Model for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation

arXiv

AI Summary

The paper introduces P$^{3}$-WAM, a unified world action model addressing the challenge of integrating 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning for egocentric dexterous manipulation. This model embeds a structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer via a mixture-of-transformers architecture, trained across Perceive, Predict, and Plan modes. P$^{3}$-WAM also generates goal-directed manipulation videos with stronger instruction adherence and action progression than video-generation baselines.

arXiv

Tianjiao Yu, Zhilin Ou, Yifan Shen, Xinzhuo Li, Yuanzhe Liu, Onkar Kishor Susladkar, Fan Wang, Ismini Lourentzou

AI Summary

The core problem is generating useful, rather than just physically valid, simulation environments and effective supervision to address robot policy failures. CoDA proposes a framework where a simulation data engine and an action policy co-evolve via a shared memory that compiles vision-language critic diagnoses into environment generation and training programs. Text2potential further converts these diagnoses into staged potentials for dense action-chunk value learning. This method significantly improved out-of-distribution success from 5.3% to 27.6%, exceeding baselines, and raised LIBERO-Pro success rates from 1% to 76% under instruction perturbations.

Grounding Generative Verification in 3D Content

ACL

Shuanglin Li, Jiaying Lin, Shuquan Ye

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the lack of reliable task-specific correctness and verification feedback for 3D generative AI, beyond visual fidelity, that can identify and explain unmet requirements grounded in 3D evidence. To tackle this, the authors introduce 3DVerifierBench, a 10-task benchmark, and develop a scalable pipeline for multi-view fact extraction and 3D editing to produce 14,126 training examples with contradiction explanations and counterfactuals. Key results show a 21.6 percentage-point performance gap between humans and the strongest model (GPT-6 Astra) on the benchmark, but demonstrate that oracle selection significantly improves factual success in 3D generation (26.7% to 90.0%) and human-guided recursive refinement achieves full requirement satisfaction.

OmniVerifier-C1: Decomposed Graded Verifier for Rule-Governed Generative World Modeling

NeurIPS

Bowei Liu, Xinchen Zhang, Xuhuan Li, Tianlin Zhang, Kaian Jiang, Jingwei Liu, Congyang Zhao, Yifei Qian, Junhong Liu, Zhiheng Li, Yujiu Yang, Yang Cai, Ling Yang

AI Summary

Conventional binary visual verifiers lack robustness and informativeness for complex multimodal scenarios with compositional requirements, only providing discrete True/False judgments. This paper introduces OmniVerifier-C1, a decomposed graded verifier that automatically breaks down complex rules into fine-grained components and assigns pointwise scores for a continuous critique, trained with a Decoupled RL strategy. The proposed method improves generalization and significantly outperforms binary verifiers, with a pairwise verification paradigm further demonstrating superior accuracy when ground-truth images are available. These accurate pairwise graded visual verifiers are critical for advancing generative models toward structured world-modeling agents.

MLExpert: Towards a Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering

arXiv

Shangheng Du, Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang, Yiming Wu, Junpeng Zhao, Shiyang Feng, Xin Li, Liang He, Xiangchao Yan, Bo Zhang

AI Summary

Autonomous machine learning engineering (MLE) systems struggle with long-horizon optimization due to costly experiments and challenges in extracting useful supervision from delayed feedback across extensive interaction trajectories. To address this, the authors propose MLExpert, a 35B Mixture-of-Experts agentic model trained with a post-training method that employs hierarchical agentic SFT and Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL). GeoState: Learning View-Decoupled Geometry States for 3D World Modeling

arXiv

Hongchang Liu, Jian Liu, Yujuan Ding, Liang Peng, Xun Jiang, Fei Ma, Xing Xu, Yi Bin

AI Summary

The core problem is that view-dependent features from geometry foundation models (GFMs) entangle scene dynamics with camera variation, making 3D world state evolution difficult to predict. GeoState proposes a view-decoupled geometry representation where a scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view GFM features into a scene state, which a geometry decoder queries via camera rays, allowing separate prediction of future scene states and camera motion. Key results show GeoState outperforms prior 3D world models on Waymo, supports geometrically consistent cross-view querying, and generalizes zero-shot to KITTI and nuScenes.

Spark-0: Scaling Spatially Aligned Tactile Pretraining for Robot Dexterous Manipulation

arXiv

AI Summary

Generalist robot policies struggle with contact-rich dexterous manipulation because key contact information like force and slip is hard to observe from images. Spark-0 is proposed as a spatially aligned vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) model, which uses tactile glove-derived contact maps for visual token supervision during pretraining and a dedicated tactile pathway for policy conditioning. Spark-0 achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, demonstrating that tactile supervision dramatically improves both vision-only and tactile-conditioned dexterous manipulation success.

Dexterity from Supervised Play

arXiv

Botao He, Yiming Dou, Yiannis Aloimonos, Jitendra Malik, Akash Sharma, Ruoshi Liu

AI Summary

Robots struggle to autonomously collect contact-rich experience for learning dexterous manipulation. The proposed DSP (Dexterity from Supervised Play) framework addresses this by learning a "play policy" from minutes of human demonstration, which then autonomously collects extensive interaction data to train a world model. This play policy subsequently guides model-predictive control (MPC), with an option to distill a closed-loop policy via self-play. In real-world experiments, DSP outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data.

Illumination-Aware Active Perception for Spacecraft Inspection

arXiv

Sagarika Rao Valluri, Benjamin Riviere

AI Summary

Orbital spacecraft inspection is compromised by varying illumination (e.g., self-shadowing, eclipse) that existing geometric visibility planners fail to account for, leading to insufficient image information. The proposed method replaces geometric visibility with a learned, continuous measure of camera information derived from a classifier variational autoencoder (VAE) that quantifies component illumination based on its latent variance. Evaluation in a photorealistic simulator demonstrated the approach achieved significantly better illumination (mean brightness 0.57, 81.6% observations > 0.25) compared to geometric planning alternatives (0.02-0.04 mean, 3.0-6.0% > 0.25).

KnowGPO: Knowledge-Grouped Policy Optimization for Search Agents

arXiv

Siqi Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan, Xingyu Wu, Qipeng Chen, Xin Zhang, Aiting Liu, Chao Deng, Jie Liu, Jin Ma, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yongliang Shen

AI Summary

Credit assignment in deep, multi-turn knowledge-intensive search agents is difficult due to long horizons, single terminal rewards, and unreliable existing process supervision in noisy environments. KnowGPO addresses this by an RL framework that aligns trajectories based on acquired task-relevant knowledge, decomposing task information into atomic conditions to define trajectory states, and using incremental knowledge gain as an environment-grounded process reward. It consistently outperforms outcome-only and process-supervision baselines by 3.8-4.2 points across multiple benchmarks (Xbench, BrowseComp-ZH, GAIA), with an 8B model rivaling or exceeding most 32B open-source systems.

arXiv

Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

arXiv

Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, Yang Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu, Fuli Feng, Chun Kai Ling, Tat-Seng Chua

AI Summary

LLM agents and multi-agent systems suffer from "goal drift," where execution deviates from the overall goal, leading to wasted resources and undesired outcomes. To address this, the paper proposes a steering agent that supervises execution, intervening on-the-fly with either a low-cost "fast path" or a high-cost "slow path." This steering intelligence is trained using reinforcement learning, combining a pairwise ranking objective with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO). The steering agent significantly improves performance and efficiency for both single and multi-agent systems, demonstrating strong generalization to out-of-domain tasks and unseen executors without retraining.
