Ask, Solve, Generate: Self-Evolving Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation via Self-Consistency Rewards

arXiv

Ritesh Thawkar, Shravan Venkatraman, Omkar Thawakar, Abdelrahman Shaker, Fahad Khan, Hisham Cholakkal, Salman Khan, Rao Muhammad Anwer

AI Summary

Unified large multimodal models (LMMs) still rely on curated supervision for visual understanding and image generation, hindering autonomous improvement with unlabeled data. This paper proposes "Ask, Solve, Generate" (ASG), a self-evolving framework where an LMM improves autonomously using a Proposer, Solver, and Generator that learn from *only self-derived consistency signals*. Key to this is Solver Token Entropy (STE) for stable learning and a multi-scale internal evaluation for generation, creating a solver-mediated coupling. The method consistently improves base models across eight understanding metrics, with BAGEL achieving a +3.5% gain on MMMU and improving GenEval image generation from 82% to 85%.

World Action Models Enable Continual Imitation Learning with Recurrent Generative Replays

arXiv

Manish Kumar Govind, Dominick Reilly, Smit Patel, Hieu Le, Srijan Das

AI Summary

Continual imitation learning faces catastrophic forgetting and the need to store past human demonstrations. This paper proposes Recurrent Generative Replay (REGEN), a framework that utilizes World Action Models (WAMs) to synthesize pseudo-replay trajectories conditioned on prior task instructions and current observations, circumventing the need to store original demonstrations. REGEN reduces catastrophic forgetting by up to 50% relative to sequential fine-tuning, closely approaching privileged experience replay. Identified limitations include long-horizon visual degradation and action-observation inconsistency in generated replays.

Paying More Attention to Visual Tokens in Self-Evolving Large Multimodal Models

arXiv

Shravan Venkatraman, Ritesh Thawkar, Omkar Thawakar, Rao Muhammad Anwer, Hisham Cholakkal, Salman Khan, Fahad Khan

AI Summary

Existing self-evolving large multimodal models suffer from "visual under-conditioning," where the decoder relies on language priors instead of visual content, leading to insufficient attention to visual tokens and poor performance on vision-language tasks. To address this, VISE (Visual Invariance Self-Evolution) proposes an unsupervised self-evolving framework that directly regularizes the model's visual conditioning policy using geometric and semantic invariance-based rewards. VISE achieves significant gains on image captioning (e.g., +16.85 CIDEr on COCO, +19.66 CIDEr on TextCaps), reduces object hallucination by 5.0 Chair-I points, and generalizes across model families, all without external supervision.

arXiv

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning for LLMs typically requires ground-truth solutions, limiting its application to complex optimization tasks without known answers. The proposed RiVER framework addresses this by training LLMs on score-based optimization tasks using continuous execution feedback and calibrated reward shaping that emphasizes top-ranked solutions while mitigating scale and frequency dominance. PhysiFormer: Learning to Simulate Mechanics in World Space

arXiv

Yiming Chen, Yushi Lan, Andrea Vedaldi

AI Summary

The core problem is to generate physically-plausible 3D object motion in a view-invariant manner, overcoming limitations of pixel-space models and ad-hoc latent spaces in neural physics. RouterVLA: Turning Smoke Tests into Supervision for Heterogeneous VLA Selection

arXiv

Xingyu Ren, Chugang Yi, Ge Ma, Youran Sun

AI Summary

Robot teams underutilize pre-deployment "smoke tests" for supervising policy selection among heterogeneous vision-language-action (VLA) policies. RouterVLA proposes an outcome-disjoint cross-fitting method where probe trials profile frozen experts, and a separate trial scores the selected expert without influencing its profile. This transparent probe-success rule significantly improved held-out success by +14.64 percentage points. The study found that commissioning itself provides the routing value, not complex learned scorers, and confirmed that outcome separation is crucial to prevent inflated performance gains.

Continual Robot Policy Learning via Variational Neural Dynamics

arXiv

Jiaxu Xing, Zhiyuan Zhu, Yunfan Ren, Ismail Geles, Yifan Zhai, Rudolf Reiter, Davide Scaramuzza

AI Summary

Robots deployed in the real world struggle to continually adapt to varying, hidden, and recurring dynamics with static, pre-trained controllers. This work proposes a continual learning framework that learns a condition-aware dynamics model, combining an analytical physics prior with a neural residual. A recurrent encoder infers the current hidden condition online, which then adapts the policy by recognition rather than re-fitting. This framework enables 5x faster recovery from recurring disturbances (approx. 1s) and reduces large-disturbance errors by 50-65% compared to state-of-the-art online adaptation on real quadrotors.

VibeAct: Vibration to Actions for Contact-Rich Reactive Robot Dexterity

arXiv

Yuemin Mao, Uksang Yoo, Jean Oh, Jonathan Francis, Jeffrey Ichnowski

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the difficulty of integrating high-bandwidth real-world vibrotactile data into sim-to-real learning for dexterous manipulation, as raw vibro-acoustic signals are hard to simulate faithfully. VibeAct proposes a framework that bridges real vibrotactile sensing and simulation by using a shared physical representation of contact and slip. A tactile estimator learns to predict contact and slip from real microphone waveforms, while manipulation policies are trained in simulation on the same contact/slip representation computed directly from simulated contacts, decoupling raw audio from policy learning. VibeAct consistently outperformed a proprioception baseline in simulation across five contact-rich tasks, with learned policies successfully transferring to a physical robot and improving success rates.

On the deformation theory of chiral quantizations

arXiv

Dylan Butson, Sujay Nair

AI Summary

The paper addresses the problem of deformation quantization for vertex Poisson algebras, a chiral analogue, by developing an operadic approach. It establishes an order-by-order deformation-obstruction theory for these chiral quantizations, controlled by their chiral Poisson cohomology. In the specific case of affine symplectic varieties, this theory is controlled by de Rham cohomology, and as an application, boundary Virasoro minimal models are proven to be rigid under deformations.

Hallucination in World Models is Predictable and Preventable

arXiv

Nicklas Hansen, Xiaolong Wang

AI Summary

World models frequently hallucinate, producing visually fluent but dynamically incorrect future rollouts, hypothesized to stem from low data coverage regions. The authors introduce MMBench2, train a large world model, identify three hallucination modes, and develop signals to predict failure. They propose coverage-aware training and curiosity-driven finetuning using these signals. This reveals hallucination is a data coverage issue, predictable, and mitigatable to adapt models to new environments with high data efficiency.
