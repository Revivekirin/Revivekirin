Agentic World Action Models

arXiv

Zhuoyang Liu, Dantong Niu, Botong Zhang, Boning Shao, Jing Wang, Ruijie Zheng, Yuvan Sharma, Runyu Lu, Jiaming Liu, Renrui Zhang, Roei Herzig, Yutong Bai, Ken Goldberg, Danfei Xu, Yuke Zhu, Guanzhi Wang, Linxi "Jim" Fan, Trevor Darrell

AI Summary

Existing world action models lack mechanisms for compositional skill reuse, while code-based agentic robotics typically lacks predictive grounding in physical dynamics. To resolve this, the authors introduce AW-0, a unified mixture-of-transformers framework that jointly couples code-based skill generation, future video prediction, and low-level motor control, supported by an automated trajectory-to-program curation pipeline. In both simulated benchmarks and physical bimanual manipulation tasks, AW-0 consistently outperforms VLA and WAM baselines in task execution, semantic reasoning, language following, and robustness.

Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models

arXiv

Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Yutong Bai, Nicolas Dufour, Patrick Pérez, David Picard

AI Summary

Interactive video world models often rely on sub-optimal autoencoders designed for offline text-to-video generation, which limits control over the spatio-temporal compression rates that govern latency and action-delay. To address this, the authors introduce CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion autoencoders that systematically explore diverse compression levels using modular bottlenecks and a few-step distilled diffusion decoder. CADRE establishes a superior latency-quality frontier across manipulation and navigation domains, revealing that high-compression diffusion decoders yield sharper reconstructions and that static cameras benefit most from temporal compression, whereas moving cameras favor spatial compression.

PhysiCalWorld: In-Context Physics Calibration for World Models

arXiv

Yunhao Luo, Wentao He, Yinpei Dai, Hyelin Nam, Hongze Fu, Joyce Chai, Nima Fazeli, Jeong Joon Park

AI Summary

Existing world models struggle to infer hidden physical properties (such as mass and friction) from limited visual history, leading to severe prediction drift in novel environments. To address this, **PhysiCalWorld** calibrates action-conditioned world models via in-context prompting, encoding a short exploratory interaction clip into a compact, reusable physics descriptor token without fine-tuning or explicit physics labels. Across four diverse environments, the method substantially improves rollout prediction accuracy and visual planning success while demonstrating robust out-of-distribution physical generalization with negligible computational overhead.

CW-WAM: A Structured Contact-Wrench World Action Model for Force-Sensitive Manipulation

arXiv

Houzhu Ding, Xinda Qi, Luqi Wang, Ming Yang

AI Summary

Force-sensitive manipulation struggles with the lack of explicit contact and load information from visual data. CW-WAM is a world action model that jointly predicts video, motion, and contact wrench, employing a unified interaction module with pair-causal attention and wrench memory to generate force references for an admittance controller. This method significantly improves insertion success rates (e.g., 25% to 70% for pegs, 40% to 95% for power adapters), enhances lifting-height accuracy, and reduces force-reference RMSE for tasks like peg insertion and wiping.

Environment Duels: A Microcosm of LLM Co-Evolution

arXiv

Shibo Jin, Zhiqiu Xu, Yiling Zou, Shangyu Gong, Mayur Naik

AI Summary

Frontier LLMs co-evolve by absorbing from predecessors and competitors, necessitating evaluation of their dual roles as creators and solvers. Environment Duels is proposed, a dynamic agentic benchmark where models author interactive environments with privileged hints, aiming for tasks that capable solvers pass with the hint but fail without. Key results show solving and authoring are distinct skills, with smaller models often being better authors and learners. Furthermore, pairwise interactions yield stable rankings, convergent environment designs, and a strong correlation between hint lift on authored environments and real-world training improvements on held-out benchmarks.

Multimodal Embodied Agents: A Unified Capability-centric Perspective from Computer-Use to Robot-Use

arXiv

Yanzhe Chen, Ziyi Yang, Jifeng Zhu, Qiming Huang, Ruihe An, Peiyao Xu, Hesen Yang, Runda Liu, Chang Gong, Zhijun Cao, Zechen Bai, Wenzheng Zeng, Yiqi Lin, Guoqiang Liang, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Kevin Qinghong Lin, Mike Zheng Shou

AI Summary

The paper addresses the core problem of understanding the fundamental shifts when multimodal agents transition from computer-use to physical robot-use, defining Multimodal Embodied Agents (MMEAs). It proposes PAPAV, a unified capability-centric framework comprising Perceive, Anticipate, Plan, Act, and Verify functions, to analyze and compare these systems. Key results indicate that five physical constraints introduce significant complexity for robot-use, and current benchmarks disproportionately evaluate "Act" while neglecting "Anticipate" and "Verify," highlighting PAPAV's utility for designing reliable physical agents and comprehensive capability assessment.

Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation Learns In-Context Exploration

arXiv

Sriyash Poddar, Yanda Bao, Jacob Krantz, Matthew Chang, Xavier Puig, Roozbeh Mottaghi, Natasha Jaques, Abhishek Gupta

AI Summary

Learning efficient exploration in sequential decision-making under partial observability is challenging due to reinforcement learning's sample inefficiency and offline imitation's inability to teach exploration. ASTEROID proposes an asymmetric on-policy distillation framework where a history-conditioned student collects contexts under partial observability, and a privileged expert provides action labels conditioned on those specific contexts. This method teaches the student to systematically resolve partial observability and explore efficiently at test-time. ASTEROID achieves ~2x higher returns with ~100x fewer interactions than RL baselines, outperforms frontier models across 11 environments, and significantly improves real-world robot manipulation success (+40%).

Machine Learning-Enabled Wireless Power Transfer: A Task-Oriented Review of Structural Optimization, State and Parameter Estimation, and Adaptive Control

arXiv

Denghao Luo, Yiheng Jiang, Jiaqi Zhao, Yuan Gao, Changsong Cai, Weiyi Huang, Yun Zhang

AI Summary

To address the challenge of maintaining efficient and safe wireless power transfer (WPT) under dynamic operational variations, this review establishes a task-oriented framework analyzing machine learning across structural optimization, state and parameter estimation, and adaptive control. It compares supervised, reinforcement, and hybrid physics-informed learning methods, finding that while supervised models excel at rapid surrogate modeling and reinforcement learning enables sequential control, hybrid approaches provide the highest data efficiency and physical consistency. Finally, it identifies key deployment bottlenecks—such as data limitations, weak out-of-distribution generalization, and embedded resource constraints—highlighting the need for physics-informed edge solutions and standardized benchmarks.

Differentially Private Sparse Reward Estimation with Preference Feedback

arXiv

Meng Ding, Mingxi Lei, Jie Zhang, Jinyan Liu, Di Wang

AI Summary

Existing differentially private RLHF methods suffer from estimation errors that scale poorly with large ambient model dimensions ($d$) due to relying on dense reward assumptions. To address this, the authors introduce a theoretical framework and efficient algorithms for private sparse reward estimation under both Sample-DP and Label-DP constraints, assuming rewards depend on a small subset of $s^* \ll d$ features. Theoretically, they establish near-optimal error bounds that depend on the sparsity level rather than the ambient dimension, while empirically demonstrating a superior privacy-utility trade-off over dense baselines in alignment tasks.

Test-Time Motion Steering for Perceptive Humanoid Fall Recovery

arXiv

Osher Azulay, Cheng-Lin Hsieh, Stella X. Yu

AI Summary

Existing humanoid fall recovery approaches lack the ability to steer the robot's motion toward specific task objectives—such as target headings, paths, or speeds—among multiple viable ways to stand up. To solve this, the authors introduce RISE, a framework combining a learned recovery action vocabulary with a visual diffusion planner that steers predicted future body states using task costs at inference time without retraining. Evaluated in simulation and on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, RISE successfully demonstrates diverse, terrain-adaptive recovery behaviors steered dynamically to meet user-specified task constraints.
