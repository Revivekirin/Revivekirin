Where Should I Join? Robot Group Joining via Language-Guided Goal Prediction

arXiv

Zilin Fang, Zishuo Wang, Gim Hee Lee, David Hsu

AI Summary

The core problem is enabling robots to autonomously join human groups by predicting a socially compliant joining pose, given a natural-language description of the target group. Their method grounds the language description by identifying group members via recursive spectral partitioning and a language-conditioned image-geometry model, then predicts joining poses using a goal predictor leveraging human-formation priors. Experiments show competitive grounding accuracy with sub-second inference, superior joining-pose prediction against baselines, and successful real-robot group joining in dynamic scenarios.

MultiVENT-Raw: A Benchmark for Retrieval and Reasoning over Raw Videos

arXiv

Reno Kriz, David Etter, Alexander Martin, Cameron Carpenter, Debashish Chakraborty, Hannah Recknor, Reihaneh Iranmanesh, Matthew Maciejewski, Kenton Murray, Eugene Yang, Benjamin Van Durme, Aaron Steven White, Andrew Yates, William Walden

AI Summary

Raw videos present a significant challenge for information retrieval and machine understanding due to their lack of structured metadata and contextual cues. To address this, the paper introduces **MultiVENT-Raw**, a multilingual benchmark comprising nearly 120,000 raw videos, event-centric queries, human relevance judgments, and key facts. This dataset supports both a video retrieval task and a video-to-report generation task. Benchmarking strong baselines reveals that both tasks remain challenging even for the latest multimodal models.

LiMA: Bridging Long-term Imagination to Real-time Dexterous Manipulation via Asynchronous Diffusion

arXiv

Ning Chen, Yankai Fu, Junkai Zhao, Qianpu Sun, Guocai Yao, Pengwei Wang, Zhongyuan Wang, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

Dexterous manipulation models face a core problem of temporal misalignment, failing to adapt long-term intent to rapid physical changes due to high inference latency in World-Action Models and insufficient fine-grained understanding in Vision-Language-Action models. LiMA proposes an asynchronous dual-system generative framework that systematically decouples slow, sparse long-horizon intent planning from fast, dense high-frequency motion refinement, aligning them via a Latent Schrödinger Bridge Coupling mechanism. This method achieves a 45.8% reduction in inference latency, an overall success rate of 70.8% across six bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, and maintains performance in unseen scenarios.

Watch, Recall, Act: Always-On Robots in Concurrent Embodied Streams

arXiv

Ding Yi, Peiwen Sun, Chenchu Rong, Jianan Wang, Xili Dai, Xiangyu Yue, Xi Lin

AI Summary

Always-on robots struggle with continuous, dynamic, and concurrent tasks in endlessly changing environments, as current action models are designed for fixed, single-step scenarios. The paper proposes ARMS (Always-on Robot in Multi-modal Streams), a policy augmenting a pretrained backbone with lightweight modules for live perception, embodied states, and past actions, which update context asynchronously to enable concurrent dual-arm operation. Trained on a novel self-labeled dataset, ARMS achieves 45% performance on a combined task, significantly outperforming baselines (28%), with ablations confirming the necessity of its memory, embodied-state, and asynchronous concurrency components.

arXiv

Frozen Flows Forget: Diagnosing and Restoring Lost Motion in a Latent-flow World Model

ACL

Xiwen Chen, Rigaudiere Z. Li, Zhiruo Zhou, Xiaojun Zhu, Houde Liu

AI Summary

Latent world models using frozen latent-flow spaces stably train but critically lose the ability to represent motion, resulting in static or teleport-like predictions due to anchor-sparse, latent-only supervision. The paper proposes Decode-augmented Rollout Training (DART), which repairs this by retraining only the flow with decode-path supervision while keeping the latent representation frozen. DART outperforms prior latent-only methods, successfully restoring the temporal structure of motion and re-coupling predictions to the scene. It significantly improves prediction quality, nearly halving the gap to an oracle reference, and reveals that pixel error alone can reward frozen predictions.

Tractable Reinforcement Learning for Full Class of Signal Temporal Logic Specifications Using Spatiotemporal Tube Reward

arXiv

Vaishnavi Jagabathula, P Sangeerth, Pushpak Jagtap

AI Summary

This paper addresses the problem of controlling robotic systems with unknown dynamics and strict actuator limits to satisfy complex Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications, overcoming limitations of existing methods regarding input constraints and state history memory. It proposes a novel time-aware Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that leverages Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) to map STL specifications into time-varying geometric boundaries. By augmenting the state space with time and training a time-aware Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent with a continuous, geometry-aware reward, the method learns history-free, computationally efficient policies. This approach robustly satisfies STL specifications while strictly adhering to system input constraints.

arXiv

Hantao Ye, Ross Worobel, Zhuoli Xie, Mingen Li, Houjian Yu, Youngjin Hong, Changhyun Choi

AI Summary

World models for robotic manipulation typically lack versatility across rigid, articulated, and deformable objects. PointCast is proposed, a mesh-free, topology-agnostic point-set world model that uses a diffusion transformer to predict future 3D point trajectories, conditioning on history and end-effector motion while maintaining individual point identity. It achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on rigid, cloth, rope, and multi-joint cabinet manipulation in both simulation and real-world datasets, outperforming baselines and enabling effective model-predictive control.

When and Where to Trust the Teacher: Unifying On-Policy Distillation and GRPO through Entropy-Calibrated Credit Assignment

arXiv

Jie Zhang, Jingxiao Yang, Zhehao Huang, Yuhang Liu, Xiaolin Huang

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning for mathematical reasoning struggles to effectively combine sparse but reliable verifier feedback with dense but potentially unreliable teacher guidance, as current hybrids integrate teacher signals late or imperfectly reweight credit. The paper proposes UECR-GRPO, which unifies verifier and teacher signals within a single GRPO-style update at both response and token levels. It uses Path-Utility Unification to combine rewards before group normalization and Entropy-Calibrated Redistribution to redistribute verifier-derived credit, attenuating uncertain teacher guidance via entropy. UECR-GRPO achieved average accuracies of 17.21% and 65.09% with Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B models respectively, exceeding the strongest baseline by 0.89 and 0.56 percentage points on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

arXiv

LEAP-CBF: A Safety Filter for Uncertain Systems with Least-Effort Adversarial Potentials

arXiv

Oswin So, Eric Yu, Chuchu Fan

AI Summary

Robust control barrier functions (CBFs) are often difficult to construct and overly conservative for uncertain, high-dimensional systems. This work proposes Least-Effort Adversarial Potentials (LEAP), a certificate that quantifies system robustness against disturbances by measuring the effort required to cause failure. LEAP, constructed using on-policy deep reinforcement learning, forms a robust safety filter for systems with bounded cumulative disturbance effort. The method was validated in simulation on multi-agent systems and through hardware experiments on quadrupeds and quadrotors, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

MicroQonv: Reshaping Convolution Tensors for Efficient Microscaling in Training and Inference

arXiv

Romain Facq, Sami Ben Ali, Olivier Sentieys

AI Summary

Applying microscaling quantization to convolutional layers naively incurs excessive memory overhead and data movement due to redundant quantizations and activation bloat from standard im2col transformations. To address this, the authors introduce MicroQonv, a method that quantizes tensors only once and quantizes activations prior to a modified channel-batch-first im2col operation during both forward and backward passes. MicroQonv reduces activation quantization costs by up to $\times9$, slashes memory movement and storage by up to $\times7.53$ with negligible accuracy loss, and improves 4-bit edge continual learning accuracy by +5.7% to +11%.

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing methods adapt pretrained generative Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for robot control primarily through future visual prediction, leaving the optimal mechanism for transferring generative priors to action learning unclear.
