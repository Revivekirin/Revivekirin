DexRoam: Learning Mobile Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Whole-Body Human Demonstrations

arXiv

Rui Zhou, Yibo Yuan, Junkai Zhao, Fangyuan Zhao, Xiaoguang Zhao, Shanghang Zhang, Sirui Han

AI Summary

Mobile bimanual dexterous manipulation faces a severe robot demonstration bottleneck, as prior human demonstration methods simplify motion and lose crucial fine-grained whole-body coordination. DexRoam presents a system for learning these tasks from egocentric human demonstrations, utilizing a tracker-free VR/stereo camera capture system and three explicit alignment stages to map whole-body human motion to the robot action space while preserving coupling. This approach consistently improves robot policy learning, boosting average success rates from ~30% to ~56% on various platforms and matching robot-only training with half the demonstrations.

Statistical Learning of Contractive Dynamical Representations for Composite Adaptive Control

arXiv

Min Kim, José Leonardo Brenes, Fred Hadaegh, Soon-Jo Chung

AI Summary

This paper tackles composite adaptive tracking control under dynamically coupled disturbances, aiming to learn predictive disturbance representations beyond fixed-decay methods. It proposes a representation-learning framework using a hard expectation-maximization (hard-EM) procedure with a Kalman smoother to identify uniformly contractive dynamical representations of disturbance. This approach learns a latent disturbance-excitation state that can be decoded into time-varying disturbances. The resulting composite adaptive controller provides predictive capabilities, provable exponential convergence, and experimentally achieves accurate disturbance prediction and improved tracking performance over ablations and baselines.

KV-streams for Efficient Compaction in Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Emiliano Penaloza, Dane Malenfant, Dheeraj Vattikonda, Roger Creus Castanyer, Siddarth Venkatraman, Abhay Puri, Jonathan Light, Matthew James Sargent, Augustine N. Mavor-Parker, Massimo Caccia, Lucas Caccia, Glen Berseth, Esmeralda S. Whitammer, Alessandro Sordoni, Minseon Kim, Marc-Alexandre Côté, Laurent Charlin, Guillaume Lajoie

AI Summary

The core problem is the GPU memory bottleneck for long context traces in agentic LLMs, made worse by inefficient compaction strategies that repeatedly prefill the context. To address this, the authors propose KV-streams, a plug-and-play method that streams the KV cache forward instead of flushing it after each compaction, significantly increasing throughput. This approach enables a 2.6-5x wall-clock speedup in training for various compaction strategies and allows the streamed KV cache to function as a recurrent state, with this behavior emerging from RL alone.

Shockingly Simple Self-retrospection Improves Agentic Models Without RL

arXiv

Jonathan Light, Christopher Zhang Cui, Jeonghye Kim, Roger Creus Castanyer, Emiliano Penaloza, Zhengyan Shi, Alessandro Sordoni, Marc-Alexandre Côté, Xingdi Yuan, Minseon Kim

AI Summary

The paper addresses how language model agents can improve their future actions by training *only* on self-generated explanations of their experiences, without reinforcement learning or external teachers. It proposes Retrospection-Only Fine-Tuning (ROFT), an online procedure where an agent performs a task, observes feedback, generates a retrospective explanation, and is fine-tuned solely on these explanation tokens. ROFT achieved competitive software-engineering solve rates (e.g., 49.2% on SWE-bench Verified) faster than existing methods, even learning to solve tasks where all base-model attempts initially failed. Behavioral analysis showed ROFT indirectly assigns credit, encouraging good actions and enabling more direct solutions, demonstrating that learning to explain can effectively improve an agent's ability to act.

Harness Learning Enables Generalizable Test-Time Adaptation

arXiv

Alvin Zhang, Xuecheng Liu, Zixuan Wang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Daniel Khashabi, Yuda Song, Andrea Zanette

AI Summary

The core problem is to enable generalizable test-time adaptation of a language model agent's "harness"—the executable program dictating its operations for specific tasks—using execution feedback. The proposed method, "harness learning," trains a "proposer model" with reinforcement learning to revise a solver's harness based on task performance feedback, framing this as meta-learning over executable programs. Key results demonstrate that this approach improves revision quality, allows adaptation to transfer to unseen tasks at test time, and enables multi-round harness improvements.

RoboCompiler: Graph-Native Compilation of Closed-Chain Robots for Consistent Modeling, Control, and Simulation

arXiv

Mehdi Heydari Shahna, Joongheon Kim, Jouni Mattila

AI Summary

Complex robots with kinematic loops and coupled actuators often face inconsistencies when their models for configuration, motion, and dynamics are separately reconstructed for control and simulation. RoboCompiler addresses this by providing a graph-native framework that compiles a canonical mechanism graph into a consistent, shared mechanical interface, constructing closure paths, analytic Jacobians, and applying tangent lifts for consistent dynamics. The framework was evaluated on several complex robots, demonstrating mechanical consistency through high-precision checks and enabling task performance and model reuse in simulations. For a Kangaroo robot, it significantly reduced residual-and-Jacobian evaluation time by 96.7% and closed-loop rollout wall time by 66.8%.

X-Reset: Scaling Object-Centric Reinforcement Learning via Cross-Embodiment Resets

arXiv

Prithwish Dan, Chenyang Ma, Wei Zhan

AI Summary

Dexterous manipulation RL faces a severe exploration problem when training generalist policies with task-agnostic rewards from scratch, making it difficult to discover complex behaviors. X-Reset addresses this by using human hand-object demonstrations, kinematically retargeting them to stable robot states, and sampling these as resets during object-centric RL training, bypassing direct imitation. This method successfully trains generalist policies across 20 diverse objects and three robot embodiments, scales with objects, generalizes to unseen items, tolerates imperfect data, and enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

Lagrangian--Hamiltonian Flows for Video Prediction and Image Generation: A Symplectic Perspective

arXiv

Jiawei Hu

AI Summary

This paper addresses the problem of learning image dynamics for deterministic video prediction and image generation. It proposes LHFM, a geometric framework that represents images as exact Lagrangian graphs and models their evolution via image-dependent Hamiltonian flows, parameterizing image velocities into transport-source fields. For video prediction, LHFM-V achieved the lowest FLOP count among comparable recurrent models with similar accuracy. For image generation, LHFM-I attained a lower FID score than a flow-matching baseline.

Humanoid Loco-Manipulation With Discrete VLA Model

arXiv

Wenxin Shao, Siqi Chai, Kun Li, Kerou Zhang, Xinzhou Jiang, Wei Xu, Qiang Liu

AI Summary

Previous discrete VLA models struggle with the high-dimensional, heterogeneous whole-body action space of humanoids, posing tokenization, training, and real-time inference challenges. Holo-M is proposed as the first discrete VLA model for humanoid loco-manipulation that intrinsically exploits a language model by extending its vocabulary with action tokens. It devises a unified action tokenizer decomposing the space into four body-part-specific tokenizers and uses grouped discrete diffusion decoding for real-time inference. Holo-M achieves the highest success rates on the SIMPLE humanoid loco-manipulation benchmark, significantly outperforming competitors.

A Unified Uncertainty Representation for Graph Neural Networks via Doubly-Spectral Stochastic Expansion

NeurIPS

Fred Xu, Thomas Markovich, Florence Regol, Yizhou Sun

AI Summary

Existing Graph Neural Network (GNN) uncertainty methods for calibration, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, and robustness to distribution shifts rely on separate models. This paper proposes a Doubly-Spectral Stochastic (DSS) expansion, modeling uncertain node embeddings as random graph signals using graph Fourier filters and polynomial chaos coordinates, to provide a unified representation for these tasks. DSS-GNN achieves the lowest Brier score for calibration, while DSS-Hybrid excels in OOD detection and robustness to concept-shift, demonstrating a single, effective framework for GNN uncertainty.
