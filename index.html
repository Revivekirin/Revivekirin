cuRoboV2-Fast: Fast Torque-Limited Collision-Free Motion Planning

arXiv

Balakumar Sundaralingam, Adithyavairavan Murali, Stan Birchfield

AI Summary

This paper addresses the core problem of reducing planning latency and execution time for collision-free, payload-aware, torque-limited robot motions. The proposed method, cuRoboV2-Fast, uses GPU-accelerated B-spline trajectory optimization with differentiable inverse dynamics and a novel TimeOpt for efficient traversal speed under dynamic constraints. Key results show a 4.4 ms mean planning time (14.7x faster than cuRoboV2) and a 34.6% reduction in pick-and-drop cycle times.

BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories

NeurIPS

Fei Lin, Tengchao Zhang, Ziyang Gong, Xiaotong Yu, Bohan Zhang, Yifan Zhou, Qihao Yang, Ji Dai, Dong Li, Yining Jiang, Qinghua Ni, Jun Huang, Qiang Zhang, Yue Zhou, Zhihang Zhong, Yonglin Tian, Fei-Yue Wang, Xue Yang

AI Summary

Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories (SDCLs) lack benchmarks to evaluate the execution-oriented embodied capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). To address this, BEAKER, an expert-curated benchmark, is introduced, comprising 1,000 question-answer samples across 11 image and video task types covering key judgments for chemical laboratory execution. Evaluation of 28 MLLMs reveals that while models can follow coarse experimental procedures, they struggle with grounding actions into operable regions, recognizing fine-grained apparatus/states/safety, and ordering steps in mid-length procedures. This highlights a clear gap between coarse procedural understanding and the precise, action-grounded perception required for laboratory execution.

arXiv

Siyuan Hu, Abulikemu Abuduweili, Yue Meng, Shipeng Liu, Quentin Leboutet, Chen Chen, Matthias Müller, Vladlen Koltun

AI Summary

Occlusion for Free: Detecting Occlusion from the Disagreement Between Two Monocular Distance Cues

NeurIPS

Chibueze Praise Onyenweaku, Toyibat Adele, Sikirulahi Opeyemi Abdulkareem, Obed Honour Eje

AI Summary

Occlusion detection in robotics is challenging due to the scarcity of training data with occlusion annotations. This paper proposes a novel method that detects occlusion by leveraging the disagreement between two monocular distance cues: apparent height (stable) and monocular metric depth (biased towards the occluder). This approach requires no training or occlusion labels, achieving an AUC of 0.909 on KITTI for heavy occlusion and 0.819 on CARLA, performing robustly across distance bands.

Rethinking Self-Repair in Code-as-Policy Agents: What Drives Retry Success?

arXiv

Siddharth Aphale, Ayushman Singh

AI Summary

The paper investigates the precise contribution of failure-specific feedback in code-as-policy agents' self-repair mechanisms, distinguishing it from general resampling or task guidance. Using simulated robot manipulation, the study compares template-based failure feedback against simple resampling and various forms of static or generic guidance. Key results show that failure feedback significantly outperforms resampling (85.3% vs 68.3% recovery); however, generic feedback and fixed static guidance achieve comparable recovery rates, implying that highly specific failure labels are often not critical. Incompatible feedback was also found to hinder performance.

Balancing hierarchical representation and quantization: A performance trade-off in text embeddings

arXiv

Kai Ponel, Marco Polignano, Erasmo Purificato, Aylin Julia Temizkan, Marco Levantesi, Giovanni Semeraro, Ernesto William De Luca

AI Summary

This study empirically benchmarks the interaction of hierarchical text representations and quantization to assess their trade-offs for text embedding computational efficiency. The study evaluated transformer models on 38 MTEB tasks, combining various hierarchical (1D/2D) representations with float32, int8, and binary quantization. Results show int8 quantization is often statistically indistinguishable from float32 with minimal performance cost, while binary quantization significantly degrades effectiveness. A combined 8x dimensionality reduction and int8 quantization yields a 32x storage reduction at 94.4% effectiveness; notably, quantization does not accelerate evaluation, but hierarchical sampling does.

ConDR: Conservative Reachability through Neural Hamilton-Jacobi Subsolutions

ACL

Kendra Givens, Taylor T. Johnson, Bryan C. Ward, Thomas Beckers

AI Summary

Classical Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability for identifying unsafe states scales poorly with dimension, and existing neural network methods can misclassify unsafe states as safe by allowing learned value functions to exceed true values. ConDR (Conservative DeepReach) proposes learning a subsolution of the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs variational inequality using a neural network, employing a one-sided loss to enforce a negative safety margin and prevent trivial solutions. This method significantly reduces unsafe state misclassification and closed-loop collision rates. ConDR demonstrates superior safety, tolerates model mismatch, and generates conservatively accurate unsafe sets that are not trivially over-approximated compared to baselines.

Message-passing graph persistence

arXiv

Jorge Luiz Franco, Arthur Queiroz Moura, Moacir Ponti, Amauri Souza

AI Summary

Message-passing GNNs are limited in expressive power, and existing persistent homology (PH) methods compute topology separately from GNN representation learning. This paper introduces Message-Passing Graph Persistence (MGP), a family of topological descriptors that integrates message passing directly into graph filtrations, updating node embeddings that determine persistence events. MGP is proven to be invariant to graph isomorphism and strictly more expressive than prior PH-based descriptors. Experiments show MGP outperforms other PH-based methods on most graph classification and regression datasets.

FedFiOT: Fisher-Weighted Optimal Transport for Model Merging in One-Shot and Few-Round Federated Learning

arXiv

Quan Minh Nguyen, Hoang M. Ngo, Bhakti Chowkwale, Yasaman Keshtkarjahromi, My T. Thai

AI Summary

One-shot and few-round federated learning underperform due to client model drift causing neuron mismatch and local data heterogeneity leading to differing parameter importance, which direct averaging fails to address. FedFiOT proposes a Fisher-weighted optimal transport framework that uses Fisher information to quantify parameter importance, guiding neuron alignment and model aggregation through alternating minimization. Theoretically, FedFiOT monotonically decreases its objective and provides a stationarity bound. Experimentally, it consistently outperforms baselines in both one-shot and few-round FL across multiple datasets.

arXiv

AI Summary

Non-compact Kogut-Susskind gauge theories: 1D lattice models with exact ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ gauge invariance

arXiv

Carlos Fulgado-Claudio, Torsten V. Zache

AI Summary

This paper addresses the core problem of extending the Kogut-Susskind (KS) formulation of gauge theories to non-compact gauge groups, specifically ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, while retaining exact gauge invariance on a spatial lattice. The proposed method involves a bottom-up construction of 1D lattice models where static and dynamical fermionic charges are introduced using a synthetic dimension. Key results include demonstrating exact ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ gauge invariance on the lattice, revealing differences like continuous Casimir spectra and infinite-dimensional matter representations, and establishing a connection to Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity. The work culminates in an interpretable interacting 1D ${\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ KS gauge theory, with analytical solutions for reduced two-body problems in strong self-interaction limits.

Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

arXiv

Shuai Dong, Kanghui Tian, Linghan Chen, Wenqi Shao, Hongrui Wu, Haowen Hou, Yongfu Zhu, Xuehui Wang, Ziyue Wang, Wenpu Liu, Weichu Xie, Yuqi Xu, Congcong Wang, Kaiwen Tuo, Caoyuan Ma, Taiqiang Wu, Ji Zhao, Wenzheng Chang, Dianyi Wang, Siyuan Wang, Zhongyu Wei, Yu Qiao, Jiaqi Wang

AI Summary

Existing streaming video RL methods suffer from trajectory-level advantage assignment, reinforcing redundant replies or discouraging useful follow-ups due to a mismatch with turn-level decisions. TurnWise introduces a turn-level GRPO framework that uses silence-completed prefix scoring for group-relative advantages and turn-mean policy optimization to weight response and waiting decisions equally. This approach improves streaming policy learning without additional components or rollouts. Experiments show TurnWise significantly outperforms prior RL methods for streaming video understanding.
