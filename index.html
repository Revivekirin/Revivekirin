Do You Miss the Nights We Spent Crafting Figures? Rethinking Teaser and Framework Figures in the Age of Generative AI

arXiv

Xiaoqi Zhao, Youwei Pang, Kailai Zhou

AI Summary

Generative AI raises questions about explanatory clarity and purposeful visual expression in scientific figures. This study retrospectively analyzed 371 figures from 200 papers, evaluating original figures across six communication dimensions and then performing and rating AI-driven revisions. AI revisions increased clarity but decreased aesthetic coherence, with overall gains larger for motivation figures compared to frameworks. These findings support researcher-led design, where AI assists exploration while authors retain responsibility for explanatory purpose and composition.

Towards interpreting any residual stream direction by scaling activation inversion

arXiv

Celeste De Schamphelaere, Juan J. Vazquez, Ari Spiesberger, Tomáš Gavenčiak, Gavin Leech, Euan Ong, Jiahai Feng

AI Summary

Interpreting language model residual stream directions is limited by the text available in corpora for finding activating examples. This paper introduces Maximally-Activating Example Metamodels (MAEMs), language models trained with a label-free RL objective to generate text exemplars that maximally activate specified residual stream directions. MAEMs empirically outperform corpus search and natural-language autoencoders in tasks like activation inversion, activating SAE features, multi-language/domain coverage, and identifying concepts behind steering vectors and LoRA backdoors, providing verifiable interpretations.

arXiv

Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, Chen Xu, Haruki Nishimura, Masha Itkina, Roberto Martín-Martín

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail during robotic deployment, requiring substantial, non-generalizable corrective data for recovery. Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation

arXiv

Yixiao Wang, Peiyi Chen, Mustafa Mukadam, Shreekant Gayaka, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Son Tran

AI Summary

Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging due to the need for both perceptual grounding and precise control. The proposed Latent RL Prior is a two-stage framework: first, a flow-matching action decoder is pretrained on large-scale RL rollouts using privileged information; then, this decoder is frozen, and a visual interface is learned to map images to its latent context using action supervision alone. This approach significantly outperforms fine-tuning baselines in low-data regimes, surpassing full-data baselines with 5-10x fewer demonstrations, while also demonstrating robustness and retention of RL-acquired control knowledge.

Unpacking the Compute Efficiency of Strong-to-Weak OPD against GRPO

arXiv

Zeyu Leo Liu, Yang Li, Bo Pang, Emre Can Acikgoz, Xuan-Phi Nguyen, Anurag Koul, Srijan Bansal, Erik Nijkamp, Shafiq Joty, Semih Yavuz

AI Summary

This paper investigates whether on-policy distillation (OPD) is more compute-efficient than reinforcement learning (GRPO) for achieving target accuracy. The authors systematically compare vanilla OPD with GRPO across diverse settings, and explore using external teachers, a student's own GRPO checkpoint, and multi-teacher OPD. While OPD with external teachers often loses to GRPO in accuracy per training FLOP, using the student's own GRPO checkpoint as a teacher recovers its accuracy with up to 50x fewer distillation FLOPs. Multi-teacher OPD further consolidates domain-specific GRPO experts into a single student with 42.0% fewer total FLOPs than a joint GRPO pipeline.

VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

arXiv

Yu-Cheng Chou, Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, Chi-Pin Huang, Chiao-An Yang, Xianzhe Fan, Jing Wang, Zhiqi Li, Yuanguo Kuang, Ruijie Zheng, Johan Bjorck, Sifei Liu, Hang Su, Abhishek Badki, Lei Zhang, Alan Yuille, Linxi "Jim'' Fan, Jan Kautz, Zhiding Yu

AI Summary

Autoregressive vision-language models generate visual plans (points, boxes, trajectories) token-by-token, making rich predictions costly and slow. VFlash proposes Flash Primitive Decoding, which leverages known primitive structure to jointly predict entire visual primitives in a single forward pass via a lightweight drafter. It trains on annotated primitives and accepts structurally valid predictions without token-sequence agreement. VFlash achieves approximately 25x faster generation and consistently reduces prediction errors, leading to improved task success (70.2% vs. 62.6% for baseline).

Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Chenruo Liu, Zihan Wang, Yijun Dong, Qi Lei

AI Summary

The paper investigates the differing dynamics of LoRA in supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). It finds RL concentrates the LoRA singular spectrum and rotates subspaces faster than SFT, attributing this to group-mean baseline subtraction which balances coherent fitting and gradient noise. Guided by these insights, tailored LoRA updates are proposed: flattening the full update spectrum for SFT to spread adaptation, and flattening the update outside the current LoRA span for RL to promote subspace rotation. Experiments confirm these stage-specific configurations yield superior performance on mathematical reasoning tasks.

T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

arXiv

Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen, Yingyan, Lin, Chaojian Li

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face high inference costs and suffer from irrelevant visual tokens and misallocated attention, leading to computational burden and reduced robustness to distribution shifts. The proposed T3R framework, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement, addresses this by first performing segmentation-guided token pruning to remove irrelevant visual patches. This is followed by saliency-guided attention biasing to reallocate attention towards action-critical regions within the remaining tokens. T3R removes 85% of visual tokens while matching unpruned success rates, improves out-of-distribution robustness by up to 7 percentage points, and achieves up to 3.3x speedup, particularly benefiting multi-camera VLA models.

Anisotropy lifts topological protection of transport in pumped spin chains

arXiv

Mainak Pal

AI Summary

This study investigates topological spin pumping and transport in anisotropic spin chains, challenging whether momentum-space topological invariants guarantee quantized transport. Mapping the system to a fermionic model predicted quantized pumping via Chern numbers, but real-space time evolution revealed that transport is generally not quantized in the anisotropic regime due to state hybridization. Quantized pumping only persists in the isotropic limit where the transported spin component is conserved. The findings show that conventional momentum-space invariants are insufficient for guaranteeing topological protection when the pumped quantity is not conserved, highlighting the importance of real-space diagnostics.

FedReason: Large Language Models as Weight-Space Meta-Aggregators for Heterogeneous Federated Learning

arXiv

Ali Mohammadi Ruzbahani, Abbas Yazdinejad, Hadis Karimipour

AI Summary

Federated learning servers face challenges in selecting optimal aggregation rules for various client heterogeneities. FedReason proposes using a Large Language Model (LLM) as a meta-aggregator that periodically inspects client model weights via a sandbox, optionally leverages a proxy dataset, and submits a custom Python aggregation program. FedReason significantly outperformed FedAvg by 1.26 percentage points and other server-side baselines on CIFAR-10/100 under label skew. It also effectively mitigated Byzantine attacks by excluding malicious clients, performing comparably to FLTrust without a root model, though it did not surpass client-side methods like SCAFFOLD.

Module-Aware Optimization for Graph Neural Networks

NeurIPS

Guy Hadad, Haggai Roitman, Moshe Eliasof, Bracha Shapira

AI Summary

Existing matrix-aware optimizers fail to account for the graph-propagated representations that shape GNN weight matrix geometry, leading to "update amplification." To mitigate this, GraphMuon is introduced, adapting Muon's updates by performing orthogonalization in a metric defined by graph-propagated layer inputs and normalizing step sizes based on their induced effect. GraphMuon theoretically mitigates this graph-induced amplification and empirically achieves 1.32x-2.42x validation-loss time-to-target speedups over AdamW on large-scale OGB benchmarks, while maintaining competitive performance.

Information Elicitation by Ordinal Agreement

arXiv

Yiling Chen, Shi Feng, Grant Schoenebeck, Fang-Yi Yu

AI Summary

Eliciting truthful, compressed ordinal information is challenging in settings without objective ground truth, as existing peer-prediction mechanisms fail to incentivize honest compression of rich private data. This paper proposes a novel ordinal peer-prediction mechanism that rewards agents based on pairwise agreement, measured by Kendall tau. Under a broad class of Bayesian Stochastically Order Consistent models, the mechanism theoretically proves truthful reporting to be stochastically dominant, offering robust incentives for diverse agents. Empirical evaluations confirm improved truthful-payment distributions and higher normalized sensitivity compared to baselines.
