FACTR 2: Learning External Force Sensing for Commodity Robot Arms Improves Policy Learning

arXiv

Steven Oh, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Philip Han, Kenneth Shaw, Satoshi Funabashi, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Deepak Pathak

AI Summary

Commodity robot arms lack dedicated force sensors, hindering contact-rich manipulation. The proposed Neural External Torque Estimation (NEXT) method estimates external joint torques from 10 minutes of free-motion data, achieving accuracy comparable to dedicated sensors. This enables Force-Informed Re-Sampling Training (FIRST), which leverages NEXT's estimates to up-sample pre-contact and contact segments during behavior cloning. FIRST improves policy learning, outperforming prior force-aware policies by over 17% in task progress across five long-horizon tasks on off-the-shelf robots.

World Pilot: Steering Vision-Language-Action Models with World-Action Priors

arXiv

Zefu Lin, Rongxu Cui, Junjia Xu, Xiaojuan Jin, Wenling Li, Lue Fan, Zhaoxiang Zhang

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with continuous manipulation dynamics due to their pretraining on static data, lacking understanding of scene evolution. World Pilot addresses this by augmenting VLA policies with priors from a World-Action Model (WAM) via two pathways: Latent Steering (scene-evolution latent) and Action Steering (anticipated trajectory). This framework achieves state-of-the-art 84.7% success on the LIBERO-Plus zero-shot OOD benchmark and superior real-robot performance, particularly under shifts in viewpoint, geometry, and deformable states.

APPO: Agentic Procedural Policy Optimization

arXiv

Xucong Wang, Ziyu Ma, Yong Wang, Yuxiang Ji, Shidong Yang, Guanhua Chen, Pengkun Wang, Xiangxiang Chu

AI Summary

Existing agentic Reinforcement Learning methods struggle with identifying influential intermediate decisions due to coarse credit assignment over heuristic units like tool-call boundaries. To address this, APPO proposes shifting branching and credit assignment to fine-grained decision points in the sequence. arXiv

arXiv

AI Summary

APT: Action Expert Pretraining Improves Instruction Generalization of Vision-Language-Action Policies

arXiv

Kechun Xu, Zhenjie Zhu, Anzhe Chen, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang

AI Summary

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit poor generalization to out-of-distribution language instructions because continuous action experts are initialized randomly and learn from imbalanced data, corrupting the VLM. The proposed APT method addresses this via a two-stage training process that factorizes the policy into a Vision-Action (VA) prior and VLA likelihood. In Stage 1, the action expert is pretrained as a VA prior using vision-action pairs from a frozen VLM, bypassing language imbalance. Stage 2 then injects language tokens via a gated fusion mechanism, achieving consistent gains on unseen instructions and compositional tasks.

arXiv

Adam Wei, Nicholas Pfaff, Thomas Cohn, Arif Kerem Dayı, Constantinos Daskalakis, Giannis Daras, Russ Tedrake

AI Summary

Robotics imitation learning is hampered by the abundance of suboptimal data, which existing co-training methods struggle to leverage effectively without incorporating harmful features. Entanglement generation between field modes mediated by a fluctuating conducting wall

arXiv

Luca Giovanni Cammarata, Tommaso Fazio, Roberto Passante, Lucia Rizzuto

AI Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of generating quantum entanglement between isolated field modes separated by a conducting boundary. The authors model a system of two sub-cavities separated by a movable, quantum-fluctuating plate using a generalized Law Hamiltonian, applying second-order perturbation theory to calculate the fields' reduced density operator and negativity. The results demonstrate that the wall's quantum fluctuations successfully mediate entanglement between the fields in separate cavities—which is impossible with a fixed wall—with the degree of entanglement being highly dependent on the wall's mass, oscillation frequency, and cavity dimensions.

ICML
