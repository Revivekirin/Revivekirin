DeVI: Physics-based Dexterous Human-Object Interaction via Synthetic Video Imitation

arXiv

Hyeonwoo Kim, Jeonghwan Kim, Kyungwon Cho, Hanbyul Joo

AI Summary

Synthetic 2D videos, despite their rich interaction knowledge, lack the physical fidelity and 3D information needed for direct use in physics-based dexterous robotic control. DeVI (Dexterous Video Imitation) addresses this by leveraging text-conditioned synthetic videos and introducing a hybrid reward system that integrates 3D human tracking with robust 2D object tracking to enable physically plausible dexterous agent control. DeVI achieves zero-shot generalization across diverse objects and interaction types, outperforming methods relying on 3D demonstrations, especially for dexterous hand-object interactions.

Parallel-SFT: Improving Zero-Shot Cross-Programming-Language Transfer for Code RL

arXiv

Zhaofeng Wu, Shiqi Wang, Boya Peng, Anuj Goyal, Melanie Kambadur, Sebastian Ruder, Yoon Kim, Chloe Bi

AI Summary

Large Language Models struggle with low-resource programming languages (PLs), as standard RL training in one PL fails to generalize to others, often degrading performance. To address this zero-shot cross-programming-language transfer problem, the paper proposes Parallel-SFT, an SFT initialization strategy incorporating "parallel programs" (functionally equivalent code in multiple PLs) into its data mixture. This method significantly improves transferability and generalization to unseen PLs after subsequent RL training. Analysis reveals Parallel-SFT creates a more functionality-centric latent space, tightly clustering equivalent programs across different PLs.

FedSIR: Spectral Client Identification and Relabeling for Federated Learning with Noisy Labels

CVPR

Sina Gholami, Abdulmoneam Ali, Tania Haghighi, Ahmed Arafa, Minhaj Nur Alam

AI Summary

Federated learning performance severely degrades due to noisy labels across distributed clients. FedSIR proposes a multi-stage framework that identifies clean and noisy clients by analyzing the spectral consistency of class-wise feature subspaces. It then enables noisy clients to spectrally relabel corrupted samples using references from clean clients, further employing a noise-aware training strategy with logit-adjusted loss, knowledge distillation, and distance-aware aggregation. FedSIR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods for FL with noisy labels.

ParetoSlider: Diffusion Models Post-Training for Continuous Reward Control

arXiv

Shelly Golan, Michael Finkelson, Ariel Bereslavsky, Yotam Nitzan, Or Patashnik

AI Summary

Current generative model alignment with RL relies on single scalar rewards or fixed weighted sums for multiple criteria, preventing inference-time control over conflicting goals like prompt adherence versus source fidelity. ParetoSlider introduces a multi-objective RL framework that trains a single diffusion model to approximate the entire Pareto front by using continuously varying preference weights as a conditioning signal. This enables users to navigate optimal trade-offs at inference time without retraining. The method matches or exceeds baselines trained for fixed trade-offs, while uniquely offering fine-grained control over competing generative goals.

arXiv

AI Summary

Gauge-Equivariant Graph Neural Networks for Lattice Gauge Theories

arXiv

Ali Rayat, Yaohang Li, Gia-Wei Chern

AI Summary

Existing machine-learning approaches lack a principled framework for learning under local gauge symmetries and nonlocal observables in quantum systems. This paper introduces a gauge-equivariant graph neural network (GNN) that embeds non-Abelian symmetry into message passing using matrix-valued, gauge-covariant features and updates, extending equivariant learning to fully local symmetries. This method enables gauge-covariant transport across the lattice, allowing nonlocal correlations to emerge naturally from local operations. Validated across pure gauge, gauge-matter, and dynamical regimes, this establishes gauge-equivariant message passing as a general paradigm for learning in systems governed by local symmetry.

AAC: Admissible-by-Architecture Differentiable Landmark Compression for ALT

arXiv

An T. Le, Vien Ngo

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the lack of a differentiable landmark-selection method for ALT shortest-path heuristics that inherently preserves admissibility without complex calibration, hindering end-to-end integration with neural networks. The proposed method is AAC (Architecturally Admissible Compressor), a differentiable landmark-selection module whose outputs are admissible by construction through a row-stochastic mixture of triangle-inequality lower bounds. Key results show AAC achieves near-optimal coverage (within 0.9-3.9% of provably near-optimal FPS-ALT), has zero admissibility violations, and is 1.2-1.5x faster than FPS-ALT at matched memory.

Near-Future Policy Optimization

arXiv

Chuanyu Qin, Chenxu Yang, Qingyi Si, Naibin Gu, Dingyu Yao, Zheng Lin, Peng Fu, Nan Duan, Jiaqi Wang

AI Summary

The core problem in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is effectively sourcing off-policy trajectories that are both "strong enough" and "close enough" to maximize the learning signal, a challenge existing mixed-policy methods fail to address simultaneously. This paper proposes Near-Future Policy Optimization (NPO), a mixed-policy scheme that learns from a policy's own near-future self (a later training checkpoint), which naturally provides trajectories balancing quality and variance cost. An adaptive variant, AutoNPO, automatically selects optimal guide checkpoints. On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, NPO improved average performance from 57.88 to 62.84, and AutoNPO further to 63.15, accelerating convergence and raising the final performance ceiling.
