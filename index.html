DynaFLIP: Rethinking Robotics Perception via Tri-Modal-Dynamics Guided Representation

arXiv

Jusuk Lee, Seungjae Lee, Jonghun Shin, Hoseong Jung, Sungha Kim, Daesol Cho, H. Jin Kim, Jia-Bin Huang, Furong Huang

AI Summary

Current robot perception models, primarily trained for static recognition, lack inherent motion understanding critical for effective manipulation, burdening downstream policies. DynaFLIP proposes a dynamics-aware multimodal pre-training framework that shapes an image encoder using image-language-3D flow triplets. This is achieved by minimizing the simplex volume spanned by these modalities in a shared hyperspherical space, regularized with cosine and contrastive objectives, thereby embedding motion understanding upstream. The resulting control-relevant representations consistently outperform baselines across diverse downstream policies, demonstrating improved generalization with gains up to +22.5% in out-of-distribution scenarios.

arXiv

Xiaona Zhou, Muntasir Wahed, Tianjiao Yu, Constantin Brif, Ismini Lourentzou

AI Summary

Large Vision-Language Models perform unsatisfactorily in time-series anomaly detection due to the lack of natural-language rationales in benchmarks for fine-tuning interpretable decisions. Uncertainty-driven 3D Gaussian Splatting Active Mapping via Anisotropic Visibility Field

CVPR

Shangjie Xue, Jesse Dill, Dhruv Ahuja, Frank Dellaert, Panagiotis Tsiotras, Danfei Xu

AI Summary

The core problem is the unreliability of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) predictions in regions unseen by training views, requiring effective uncertainty quantification and active mapping. The proposed method, GAVIS, quantifies an anisotropic visibility field for each 3DGS particle using spherical harmonics, integrating it into a Bayesian Network-based rasterizer for real-time uncertainty estimation and subsequent maximum information gain active mapping. GAVIS achieves real-time (200 FPS) uncertainty quantification, consistently and significantly outperforms prior approaches in both accuracy and efficiency, and can also improve existing methods.

Fairness-Aware Federated Learning with Trajectory Shapley Value

arXiv

Daniel Kuznetsov, Ziqi Wang

AI Summary

Federated learning suffers from biased and unstable learning due to conventional aggregation schemes using fixed weights that fail to reflect heterogeneous and time-varying client contributions. To address this, the paper proposes the Trajectory Shapley Value (TSV), a metric that evaluates each client's influence on the global model's optimization trajectory using a validation-based, temporally consistent utility. Building on TSV, FedTSV is designed as an adaptive aggregation method that converts per-round TSV evaluations into dynamic client weights. Experiments demonstrate that FedTSV accelerates convergence, improves robustness, and yields more equitable contribution assessments.

When, why, and how do diffusion posterior samplers fail? A finite-sample lens

arXiv

Benjamin A. Burns, Sara Fridovich-Keil

AI Summary

Diffusion posterior samplers for inverse problems often fail due to inexact likelihood approximations at intermediate timesteps, resulting in poorly understood, erroneous posterior distributions. To diagnose these failures, the authors introduce a finite-sample perspective that approximates the true posterior with arbitrary precision. This lens reveals that popular approximations misestimate posterior spread at intermediate timesteps, causing issues like mode hallucination and sensitivity to stopping time, often stemming from multimodal priors. This agnostic approach serves as a diagnostic to evaluate existing and future posterior samplers.

Reasoning with Sampling: Cutting at Decision Points

arXiv

Felix Zhou, Anay Mehrotra, Quanquan C. Liu

AI Summary

The core problem is the inefficiency of sampling from sharpened base model distributions (power distributions) to elicit strong reasoning, as prior uniform-cut samplers tend to modify local details instead of crucial decision points in reasoning traces. This paper introduces Entropy-Cut Metropolis-Hastings, an algorithm that identifies and resamples from key decision points using the base model's next-token entropy as a proxy. The method empirically verifies entropy jumps as useful proxies, proves mixing time scales with the number of decisions rather than tokens, and consistently improves performance over baselines and RL-trained models on MATH500, HumanEval, GPQA Diamond, and AIME26.

Self-Trained Verification for Training- and Test-Time Self-Improvement

arXiv

Chen Henry Wu, Aditi Raghunathan

AI Summary

Self-improvement for reasoning models is bottlenecked by verifiers that struggle to catch self-generated errors due to a lack of training signal, hindering both test-time verification-refinement and training-time self-training. The proposed Self-Trained Verification (STV) method addresses this by training verifiers to imitate a more informed version of themselves, leveraging the observation that a model *can* catch errors when shown a reference solution. STV substantially improves test-time verification-refinement, roughly doubling accuracy on hard math and increasing scientific reasoning accuracy 14-fold. Furthermore, Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) training, which uses STV's feedback for generator training, boosts standalone generator pass@1 by 30% relative to standard RL-converged models.

arXiv

AI Summary

Vision-Language Models suffer from "carrier sensitivity," where replacing textual questions with semantically equivalent rendered images drastically degrades performance due to training data bias leading to misaligned cross-modal representations. arXiv

Reinforcement Learning with Robust Rubric Rewards

arXiv

Ya-Qi Yu, Hao Wang, Fangyu Hong, Xiangyang Qu, Gaojie Wu, Qiaoyu Luo, Nuo Xu, Huixin Wang, Wuheng Xu, Yongxin Liao, Zihao Chen, Haonan Li, Ziming Li, Dezhi Peng, Minghui Liao, Jihao Wu, Haoyu Ren, Dandan Tu

AI Summary

Reinforcement Learning for vision-language tasks faces challenges in providing accurate multi-criteria supervision for partially verifiable tasks, despite rubrics offering fine-grained guidance. The proposed Reinforcement Learning with Robust Rubric Rewards ($\text{RLR}^3$) extends RLVR to criterion-level verification, employing dual execution paths (LLM-as-extractor+verifier or LLM-as-Judge) with a minimal exposure strategy to ensure faithful scoring. $\text{RLR}^3$ also uses hierarchical aggregation and mitigates score saturation. Evaluated across 15 benchmarks, $\text{RLR}^3$ consistently outperforms RLVR, yielding a 4.7-point improvement over the base model and significantly reducing exploitable false positives.
