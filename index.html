Is One Layer Enough? Training A Single Transformer Layer Can Match Full-Parameter RL Training

arXiv

Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu, Athanasios Glentis, Dawei Li, Chung-Yiu Yau, Hongzhou Lin, Mingyi Hong

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training in LLMs often updates all parameters uniformly, despite a lack of understanding regarding how RL adaptation distributes across transformer layers. This work challenges that assumption via a systematic layer-wise study, introducing "layer contribution" to quantify gains from training individual layers. Surprisingly, training a single transformer layer can recover most or even surpass full-parameter RL gains. Key results show RL benefits are highly concentrated in a small subset, often a single, middle-stack transformer layer, a pattern consistent across various models, algorithms, and tasks.

Language-Critique Imitation Learning from Suboptimal Demonstrations

arXiv

Chih-Han Yang, Dai-Jie Wu, Yun-Ping Huang, Ping-Chun Hsieh, Kenneth Marino, Shao-Hua Sun

AI Summary

Imitation learning from suboptimal demonstrations is limited by scalar supervision that cannot express explicit reasoning or corrective actions. This paper proposes a language-critique framework that constructs natural language labels describing task progress, suboptimal behaviors, and fine-grained corrective guidance. It then introduces a language-critique loss to train policies (LC-BC, LC-DP) directly with these structured signals. The method theoretically upper-bounds the expert performance gap and empirically outperforms strong baselines across diverse continuous control tasks.

arXiv

GPU-Parallel Linearization Error Bounds for Real-Time Robust Optimal Control of Nonlinear and Neural Network Dynamics

arXiv

Jeffrey Fang, Keyi Shen, Anutam Srinivasan, Glen Chou

AI Summary

Real-time robust optimal control for uncertain nonlinear systems requires reliable linearization error bounds (LEBs) to guarantee constraint satisfaction when using linear time-varying (LTV) approximations. This paper proposes tight, differentiable, GPU-parallel LEBs for both analytic (using path-based Hessian bounds) and neural network dynamics (via NN verifiers and Jacobian corrections). These LEBs are integrated into a GPU-parallel system-level synthesis robust control solver, adapted for enhanced uncertainty propagation, creating the GPUSLS-LEO method. GPUSLS-LEO computes formally verified robust control policies for high-dimensional systems (up to 168 states) at rates up to 67 Hz, achieving reduced conservativeness and faster solve times while maintaining real-time performance.

ACL

AI Summary

AI Summary

QuasiMoTTo: Quasi-Monte Carlo Test-Time Scaling

arXiv

Michael Y. Li, Anthony Zhan, Kanishk Gandhi, Noah D. Goodman, Emily B. Fox

AI Summary

Independent sampling for scaling language model inference wastes compute on redundant solutions due to redundant samples. QuasiMoTTo addresses this by generating correlated, marginal-preserving samples via quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) applied to an inverse-CDF reparameterization of autoregressive sampling, ensuring wider output space coverage. On reasoning benchmarks, QuasiMoTTo matches i.i.d. pass@k accuracy with 25-47% fewer samples and often saturates an upper bound. Furthermore, it achieves i.i.d. performance in policy-gradient reinforcement learning with 50% fewer training steps, attributed to higher coverage yielding a stronger learning signal.

Diffusion-GR2: Diffusion Generative Reasoning Re-ranker

arXiv

Zhuoxuan Zhang, Kangqi Ni, Yuhang Chen, Mingfu Liang, Xiaohan Wei, Yunchen Pu, Fei Tian, Chonglin Sun, Frank Shyu, Adam, Song, Sandeep Pandey, Luke Simon, Tianlong Chen, Xi Liu

AI Summary

Generative reasoning re-rankers provide strong recommendations but suffer slow inference due to autoregressive decoding, with naive block-diffusion conversion leading to invalid rankings and off-policy decoding issues. Diffusion-GR2 addresses this by converting AR re-rankers into block-diffusion models through Conversion Fine-tuning (CFT) for valid permutations, On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for self-supervision, and Reinforcement Learning (RL). This method recovers near-parity accuracy with the AR re-ranker while boosting decode throughput by 2.4-3.5x. CFT and OPD are critical for closing the accuracy gaps introduced by the conversion.

Towards Metric-Agnostic Trajectory Forecasting

arXiv

Markus Knoche, Daan de Geus, Bastian Leibe

AI Summary

State-of-the-art trajectory forecasting models struggle with conflicting optimization objectives due to tailoring training to specific benchmark metrics. The proposed method introduces a metric-agnostic paradigm: models are trained with probabilistic objectives to directly optimize the predictive distribution, while metric optimization is handled as a downstream task using "Trajectory Distribution Evaluation (TraDiE) policies." These policies map the predictive distribution to the required set of trajectories and confidences. Applying this framework, their DONUT-NLL model with TraDiE policies achieved state-of-the-art results on all metrics of the Waymo motion prediction benchmark.

ZO-Act: Efficient Zeroth-Order Fine-Tuning via One-Shot Activation-Informed Low-Rank Subspaces

arXiv

Xun Dong, Yibo Xu, Naigang Wang, Xin Li, Penghang Yin, Zi Yang

AI Summary

**Core Problem:** Existing zeroth-order (ZO) fine-tuning methods for large language models suffer from high-variance gradient estimates and limited performance because they perturb full model weights or random low-dimensional subspaces. **Proposed Method:** The authors introduce **ZO-Act**, which restricts perturbations to a fixed, low-rank subspace derived from input activations at initialization and optimizes only lightweight coefficient matrices using forward-only evaluations. This approach reduces estimator variance, enables the use of momentum-based optimizers, and naturally supports fine-tuning for quantized (low-bit) LLMs. **Key Results:** Theoretical analysis shows that ZO-Act reduces both the variance-dependent convergence term and the finite-difference error, and empirical evaluations on Llama-3-8B, OPT-13B, and INT4 Llama-3-8B demonstrate consistent performance gains over strong ZO baselines across language understanding, question answering, and commonsense reasoning tasks.

FAR: Failure-Aware Retry for Test-Time Recovery and Continual Policy Improvement

arXiv

Haoran Hao, Shahram Najam Syed, Jeffrey Ichnowski, Jeff Schneider

AI Summary

To address the issue of robots repeating the same mistakes during execution failures without relying on human intervention, the authors propose Failure-Aware Retry (FAR), a framework for autonomous test-time recovery and continual policy improvement. FAR combines Failure-Contrastive Preference Adaptation—which leverages failure data to steer the robot's policy away from unsuccessful actions—with lightweight action perturbations to encourage local exploration during retries. Evaluation on manipulation tasks shows that FAR improves success rates over standard diffusion policies by an average of 17.6% in simulation and 11.7% in the real world, while significantly boosting data efficiency during continual training.
