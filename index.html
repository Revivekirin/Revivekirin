RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering

arXiv

Tuna Han Salih Meral, Kaan Oktay, Alec Helbling, Atakan Kara, Duen Horng Chau, Pinar Yanardag

AI Summary

The core problem is understanding if substantial gains from RL post-training in diffusion transformers, particularly for prompt-dependent tasks, necessitate complex, input-dependent model transformations. The proposed method introduces a highly-parameter-efficient alternative: fixed, input-independent "steering vectors" added to the frozen base model's block outputs, fitted to match RL-tuned model velocity predictions. These simple vectors recover 90-112% of the reward gains from various RL-tuned models, and when trained with online RL, can even surpass weight-based RL on key metrics, demonstrating that RL post-training is largely activation steering without learning new input-dependent functions.

AnyVIO: Feed-Forward Streaming Visual-Inertial Odometry Across Platforms

arXiv

Omoruyi Atekha, Marcus Greiff, Ray Zhang, Luca Carlone

AI Summary

Geometric foundation models (GFMs) for camera motion lack metric scale, and integrating inertial data via traditional visual-inertial odometry (VIO) methods introduces complex tuning and growing optimization costs. AnyVIO proposes a real-time, feed-forward streaming monocular VIO solution combining a GFM and an Inertial Foundation Model (IFM), which predict metric motion fused by a lightweight learned head in a single pass. This method reduces the visual model's metric position error by 36% with only 70ms latency, demonstrating accuracy across nine diverse datasets and robustness in challenging real-world scenarios at high speeds.

One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning

arXiv

Yuxin Jiang, Wenqiang Sun, Jiaqi Li, Yuchao Gu, Lvmin Zhang, Ivor Tsang, Mike Zheng Shou

AI Summary

Latent action learning from unlabeled videos often inefficiently learns dynamics from scratch. This paper proposes O$^{2}$LaF, which repurposes a single pretrained video generator as a prior for *both* inverse dynamics (learning actions via a transition encoder) and forward dynamics (predicting consequences via an action-conditioned generator). Optimized end-to-end, O$^{2}$LaF achieves new state-of-the-art in few-shot action probing and enables stronger zero-shot action transfer across diverse contexts and embodiments. The framework is also extensible to other predictive representation priors.

Fractional Charge at Dislocations from Band-Inversion Cancellation

arXiv

Vladimir Juričić, Rodrigo Soto-Garrido

AI Summary

A bulk probe for topology is lacking when band inversions with opposite Chern numbers cancel, yielding gapped-out boundary modes and a vanishing Hall response. This work proposes that dislocations in inversion-symmetric two-band crystals with such cancelling band inversions bind a robust half-integer charge, serving as a quantized bulk topological invariant. They demonstrate this by deforming lattice models, showing the fractional charge is insensitive to local perturbations and persists even after the in-gap core state is removed. This crystalline charge is distinct from Chern number contributions and is detectable in artificial lattices.

AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control

arXiv

Yiyang Du, Yuxuan Kuang, Qitao Zhao, Yixuan Wang, Zhanqiu Guo, Yi Xu, Xin Li, Wenbin He, Liu Ren, Chenyan Xiong

AI Summary

Existing benchmarks for frontier agents controlling robots via low-level actions only report episode-level success, lacking detailed insights into their action-level capabilities and failures. This paper introduces AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework where agents state local intents and a critic evaluates action outcomes and failure modes. Local velocimetry for atomic superfluids: from Riemann rarefaction waves to quantized vortices

arXiv

Yansheng Zhang, Yi Jiang, Feiyang Wang, Louis Depaepe, J. Q. Matthew Chia, Christoph Eigen, Gehrig Carlse, Zoran Hadzibabic

AI Summary

A method for local velocimetry in 2D atomic superfluids is demonstrated. It utilizes matter-wave interferometry with Raman-Bragg beam splitters to reconstruct 2D velocity fields by mapping local superfluid phase gradients onto interference fringe displacement. Key results include directly observing self-similar velocity field evolution in 1D Riemann rarefaction flows and mapping 2D velocity fields around quantized vortices, explicitly demonstrating velocity circulation quantization.

A Tutorial on Complementarity-Based Contact Models for Robotics

arXiv

Onur Beker, A. René Geist, Michael Posa, Marc Toussaint, Justin Carpentier, Georg Martius

AI Summary

Simulating rigid-body contact in robotics using cone complementarity formulations lacks a unified, pedagogical treatment explaining their physical assumptions and numerical solutions. This tutorial bridges that gap by connecting rigid-body dynamics, conic optimization, and frictional contact, detailing how complementarity conditions encode non-penetration and friction without prescribed modes. It distinguishes equivalent transcriptions from relaxations, examines existence and uniqueness of solutions, and compares solver methods (first-order vs. Newton-based) and their trade-offs. The work helps robotics practitioners interpret simulation results, choose suitable formulations and solvers, and develop custom contact dynamics routines.

Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher

ACL

Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, Yuxin Chen

AI Summary

Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) struggles with existing methods that train policies using self-constructed utility targets rather than explicit teachers, leading to costly feature acquisition. This paper proposes OracleImitator, which constructs an explicit "oracle teacher" at training time to rank features by their impact on downstream error and distills this ranking into a deployable policy via soft-target imitation. OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods, recovering 90% of attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost across benchmarks.

PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Kai Lu, Chenyang Ma, Ioannis Havoutis

AI Summary

Vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation tasks due to monolithic coupling of temporal structure, skills, and precision. PGX addresses this by offloading temporal and precision control to training-free components: a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, while VLA skills operate in active manipulation phases, and World-Coordinate Locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency. Evaluated on simulated and real-world tasks with a Unitree G1 humanoid, PGX significantly improves stage progression and task success rates for long-horizon loco-manipulation.

Probing the "Conscious" Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models

arXiv

Jiahao Huang, Jun Wang

AI Summary

Vision-language models' internal concept representations and their "conscious-like" processing, such as selective access, remain opaque. This paper introduces the Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens), a multimodal Jacobian lens, which maps intermediate VLM states to a shared semantic space and enables internal concept interventions. These patterns, including the later development of selective access compared to concept accessibility, are consistently observed across various VLM architectures.

Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs

arXiv

Ziqing Yang, Yixin Wu, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang

AI Summary

Multimodal image editing systems currently lack safety mechanisms that jointly consider both unsafe visual content in images and risky text prompts. To evaluate this, the authors introduced EditSafetyBench, a benchmark with 5,940 image-instruction pairs covering 11 risk categories, and probed four advanced systems. Evaluations revealed a systematic text-vision safety mismatch, where systems strongly refused unsafe text prompts but poorly handled unsafe images with neutral instructions, demonstrating inconsistent risk management across categories, except for sexual content. This highlights the urgent need for safety guardrails that jointly reason over both visual and textual modalities.

arXiv

Jinzhe Li, Qi Wang, Dandan Guo, Li Liu, Yuan Wu, Yi Chang

AI Summary

Current evaluations lack a common protocol to assess the reliability of multimodal decision models, specifically their stability against presentation changes, irrelevant information, and conflicting evidence, compared to their backbones. This paper introduces MM-SystemOneBias, an evaluation framework applying six paired interventions (e.g., option order, irrelevant information, external judgment) to 13 models across diverse tasks. Decision models consistently demonstrated greater stability under various input changes and robustness to incorrect external judgments or conflicting descriptions compared to their backbones. Thermal-Guided Ensemble Reinforcement Learning for Regime-Adaptive Short-Term GPU Power Forecasting in AI Data Centers

arXiv

Yuanshuo Zhang, Ying Zhang, Ziying Wang, Fanping Sui

AI Summary

AI data centers' bursty, regime-dependent loads challenge reliable energy management and grid control, necessitating accurate short-term GPU power forecasting. This paper proposes a thermal-guided ensemble reinforcement learning framework where a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent adaptively weights multiple deep learning predictors (LSTM, TCN, Transformer). The method achieves a normalized mean absolute error of about 1% across all computing regimes, significantly outperforming deep learning and ensemble benchmarks (4-11%) while converging faster with narrower prediction intervals.

Grid Cells as Spectral Successor Features: A Reusable Basis for Continual Reinforcement Learning

NeurIPS

Élodie Côté-Gauthier, Doina Precup, Isabeau Prémont-Schwarz

AI Summary

Continual reinforcement learning faces challenges in effectively reusing environmental knowledge and adapting to new tasks, largely dependent on the chosen base features for successor representations. This paper proposes a method inspired by grid cells, constructing base features from eigenvectors of a fixed reference policy's transition matrix. This enables a closed-form computation for successor features, allowing long-term return prediction and new task adaptation via simple linear regression. Crucially, agents using this approach maintained near-maximal return across multiple task changes in a gridworld environment, substantially outperforming existing SimpleSF and DQN methods.

arXiv
