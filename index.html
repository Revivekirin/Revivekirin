Evolution of Accuracy and Visual-Cognitive Errors in a Decade of Vision-Language AI Models

arXiv

Shravan Murlidaran, Miguel P. Eckstein

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the inadequate evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on complex social scenes and their specific error types. To address this, the authors introduced the Complex Social Behavior (CSB) dataset and analyzed the accuracy and five visual-cognitive error types of a decade of VLMs (pre-MLLM and MLLM) against human descriptions. Key results show that MLLMs achieved human-level accuracy on CSB, closing the performance gap with simpler scenes, and largely eliminated most error types except for occasional spatial dependence differences from humans. Detection, recognition, and hallucination errors were identified as having the highest impact on scene description accuracy.

Revisiting Euler-Angle Regression with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks

arXiv

Yangting Sun, Zijun Cui, Yufei Zhang

AI Summary

Regressing Euler angles is challenging due to their discontinuities and singularities, which often destabilize neural network training. This work proposes a new framework combining range-aware Euler modeling with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), supported by theoretical analysis showing that bounded Euler ranges favor KAN's additive functional form. Extensive experiments across rotation regression, pose estimation, and inverse kinematics tasks demonstrate consistent improvements in accuracy, convergence, and efficiency.

B-spline Policy: Accelerating Manipulation Policies via B-spline Action Representations

arXiv

Xiaoshen Han, Haoyu Xiong, Haonan Chen, Chaoqi Liu, Antonio Torralba, Yuke Zhu, Yilun Du

AI Summary

Robot manipulation policies are often slow due to discrete-time action representations. The B-spline Policy (BSP) addresses this by parameterizing actions as continuous B-spline curves, defined by knots and control points, which are directly predicted by standard policy learning pipelines. This method generates smooth, temporally scalable trajectories executable at higher frequencies, significantly reducing task completion time and improving speed while maintaining high success rates in simulated and real-world tasks.

Semantic Pareto-DQN: A Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning Framework for Financial Anomaly Detection

arXiv

Cláudio Lúcio do Val Lopes, Lucca Machado da Silva

AI Summary

Financial anomaly detection struggles with extreme class imbalance, leading to "fraud collapse" in single-objective algorithms that fail to balance anomaly interdiction with customer friction. To overcome this, Semantic Pareto-DQN is proposed, a multi-objective reinforcement learning framework that uses large language models to encode transaction features into robust natural-language narratives as state representations. Evaluations demonstrate it successfully shatters the "zero-recall trap," achieving superior minority-class recall compared to scalarized baselines.

PAC-ACT: Post-training Actor-Critic for Action Chunking Transformers

arXiv

Yujie Pang, Zudong Li

AI Summary

Behavior cloning-trained Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) suffer from distribution shift in contact-rich industrial tasks, compromising policy reliability under perturbations and constraints. PAC-ACT proposes a reinforcement learning post-training framework for pretrained ACT policies, reformulating chunk-level optimization, using an ACT-transferred actor-critic, and introducing a hybrid behavior-prior constraint to preserve the pretrained action distribution. CoDiMAD: Diffusion-Based Privileged Distillation for Communication-Free Multi-Robot Coordination

ACL

Jiyue Tao, Shunheng Xin, Tongsheng Shen, Dexin Zhao, Feitian Zhang

AI Summary

Decentralized multi-robot coordination without communication is challenged by the multi-modal nature of optimal actions under partial observability, which deterministic privileged distillation fails to capture by mode-averaging. CoDiMAD proposes a three-stage framework to distill a privileged oracle policy into decentralized students parameterized as conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models. This method effectively approximates the multi-modal oracle-action distribution, enabling students to sample decisive actions from coherent coordination modes rather than averaging. Experiments on three cooperative tasks demonstrate CoDiMAD consistently outperforms direct local MARL and deterministic distillation baselines, supported by theoretical analysis.

arXiv

arXiv

Steven Roche, Milo Van Mooy, Nathan McGuire, Levi Cai, Jonathan P. How, Yogesh Girdhar

AI Summary

Traditional AUV control struggles with robustness and sim-to-real transfer due to inaccurate drag modeling, as high-fidelity CFD is too computationally expensive for direct RL training. How Mobile Gas Sensor Trajectories Govern Hydrogen Leak Detection: A Safety Gap in Manual Leak Inspection of Hydrogen System Components

arXiv

Christian Masuhr, Arne Wendt, Thorsten Schüppstuhl

AI Summary

Manual hydrogen leak detection, relying solely on operator execution without spatial probe guidance, is unreliable and poses a significant safety risk. This study quantifies how sniffer trajectory kinematics affect detection reliability using a robotically guided test bench to eliminate operator variability. Key results show that scanning velocity and spatial probe orientation strongly dictate detectability; conventional linear trajectories frequently miss leaks, while geometry-specific routing (e.g., circumferential paths) maintains a high safety margin. The findings reveal a tangible safety risk in current standard operating procedures, leading to the derivation of geometry-specific routing rules and a proof-of-concept software pipeline for validated trajectory generation.

What VGGT Knows About Overlap: Probing Geometric Foundation Models for Co-Visibility

arXiv

Filippo Ziliotto, Luciano Serafini, Lamberto Ballan, Tommaso Campari

AI Summary

The core problem is accurately determining co-visibility—which image pairs share overlapping visible surfaces, particularly with minimal overlap—essential for 3D reconstruction and robotic localization. The proposed method, Co-VGGT, leverages the observation that the VGGT geometric foundation model implicitly encodes co-visibility hierarchically, with specialized layers (e.g., L17 for non-co-visible pairs). Co-VGGT freezes VGGT and adds a lightweight layer-wise mixture-of-experts head that adaptively weights each layer's geometric abstraction to classify co-visibility from RGB. Co-VGGT significantly outperforms human baselines and prior work (25% pairwise, 10% multiview improvement on Co-VisiON), providing well-calibrated predictions directly usable in SfM/SLAM pipelines.
