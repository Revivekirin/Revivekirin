$ω$-0: A Latent Predictive World Action Model for Concurrent Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Zhe Li, Zhenzhe Zhang, Yangyang Wei, Wenjie Zhang, Xichen Yuan, Peiyuan Zhi, Gen Li, Xinying Guo, Fengjie Gao, Jianfei Yang, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

The core problem is the inability of existing humanoid policies to effectively handle concurrent whole-body locomotion and manipulation for complex household tasks, often relying on decomposed behaviors or arm-centric/video-heavy world models. The proposed method, $ω$-0, is a latent predictive whole-body world-action model that predicts controller-compatible action latents directly from language, vision, and proprioception. It achieves this by coupling lightweight latent visual foresight (predicting future observation embeddings) with diffusion-based whole-body action generation. Key results show that $ω$-0 produces smooth manipulate-while-moving behaviors on 11 real-world household tasks, consistently outperforming various imitation learning, VLA, and WAM baselines.

DyPES-VLA: Learning Shared Dynamics Priors and Embodiment-Specific Control for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

arXiv

Junfeng Li, Junjie He, Zhide Zhong, Yangyang Zheng, Pingyue Sheng, Jiayu Dong, Ruixin Li, Haodong Yan, Jiaguan Zhu, Tianran Zhang, Runze Yu, Wen Chen, Liuqing Yang, Yuxiang Gao, Haoang Li

AI Summary

Training a single generalist VLA policy for heterogeneous robot embodiments is challenging due to underutilized shared dynamics priors and the need for manual action space alignment. DyPES-VLA addresses this by first learning shared dynamics priors from cross-embodiment data via a future-prediction objective for the VLM. It then translates these priors into executable controls directly in each robot's native action space using an embodiment-specific Mixture-of-Experts action head, bypassing manual preprocessing. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance, reaching 98.0% success on LIBERO, 59.25% on RoboCasa-GR1, and 89.02% on RoboTwin 2.0.

AI Summary

Generalist robot world models struggle with action controllability, generalization to unseen environments, and costly real-world scaling. RRC: Unlocking Generative Reward Models in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Ranking-Based Reward Construction

arXiv

Chenglong Wang, Ziming Zhu, Yifu Huo, Bei Li, Qiaozhi He, Yan Ding, Xiaoyang Hao, Yuxin Gao, Tianhua Zhou, Xiaojia Chang, Tongran Liu, Jingbo Zhu

AI Summary

Generative reward models (GRMs) underperform in LLM reinforcement learning due to a mismatch between their comparative nature and RL's scalar scoring paradigm. To address this, the paper proposes Ranking-based Reward Construction (RRC), which derives effective RL learning signals by transforming GRM-generated relative preference rankings into scalar rewards. RRC employs self-competitive ranking among sampled responses and anchor-guided ranking with reference responses for scalability. Experiments show RRC substantially improves RL training with GRMs, achieving consistent gains over existing reward construction approaches on chat and reasoning benchmarks.

BaKron: Efficient Quantization with Kronecker-Factored Hessians

arXiv

Johann Birnick, Rayan Saab

AI Summary

The core problem is the computational expense of applying rich two-sided Kronecker-factored Hessian approximations for neural network quantization, as existing methods like GPTQ use limited one-sided information. BaKron is introduced as an efficient solver that combines anti-diagonal parallelism with a recursive divide-and-conquer approach to incorporate these Hessians. This method reduces total work from $O(m^2n^2)$ to $O(mn(m+n))$ for an $m \times n$ weight matrix, achieving cubic scaling comparable to GPTQ but leveraging richer curvature information. BaKron is also modular with respect to both the base quantizer and the Hessian estimator, demonstrating practical efficiency.

Nonlinear Compton scattering in a quantized pump field

arXiv

Kenan Qu

AI Summary

A fully quantized theory of nonlinear Compton scattering (NCS) driven by a quantum pump field is developed, utilizing exact quantum-Volkov states that incorporate pump depletion, back-action, and final-state correlations through Fock-state ladders. The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

arXiv

Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu

AI Summary

Multimodal LLMs equipped with active visual operations ("thinking-with-images") often yield marginal gains at high costs, prompting the question of whether the returned visual evidence *causally affects* the final answer. To investigate, the paper formulates visual tool-use as a causal graph and audits it through policy, trajectory, and step-level interventions, introducing "Visual Evidence Gain" to isolate individual observation contributions. The study reveals "policy miscalibration" with two failure modes: "Calling Without Looking" (observations have no causal effect) and "Looking Without Planning" (informative observations but incoherent call schedule). This demonstrates an "illusion of visual tool-use," where aggregate accuracy gains do not translate to causal effectiveness across a broad range of rollouts.

arXiv

AI Summary

Standard on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards underexploits temporal rollout structure by uniformly weighting token-level divergences, failing to adapt to the evolving teacher-student mismatch during generation. To address this, DASH (Divergence-Adaptive Supervision Horizons) maps local distillation signal gaps to an adaptive propagation gate, controlling backward multi-step aggregation to dynamically adjust token-level supervision weights based on divergence evolution. DASH improves reasoning capabilities over vanilla OPSD across three mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model scales, achieving gains with no additional computational cost.

VIDP: Variable Impedance Diffusion Policy for Compliant Robot Manipulation from Diverse Demonstrations

arXiv

Hisham Khalil, Neil Fernandes, Thomas M. Kwok, Hsiu-Chin Lin, Yue Hu

AI Summary

Learning variable impedance policies for contact-rich manipulation from demonstrations is challenging because compliance is a hidden variable in kinematic data, and trajectory variations are often ambiguous. The Variable Impedance Diffusion Policy (VIDP) addresses this by using a Task-Parameterized Directionality-Aware Mixture Model (TP-DAMM) to extract physically consistent trajectory distributions from diverse demonstrations. This model maps distributions to stiffness profiles, enabling joint prediction of pose actions and task compliance without force sensors. Real-world experiments demonstrate VIDP significantly improves task success over fixed-impedance baselines, reducing interaction forces and tracking errors.

ErgoSurf: Ergodic Control for the Coverage of Unknown Surfaces

arXiv

Stefan Schneyer, Timo Bachmann, Maged Iskandar, Korbinian Nottensteiner, Alin Albu-Schäffer, Freek Stulp, João Silvério

AI Summary

Traditional ergodic control methods struggle with systematic surface coverage for contact-centric tasks on surfaces with unknown geometry, requiring prior knowledge or vision systems. ErgoSurf proposes a novel online ergodic control framework that simultaneously achieves surface coverage and reconstructs unknown geometry. It uses a Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS) model with intrinsic tactile sensing to learn geometry during execution, approximating the surface locally for efficient planning via tangent planes and heat-diffusion fields. Experiments validate simultaneous ergodic coverage and online surface learning, demonstrating geometry reconstruction errors approaching ground truth.
