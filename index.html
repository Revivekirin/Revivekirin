Unified Multimodal Autoregressive Modeling with Shared Context-Visual Tokenizer is Key to Unification

ICML

Wujian Peng, Lingchen Meng, Yuxuan Cai, Xianwei Zhuang, Yuhuan Yang, Rongyao Fang, Chenfei Wu, Junyang Lin, Zuxuan Wu, Shuai Bai

AI Summary

Existing unified multimodal models are hampered by disparate visual tokenizers for understanding and generation, fragmenting their representation space. UniAR proposes a single discrete visual tokenizer, derived from a pretrained vision encoder with multi-level bitwise quantization, to establish a shared context for both tasks. It employs parallel-bitwise-prediction for efficient autoregressive generation, decoded by a diffusion model. UniAR achieves state-of-the-art performance in image generation and editing, while remaining competitive in multimodal understanding benchmarks.

Visual Verification Enables Inference-time Steering and Autonomous Policy Improvement

arXiv

Mingtong Zhang, Dhruv Shah

AI Summary

Robots need to learn from real-world experience and improve over time. This paper proposes VERITAS, a generator-verifier framework where a pre-trained generalist policy acts as a generator, paired with a gradient-free visual verifier that evaluates actions at inference time. MOCHI: Motion Enhancement of Collaborative Human-object Interactions

arXiv

Jiye Lee, Yonghun Choi, Jungdam Won

AI Summary

Modeling collaborative multi-human object interaction (MHOI) is challenging due to complex, noisy data characterized by contact misalignment, motion jitter, and missing finger details. MOCHI is a two-stage framework that first optimizes physically plausible hand grasps from noisy body input, then refines full-body motion using a diffusion-based noise optimization framework that incorporates single-person motion priors and interaction-specific objectives. The method effectively enhances diverse MHOI data, demonstrating robustness across varying participants and interactions, enabling applications like keyframe-based MHOI creation and data augmentation.

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing benchmarks for generalist mobile manipulation policies relying on a single success-rate provide insufficient diagnostic detail. This paper introduces EBench, a simulation benchmark of 26 diverse tasks annotated along 5 capability and 4 generalization dimensions to enable elemental diagnosis. ICML

AI Summary

Zone of Proximal Policy Optimization: Teacher in Prompts, Not Gradients

arXiv

Byung-Kwan Lee, Ximing Lu, Shizhe Diao, Minki Kang, Saurav Muralidharan, Karan Sapra, Andrew Tao, Pavlo Molchanov, Yejin Choi, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang, Ryo Hachiuma

AI Summary

Knowledge distillation is brittle for small students, over-focusing on teacher modes, while injecting teacher responses into RL policy gradients for hard questions breaks on-policy assumptions and induces drift. Zone of Proximal Policy Optimization (ZPPO) addresses this by keeping the teacher within the prompt rather than the policy gradient, constructing Binary Candidate-included Questions (BCQ) and Negative Candidate-included Questions (NCQ) for difficult examples, amplified by a prompt replay buffer. ZPPO significantly outperforms off/on-policy distillation and GRPO on the Qwen3.5 family (0.8B-9B) across a 31-benchmark suite, with the largest gains observed at the smallest student scales.

Memory as a Wasting Asset: Pricing Flash Endurance for Embodied Agents, and the Limits of Doing So

arXiv

Josef Liyanjun Chen

AI Summary

Robot memory systems fail to account for flash endurance limits, causing inefficient wear and potential premature failure. This paper proposes an "endurance shadow price" to model flash as depreciating capital, enabling a wear-augmented cost index for optimal memory placement across hierarchical storage (RAM/NVM/cloud). This method is cost-optimal, routing valuable memories off flash if their value positively correlates with write frequency ($\chi > 0$), a condition found to vary empirically by robot task regime. While this approach primarily extends device lifetime for commodity flash, its direct impact on task performance for embodied agents remains an open question.

Knowledge Reutilization in Meta-Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Yuan Meng, Bo Wang, Juan de los Rios Ruiz, Xiangtong Yao, Zhenshan Bing, Fuchun Sun, Alois Knoll

AI Summary

End-to-end meta-RL methods suffer from tight coupling of task inference and embodiment control, reducing sample efficiency and limiting cross-agent reuse. This work proposes a meta-knowledge reutilization framework that learns dynamics-agnostic, task-level knowledge on a simplified agent using a Bayesian non-parametric prior and a high-level policy. This frozen meta-knowledge is then transferred to heterogeneous agents via a semantic-magnitude interface and temporal adaptor to generate subgoals. Experiments show the framework significantly reduces tracking error (94.75-99.79%) and achieves comparable performance with 23.8% of the interaction data of state-of-the-art baselines.

Double quantum spin Hall phase in bilayer ZrTe$_5$

arXiv

Chao Chen Ye, Gábor Kalla, Jianting Ye, Jagoda Sławińska

AI Summary

Addressing the rarity of robust, experimentally accessible even-channel quantum spin Hall (QSH) phases, this work uses first principles calculations to identify bilayer ZrTe$_5$ as realizing a double QSH phase in its stable structure. This phase hosts two pairs of helical edge states, resulting in enhanced edge conductance and a robust, quantized spin Hall response over an energy window of ~100 meV. Furthermore, uniaxial strain can tune this material to transition into a conventional single QSH phase.
