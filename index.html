DexLink: Synthesizing Dexterous Behaviors from Chained Hand-object Poses

arXiv

Meenal Parakh, Deep Patel, Beining Han, Jia Deng

AI Summary

Synthesizing diverse dexterous manipulation policies is challenging due to difficulties in scaling human demonstrations or controlling generic reward functions. DexLink addresses this by synthesizing behaviors from sparse, reusable hand-object interaction templates. It retargets these templates to objects via optimization, links them into an approximate reference trajectory, and refines them with residual reinforcement learning. This method provides fine-grained control, ensures modular templates transfer across objects/categories, matches dense reference-tracking performance without demonstrations, and shows promise for zero-shot sim-to-real deployment.

Can image generation help visual reasoning? An empirical study

arXiv

Xueyang Yu, Yifan Jing, Taiming Lu, Linrong Cai, Zhipeng Cai, Yunyang Xiong, Gabriel Sarch, Zhuang Liu

AI Summary

This study empirically investigates if generated "helper" images improve visual reasoning performance for visual question answering. They tested 13 vision-language model and generator pairs, where models described and then used a generated image alongside the original to answer questions. The key finding is that image generation generally lowers mean accuracy by 3.7 percentage points, with declines observed on nine of ten tasks and significant drops for unified models and on VSTAR. While repeated edits yielded mixed results for some tasks, most, like ChartQAPro and MathVista, performed worse than their no-generation baselines.

Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators

arXiv

Charlie Griffin, Oliver Makins, Asa Cooper Stickland, Alessandro Abate, Yarin Gal, Abby D'Cruz, Xander Davies

AI Summary

AI monitor evaluations are susceptible to misleading results because measured false negative rates are highly sensitive to the method and optimization level used to generate adversarial attack policies. A case study on an ex-production OpenAI monitor showed false negative rates varied drastically, from 0.5% to 20.8% within one method and 6.4% to 23.0% across different optimized methods, with all attacks transferring to a held-out target monitor. These findings indicate that evaluations employing only basic attack generation methods can provide false confidence in a monitor's strength, underscoring the critical need for robust justification of attack policy choices.

MERLoc: Robust Robot Localization in Dynamic Environments with Pose-Based Retrieval over 3D Gaussian Splatting References

arXiv

Michael Baumgartner, Dario Mylonopoulos, Sammy Christen, Markus Gross, Moritz Bacher

AI Summary

Robots require robust localization in dynamic environments with moving people and rearranged objects, but current appearance-based reference retrieval is brittle under such scene changes. MERLoc addresses this by transforming a 3D Gaussian Splatting scan into a dense, pre-encoded functional reference memory of rendered views. It employs a novel pose-based retrieval strategy that selects references by spatial proximity and viewing direction, rather than visual appearance, to sidestep ambiguities. This method consistently matches or outperforms appearance retrieval, significantly lowering position RMSE by 15-60% on real data, and runs efficiently onboard to enable successful navigation in dynamic real-world scenarios.

arXiv

Shuaijun Liu, Qifu Wen, Xingwei Chen, Xinwen Hu, Feiyang You, Sijia Chen, Ningxin Su

AI Summary

An algebraic quantization of gravity from the null cone

arXiv

Venkatesa Chandrasekaran

AI Summary

This paper proposes a background-independent algebraic quantization of vacuum general relativity on Lorentzian spacetimes. It achieves this by solving gravitational constraints on null cones to define a reduced phase space, which is then quantized to construct a nonperturbative Hilbert space and a convergent Lorentzian path integral. Key results include a finite graviton pair creation/annihilation amplitude, a finite-dimensional Hilbert space for causal diamonds, the derivation of Bekenstein-Hawking entropy with matching logarithmic corrections, and the recovery of semiclassical Einstein equations.

Memory Is Not Ground Truth: Revising Navigation Authority under Unreliable VLM Evidence for Persistent Semantic Navigation

arXiv

Sumukh Shrote, Shenbagaraj Kannapiran, Eric Eaton, Camillo Jose Taylor

AI Summary

VLM-based persistent memories for robot navigation suffer from ambiguous negative evidence, making it difficult to discern true object absence from VLM false negatives and decide when to abandon a memory as a navigation goal. The paper proposes a dependence-aware, reversible memory-update mechanism that stops using unsupported memories as direct navigation goals without claiming object absence, avoiding double-counting correlated misses. Real-robot experiments showed this method successfully prevented navigation to stale memories, instead prioritizing semantic frontiers, and could restore memory actionability upon later positive evidence, outperforming a baseline that consistently pursued stale objectives.

Cairn: Object-Centric 3D Trajectory Priors for Steering Frozen VLAs

ACL

Agelos Kratimenos, Kostas Daniilidis, Evangelos Chatzipantazis

AI Summary

Vision-language-action (VLA) models fail in novel layouts with displaced objects or obstacles, requiring costly fine-tuning that erodes existing skills. Cairn addresses this by keeping the VLA frozen and steering it at test time using a learned object-centric 3D trajectory prior. This prior samples SE(3) trajectories based on scene and object point clouds to navigate around obstacles and through gaps, guiding the VLA's predicted actions toward waypoints. Cairn boosts a frozen π0.5's success from 24.5% to 95.1% on held-out LIBERO scenes with obstacles, outperforming fine-tuned models, and shows improved performance on LIBERO-PRO and real-world obstacle avoidance.

Agentic LongLive: Asynchronous Agentic Memory for Long Video Generation

arXiv

Shaoteng Liu, Shuai Yang, Wei Huang, Luozhou Wang, Song Han, Yukang Chen

AI Summary

Real-time autoregressive video generators struggle with consistency of people, objects, and places over long, interactive streams, unlike offline agent systems. Agentic LongLive proposes an asynchronous agentic memory where a separate memory agent maintains evolving text states and images for entities, composing global and per-shot memory images. This entity memory is asynchronously prefilled into the generator's attention sink, with a small RL-trained LLM deciding what to remember. The method significantly improves persistent-state accuracy (26.3 pts), protagonist identity (44.9 pts), and revisited-place consistency (40.4 pts) on the LongStory-Online benchmark, operating faster than real-time with minimal overhead.

ProbFaS: Probabilistic Video Frame Selection for MLLMs via Multi-Fidelity Calibration

arXiv

Tianru Zhang, Mayank Nautiyal, Hugo Eidmann, Prashant Singh, Ekta Vats

AI Summary

Processing long videos with MLLMs is prohibitively expensive, and existing frame selection methods (uniform or image-text alignment score-based) are insufficient, as ITA scores poorly reflect MLLM needs. This paper introduces ProbFaS, a training-free probabilistic pipeline that calibrates frame relevance by fusing dense, biased ITA scores with sparse, accurate MLLM internal judgments via multi-fidelity co-kriging, followed by a temporal determinantal point process for selection. ProbFaS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across three benchmarks, outperforming 14 baselines in most settings and enabling 32 frames to match performance requiring 128-768 frames from other methods.

FineDex: Bridging Fine-Grained Human-Object Interaction Generation to Dexterous Humanoid Manipulation

arXiv

Yujia Zeng, Yuxin Chen, Yuzhen Song, Runhan Huang, Chensheng Peng, Yiyang Shao, Koushil Sreenath, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Yilun Du

AI Summary

Existing dexterous manipulation and human-object interaction (HOI) methods lack fine-grained, finger-level control and expressibility for complex interactions. FineDex proposes a framework that learns coordinated human body, hand, and object motions from fine-grained instructions on hand, finger, and contact usage. Stranger Things: When Objects Appear Without Their Typical Neighbors

NeurIPS

Siddhartha Gairola, Jiahao Xie, Anna Kukleva, Francesco Locatello, Bernt Schiele

AI Summary

Modern vision models suffer from "typicality bias," over-relying on contextual co-occurrences rather than intrinsic visual features, leading to significant performance drops when objects appear without their typical neighbors. To diagnose this, the authors introduce a framework utilizing NPMI and STRANGE-Bench, revealing pervasive 5-11 p.p. performance gaps across various modern AI models that do not consistently shrink with scale. To mitigate this, they propose Anchor-Atypicality Sampling (AAS), a data augmentation rule that biases training towards atypical object pairings. AAS reduces the typicality gap by up to 1.6 p.p. on natural images and 23.0 p.p. on synthetic data, without sacrificing overall performance.

Spin Loop Currents and Bulk-Edge Structure in Correlated Topological States of WSe$_2$/MoTe$_2$ Bilayers

arXiv

Po-Jui Chen, Kevin P. Lucht, Andreas Gleis, Andrew J. Millis, J. H. Pixley, Daniele Guerci

AI Summary

This theoretical study investigates correlated topological states in AB-stacked WSe$_2$/MoTe$_2$ bilayers using a parton mean-field framework incorporating various Hubbard interactions. It shows that non-local interactions drive a transition between a topological Kondo semimetal and a topological Kondo insulator at $\nu=2$. This transition is attributed to an emergent spin-loop current arising from the interplay of Ising spin-orbit coupling and non-local electron correlations. This emergent current also dictates edge transport, leading to quantized two-point conductance at specific bias voltages.

SoK: When FL Clients Cannot Train -- Delegated Federated Learning for IoT

arXiv

Hamed Mohammed Alkharsh, Aydin Abadi, Jakub Szelag, Suleiman Muhammad Sabo, Ishak Semuel Beno

AI Summary

Federated learning (FL) assumes clients can perform necessary computation, but resource-constrained IoT devices often require delegating training tasks, which this paper systematizes as Delegated FL. It introduces a framework to analyze this setting across delegate-view privacy, delegated attribution, and constrained-owner feasibility over the same delegated transition. An audit of 29 systems and controlled studies reveal significant "composition gaps," demonstrating that individual privacy or attribution mechanisms do not guarantee end-to-end claims. This highlights unaddressed risks where sensitive information can leak and prescribed computations may not be verifiable in Delegated FL.

Exact monogamy of steering and remote Wigner negativity for multimode Gaussian nodes

arXiv

Sudip Chakrabarty

AI Summary

The paper addresses the unknown maximum number of neighbors that can steer a $k$-mode Gaussian node. By assigning a "share" based on the covariance matrix to each neighbor and showing these shares sum to at most $k$, the study establishes that exactly $2k-1$ neighbors can steer the node using Gaussian measurements. This same limit governs remote Wigner negativity, which can be simultaneously achieved for all $2k-1$ neighbors in specific networks. Exceeding this number for steering or Bell inequality violation necessitates non-Gaussian measurements with at least three settings.

arXiv

AI Summary

The core problem in real-to-simulation transfer is the fragmented evaluation of methods across diverse benchmarks, necessitating $N \times M$ integrations. Real2SimLab is proposed as a unified platform that evaluates methods by executing their generated programs and scoring the output simulations using original benchmark scorers.
