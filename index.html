ATLAS: Agentic or Latent Visual Reasoning? One Word is Enough for Both

arXiv

Ziyu Guo, Rain Liu, Xinyan Chen, Pheng-Ann Heng

AI Summary

Existing visual reasoning methods face challenges like computational expense, context-switching latency, or generalization and training difficulties. ATLAS proposes a novel framework utilizing a single discrete 'functional token' that serves as both an agentic operation and a latent visual reasoning unit, avoiding explicit intermediate image generation and integrating with standard training. To stabilize the training of these sparse tokens, Latent-Anchored GRPO (LA-GRPO) is introduced. ATLAS achieves superior performance on challenging benchmarks while maintaining clear interpretability.

RAVEN: Real-time Autoregressive Video Extrapolation with Consistency-model GRPO

arXiv

Yanzuo Lu, Ronglai Zuo, Jiankang Deng

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the quality degradation over long horizons in real-time autoregressive video diffusion models due to a mismatch between training and inference history distributions. The proposed Real-time Autoregressive Video Extrapolation Network (RAVEN) is a training-time framework that repacks self-rollouts to align training attention with inference-time extrapolation and supervise history representations. Additionally, Consistency-model Group Relative Policy Optimization (CM-GRPO) applies online Reinforcement Learning directly to consistency sampling steps. Experiments demonstrate RAVEN surpasses prior causal video distillation baselines, with CM-GRPO offering further improvements when combined.

arXiv

Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, Zhuowen Han, Zi-Han Wang, Jinyang Wu, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Yongliang Shen

AI Summary

Reinforcement Learning (RL) provides coarse supervision for LLM agents, and existing On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) struggles with multi-turn instability and negative teacher rejections. SDAR (Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning) proposes treating OPSD as a gated auxiliary objective, with RL as the primary backbone, using a sigmoid gate to selectively strengthen distillation on positive teacher signals and attenuate negative ones. Widening the Gap: Exploiting LLM Quantization via Outlier Injection

arXiv

Xiaohua Zhan, Kazuki Egashira, Robin Staab, Mark Vero, Martin Vechev

AI Summary

The core problem is that existing attacks exploiting LLM quantization security risks fail against sophisticated, popular quantization methods, limiting their practical impact. This work introduces the first attack that consistently induces malicious behavior across advanced techniques like AWQ, GPTQ, and GGUF I-quants. The method exploits how large outliers, when injected into specific weight blocks, cause other weights to round to zero during quantization, inducing a targeted weight collapse. This attack achieves high success rates, demonstrating for the first time that quantization security risks are broadly relevant across complex, widely-used methods.

Extensive long-range magic in non-Abelian topological orders

arXiv

Yuzhen Zhang, Isaac H. Kim, Yimu Bao, Sagar Vijay

AI Summary

Low-energy states of non-Abelian topological orders possess extensive, long-range "magic" that cannot be eliminated by constant-depth local unitary circuits, refining their complexity and resource-theoretic characterization. This is established by a no-go theorem demonstrating that stabilizer states cannot approximate low-energy states of non-Abelian string-net models. The study further shows that violations of quantized mutual braiding phases in Abelian string-net phases necessarily imply this extensive long-range magic. This phenomenon extends to higher-dimensional quantum double models with an entanglement area law and non-trivial fusion spaces.

Forgetting That Sticks: Quantization-Permanent Unlearning via Circuit Attribution

arXiv

Saisab Sadhu, Pratinav Seth, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu

AI Summary

Machine unlearning methods often fail under quantization because their subtle parameter updates are too small to clear quantization bin widths, leading to unlearning reversal or ineffective forgetting. This paper proposes MANSU (Mechanistic-Aligned Null-Space Unlearning), which uses causal circuit attribution to pinpoint minimal forget-set subgraphs, applies circuit-restricted null-space projection with a retain bound, and includes a per-parameter magnitude floor to guarantee updates persist through quantization. MANSU is the first method to jointly achieve meaningful, quantization-permanent unlearning, retain preservation, and structural erasure, significantly outperforming gradient-based baselines which show unlearning recovery under compression.

CoCo-InEKF: State Estimation with Learned Contact Covariances in Dynamic, Contact-Rich Scenarios

arXiv

Michael Baumgartner, David Müller, Agon Serifi, Ruben Grandia, Espen Knoop, Markus Gross, Moritz Bächer

AI Summary

Robust state estimation for dynamic, contact-rich legged robot motion is challenging due to traditional binary contact models failing to capture nuanced partial contact or slippage. CoCo-InEKF proposes an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter that replaces binary contact states with continuous, learned contact velocity covariances, predicted by an end-to-end trained neural network using a state-error loss. This method achieves a superior accuracy-efficiency tradeoff for linear velocity estimation and improved filter consistency, enabling robust execution of challenging motions on a bipedal robot in simulation and reality.

CLOVER: Closed-Loop Value Estimation \& Ranking for End-to-End Autonomous Driving Planning

arXiv

Sining Ang, Yuguang Yang, Canyu Chen, Yan Wang

AI Summary

End-to-end autonomous driving planners face a training-evaluation mismatch, as imitation learning from single logged trajectories doesn't align with rule-based safety and comfort metrics. CLOVER proposes a Closed-LOop Value Estimation and Ranking framework utilizing a generator-scorer formulation: the generator produces diverse candidates, and the scorer predicts planning-metric sub-scores. This is achieved through evaluator-filtered pseudo-expert trajectories for generator training with set-level coverage and conservative closed-loop self-distillation to refine both components. CLOVER sets a new state of the art on NAVSIM (94.5 PDMS, 90.4 EPDMS), matches the strongest result on NavHard (48.3 EPDMS), and achieves the lowest L2 error and collision rate on nuScenes.

arXiv

AI Summary

Fast contracted Clebsch--Gordan tensor products for equivariant graph neural networks

arXiv

Anton Bochkarev, Yury Lysogorskiy, Ralf Drautz

AI Summary

The core problem is the computational cost and difficulty of capturing antisymmetric parity-odd channels when evaluating contracted Clebsch--Gordan tensor products in $\mathrm{O}(3)$-equivariant machine learning. The proposed $\mathcal{O}(L^3)$ algorithm maps angular integrals to a structured tensor-product grid, decoupling radial and angular components, and recovers parity-odd channels via a surface-curl pairing (spherical Poisson bracket). This method achieves empirical $L^2$ wall-clock scaling for practical $l_{\max}$ (transitioning to $L^3$ for large $l_{\max}$) and enables efficient, parity-aware equivariant message passing in atomic-cluster-expansion-style architectures.

arXiv

AI Summary
