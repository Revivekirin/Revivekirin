Coding Agents with an Obstacle-Aware Harness for Safe Robot Manipulation

ACL

Bingxin Xu, Yuzhang Shang, Zhen Dong, Emilio Ferrara

AI Summary

Coding agents for robot manipulation, despite explicit instructions, fail to prioritize safety, frequently colliding with obstacles due to planning deficiencies in route selection and contact execution. The proposed SafeHarness equips these agents with two obstacle-aware harnesses: one for route planning that uses bounding boxes to verify and replan routes, and another for contact execution that selects safe contact positions. This approach significantly improves safety and task success, achieving 71.9% task success and 87.5% collision avoidance, surpassing prior SOTA by 6.5% and 27.0%, respectively.

Workspace Models: Lightweight Robotic Memory via Saliency-Driven Supervision

arXiv

Nitish Dashora, Douglas Chen, Idan Shenfeld, John Marangola, Pulkit Agrawal, Max Simchowitz

AI Summary

Robotic manipulation tasks require long-term memory, but conditioning on full histories causes performance degradation and existing VLM-based memory compression is computationally expensive at deployment. This paper proposes "workspace tokens," a lightweight latent memory learned by distilling VLM-identified task-salient information during train-time using a set-reconstruction decoder loss. Workspace tokens efficiently replace observations at deployment, enabling policies to solve memory-intensive tasks without in-the-loop VLM reasoning, leading to improved performance and computational lightness in simulation and hardware.

FAMOS: Feed-Forward 3D Articulation Modeling from Sparse Observations

arXiv

Kevin Qu, Tao Sun, Massimiliano Viola, Liyuan Zhu, Zhizhuo Zhou, Sayan Deb Sarkar, Konrad Schindler, Iro Armeni

AI Summary

Modeling articulated objects from sparse, partial 3D observations is challenging for feed-forward methods, which often rely on single views and shape priors. FAMOS is a feed-forward model that predicts movable-part segmentation and joint parameters from a variable number of sparse point clouds, employing a Multi-state Articulation Transformer for multi-observation reasoning, an observed articulation span objective, and a procedural data generator to overcome data limitations. It consistently demonstrates improvements over both feed-forward and optimization-based baselines across multiple benchmarks.

Score Centering Stabilizes Off-policy Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Martin Marek, Max Ryabinin

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning for large language models is unstable due to the training-inference mismatch (TIM), caused by a persistent bias (drift) between training and inference engines, which is impractical to eliminate. The paper proposes "score centering," an additive correction term that stabilizes RL under TIM by canceling this drift. Score centering alone matches or outperforms importance sampling methods, especially with severe mismatches, across models up to 30B parameters. Furthermore, it composes with importance sampling, surpassing pure importance-sampling baselines in staleness experiments.

PosteriorBench: From Point Estimates to Posterior Matching in Evaluating Generative Inverse Solvers

arXiv

Jiachen Yao, Zi-Siang Hsu, Xi Deng, Aditi Gupta, Xin Ju, Sally M Benson, Gege Wen, Anima Anandkumar

AI Summary

Existing evaluations of generative inverse solvers inadequately assess ill-posed problems by focusing on single plausible reconstructions, failing to capture the true posterior distribution. This paper introduces PosteriorBench, a benchmark for evaluating the distributional accuracy of these solvers across four physics-based inverse problems. It constructs high-fidelity reference posteriors using computationally heavy but established sampling methods, paired with a five-metric suite to assess pointwise accuracy, marginal uncertainty, and distributional alignment. Experiments reveal substantial distribution-matching gaps in current solvers, demonstrating that neural operators improve resolution robustness and that guidance weights/generation noise are key to posterior-variance calibration.

arXiv

Jinbang Huang, Yuanzhao Hu, Zhiyuan Li, Ran Qi, Yixin Xiao, Yangzheng Wu, Tengyue Ba, Zhanguang Zhang, Yingxue Zhang

AI Summary

The core problem is accurately and efficiently determining skill termination and transition in long-horizon robot tasks, as existing methods rely on hard-to-obtain completion signals and VLMs are slow and misaligned for real-time use. RetireOPD: Self-Retiring On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Yan Yu, Zhengxi Lu, Yizhou Liu, Yichen Pan, Aozhe Wang, Qipeng Chen, Hua Yang, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the unreliability and stage-dependency of teacher supervision in self on-policy distillation (OPD) for multi-turn RL agents, which are plagued by sparse rewards. RetireOPD proposes first optimizing a decoupled, skill-conditioned teacher, then training a skill-free student with RL and OPD. Its key innovation is "Adaptive Retirement," where the student independently drops the teacher once their discrepancy stops shrinking and a target success rate is met. RetireOPD achieved 14.1% to 18.8% higher ALFWorld success and 11.8% to 19.0% higher WebShop accuracy over RL baselines, consistently outperforming its own skill-conditioned teacher.

Agile-WAM: An Agile Tactile World Action Model for Contact-Rich Robot Control

arXiv

Hanchu Zhou, Brendan Lynch, Raman Goyal, Dechen Gao, Begum Kasap, Boqi Zhao, Junshan Zhang

AI Summary

Existing tactile World Action Models (WAMs) for contact-rich robot control are inefficient and inflexible due to reliance on large-scale generative backbones. Agile-WAM (Agile-WAM) addresses this by encoding visual and tactile data into a shared latent space, utilizing a direct flow-matching process to jointly generate action chunks and future visual/tactile latents. It employs multi-horizon multimodal prediction, supervising visual latents at a larger temporal offset and tactile latents in the next frame to account for their differing timescales. This method achieves superior success rates (29.4% relative gain in real-world tasks) with low inference latency (11.9 ms), enabling agile and precise high-frequency robot control.

OPTED: On-Policy Fine-Tuning for End-to-End Driving using a Render-Free Teacher

arXiv

Damiano Da Col, Maximilian Igl, Peter Karkus, Kashyap Chitta, Boris Ivanovic, Marco Pavone, Konrad Schindler, Christos Sakaridis

AI Summary

End-to-end driving policies, pre-trained via behavior cloning, suffer from compounding errors and distribution shifts during closed-loop deployment, while direct RL post-training for sensor-based models is prohibitively expensive due to rendering requirements. OPTED proposes decoupling reinforcement learning by training a privileged, render-free teacher with RL on vectorized inputs. This teacher then provides supervision to the pre-trained sensor-based student during closed-loop post-training. This method significantly improved driving scores by up to 9.5x and achieved comparable closed-loop performance to direct RL with approximately three orders of magnitude fewer simulator interactions, better retaining the human prior.

arXiv

AI Summary

Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for RL agents typically supervises only action tokens, neglecting environment observations and potentially hindering downstream reinforcement learning. MoWAM: Explicit Future Motion Prediction for Efficient World Action Models

arXiv

Jiayu Wang, Bin Zhu, Yue Yu, Jingjing Chen

AI Summary

World Action Models (WAMs) suffer from high inference-time computational overhead due to explicit future video generation or reduced robustness when future dynamics are only implicitly encoded. MoWAM proposes to replace future video generation with explicit future *motion* prediction, using a Mixture-of-Transformer to jointly learn future visual dynamics and predict motion and action, enabling efficient inference without generating full videos. This compact motion representation further allows efficient inference-time scaling by sampling and verifying multiple motion/action candidates. MoWAM achieves strong in-distribution performance, improved out-of-distribution robustness, higher real-world success, and efficient performance scaling compared to WAM baselines.

ACL

Pranay Meshram, Charuvahan Adhivarahan, Prithvi Poddar, Ehsan Tarkesh Esfahani, Chen Wang, Souma Chowdhury, Karthik Dantu

AI Summary

Repeated, efficient path and mission planning for UGVs in dynamic, large-scale off-road environments is challenging due to the computational cost of pixel-grid search and the fragility of semantic abstractions. HOPHY (Hierarchical Off-Road Planning using Hypergraphs) addresses this with a reusable hierarchical terrain representation. FunArt: Decoding Functional Structure and Articulation from Generative 3D Latents

arXiv

Dennis Rotondi, Abdelrhman Werby, Kai O. Arras

AI Summary

Robots require identifying articulated objects, segmenting their movable and functional parts, and estimating kinematics from static scenes, a challenge current methods face by either requiring interaction or decoupling these elements. FunArt proposes a framework that constructs articulation-aware functional 3D scene graphs from static RGB-D observations, leveraging a generative 3D VAE (TRELLIS.2's O-Voxel) as a structural prior and a query-based decoder to jointly segment parts and estimate full kinematic models. arXiv

Haozhe Lei, Ruibin Chen, Yuhan Jiang, Ali Rasteh, Aditya Dhananjay, Sundeep Rangan

AI Summary

Robots need robust joint transmitter position and heading localization in cluttered environments using directional upper-mid-band signals, often facing multipath ambiguity requiring full posterior distributions. MAGNETAR addresses this by inferring a joint posterior over planar transmitter position and heading from a single multipath RF snapshot (AoA/SNR) using a shared 2D U-Net neural scorer, conditioned on candidate heading and room layout. Learning Foresight without Explicit Trajectories for 3D Diffusion Policies

arXiv

Zhongbo Zhang, Zaibin Zhang, Yifan Wang, Changbo Yan, Lijun Wang, Huchuan Lu

AI Summary

3D diffusion policies struggle with foresight, implicitly learning rather than explicitly anticipating interaction progression, which is crucial for successful manipulation. The proposed Movement Trend Guidance (MTG) addresses this by learning a compact latent representation of interaction evolution from observation history, supervised by sparse future gripper states, which then conditions action generation. MTG significantly improves performance over baseline DP3, achieving 62.8% vs. 56.1% on RoboTwin2.0, 71.93% vs. 37.08% on LIBERO-40, and 72.0% vs. 49.0% on real-robot tasks, demonstrating the benefit of foresight without explicit trajectories.
