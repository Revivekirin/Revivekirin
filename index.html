Hierarchical Denoising For Multi-Step Visual Reasoning

arXiv

Zezhong Qian, Xiaowei Chi, Chak-Wing Mak, Tianze Zhou, Ruibin Yuan, Yuhan Rui, Hengzhe Sun, Zhuoqun Wu, Yuming Li, Siyuan Qian, Sirui Han, Shanghang Zhang

AI Summary

Video models lack human-like multi-step reasoning, struggling with logical consistency and low-latency streaming. This paper proposes HDR (Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning), a unified framework that integrates tree-structured hierarchical latents for coarse-to-fine reasoning with a sparse attention pattern into causal video generation. HDR significantly improves multi-step reasoning success (34.22% to 60.29%) and average progress, while achieving 54.2 times faster inference than bidirectional diffusion and maintaining low-latency streaming. It also demonstrates superior data efficiency and real-world robot performance.

RoboTTT: Context Scaling for Robot Policies

arXiv

Yunfan Jiang, Yevgen Chebotar, Ruijie Zheng, Fengyuan Hu, Yunhao Ge, Jimmy Wu, Tianyuan Dai, Scott Reed, Li Fei-Fei, Yuke Zhu, Linxi "Jim" Fan

AI Summary

Existing robot foundation models are limited by short visuomotor context. RoboTTT addresses this by scaling visuomotor context to 8K timesteps, integrating Test-Time Training with fast weights as recurrent state for long-context conditioning without increasing inference latency. This enables new capabilities like one-shot imitation and on-the-fly policy improvement, achieving an 87% performance boost over baselines and successfully completing complex, multi-stage tasks previously unsolvable. The work demonstrates that context length is a significant new scaling axis for robot foundation models.

MeanFlowNFT: Bringing Forward-Process RL to Average-Velocity Generators

arXiv

Yushi Huang, Xiangxin Zhou, Jun Zhang, Liefeng Bo, Tianyu Pang

AI Summary

The core problem is adapting efficient forward-process reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks, designed for instantaneous velocities, to MeanFlow generators which sample with average velocities. MeanFlowNFT is proposed, which bridges this gap by using the MeanFlow identity to construct an induced instantaneous-velocity predictor, allowing the DiffusionNFT objective to be applied while preserving MeanFlow's fast average-velocity sampling. MeanFlowNFT consistently improves baselines, significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art RL-tuned few-step generators, and can even surpass multi-step RL-tuned diffusion models with fewer sampling steps.

Relativistic time-commutative dynamics with $κ$-plane noncommutativity

arXiv

Alessandro Moia, Stefano Stocchetti, Giovanni Amelino-Camelia

AI Summary

The physical significance of spacetime noncommutativity and the associated deformed relativistic symmetries are poorly understood, with existing first-quantized models lacking full descriptions. This paper develops the first fully consistent, relativistic first-quantized model of two interacting particles on a time-commutative $κ$-plane noncommutative spacetime. It fully characterizes the deformed Poincaré and Galilean symmetry algebras and constructs a single-particle quantum model. The study provides two consistent Galilean-relativistic descriptions for two particles interacting via a deformed harmonic potential, revealing an intimate connection between the two-particle symmetry generators and the interaction law's deformation.

arXiv

AI Summary

BadWAM: When World-Action Models Dream Right but Act Wrong

arXiv

Qi Li, Xingyi Yang, Xinchao Wang

AI Summary

World-action models (WAMs), assumed robust due to coupling action with future world prediction, are shown to be fragile against "World-Action Drift Attacks" where imagined futures decouple from executed actions. The paper introduces BadWAM, a unified framework for modeling these WAM-specific adversarial attacks via small visual perturbations, instantiated as either action-only (disruptive) or imagination-preserving (stealthy) attacks. Evaluations reveal BadWAM substantially reduces task success rates (e.g., 96.5% to 43.1%), demonstrating that harmful actions can be induced even while the WAM maintains a seemingly plausible, imagined future.

Mask-Aware Policy Gradients for Diffusion Language Models

arXiv

Haran Raajesh, Kulin Shah, Adam Klivans, Philipp Krähenbühl

AI Summary

Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) is challenging due to intractable log-likelihood estimation and existing methods neglecting the unmasking order. This paper formalizes MDLM generation as a two-stage action Markov Decision Process (MDP), decomposing the policy gradient into distinct token prediction and masking terms. Jointly optimizing both terms achieves state-of-the-art results on mathematical reasoning (GSM8K: 87.1%) and coding (MBPP: 53.4%) benchmarks.

SciPhy Reinforcement Learning for Portfolio Optimization

ACL

Igor Halperin, Andrey Itkin

AI Summary

This paper addresses dynamic portfolio optimization for large institutional investors by introducing Scientific Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (SciPhyRL). The method reduces the continuous-time optimization problem to solving a pathwise Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation directly from offline data using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN), also recasting control as discrete target holdings. Evaluated on a 14-asset ETF universe, the learned Gibbs policy achieved substantial out-of-sample Sharpe ratio improvements over static and myopic baselines. This demonstrates a robust, multi-period, and cost-aware allocation mechanism with controlled volatility and turnover.

Can We Trust Item Response Theory for AI Evaluation?

arXiv

Han Jiang, Sunbeom Kwon, Jinwen Luo, Ziang Xiao, Susu Zhang

AI Summary

AI benchmarks increasingly employ Item Response Theory (IRT), but its reliability is challenged by significant data regime mismatches compared to human testing, including fewer models, more items, and non-normal capability distributions. To investigate, the study simulates response matrices from six LLM benchmarks under common IRT models, comparing four estimation tools across 18,000 conditions for feasibility, scalability, and inference reliability. Key results show classical estimators are infeasible in large benchmarks, while scalable estimators yield unreliable item-level and ranking inferences with small or non-normally distributed model sets. This identifies critical conditions for trustworthy IRT application in AI evaluation.

arXiv

AI Summary

On-Policy Delta Distillation

arXiv

Byeongho Heo, Jaehui Hwang, Sangdoo Yun, Dongyoon Han

AI Summary

On-policy distillation (OPD) for transferring reasoning capabilities is limited by its fundamental design of directly imitating the teacher's output distribution. This paper proposes On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD$^2$), which introduces a "delta signal" as a distillation reward, defined as the difference between the teacher model and its pre-instruction tuned base model. This signal directly captures changes induced by reasoning tuning. Experiments show OPD$^2$ substantially and consistently outperforms conventional OPD across mathematics, science, and code-reasoning benchmarks, achieving strong reasoning LLM performance with reduced post-training.
