Patch Policy: Efficient Embodied Control via Dense Visual Representations

arXiv

Gaoyue Zhou, Zichen Jeff Cui, Ada Langford, Bowen Tan, Yann LeCun, Lerrel Pinto

AI Summary

The core problem is that powerful dense visual features from pre-trained Vision Transformers (ViTs) are underutilized in robot learning due to methods either losing spatial detail or incurring high computational costs. Patch Policy proposes a minimal architectural extension allowing transformer-based policies to directly consume dense pre-trained patch tokens efficiently, employing a block-causal attention mask. This approach achieves a 40% relative improvement over state-of-the-art global-pooled representations and outperforms fine-tuned OpenVLA-OFT by 18% with only 0.7% of its parameters across simulated and real-world environments.

FM-VLA: Force-based Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models in Contact-Rich Manipulation

arXiv

Ruicheng Li, Qixiu Li, Ruichun Ma, Yu Deng, Lin Luo, Zhiying Du, Jianfeng Xiang, Huizhi Liang, Ruicheng Wang, Jiaolong Yang, Baining Guo

AI Summary

Vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle with non-Markovian, contact-rich manipulation due to limitations and computational expense of vision-based memory for visually ambiguous temporal events. FM-VLA proposes a force-based memory where force histories are encoded into compact tokens using a VAE and used to condition the VLA action expert. This lightweight force memory achieves over 80% success rate on memory-dependent tasks with minimal inference overhead, significantly outperforming baselines.

arXiv

Juraj Gavura, Igor Farkaš

AI Summary

Learning Adaptive Safety Margins for Visual Navigation

arXiv

Junyi Hu, Shuaihang Yuan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Anthony Tzes, Yi Fang

AI Summary

Robots in cluttered indoor spaces often fail due to mis-calibrated, fixed safety margins causing either conservative detours or risky shortcuts, despite generating collision-free paths. This paper proposes a context-conditioned safety critic to learn adaptive clearance preferences for ranking diffusion-based trajectory proposals, decomposed into safety, efficiency, and distance-constraint matching terms. The critic is trained with privileged ESDF geometry in simulation and distilled into a perception-only selector. The method achieves the highest success rate and SPL on PointGoal navigation benchmarks and successfully transfers to real-world robots without task-specific tuning.

Sharp Asymptotics for Regularized Optimal Transport

arXiv

Carlos Cardoso-Perelló, Alberto González-Sanz, Marcel Nutz

AI Summary

This paper investigates the small-regularization limit for $L^p$-regularized and entropically regularized optimal transport. It proposes a novel, unified approach that separates local optimal profile computation (via convex duality yielding Gaussian or Barenblatt profiles) from global marginal constraint enforcement (via quantization). The method explicitly determines exact first and second-order asymptotics under mild assumptions, generalizing existing results and demonstrating a natural interpolation between quadratic and entropic regularization via $p \in (1,2)$.

World Translation: Minimizing Sim-to-Real Gap with Backward Dynamics Extraction and Unpaired Domain Translation

arXiv

Xinchen Yao, Leixin Chang, Hua Chen

AI Summary

The sim-to-real gap is often widened by real-to-sim methods that struggle with partial observability, where unobservable factors like sudden contacts make observation history insufficient for accurate dynamics modeling. To address this, "World Translation" proposes extracting unobservable dynamics information *backward* from an observed transition, then translating this feature across simulation and reality as an unpaired domain-translation problem. This method leverages the complementary strengths of simulators and learned models, achieving more accurate dynamics modeling—especially when unobservable factors are critical—and demonstrates improved policy transfer in real-robot deployment.

arXiv

Enhancing Rubric-based RL via Self-Distillation

arXiv

Mingxuan Xia, Yuhang Yang, Chao Ye, Shuai Zhu, Shenzhi Yang, Guangcheng Zhu, Yuhang Zhang, Cheng Peng, Haobo Wang, Siqing Wang

AI Summary

Rubric-based RL for LLMs struggles with limited exploration (Unexplored Criteria, UC) and lost learning signals for satisfied criteria (Suppressed Criteria, SC) due to scalar reward aggregation, while existing UC solutions introduce a train-inference mismatch. To address both UC and SC without this mismatch, the paper proposes Criterion-Distilled Policy Optimization (CriPO), an on-policy self-distillation method. CriPO employs a criterion-injection self-teacher for UC to inject missing behaviors via forward-KL loss and a counterfactual self-teacher for SC to preserve useful patterns by flipping token-level advantages in negative-advantage rollouts. Experiments demonstrate CriPO consistently outperforms rubric-based RL, achieving stronger performance in approximately half the optimization steps.
