QF3: Fast Flow RL with Filtered Q-Gradients

arXiv

Chung Min Kim, Brent Yi, David McAllister, Hongsuk Choi, Himanshu Gaurav Singh, Jinkun Cao, Ken Goldberg, Pieter Abbeel, Carmelo Sferrazza, Angjoo Kanazawa

AI Summary

The core problem is the need for faster, off-policy reinforcement learning to train or refine robot flow policies, which are common for behavior learning from demonstrations. The proposed method, QF3 (Fast Flow RL with Filtered Q-Gradients), is an online off-policy algorithm that combines flow matching with a critic's action gradient, selectively applied only to reliable action dimensions near replay actions. QF3 is the first off-policy flow RL method to train humanoid locomotion from scratch with zero-shot hardware transfer, achieving a 10x wall-clock speedup over on-policy alternatives and successfully fine-tuning pre-trained manipulation policies.

PEARS: Physical-Prior-Guided Efficient Adaptation via Failure Reasoning and Diffusion Steering for Tactile Manipulation

arXiv

Kun Song, Yiming Wang, Yilin Chen, Tianyi Ding, Jiaxin Tian, Tianqi Gong, Daolin Ma, Jia Pan

AI Summary

Pretrained robotic policies suffer performance degradation in out-of-distribution conditions, necessitating real-world adaptation which is typically sample-inefficient for RL. PEARS proposes a physics-prior-guided hybrid RL framework that combines a Physics-guided Force Reasoning (PFR) module, using VLM-encoded priors to update contact-force bounds, with tactile-conditioned diffusion steering to adjust a frozen policy's latent noise for free-space and contact timing correction. This method significantly improves success rates by 12.4-37.4 percentage points in simulation and reduces adaptation episodes by up to 53.2%, achieving 90-95% success in real-world manipulation tasks.

IdeaAnchor: Teaching LLMs to Turn Literature into Research Ideas

arXiv

Ziyu Chen, Yilun Zhao, Jiashuo Sun, Yiling Ma, Manasi Patwardhan, Arman Cohan

AI Summary

Training LLMs for literature-grounded research ideation is challenging due to the lack of structured supervision on how to synthesize papers. IdeaAnchor is proposed, a paradigm that trains LLMs using structured specifications, mined from published papers, encoding functional roles and relationships for idea synthesis. Models are trained via demonstration, self-distillation, and reinforcement learning, enhanced by retrieval at inference. DepthWorld: 3D World Model for Robot Manipulation

arXiv

Jai Bardhan, Josef Sivic, Vladimir Petrik

AI Summary

Current video-based world models for robotics lack consistent 3D geometry crucial for manipulation due to being trained solely on RGB. This paper introduces a calibration pipeline to generate DROID-3D, a large-scale 3D dataset providing dense metric depth and precise multi-view extrinsics. Leveraging this, they train DepthWorld, a Stable Video Diffusion-based model that jointly predicts multi-view RGB and depth via spatial latent tiling. Sherpa: Teaching LLMs to Teach Adaptively

arXiv

Weixian Xu, Yanzhe Zhang, Zora Zhiruo Wang, Changyu Chen, Diyi Yang

AI Summary

Large language models struggle to teach adaptively because current training methods lack grounding in individual student learning outcomes and cannot adapt to diverse learning preferences. Sherpa is introduced as a multi-turn reinforcement learning framework that trains a teacher LLM to adapt its instruction by directly maximizing the learning outcomes of distinct LLM-conditioned student archetypes. This method improved instructed students' performance by an average of 20.5 percentage points, raised the pedagogy score on MathTutorBench from 52.5% to 79.2%, and was preferred over base models in 79.6% of human evaluations.

arXiv

Dimitrios Nikou, Nikolaos Kekatos, Sophia Petridou, Stylianos Basagiannis

AI Summary

Traditional integrity attestation for time-critical autonomous actions treats evidence as a binary predicate, ignoring its age, decision latency, and physical system deadlines, leading to ambiguous refusals. LBA-CBF: Rapidly Adaptive Safety Filters via Parallel Dynamics Inference

ACL

Maitham F. AL-Sunni, Timeea-Andreea Radu, Hassan Almubarak, Henry Z. Liao, Michael Görner, Francesco Maurelli, John M. Dolan

AI Summary

Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) are vulnerable to unmeasured, abrupt system changes because they rely on fixed dynamics models, compromising safety. The proposed Look-Back Adaptive Control Barrier Functions (LBA-CBF) mitigate this by ranking a finite bank of candidate nonlinear dynamics models based on recent prediction error and enforcing the CBF condition against all models within a tolerance of the best. LBA-CBF achieved 100% safety and goal attainment in quadrotor simulations and real-world Crazyflie/F1TENTH experiments (adapting to wind, payload, friction), outperforming adaptive and robust baselines (0-88% success) with large model banks running inside the control loop.

arXiv

AI Summary

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing point-to-CAD reconstruction methods often fail to accurately reconstruct intricate primitive structures due to their general point-cloud encoding and token prediction approach, neglecting CAD-specific primitive tokenization. PrimitiveCAD addresses this with a multi-stage LLM-based paradigm, introducing primitive-aware point cloud tokenization, supervised finetuning with an operation alignment loss, and reinforcement learning with a feature-line alignment reward. NMPP: Nonlinear Model Predictive Planning for Agile UAV Flight in Cluttered Environments

ACL

Ondřej Procházka, Juraj Pauko, Robert Pěnička, Martin Saska

AI Summary

The core problem is planning dynamically feasible, collision-free, and agile quadrotor trajectories in cluttered environments, where existing methods either limit agility or treat obstacles as soft costs. The proposed method is Nonlinear Model Predictive Planning (NMPP), which imposes perceived obstacles as hard geometric constraints to generate a full-state reference for an obstacle-blind SE(3) controller. NMPP achieves 58-67% lower position RMSE than linear MPC and 41-70% lower than polynomial planners. It also ensures 100% collision-free flights at 9.5 m/s and an 86% success rate under aggressive speed profiles, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art planners in simulation and real-world deployment.

Selective Transfer of RL Updates for Visual Reasoning

arXiv

Suxin Ji, Hungtao Wan, Mingjun Liu, An Zhang

AI Summary

Current model merging for transferring reasoning capabilities to VLMs suffers from conflating pre-existing differences and suboptimal full RL update transfer due to varying component transferability. The proposed Selective-RL method isolates the RL-stage update, identifies and retains its dominant matrix-wise directions with magnitude preservation, and transfers them to VLM language modules. Selective-RL outperforms full-update interpolation in 12 of 15 comparisons across three model families and five benchmarks, achieving an 8.55 percentage-point MathVision gain on Qwen, demonstrating that selective transfer of dominant update directions is crucial. Matched controls confirm these gains are not reproducible by arbitrary low rank or update magnitude alone.

RIWANav: Recursive World-Action Models with Self-Improvement for Urban Navigation

arXiv

Jing Xie, Shouwei Ruan, Yubin Wang, Yuxiang Zhang, Haitao Yang, Songchang Jin, Dianxi Shi

AI Summary

Long-horizon urban navigation suffers from compounding errors and the limitations of current learning methods, exacerbated by world models becoming unreliable as policies evolve. RIWANAV proposes a recursive self-improvement (RSI) framework that alternately updates a world model and an action model (policy). The world model provides imagined feedback for policy optimization, and the improved policy then constructs a self-curriculum to refine the world model. This approach outperforms training baselines and prior methods, validating the recursive self-improvement loop and demonstrating practical applicability in real-world urban navigation.
