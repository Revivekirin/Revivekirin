ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics

arXiv

Bingxin Xu, Wenrui Bao, Xinxin Liu, Jiawei Zhou, Yuzhang Shang, Emilio Ferrara

AI Summary

Agentic robotics is inefficient due to redundant LLM reasoning for every primitive action, even when sub-goal primitives are routine. ChunkAct addresses this by compiling memory into autonomous action chunks offline, enabling a single LLM call to execute a chunk online and reserving the planner for high-level decisions. This method improves task success by 5.0 points on LIBERO-Pro while significantly reducing LLM calls (2.9x), input tokens (4.4x), and output tokens (2.3x).

LoopWAM: Closed-Loop Generalist Policy Improvement from Deployment Rollouts

arXiv

Wenxuan Guo, Yingping Liang, Zhaoguo Wang, Ziyuan Li, Angyuan Ma, Zhenyu Wu, Chengkun Wang, Bingyao Yu, Ze Chen, Borui Zhang, Xiuwei Xu, Meng Zhang, Erjin Zhou, Jiwen Lu

AI Summary

Generalist robot policies struggle to effectively utilize mixed-quality deployment rollouts, as standard imitation reinforces suboptimal behavior while filtering out failures discards critical physical interaction data. To address this, the authors propose **LoopWAM**, a closed-loop world-action model that learns across the full quality spectrum by coupling parallel video-action denoising with source-dependent noise sampling and quality-guided supervision to regulate action imitation. Across major benchmarks, LoopWAM achieves state-of-the-art performance, notably improving success from 23.67% to 43.33% on RoboChallenge Table30-V2 and boosting complex shirt-folding success from 13.20% to 55.56% through iterative deployment rounds.

EgoExplorer: A Vision-Motion-Touch Observatory for Human Loco-Manipulation In Situ

arXiv

Yunsong Zhou, Xuekun Jiang, Haiqi Shen, Hangxu Liu, Chaojian Shi, Yujie Liu, Jingyuan Li, Haoran Yang, Jiafei Cao, Dongzhan Zhou, Dong Wang, Yao Mu, Chunhua Shen, Weinan Zhang

AI Summary

Existing datasets for humanoid loco-manipulation lack comprehensive, coordinated observations of locomotion, posture, and object interaction, hindering learning from human demonstrations. To address this, the authors introduce EgoExplorer, a 100-hour vision-motion-touch dataset captured by a wearable system, and develop the Calibrate-Complete-Constrain pipeline to accurately reconstruct whole-body motion from sparse sensing. This pipeline reduces body-point error by 71.9% compared to baselines, improves hand stability and contact estimation, and facilitates preliminary studies in multimodal generation and humanoid retargeting.

Cepheid: Decoupling Scale from Structure for Cross-Family Motion Completion

arXiv

Chih-Hai Su, Tsung-Wei Ke, Yu-Lun Liu

AI Summary

Sparse-to-dense mesh motion completion struggles to generalize across diverse geometry families because residual motion amplitude varies by orders of magnitude and cannot be directly measured at test time. To overcome this scaling barrier, Cepheid decouples motion structure from physical scale by learning a dimensionless residual prior and predicting amplitude from observable signals with a lightweight correction. The unified model consistently outperforms harmonic extension baselines across diverse unseen families—such as cloth and legged bodies without requiring skeletons or correspondences—and successfully transfers zero-shot to real-world animal video tracking and robotic soft-body manipulation.

What Matters in Quality-Aware VLA Pretraining: A Benchmark and Empirical Study

arXiv

Mingxuan Yan, Litian Gong, Dihong Huang, Zhixu Li, Tianyu Zhang, Wenqian Zhang, Hefeifei Jiang, Peihao Li, Jian Zhang, Mengfei Zhao, Yikai Tang, Hai Zhai, Zehao Wang, Ruijian Liang, Yanjia Huang, Lin Shao, Changliu Liu, Kaiyu Hang, Zhiwen Fan, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Jianfei Yang, Jiachen Li

AI Summary

The core problem is determining how to effectively utilize demonstration quality during vision-language-action (VLA) pretraining to improve performance after target-task fine-tuning. The proposed method compares weighting, filtering, and conditioning approaches across reward definitions and label granularities (episode/chunk) using 5,000 suboptimal human demonstrations, 24 target tasks, and three VLAs, introducing AXIS-Bench for controlled experiments. Key results show approach-aware episode conditioning improves mean success over plain behavioral cloning; conditioning favors episode labels while filtering favors chunk labels (with model-dependent reversals); and constant-tag fine-tuning enhances performance, with simulation pretraining boosting real-world success from 9.6% to 31.7%.

EmbodiedEvo: Self-improving Coding Agents for Physical Intelligence

arXiv

Wenyi Li, Yizhe Chi, Youjie Zheng, Chunyu Liu, Xiaoqiu Wang, Mingju Gao, Zheng Jiang, Deyao Hong, Calvin Xiao, Kaisen Yang, Qinhuai Na

AI Summary

While coding agents provide an explicit and reusable policy representation for physical intelligence, their physical execution remains prone to recurring failures without persistent experience retention. To resolve this, the authors propose **EmbodiedEvo**, a framework where an agent iteratively evolves reusable "Skills" and "Lessons" via feedback in a single playground scene before freezing the resulting harness to tackle novel scenes. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves a state-of-the-art 37.82% success rate, effectively transfers experience to boost weaker executor models, and reduces execution costs by 53.0% relative to raw interaction history.

AutoAgent0: An Agentic Runtime for Safe Closed-Loop Driving

arXiv

Jiageng Liu, Seth Z. Zhao, Zewei Zhou, Alejandro Huerta, Aidan Bayer-Calvert, Yuxin Bao, Haotian Lin, Robert Spataru, Zijun Cui, Yining Hong, Bolei Zhou, Jiaqi Ma

AI Summary

End-to-end autonomous driving policies struggle with accumulated closed-loop errors and lack reliable recovery mechanisms in unseen, safety-critical scenarios. To address this, the authors introduce AutoAgent0, a training-free agentic runtime framework that augments existing driving experts with independent perception, rule-based trajectory verification, and iterative, feedback-driven recovery planning. Evaluated on the Fail2Drive and NavSafe-$\infty$ benchmarks, AutoAgent0 significantly improves closed-loop robustness and safety across multiple driving policies in long-tail driving conditions.

AesCode: Aesthetic Code Generation with Decoupled Cross-Modal Rewards

arXiv

Xin Gao, Yifan Yang, Xin Li, Pan Wang, Yan Li, Muzhao Tian, Mingxi Cheng, Bei Liu, Kai Qiu, Qi Dai, Dong Chen, Yue Dong, Leandros Tassiulas, Rex Ying, Ji Li, Chong Luo

AI Summary

Code generation models struggle with layout and aesthetics when rendering visual artifacts, while image generators excel visually but fail to reliably ground requested content. To address this, AesCode guides code generation using prompt-derived reference images and optimizes a design graph through reinforcement learning with decoupled cross-modal rewards, combining deterministic code verifiers with a visual-language model aesthetic judge. Consequently, AesCode-8B and AesCode-32B improve visual quality by 31.1 and 22.4 percentage points over comparable baselines, achieving visual performance competitive with frontier models like GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8.

Visual Tokens on Demand: Visual Budgeting for Scalable and Efficient Multi-Image Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs

arXiv

Aimon Rahman, Joymallya Chakraborty, Vidit Bansal, Abhishek Dan

AI Summary

Multi-image reasoning in multimodal LLMs is computationally expensive due to the massive influx of visual tokens, yet existing token-reduction methods fail to effectively address cross-image redundancy and often require base model retraining or inference-inefficient attention matrix access. To solve this, the authors introduce mmVB, a lightweight (1.6M parameters), plug-and-play module that dynamically assigns query-conditioned token budgets across images without modifying the base model. Evaluated across eight multi-image benchmarks, mmVB reduces visual tokens by 9×, yielding a 5× faster time-to-first-token and 7× fewer prefill FLOPs while retaining 94.4% of full-resolution accuracy.
