SimWAM: A Simple World Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

arXiv

Zongchuang Zhao, Xin Zhou, Tianyang Xu, Zhengyang Sun, Kaixuan Zhou, Honglin Li, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai

AI Summary

Existing World-Action Models (WAMs) for end-to-end autonomous driving are computationally expensive at inference due to requiring future video generation. SimWAM addresses this by using video generation solely as a training signal, co-training a pretrained video expert and a lightweight action expert with joint flow matching, enabling the video branch to be discarded post-training for efficient inference. This allows for a self-contained planner that directly predicts trajectories and can be further optimized with reinforcement learning. SimWAM achieves 91.5 PDMS on NAVSIM, surpasses state-of-the-art WAMs with substantially lower latency, and demonstrates zero-shot transfer to nuScenes.

SemBridge: Semantic Token Anchoring for Continuous-Latent Autoregressive Speech Generation

arXiv

Hanke Xie, Haopeng Lin, Jiale Qian, Dake Guo, Yuepeng Jiang, Zhichao Wang, Wenxiao Cao, Jingbin Hu, Guobin Ma, Wenhao Li, Huakang Chen, Chengyou Wang, Ming Tao, Zhonghua Fu, Lei Xie, Xinsheng Wang

AI Summary

Continuous-latent autoregressive speech generation suffers from compromised content fidelity because its continuous acoustic targets lack explicit linguistic structure, forcing the autoregressive language model to infer it indirectly. SemBridge addresses this by using discrete semantic tokens to directly supervise autoregressive LM states during training, coupled with a Semantic-Aligned Acoustic VAE, without affecting the continuous inference process. This approach significantly improves content accuracy (lower WER/CER) in zero-shot text-to-speech and score-conditioned singing voice synthesis, while preserving speaker similarity and perceptual quality.

Active movement of foraging sea turtles generates anomalous looping

arXiv

Vijay Kumar, Vladimir V. Palyulin, Perla Roman-Torres, Christophe Eizaguirre, Rainer Klages

AI Summary

Existing mathematical models often fail to explain complex, apparently random animal movement patterns that exhibit long-term non-Markovian memory. To address this, the authors constructed a stochastic generalised Langevin equation using satellite tracking data from foraging loggerhead sea turtles. They found that turtles engage in active movement characterized by large-scale loops, independent of ocean currents, which maintain foraging within a specific region and generate superdiffusion similar to Lévy walks, identifying a novel loop-based anomalous search strategy.

A quantum geometric mechanism for chiral domain wall metastability: Application to twisted transition-metal dichalcogenides

arXiv

Nisarg Chadha, Qiang Gao, Eslam Khalaf, Zhaoyu Han

AI Summary

The paper addresses the existence of exotic excitations in ferromagnets with conjugate Chern bands, where a net charge response is absent. It proposes a quantum geometric mechanism where domain walls (DWs) with in-plane winding can bind a transverse dipole density, quantified by a geometric dipole coefficient ($c_G$), without net charge. This dipole decoration renders the DWs metastable. The theory explains recent experiments in twisted MoTe$_2$ where long-lived excitations disappear upon topological transition (a sharp drop in $c_G$), serving as a sensitive probe of band quantum geometry.

arXiv

AI Summary

Trajectory-Relative Hindsight Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Haoyu Zheng, Yun Zhu, Qing Wang, Wenqiao Zhang

AI Summary

Agentic reinforcement learning struggles with optimally allocating multiple hindsight signals across decision turns within a completed rollout. This paper introduces TRIAL, a trajectory-relative hindsight distillation framework, which uses a turn-aligned scoring protocol based on the log-probability gap between ordinary and hindsight-conditioned contexts to determine token-level supervision and normalize turn-level magnitudes across the trajectory for dense supervision redistribution. Depth-Wise Probing and Pruning of the Planning Token in a Driving Vision-Language-Action Model

arXiv

Harisankar Babu, Benjamin Coors, Christopher Lang, Hendrik Berkemeyer, Tamim Asfour, Simon Foell

AI Summary

The core problem addresses the unknown depth requirement for the "planning token" carrying driving decisions in deep vision-language-action (VLA) models. The study's method involves probing a driving VLA's single planning token at each of its 32 decoder layers, measuring semantic command decodability and trajectory compatibility with the native planner. Key results show that high-level semantic intent is decodable very early, but optimal trajectory generation for the native planner requires deeper layers. Nevertheless, pruning 8 decoder layers, based on planning token deviation, yields a 1.33x speedup with an approximate 5% open-loop error increase.

Token Communication for Multimodal Large Language Model

arXiv

Jingkai Ying, Zhijin Qin, Yuan Shen, Khaled B. Letaief

AI Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of reducing data transmission for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) while maintaining their understanding performance, given the increasing importance of efficient token communication. It proposes a token communication framework that integrates a neural codec into the vision tokenizer to control bit rates, where decoded latents are converted into visual tokens for MLLM injection, alongside an image reconstruction path. The method employs a two-stage visual-language semantic alignment training scheme and an adaptive adapter for multiple codec rates. Extensive simulations confirm that this scheme achieves superior MLLM task performance compared to other methods under the same transmitted data amount.
