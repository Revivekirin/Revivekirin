ATLAS: Agentic or Latent Visual Reasoning? One Word is Enough for Both

arXiv

Ziyu Guo, Rain Liu, Xinyan Chen, Pheng-Ann Heng

AI Summary

Existing visual reasoning methods are hampered by high computational costs, context-switching latency, or poor generalization and training difficulties. ATLAS proposes using a single discrete "functional token" as both an agentic operation and a latent visual reasoning unit, generated via next-token prediction without explicit visual supervision. To stabilize the training of these sparse tokens, it introduces Latent-Anchored GRPO (LA-GRPO). This framework achieves superior performance on challenging benchmarks while maintaining clear interpretability.

RAVEN: Real-time Autoregressive Video Extrapolation with Consistency-model GRPO

arXiv

Yanzuo Lu, Ronglai Zuo, Jiankang Deng

AI Summary

Causal autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from quality degradation over long horizons due to a mismatch between training and inference history distributions. To address this, the Real-time Autoregressive Video Extrapolation Network (RAVEN) introduces a training-time framework that realigns training attention with inference extrapolation. Complementing this, Consistency-model Group Relative Policy Optimization (CM-GRPO) applies online Reinforcement Learning directly to consistency sampling steps. Experiments show RAVEN surpasses baselines in quality, semantics, and dynamics, with CM-GRPO providing additional gains.

arXiv

AI Summary

Hand-in-the-Loop: Improving Dexterous VLA via Seamless Interventional Correction

arXiv

Zhuohang Li, Liqun Huang, Wei Xu, Zhengming Zhu, Nie Lin, Xiao Ma, Xinjun Sheng, Ruoshi Wen

AI Summary

VLA models for dexterous manipulation are prone to compounding errors, exacerbated by "gesture jumps" during human intervention via Interactive Imitation Learning due to command mismatches. The paper proposes Hand-in-the-Loop (HandITL), a seamless human-in-the-loop intervention method that blends human corrective intent with autonomous policy execution to prevent these jumps. Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, Zhuowen Han, Zi-Han Wang, Jinyang Wu, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Yongliang Shen

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning provides coarse supervision for LLM agents, and existing self-distillation methods struggle with multi-turn instability and asymmetric handling of teacher rejections. SDAR (Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning) addresses this by treating On-Policy Self-Distillation as a gated auxiliary objective, using a sigmoid gate to strengthen distillation on positive teacher-endorsed tokens and softly attenuate negative rejections. This method substantially improves performance over GRPO and hybrid RL-OPSD baselines across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA (e.g., +9.4% on ALFWorld), avoiding instability and outperforming prior methods across model scales.

Widening the Gap: Exploiting LLM Quantization via Outlier Injection

arXiv

Xiaohua Zhan, Kazuki Egashira, Robin Staab, Mark Vero, Martin Vechev

AI Summary

Existing quantization-conditioned attacks, which make full-precision LLMs malicious only upon user quantization, fail against popular, sophisticated quantization schemes. This paper proposes the first such attack effective against advanced methods (AWQ, GPTQ, GGUF I-quants) by exploiting how large outliers in these schemes can cause other weights to round to zero, enabling targeted weight collapse through outlier injection. The attack achieves high success rates across various advanced quantization techniques, demonstrating that the security risks of quantization are broad and not limited to simpler schemes.

Extensive long-range magic in non-Abelian topological orders

arXiv

Yuzhen Zhang, Isaac H. Kim, Yimu Bao, Sagar Vijay

AI Summary

Low-energy states of non-Abelian topological orders exhibit extensive, long-range "magic" that refines conventional complexity notions and cannot be removed by constant-depth local unitary circuits. The authors establish this via a no-go theorem demonstrating that stabilizer states cannot approximate low-energy states of non-Abelian string-net models satisfying entanglement bootstrap axioms. This "magic" is also implied by violations of specific braiding phase quantization in Abelian phases, and the concept extends to higher-dimensional quantum double models.

Forgetting That Sticks: Quantization-Permanent Unlearning via Circuit Attribution

arXiv

Saisab Sadhu, Pratinav Seth, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu

AI Summary

Machine unlearning methods are systematically reversed by post-training quantization (PTQ) because their subtle parameter updates are too small to clear quantization bin boundaries, leading to unlearning reversal or ineffective change. The proposed method, MANSU (Mechanistic-Aligned Null-Space Unlearning), resolves this by combining causal circuit attribution to isolate the minimal "forget-set," circuit-restricted null-space projection with a retain bound, and a per-parameter magnitude floor ensuring quantization survival. MANSU is the first method to jointly achieve meaningful forgetting, retain preservation, non-positive PTQ gap, and structural erasure, outperforming baselines whose unlearning recovers under compression.

CoCo-InEKF: State Estimation with Learned Contact Covariances in Dynamic, Contact-Rich Scenarios

arXiv

Michael Baumgartner, David Müller, Agon Serifi, Ruben Grandia, Espen Knoop, Markus Gross, Moritz Bächer

AI Summary

Robust state estimation for dynamic, contact-rich legged robot motion is challenging due to traditional methods' reliance on insufficient binary contact states. This paper proposes CoCo-InEKF, an invariant extended Kalman filter that uses learned continuous contact velocity covariances, rather than binary states, to dynamically modulate contact confidence for nuanced contact conditions. A lightweight neural network predicts these covariances end-to-end using a state-error loss, eliminating heuristic contact labels. Experiments on a bipedal robot demonstrate superior accuracy-efficiency for linear velocity estimation and improved filter consistency, enabling robust execution of complex real-world motions.

CLOVER: Closed-Loop Value Estimation \& Ranking for End-to-End Autonomous Driving Planning

arXiv

Sining Ang, Yuguang Yang, Canyu Chen, Yan Wang

AI Summary

End-to-end autonomous driving planners suffer from a training-evaluation mismatch, as they imitate single logged trajectories but are evaluated by distinct rule-based planning metrics. CLOVER proposes a Closed-Loop Value Estimation and Ranking framework, employing a generator to produce diverse candidate trajectories and a scorer to rank them by predicting planning-metric sub-scores. It mitigates the mismatch by training the generator with evaluator-filtered pseudo-expert trajectories and refines it through conservative closed-loop self-distillation, where the scorer learns true evaluator sub-scores and the generator targets scorer-selected top proposals. CLOVER achieves new state-of-the-art on NAVSIM (94.5 PDMS, 90.4 EPDMS) and matches top performance on NavHard, additionally demonstrating the lowest L2 error and collision rate on nuScenes.

Fast contracted Clebsch--Gordan tensor products for equivariant graph neural networks

arXiv

Anton Bochkarev, Yury Lysogorskiy, Ralf Drautz

AI Summary

The core problem is the computational expense and recovery of antisymmetric parity-odd channels when evaluating contracted Clebsch--Gordan tensor products in $\mathrm{O}(3)$-equivariant machine learning potentials. An $\mathcal{O}(L^3)$ algorithm is proposed, which maps angular integrals to a structured tensor-product grid to decouple radial and angular computations, and recovers parity-odd channels via a rotationally-equivariant surface-curl pairing (spherical Poisson bracket). This method achieves an empirical $L^2$ wall-clock scaling for practical $l_{\max}$ (transitioning to $L^3$ for large $l_{\max}$ on-site, and remaining $L^2$ for message passing due to memory bandwidth), effectively addressing these bottlenecks.

On the Cultural Anachronism and Temporal Reasoning in Vision Language Models

arXiv

Mukul Ranjan, Prince Jha, Khushboo Kumari, Zhiqiang Shen

AI Summary

Vision-Language Models suffer from cultural anachronism, misinterpreting historical artifacts using temporally inappropriate concepts. To quantify this, the Temporal Anachronism Benchmark for Vision-Language Models (TAB-VLM) was introduced, comprising 600 questions on 1,600 Indian cultural artifacts.
