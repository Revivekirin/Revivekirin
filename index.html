Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding

arXiv

Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang, Shengyi Qian, Shang-Wen Li, Mike Lewis, Marjan Ghazvininejad, Luke Zettlemoyer, Lucas Beyer, Mengye Ren, Jakob Verbeek, Saining Xie, Hu Xu

AI Summary

The optimal approach for multimodal language models to encode visual input, specifically regarding visual priors, dedicated capacity, and placement, remains unclear due to confounded comparisons. This paper disentangles these factors via a controlled framework, pretraining models from scratch on common data with matched compute to isolate each design choice. They discover that a visual prior is unnecessary, but dedicated vision capacity is crucial for visual understanding. Furthermore, this capacity can be effectively placed *in-fusion* within early backbone layers, performing comparably to pre-fusion encoders and potentially reducing latency.

ProtoVis-Nav: Prototypical Visual Imagination for Visual-Spatial Aligned UAV Navigation

arXiv

Guangyuan Sui, Yunzhong Hou, Chi Harold Liu

AI Summary

Existing UAV vision-language navigation (VLN) models struggle with accurate 3D motion prediction and waypoint generation due to an inability to imagine execution outcomes, leading to navigation errors. To address this, ProtoVis-Nav introduces a Prototypical Visual Imagination framework that forces the model to imagine an outcome-associated local visual representation for the intended next UAV movement. This is achieved by predicting feature prototypes and linearly combining them to estimate the visual feature of the target region, which then guides action generation. The framework promotes strong visual context-waypoint alignment and demonstrates substantial improvements over existing baselines.

Internalizing Investment Mandates: A Neurosymbolic RL Approach to Portfolio Allocation

arXiv

Zeynep Yıldız, Satchit Chatterji, Ercan Atam, Erman Acar

AI Summary

Deep reinforcement learning for portfolio allocation struggles to enforce complex, continuous-action investment mandates specified by institutional policy statements, hindering real-world deployment. This paper proposes a neurosymbolic RL method that estimates the probability of a continuous action satisfying the investment mandate via reparameterized Monte Carlo sampling, adding this to the training objective for internal compliance learning. The approach reduces regime-conditional mandate violations from 96-98% for unconstrained RL and 33.4% for a reward-penalty baseline to just 4.8%, effectively internalizing symbolic constraints.

Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards

NeurIPS

Jiacheng Xu, Shuo He, Fuxiang Zhang, Chaojie Wang, Bo An

AI Summary

Standard Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is unreliable under one-sided verifiability (OSV), where only failures are reliably verifiable, leading to rewarding untrustworthy "verified positives" and incorrect shortcuts. To address this, the paper proposes Negative-Only Policy Optimization (NOPO), which treats verified positives as unlabeled and updates the policy solely by suppressing trusted verified negatives via a reference-relative loss and a group-level adapter. NOPO consistently improved pass@k metrics over baselines across code, logic, and math benchmarks, demonstrating robustness under inference-time scaling and cross-benchmark transfer.

Global Fit Inference for LISA with samsara++

arXiv

Lorenzo Valbusa Dall'Armi, Gabriele Astorino, Riccardo Buscicchio, Angelo Ricciardone, Walter Del Pozzo

AI Summary

LISA observations require disentangling a superposition of an unknown number of overlapping gravitational-wave sources and instrumental noise, a trans-dimensional Global Fit problem. arXiv

Billur Kaymakçalan, Behiye İlayda Selçuk

AI Summary

The paper addresses formulating Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron dynamics on arbitrary Hilger time scales while preserving its fundamental structure and passive membrane physics, which direct transcriptions fail to do. It proposes using the inverse cylinder transformation to construct a positively regressive coefficient, ensuring the delta-dynamic equation is an exact restriction of the classical continuous-time LIF trajectory. The de Haas--van Alphen effect in a two-dimensional nematic Fermi fluid

arXiv

Luís E. Leite Macêdo, Daniel G. Barci

AI Summary

The study addresses the complex interplay between quantum oscillations and spontaneous Fermi surface deformation from nematic electronic order. Using a uniform mean-field approximation in a 2D interacting electron model, it demonstrates that Landau quantization can favor or suppress nematic order, creating alternating isotropic and nematic regions. Conversely, the evolving nematic order modifies de Haas--van Alphen oscillations: the frequency can change significantly at fixed chemical potential, while at fixed particle density, the leading Onsager frequency is preserved, but cyclotron mass and harmonic amplitudes remain sensitive. These findings establish that quantum oscillations both reveal and influence electronic order.

Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning

arXiv

Benjamin A. Christie, Tianyu Qiu, Amy H. Choi, David Fridovich-Keil, Dylan P. Losey

AI Summary

Robots learning from demonstrations struggle with robustness to state perturbations and distribution shifts. Prior regularization methods consider states individually, but this paper proposes Inner Product Regularization (InneR) by enforcing that local policy responses to perturbations are consistent across nearby states. InneR achieves this by shaping the policy's state-space inner product to share a common null space among clustered states. This method enhances robustness to irrelevant state dimensions and domain shifts, significantly improving success rates on simulated and real-world robot tasks compared to standard imitation learning and norm-based regularizers.

Reentrant Nematicity from Landau Quantization near a Pomeranchuk Instability

arXiv

Luís E. Leite Macêdo, Daniel G. Barci

AI Summary

This paper investigates how Landau quantization induces reentrant nematic order in an isotropic two-dimensional Fermi liquid. Using a mean-field theory near a quadrupolar Pomeranchuk instability, the authors show that varying magnetic fields produce alternating nematic and isotropic regions with both continuous and first-order transitions. The resulting nematic state leaves distinct de Haas--van Alphen oscillation signatures: fixed chemical potential yields substantial frequency changes, while fixed particle density preserves the leading frequency but alters cyclotron mass and harmonic amplitudes. These contrasting behaviors provide key experimental signatures of field-induced nematicity.
