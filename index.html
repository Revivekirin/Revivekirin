CAPEval: A Decoupled Caption Evaluation across Understanding and Generation

arXiv

Zhipeng Liu, Haochen Wang, Zhaoxiang Zhang

AI Summary

Previous caption evaluations conflate a caption's coverage of visual information with the factual correctness of its claims, hindering fine-grained diagnosis. This paper introduces CAPEval, a decoupled benchmark assessing captions based on Coverage (completeness relative to ground truth) and Precision (factual correctness of claims), utilizing human-verified data. Empirically, CAPEval reveals a task-dependent dissociation: Coverage strongly correlates with understanding performance, while Precision is the dominant predictor for text-to-image generation performance. This provides actionable guidance for optimizing captioners for specific downstream tasks.

UEmbed: Unified Sparse and Dense Multimodal Embeddings

arXiv

Tingyu Song, Mingxin Li, Yanzhao Zhang, Dingkun Long, Pengjun Xie, Zhijie Nie, Yilun Zhao, Shu Wu

AI Summary

Learned Sparse Retrieval (LSR) is limited by encoder-style architectures and requires auxiliary modules for multimodal extensions. UEmbed proposes a decoder-only multimodal embedding model that produces both sparse lexical and dense representations in one causal forward pass by using special tokens to predict sparse weights over partitioned vocabulary subsets. UEmbed-9B achieves 71.8 (dense) and 71.0 (sparse) on MMEB-v2, outperforming other publicly trained multimodal embedding models, and remains competitive with strong dense/sparse baselines on BEIR, showcasing practical utility and efficiency.

AI Summary

CoWAM: Coordination Contracts for Selective Policy Intervention with WAMs

ACL

Shuaijun Liu, Qifu Wen, Shuyang Hao, Qi Luo, Chenglong Zhang, Feiyang You, Chengyu Wu, Ningxin Su

AI Summary

World Action Models (WAMs) lack a principled mechanism for selectively intervening in bimanual robot policies to improve coordination, as plausible predicted futures alone don't justify action changes. CoWAM is proposed as a selective intervention layer that defines synchronization, role compatibility, and collision convergence as coordination contracts, using admissibility checks and calibrated intervention gates to preserve nominal actions unless alternatives offer clear, low-risk improvements. CoWAM improved coordination-valid selection by 16.7 percentage points and closed-loop success by 9.6 percentage points over strong baselines in simulated bimanual tasks, while maintaining harmful interventions below 1%.

Pairwise-Independent Dithering for Single-Stage Hadamard Quantization

arXiv

Honghao Lin, Vahab Mirrokni, David P. Woodruff

AI Summary

The core problem addresses the inefficiency of a two-stage dithered quantizer for high-dimensional vectors, which uses a second randomized transform and residual quantization, increasing communication and the error constant. The proposed method introduces a single-stage unbiased estimator that uses pairwise-independent dithers across Hadamard coordinates, eliminating the need for the extra transform and residual quantization. This approach reduces the leading upper-bound constant by approximately 5.93 and eliminates the $O(d)$-bit payload associated with the residual stage, achieving an improved error bound.

House-monotone multi-level apportionment has logarithmic quota discrepancy

arXiv

Lav R. Varshney

AI Summary

The paper addresses whether a single house-monotone rule can simultaneously satisfy lower and upper quotas in multi-level apportionment, even when quota is relative to the root. Using a full binary comb structure, the authors analyze the discrepancy induced by house-monotone allocation sequences, leveraging existing logarithmic lower bounds and constructing a van der Corput schedule for an upper bound. They prove that for sufficiently large systems, no such house-monotone quota rule exists, demonstrating an optimal worst-case discrepancy of $\Theta(\log D)$. This highlights a "static–dynamic gap" where individually quota-feasible allocations cannot be embedded into a single monotone path.

Benchmarking Sheaf Neural Networks for Inductive Tasks

arXiv

Stefano Fiorini, Edoardo Coppola, Pietro Liò

AI Summary

Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) lack systematic evaluation on inductive tasks, despite their promising transductive performance. This work provides the first comprehensive benchmark of SNNs for inductive settings, exploring various sheaf design choices (diffusion, restriction maps, stalk dimensions) and modern GNN architectural components using a message-passing reformulation. Key results show restriction maps are the dominant sheaf design choice, but surrounding architectural components explain more performance variation than the sheaf operator itself. While SNNs transfer to inductive tasks, they generally do not outperform the strongest baselines, suggesting tuning architectural recipes is more impactful than the sheaf operator.

Why Does Action Chunking Improve Behavioral Cloning Performance in Robotic Control?

arXiv

Filippo Lazzati, Kyle Stachowicz, William Chen, Alberto Maria Metelli, Andrew Wagenmaker, Sergey Levine

AI Summary

The paper addresses the lack of understanding of why action chunking improves robotic control, showing that existing hypotheses (temporal consistency, horizon reduction, representation learning) are insufficient. It proposes that action chunking benefits from greater non-Markovian expressivity, reduced compounding error, and a novel "implicit ensembling" effect by learning diverse temporal relationships. Building on this, the authors demonstrate that action chunking performance can be matched without chunking using an ensemble of delayed policies. Furthermore, they introduce a new explicit ensemble policy class that significantly improves upon action chunking in both simulated and real-world settings.

Probabilistic Reachable-Action Verification of Visuomotor Policies via Set-Based Training

arXiv

Yanliang Huang, Zhuocheng Zhang, Peng Xie, Zhen Zhang, Wenyuan Wu, Majid Khadiv, Zhuoqi Zeng, Amr Alanwar

AI Summary

Reachability analysis for visuomotor policies is challenging due to the computational expense and conservativeness of end-to-end set propagation through large visual encoders. The proposed method freezes the visual encoder, confining set propagation to a low-dimensional, calibrated interface between the encoder and policy, using zonotopes with set-based training to optimize the terminal output-enclosure width. During evaluation, a probabilistic reachable-action radius with finite-sample coverage is determined via rollout-level split conformal calibration. This set-based training successfully reduces the reachable-action radius while preserving closed-loop task capability, significantly outperforming other control methods.

arXiv

AI Summary

Twinned crystals obscure intrinsic spin dynamics by introducing replica features from domains, making it challenging to reveal intrinsic magnetic excitation spectra in quantum magnets. This study proposes a multimodal optical approach, combining magneto-infrared spectroscopy with domain-resolved micro-Raman spectroscopy, to isolate single-domain magnon responses. Analytic Planning under Uncertainty with Moment Closure

arXiv

Shishir Sharma, Doina Precup

AI Summary

Model-based reinforcement learning requires effective planning under predictive uncertainty, but current methods suffer from high target variance (sampling) or ignore covariance (point estimates). This paper proposes a compatibility principle between a Gaussian transition model and a radial-basis value function, which, combined with a quadratic action-value parameterization, yields a closed-form Bellman backup that analytically propagates predictive mean and covariance. RoMeRL: Balancing Feedback Coverage and the Memory-Reward Trap in Self-Evolving Agent Memory via Reduced-Order Utility States

arXiv

Yi Yang, Zhennan Chen, Yihong Zhuang, Tiehan Fan, Yinan Chen, Jian Li, Jian Yang, Ying Tai

AI Summary

Self-evolving LLM agent memory systems struggle with feedback dispersion across ever-expanding utility spaces and the memory-reward trap, where irrelevant experiences receive misleading utility updates. RoMeRL addresses this by representing the growing trajectory-indexed utility space with fixed-dimensional, reduced-order utility states that factorize by outcome and dynamics, concentrating feedback over a bounded support via updated semantic coordinates. Theoretically, this increases average feedback and characterizes the occupancy of erroneous coordinates, while empirically, RoMeRL significantly improves task performance, reduces memory size and LLM calls, and increases feedback density across ALFWorld and LifelongAgentBench.

arXiv

Zhaokai Yin, Zhipeng Zhang

AI Summary

EchoCache: Energy-Guided Cross-Modal Caching for Efficient Audio-Driven Video Generation

arXiv

Jiayu Chen, Xiaoyu Wu, Rongshan Gao, Maoliang Li, Zihao Zheng, Xinhao Sun, Hailong Zou, Guojie Luo, Xiang Chen

AI Summary

Audio-driven video generation (A2V) using diffusion models faces high inference costs, as existing caching methods overlook crucial cross-modal alignment and the non-uniform temporal importance of audio, leading to temporal-semantic and computation-storage misalignments. To address this, EchoCache is proposed, an energy-guided cross-modal caching framework that leverages audio time-frequency energy to guide latent-level cache updates and employs a dynamic timestep-latent caching mechanism with quantized cache management.
