Ask, Solve, Generate: Self-Evolving Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation via Self-Consistency Rewards

arXiv

Ritesh Thawkar, Shravan Venkatraman, Omkar Thawakar, Abdelrahman Shaker, Fahad Khan, Hisham Cholakkal, Salman Khan, Rao Muhammad Anwer

AI Summary

Unified large multimodal models (LMMs) currently rely on extensive curated supervision for improving both visual understanding and image generation abilities. This paper proposes "Ask, Solve, Generate" (ASG), a self-evolving training framework where a Proposer, Solver, and Generator autonomously improve via self-derived consistency signals from unlabeled images, eliminating human annotations or external reward models. It introduces Solver Token Entropy (STE) for stable learning and a multi-scale internal evaluation for generation, creating a solver-mediated coupling. The method consistently improves base models across eight understanding metrics and, on BAGEL, achieves a +3.5% absolute gain on MMMU and boosts GenEval image generation from 82% to 85%.

World Action Models Enable Continual Imitation Learning with Recurrent Generative Replays

arXiv

Manish Kumar Govind, Dominick Reilly, Smit Patel, Hieu Le, Srijan Das

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is catastrophic forgetting in continual imitation learning, specifically avoiding the storage of original human demonstrations for replay. The proposed method, Recurrent Generative Replay (REGEN), leverages World Action Models (WAMs) to synthesize pseudo-replay trajectories conditioned on prior task instructions and current observations. Experiments show REGEN reduces catastrophic forgetting by up to 50% relative to sequential fine-tuning, approaching the performance of methods requiring real replay data. Identified limitations include long-horizon visual degradation and action-observation inconsistency.

Paying More Attention to Visual Tokens in Self-Evolving Large Multimodal Models

arXiv

Shravan Venkatraman, Ritesh Thawkar, Omkar Thawakar, Rao Muhammad Anwer, Hisham Cholakkal, Salman Khan, Fahad Khan

AI Summary

Existing self-evolving LMMs suffer from "visual under-conditioning," where their decoders rely on language priors rather than visual content, manifesting as insufficient attention to visual tokens and hindering performance on vision-language tasks. To address this, VISE (Visual Invariance Self-Evolution) proposes an unsupervised framework that directly regularizes the model's visual conditioning policy using geometric and semantic invariance-based rewards. Operating within a single model on raw unlabeled images, VISE achieves significant gains, including +16.85 CIDEr on COCO and +19.66 CIDEr on TextCaps, and reduces object hallucination by 5.0 Chair-I points across 18 benchmarks.

Reinforcement Learning without Ground-Truth Solutions can Improve LLMs

arXiv

Yingyu Lin, Qiyue Gao, Nikki Lijing Kuang, Xunpeng Huang, Kun Zhou, Tongtong Liang, Zhewei Yao, Yi-An Ma, Yuxiong He

AI Summary

Reinforcement learning for LLMs typically requires ground-truth answers, limiting its applicability to score-based optimization tasks lacking such solutions. The proposed RiVER (Ranking-induced VERifiable) framework uses deterministic execution feedback as continuous supervision, employing calibrated reward shaping with instance-wise comparisons to mitigate "scale dominance" and "frequency dominance" when learning from continuous scores. RiVER significantly improves LLMs' performance on score-based tasks (e.g., ~9% ALE rating rank) and, crucially, also transfers these improvements to exact-solution benchmarks (2.4-3.5% on LiveCodeBench/USACO) despite not training on them, unlike baselines. This demonstrates that properly calibrated score-based training can enhance general coding ability without ground-truth supervision.

RouterVLA: Turning Smoke Tests into Supervision for Heterogeneous VLA Selection

arXiv

Xingyu Ren, Chugang Yi, Ge Ma, Youran Sun

AI Summary

RouterVLA addresses the problem of reusing pre-deployment evaluation rollouts (smoke tests) to supervise selection among heterogeneous vision-language-action (VLA) policies for robot teams. The proposed method utilizes outcome-disjoint cross-fitting, where probe trials build expert profiles separately from the scoring trials to ensure credible performance measurement. Key results show this approach increased held-out success by +14.64pp (from 0.4686 to 0.6149) across 34,752 records, demonstrating that commissioning carries routing value. The study also found that reusing scored trials inflates measured gains by 1.87x, underscoring the necessity of outcome separation for reliable ledger routing.

Continual Robot Policy Learning via Variational Neural Dynamics

arXiv

Jiaxu Xing, Zhiyuan Zhu, Yunfan Ren, Ismail Geles, Yifan Zhai, Rudolf Reiter, Davide Scaramuzza

AI Summary

Robots deployed in the real world struggle to continually adapt to unknown and recurring dynamics caused by environmental or internal changes. This paper proposes a continual learning framework that learns a condition-aware dynamics model by combining a physics prior with a neural residual. A recurrent encoder infers the current hidden condition online, which then parameterizes both the learned residual model and the robot policy. The method improves policy performance under diverse unobserved disturbances, achieving 5x faster recovery (approx. 1s) from recurring disturbances and reducing large-disturbance errors by 53-65% compared to state-of-the-art online adaptation.

On the deformation theory of chiral quantizations

arXiv

Dylan Butson, Sujay Nair

AI Summary

This paper addresses the core problem of deformation quantization for vertex Poisson algebras, a chiral analogue of traditional Poisson algebras, by proposing an operadic approach. The key result is an order-by-order deformation-obstruction theory for these quantizations, controlled by the chiral analogue of Poisson cohomology. In specific cases, such as chiral quantizations of affine symplectic varieties, this theory is shown to be controlled by de Rham cohomology, and the boundary Virasoro minimal models are proven to be rigid under deformations.

Hallucination in World Models is Predictable and Preventable

arXiv

Nicklas Hansen, Xiaolong Wang

AI Summary

Generative world models frequently hallucinate, producing visually fluent but dynamically incorrect futures, primarily due to low-coverage regions in the state-action space. The authors introduce MMBench2, identify three distinct hallucination modes, and develop predictive signals, which are then used in a coverage-aware sampling technique for training and as curiosity rewards for data-efficient finetuning. Key results show hallucination is inherently a data coverage issue, and the developed signals accurately predict and effectively mitigate failures, allowing adaptation to new environments with minimal data.

Generative Models on Analog Hardware with Dynamics

arXiv

Yu-Neng Wang, Sara Achour

AI Summary

Modern generative models rely on flexible software-defined dynamics, creating a fundamental mismatch with analog hardware which imposes fixed, physics-determined differential equations. To address this, the authors propose Analog Interaction Systems (AIS), a framework that bridges this expressivity gap using time-varying piecewise parameters, hidden physical states, and a trajectory-free Wasserstein GAN training procedure. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, the oscillator-based AIS outperforms prior analog generative models by 3-4x in FID score while achieving a 2-orders-of-magnitude energy reduction (estimating 23uJ per image) compared to digital baselines using a practical 4-bit sparse architecture.
