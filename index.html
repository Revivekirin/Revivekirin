MemoryWAM: Efficient World Action Modeling with Persistent Memory

arXiv

Sizhe Yang, Juncheng Mu, Tianming Wei, Chenhao Lu, Xiaofan Li, Linning Xu, Zhengrong Xue, Zhecheng Yuan, Dahua Lin, Jiangmiao Pang, Huazhe Xu

AI Summary

Existing World Action Models (WAMs) for robotic manipulation struggle to efficiently incorporate long-term memory needed for non-Markovian environments without incurring substantial computational costs. To address this, MemoryWAM is proposed, utilizing a hybrid memory design that combines recent frames, event-boundary anchor frames, and compact gist tokens to summarize long-range history. A tailored attention mechanism efficiently retrieves both detailed short-term and compressed long-term context, enabling memory-dependent decision-making with reduced latency and GPU usage. MemoryWAM outperforms strong VLA and WAM baselines on long-horizon, memory-dependent manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, while maintaining favorable computational efficiency.

Generating Robot Hands from Human Demonstrations

arXiv

Sha Yi, Nicklas Hansen, Xueqian Bai, Carmelo Sferrazza, Michael T. Tolley, Xiaolong Wang

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the difficulty of jointly optimizing robot body design and control, particularly for robot hands, due to the vast combinatorial search space. The proposed method is a data-driven framework that generates tree-structured robot hands by optimizing their design to reproduce desired fingertip motions from over 4 million frames of human demonstrations, using a simple inverse kinematics control policy. An RL actor is employed to significantly accelerate this design search. Key results include the generation of a 6-DoF general-purpose hand achieving teleoperated fingertip tracking superior to commercial hands, and lower-DoF task-specific hands with reduced mechanical complexity, demonstrating the utility of human motion data for robot embodiment optimization.

The Token Is a Group Element: On Lie-Algebra Attention over Matrix Lie Groups

arXiv

Przemyslaw Musialski

AI Summary

Traditional attention mechanisms struggle to intrinsically handle matrix Lie group elements, particularly non-compact and non-abelian affine groups, often relying on complex representation theory or breaking invariance. This paper introduces "Lie-Algebra Attention," where tokens are bare matrix Lie group elements and the attention score is derived directly from the closed-form algebra norm of the relative pose's logarithm. This method inherently ensures equivariance and applies to affine groups previously unreachable by other attention approaches. Experiments demonstrate that this intrinsic score matches or outperforms learned kernels with 50-80x fewer parameters, while maintaining invariance where vector-token baselines significantly fail.

Execution-State Capsules: Graph-Bound Execution-State Checkpoint and Restore for Low-Latency, Small-Batch, On-Device Physical-AI Serving

arXiv

Liang Su

AI Summary

The core problem is that mainstream LLM serving, relying on KV caches, inefficiently manages only a fragment of execution state, making it unsuitable for low-latency, small-batch, on-device physical-AI that requires frequent, complete state management. This paper proposes "execution-state capsules," a graph-bound checkpoint and restore mechanism that captures the *entire* restorable execution state, including KV, recurrent, and other states, via a custom kernel runtime called FlashRT. This approach enables sub-millisecond GPU-resident snapshot and restore, yielding a Time to First Token (TTFT) speedup over cold prefill from 3.9x at 2k tokens to 27x at 16k tokens, demonstrating the necessity of full state capture.

StylisticBias: A Few Human Visual Cues Drive Most Social Biases in MLLMs

ICML

Shaghayegh Kolli, Timo Cavelius, Nafiseh Nikeghbal, Samantha Dalal, Jana Diesner

AI Summary

The core problem is the poor understanding of specific visual cues that drive social biases in MLLMs, with prior methods failing to isolate appearance effects from identity. The proposed method is StylisticBias, a controlled benchmark of 25K images systematically generated by varying single visual attributes on 500 fixed identities. Key results show that fashion style and other cues cause significant attribute-level shifts, with approximately 15 attributes accounting for 80% of total bias, especially in appearance-aligned judgments like socioeconomic status.

HumanScale: Egocentric Human Video Can Outperform Real-Robot Data for Embodied Pretraining

arXiv

Juncheng Ma, Jianxin Bi, Yufan Deng, Xuanran Zhai, Kewei Zhang, Ye Huang, Bo Liang, Shukai Gong, Jiankai Tu, Xiaotian Tang, Jiaxin Li, Kaiqi Chen, Duomin Wang, Yuqi Wang, Bingyi Kang, Eric Huang, Zhiyang Dou, Zhen Dong, Enze Xie, Wojciech Matusik, Tat-Seng Chua, Daquan Zhou

AI Summary

Embodied foundation models face a severe data bottleneck due to the high cost and limited diversity of traditional teleoperated robot data, leaving the effectiveness of scalable egocentric human video for pretraining underexplored. This study systematically compared pretraining with carefully filtered and labeled egocentric human video against real-robot trajectories. Surprisingly, egocentric data not only served as a viable substitute but led to superior performance, achieving a 24% lower validation loss and significantly higher success rates (52.5% in-distribution, 90% out-of-distribution) on real-robot tasks. This validates a scalable paradigm: pretrain on diverse egocentric human video then adapt with small amounts of real-robot data.

CLUSTER: Derivative-free optimization of smooth functions with parameter-change costs

arXiv

Serena Landers, Sahil Pontula, Shiekh Zia Uddin, Sachin Vaidya, Marin Soljačić, Steven G. Johnson

AI Summary

This paper addresses derivative-free optimization problems where changing parameters incurs distinct costs, common in robot-controlled experiments. It introduces the CLUSTER algorithm, an extension of Powell and Conn's quadratic-interpolation methods, specifically designed to optimize under such cost models. CLUSTER variants achieved roughly 50% performance improvement on diverse test problems, including a real-world optics experiment, significantly surpassing Bayesian optimization and Nelder-Mead, and offer similar convergence guarantees.
