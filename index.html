Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success

arXiv

Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

AI Summary

Robotic foundation models require expensive closed-loop evaluations, necessitating cheaper offline proxies for capability. This paper proposes Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric measuring how well a model's sensory representation aligns with physical motion geometry, extending the Platonic Representation Hypothesis. $S^2$ positively correlates with downstream robot task success, serving as a low-cost proxy for prioritizing models and identifying promising VLM backbones, while also offering representation-level explanations for embodied AI phenomena.

OpenTac: An Open Ecosystem for Customizable, Scalable, and Transferable 3D Tactile Sensing

arXiv

Ariel Slepyan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Jiafeng Gu, Anagha Rajendra Dangle, Yinzhe Zhou, Hongyuan Fu, Jade Sim, Tuhin Basu, Xinyi Yin, Md Faizal Karim, Gregory Hager, Homanga Bharadhwaj, Anand Bhattad, Krishna Murthy Jatavallabhula, Nitish V. Thakor

AI Summary

To overcome the limited customizability, scalability, and complex fabrication of 3D tactile sensors in robotics, the authors introduce OpenTac, an open-source ecosystem centered on a scalable resistive tactile skin that decouples normal and shear forces via a structured deformable overlay. The system features an automated CAD pipeline that generates fabrication files from 2D/3D geometries or text descriptions, supported by sensor simulation and high-speed adaptive data acquisition. Evaluated across over 30 fabricated designs and four real-world robot embodiments, OpenTac successfully enables contact-rich feedback control and policy learning, while its adaptive readout achieves kilohertz-rate sampling with up to a 19.2× speedup over full-array scanning.

BeNice! Closing Looping's Inference-Memory Gap via KV-Sharing

arXiv

Hayden Prairie, Jacob M. Ryan, Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick, Daniel Y. Fu

AI Summary

Looped architectures incur linear memory growth from independent KV-caches per recurrence, and existing KV-sharing methods lack consistent effectiveness. This paper introduces a two-stage method: stochastic depth during pretraining to align recurrence representations, followed by BeNice!, a post-training technique that broadcasts the final recurrence's KV-cache to enable sharing. BeNice! enables KV-shareability without quality loss, complements other KV-compression methods, and significantly improves cache hit rates, throughput, and time-to-first-token in long-context inference.

Questioning Instrumental Convergence in RLVR: Checkpoint Evidence from OLMo-3

arXiv

Barış Alparslan, Sandy Tanwisuth, Caleb Petterson

AI Summary

This study investigates whether reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) inherently induces dangerous instrumental convergence behaviors by tracking behavioral trajectories across intermediate training checkpoints of OLMo-3 models using the InstrumentalEval benchmark. The authors find that instrumental behavior follows a non-monotonic trajectory, surging from 25.0% at SFT to an early peak of 46.1% before steeply dropping to 11.8% at the final RLVR checkpoint. These findings, robust across prompt framing variations and supported by preliminary 32B model tests, challenge the assumption that RLVR progressively amplifies instrumental convergence, showing it can ultimately fall below pre-RLVR baselines.

arXiv

On the Token Collapse Phenomenon in Textual Graph Soft Prompting

arXiv

Andrea Giuseppe Di Francesco, Rishabh Jain, Franco Maria Nardini, Nicola Tonellotto, Pietro Liò, Fabrizio Silvestri

AI Summary

The paper identifies "token collapse" in graph soft prompting methods like G-Retriever, where learned graph tokens become almost indistinguishable across inputs and fail to convey specific graph content, effectively acting as generic soft prompts. This pathological behavior is widespread, evidenced by the fact that token swapping has no effect on accuracy and a constant prompt matches G-Retriever's performance. A two-stage procedure is proposed to yield non-collapsed tokens, but further analysis reveals that token variability alone does not confirm graph information usage, emphasizing the need for direct evaluation of graph-token alignment beyond task accuracy.

AI Summary

Existing Vision Language Action (VLA) policies face challenges in robustly recognizing task completion and failure, often requiring complex supervision or external models, and underutilizing status signals for action guidance. Echo proposes a framework that combines task status signal learning (stop/failure) without frame-level annotations with a compact design for joint status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Real-world manipulation experiments show Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

When to Follow the Teacher: Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation

arXiv

Mahdi Sabbaghi, George Pappas, Hamed Hassani, Adel Javanmard

AI Summary

Standard Online Policy Distillation (OPD) indiscriminately trains students to follow teachers, assuming all guidance is beneficial, which often isn't optimal for the current student. To address this, Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation (AOPD) is proposed, which selectively weights teacher actions by their counterfactual advantage under the student, reinforcing helpful guidance. AOPD efficiently estimates this objective using only standard student rollouts and terminal rewards via importance weighting, without additional computational overhead. Experiments show AOPD significantly outperforms distillation and RL baselines across math, coding, and Medqa tasks.

Same Luck, Different Choice: Twin-World Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv

Zeropy Surio, Tobias Engel, Mei-Lin Chou, Anika Raman, Samuel Okafor

AI Summary

Agentic reinforcement learning faces challenges with accurate credit assignment for individual decisions because standard counterfactuals mix decision effects with independent downstream sampling noise. This paper introduces TWICE (TWIn-world Credit Estimation), which exploits the deterministic components of agentic search to create "twin worlds" where counterfactual episodes inherit the factual trajectory's downstream noise, isolating the specific decision's impact. TWICE provides unbiased credit with reduced variance (scaled by $1-ρ$), accelerates advantage resolution from $Θ(Δ^{-2})$ to $Θ(Δ^{-1})$, and significantly improves exact match on question-answering benchmarks (e.g., 5.3 points on Qwen2.5-3B) while using 68% fewer auxiliary tokens than baselines.

Dispersive Tree Agreements for Multi-Robot Coordination

arXiv

Shashank A. Deshpande, Blake T. Edwards, Jonathan P. How

AI Summary

The core problem is achieving decentralized, real-time, near-optimal coordination for multi-robot nonlinear systems, where existing methods are either centralized/offline or decentralized but sub-optimal. The proposed WarpWeaver protocol extends single-robot dispersive tree search to multi-robot teams by replacing inter-robot coupling with local constraints, employing decentralized column generation and negotiation of shared space "rents" to find a certifiable near-optimal agreement.
