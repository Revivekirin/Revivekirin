Never Look Back: Understanding Persistence in 3D Object Memory from Egocentric Videos

arXiv

Shravan Chaudhari, William Paul, Suchi Saria, Rama Chellappa, Homanga Bharadhwaj

AI Summary

This paper addresses the core problem of enabling an embodied AI assistant to build a persistent 3D object memory from egocentric videos, recalling objects even after they leave view. It proposes Ledger, a system that combines object locations, histories, and contextual descriptions, associating observations across recordings and reducing noise by clustering observations by resting locations and requiring repeated evidence for moves. Ledger significantly improves object memory accuracy on HD-EPIC (29.7% to 42.6%) and UCS-Bench (33.8% to 38.5%), localizing Ego4D objects with a 0.99m median error, demonstrating the complementary roles of temporal persistence, contextual descriptions, and retrieval.

Decoupling Exploration from Optimization in RLVR

arXiv

Saif Punjwani, Micah Goldblum

AI Summary

Modern RLVR struggles to leverage novelty incentives for new reasoning strategies, often degrading model quality due to the narrow scope of verifiable rewards. The proposed Exploration-Distillation (ExpDis) framework decouples exploration from optimization: explorer policies are trained with novelty bonuses, their correct and quality trajectories are filtered, and then distilled into a separate student policy without novelty bonuses, iterating this process. This enables aggressive exploration without student degradation, outperforming DAPO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and yielding more diverse correct solutions.

RoboPrompt: Intuitive Robot Policy Steering with Sparse Human Input

arXiv

Yanwen Zou, Chenyang Shi, Guoxuan Xu, Wenye Yu, Wendi Chen, Ye Pan, Cewu Lu, Chuan Wen

AI Summary

End-to-end robot policies face challenges with generalization, and existing human guidance methods are either complex or require policy architectural modifications. RoboPrompt is a general-purpose system that allows intuitive, sparse human inputs (e.g., traces, target points) to steer robot policy behavior. It translates human guidance into action drafts, which are refined by the base policy's diffusion or flow-matching dynamics in noise space, without altering the underlying policy. This system effectively steers various policy types and, when used for online improvement via DAgger, significantly increases success rates (e.g., 21.3%) while drastically reducing human interventions (up to 81.9%).

Rephrase Before You Act: Characterizing and Mitigating Language Sensitivity in Vision-Language-Action Models

ACL

Mikey Watts, Yuchen Cui

AI Summary

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) suffer from extreme sensitivity to instruction phrasing, where minor wording changes drastically impact task success and they fail to inherit the language robustness of their vision-language model foundations. To address this without policy modification, the authors propose distilling a set of rephrasing rules from an LLM by scoring various phrasings of training tasks. At deployment, incoming instructions are rewritten once according to these rules. This method improves frozen VLA performance by 16-27% relative on held-out tasks, particularly for out-of-distribution scenarios, and applies zero-shot without retraining.

Trend formation with sparse global sampling

arXiv

Sarath Sankar, Abhijit Sinha, Shankar Ghosh, Vikram Tripathi, Amitava Bhattacharya

AI Summary

Achieving global coordination in swarms without central control or dense communication is a core problem. This paper proposes a minimal model where agents periodically reorient towards the centroid of a small, randomly sampled subset of peers. This sparse, non-local sampling reliably drives global alignment, with smaller sampled subsets (down to two agents) accelerating ordering due to estimation noise acting as the symmetry-breaking engine. The mechanism is supported by an analytical criterion and experimental validation with robots.

arXiv

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is the lack of a principled way to estimate how robotic latent world model capabilities scale with model size, data, and compute. The proposed method introduces RoboJEPA, a large-scale Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) model trained on a multi-embodiment robotic dataset. This work establishes the first scaling laws for multi-embodiment robotic world models.

CMP-IRRT*: A Perception-Assisted Height-Adaptive Planner for Quadruped Robots

ACL

Mingfan Zhao, Wendong Mao, Zhongfeng Wang

AI Summary

Existing 2D planning for quadruped robots models obstacles as binary occupied regions, ignoring their ability to traverse low obstacles, and relies on inefficient sampling-based search. This paper proposes CMP-IRRT*, a Channel Mamba PointNet-guided Informed RRT* planner, which uses a perception module to create a height map from RGB observations, enabling height-conditioned collision checking where low obstacles are traversable, and employs a Mamba-based guide to bias sampling. CMP-IRRT* reduces explored nodes and iterations compared to baselines, decreases path length by up to 16.3% in traversability-aware scenarios, and was demonstrated on a Unitree Go2 robot.

AI Summary

RFPO: Rectified Flow Policy Optimization for Embodied Control

arXiv

Ting Huang, Lisiyu Pan, Haoyu Wang, Zeyu Zhang, Siyuan Qian, Yanjun Li, Yandong Guo, Boxin Shi, Hao Tang

AI Summary

Flow-based policies for continuous robot control incur high inference costs from iterative ODE integration, leading to a "few-step discretization gap" where performance degrades under coarse integration. RFPO (Rectified Flow Policy Optimization) addresses this by introducing reward-aware online Reflow, which rectifies transport paths for robustness to coarse integration, alongside action-space supervision from a frozen Gaussian PPO controller. The deployed policy then uses a single flow student with one Euler step. This method consistently preserves full-step control performance, yielding returns within 2.4% of 64-step values and achieving a 54.9x speedup (e.g., 4.39 ms to 0.08 ms) in inference latency, validated by stable real-robot locomotion.

A Good Self-Teacher Meets the Student Where They Are: Joint On-Policy Learning and Teaching

arXiv

Randy Ardywibowo, Arnav Dalal, Jiantao Jiao

AI Summary

Reinforcement Learning (RL) self-distillation with privileged teachers can degrade student performance by providing mismatched supervision that exploits shortcuts unavailable to the student. To address this, the authors derive conditions for effective distillation and propose Joint On-Policy Learning and Teaching (JOLT). JOLT jointly trains a single policy in two roles: a privileged teacher using a KL-regularized objective towards the student, and an unprivileged student using dense on-policy distillation. JOLT significantly improves training efficiency and performance across diverse tasks, including mathematical reasoning, coding, and tool use.
