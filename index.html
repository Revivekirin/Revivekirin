KaliBench: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Cybersecurity Tool Use on Kali Linux with Runtime-Free Verifiable Rewards

NeurIPS

Pengfei Li, Naufal Suryanto, Sicheng Zhang, Muzammal Naseer

AI Summary

Existing LLM evaluations for cybersecurity lack direct measurement of their ability to generate *executable commands* for real-world tools, a critical gap due to strict CLI syntax. To address this, KaliBench is introduced as a fine-grained benchmark and dataset (8,504 query-command pairs for 1,642 tools) for natural-language-to-CLI translation on Kali Linux, featuring a multi-stage verification pipeline and enabling runtime-free verifiable rewards for training. Evaluations show that current open-weight models achieve less than 42% exact-command accuracy, but supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning with KaliBench's rewards significantly improve an 8B model, matching the performance of a 685B MoE model.

FERPO: Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization

arXiv

Sebastian Sanokowski, Alireza Sarmadi, Majid Khadiv

AI Summary

Online reinforcement learning often faces unreliable policy updates because critics, trained for return prediction, yield inaccurate action derivatives. FERPO proposes a novel on-policy maximum entropy algorithm that bypasses critic differentiation by deriving an optimal, entropy- and KL-regularized target action distribution. The actor is then fitted to this target using a forward-KL objective with self-normalized importance sampling, which encourages broad exploration of high-value actions. FERPO demonstrates competitive performance, sample-efficiency gains on various benchmarks, and faster actor updates compared to REPPO.

arXiv

AI Summary

InterEvolve: Test-Time Evolution of Reward Programs for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

arXiv

Zhuo Lin, Sirui Xu, Liuyu Bian, Yu-Xiong Wang, Liang-Yan Gui

AI Summary

This paper addresses adapting pre-trained humanoid loco-manipulation controllers to novel tasks at test-time without retraining, as human-designed rewards often leave competence untapped. InterEvolve proposes a method combining an object-aware forward-backward behavioral foundation model with test-time evolution of reward programs. AI Summary

From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

arXiv

Siqi Zhu, Suozhi Huang, Kaixuan Zhang, Yuheng Yang, Zhanyang Jin, Yihang Sun, Jiaxuan You

AI Summary

The core problem in Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) is understanding how teacher signals influence student parameter updates. Researchers investigated this using a Qwen3-1.7B student with four RL-trained domain teachers, analyzing gradients, optimizer updates, and learning curves. They found that loss averaging implicitly weights responses, Adam's first moment reduces parameter update differences, and BF16 rounding obscures minor weight changes. Crucially, the choice of averaging rule significantly impacts task performance.

Effective Resistance and Graph Neural Network Reliability in Tissue-Specific Interactomes

arXiv

Jianru Shen

AI Summary

Protein function annotation requires identifying unreliable GNN predictions, a problem explored by leveraging tissue-specific interactome structure. This study investigates effective resistance as a signal for GNN prediction reliability across 24 tissue-specific interactomes. While effective resistance strongly correlates with inverse degree, its residual component (beyond degree) significantly explains additional per-node GNN loss in most networks, with the effect strengthening monotonically with depth. However, direct selective prediction improvements are negligible, and degree degeneration bounds the signal's utility.

When Do Intrinsic Rewards Lead to Exploration?

arXiv

Scott W. Viteri, Laura Gomezjurado Gonzalez, Clark Barrett

AI Summary

Intrinsic rewards for RL exploration often fail to produce the most informative experiences. The paper proposes a new formal criterion for exploration based on "counterfactual information," assessing how well a policy's history can substitute for experiences under alternative policies. They show that common intrinsic rewards (e.g., count-based, prediction-error) are Pareto-suboptimal under this criterion in a simple environment, explain these failures, and develop a new objective aligned with their optimal exploration criterion.

arXiv

GlassGuard: Verified Glass Plane Mapping for Robot Navigation

ACL

Hanwen Guo, Zhengzhi Lin, Yusen Xie, Ji Zhang

AI Summary

LiDAR-based navigation struggles with transparent glass, as lasers pass through, leaving unmapped collision boundaries or prior reconstruction methods inaccurately block free space. GlassGuard proposes a framework to reconstruct planar architectural glass by combining vision model-derived glass masks, 3D cues for plane hypotheses, and 2D projective geometry for orientation verification, focusing on both glass coverage and minimizing free-space contamination. It achieves 85% glass coverage (82% for pinhole), significantly outperforming baselines (max 61%), and produces 5-17x fewer false voxels, enabling accurate path blocking through glass while preserving traversable space.
