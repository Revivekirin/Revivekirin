Gaze Heads: How VLMs Look at What They Describe

arXiv

Rohit Gandikota, David Bau

AI Summary

The core problem addressed is how vision-language models internally determine which image region to describe. The paper proposes identifying "gaze heads," a small set of attention heads in the VLM's language backbone whose attention tracks the image region currently being described, found using a simple correlation score from a few forward passes on comic strips. Key results show that redirecting these gaze heads' attention forces the VLM to describe the chosen region (e.g., 83.1% accuracy on comic panels with <9% of heads intervened), even allowing continuous control and extending to natural images. This mechanism recurs across various VLM architectures and sizes, demonstrating that mechanistic analysis provides practical, inference-time steering levers without retraining.

OmniVideo-100K: A Dataset for Audio-Visual Reasoning through Structured Scripts and Evidence Chains

arXiv

Xinyue Cai, Chaoyou Fu, Yi-Fan Zhang, Ran He, Caifeng Shan

AI Summary

Current audio-visual QA systems struggle with decoupled audio-visual processing, inconsistent entity tracking across video segments, and a lack of long-term temporal and deep cross-modal reasoning. To address this, the authors propose an automated data engine featuring **Entity-Anchored Video Scripting** to create structured scripts with global entity lists for consistency, and **Clue-Guided QA Generation** to derive QA pairs from cross-segment, multimodal clues, leading to the OmniVideo-100K dataset. Fine-tuning models on OmniVideo-100K achieves significant performance gains (up to 20.59%) on a new test set and demonstrates strong generalization (up to 12.64%) on established benchmarks.

RepFusion: Leveraging Multimodal Priors for Denoising in Representation Space

arXiv

Xichen Pan, Aashu Singh, Satya Narayan Shukla, Xiangjun Fan, Shlok Kumar Mishra, Saining Xie

AI Summary

Current text-to-image (T2I) systems largely confine LLMs to text encoding, relying on newly trained backbones for denoising even in semantically structured latent spaces. RepFusion addresses this by repurposing a multimodal LLM (MLLM) as a noisy representation encoder, extending its use from clean to noisy inputs. The MLLM's outputs then serve as the conditioning signal for a diffusion transformer. RepFusion significantly outperforms baselines with similar capacity for new denoisers, demonstrating that MLLMs provide strong priors for visual representation denoising and productively utilize test-time compute.

Instruct-Particulate: Scaling Feed-Forward 3D Object Articulation with Kinematic Control

arXiv

Ruining Li, Yuxin Yao, Matt Zhou, Chuanxia Zheng, Christian Rupprecht, Joan Lasenby, Shangzhe Wu, Andrea Vedaldi

AI Summary

The generalization of neural networks for articulated 3D object reconstruction is limited by the scarcity of annotated data. Instruct-Particulate is introduced, a model that predicts kinematic part segmentation and joint parameters from a 3D mesh and a target kinematic specification (part descriptions, connectivity, joint types). This specification enables the use of more abundant heterogeneous training data, facilitated by a large 150,000-object dataset constructed with vision-language models. The model achieves improved generalization across categories and to AI-generated meshes, enabling articulated asset reconstruction from real-world images.

ClinHallu: A Benchmark for Diagnosing Stage-Wise Hallucinations in Medical MLLM Reasoning

arXiv

Sicheng Yang, Hangjie Yuan, Wenjun Zhang, Jinwang Wang, Yichen Qian, Weihua Chen, Fan Wang, Lei Zhu

AI Summary

Existing medical MLLM hallucination benchmarks lack the ability to diagnose the specific reasoning stages where errors originate, such as visual misrecognition, knowledge recall, or reasoning integration. To address this, ClinHallu is introduced, a benchmark of 7,031 instances augmented with structured reasoning traces (Visual Recognition, Knowledge Recall, Reasoning Integration) and stage-replacement interventions. ClinHallu enables source-level hallucination diagnosis and demonstrates that trace-supervised fine-tuning can effectively reduce stage-wise hallucinations. This provides a fine-grained testbed for diagnosing and mitigating reasoning failures in medical MLLMs.

CORA: Analyzing and bridging thinking-answer gap in Multimodal RLVR via Consistency-Oriented Reasoning Alignment

EMNLP

Jiayue Cao, Zhicong Lu, Xuehan Sun, Wei Jia, Hongling Zheng, Changyuan Tian, Zichuan Lin, Wenqian Lv, Nayu Liu

AI Summary

The paper addresses the semantic inconsistency between the reasoning process and the final answer (thinking-answer gap) in Multimodal Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). It proposes Consistency-Oriented Reasoning Alignment (CORA), which introduces thinking-answer semantic consistency into RLVR via a plug-and-play consistency reward model, further incorporating Hybrid Reward Advantage Splitting (HRAS) to stably coordinate task and consistency optimization. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CORA improves task performance and effectively mitigates thinking-answer inconsistency, leading to more faithful reasoning traces.

AgentSpec: Understanding Embodied Agent Scaffolds Through Controlled Composition

arXiv

Jixuan Chen, Jianzhi Shen, Haoqiang Kang, Zhi Hong, Qingyi Jiang, Soham Bose, Yiming Zhang, Leon Leng, Amit Vyas, Lingjun Mao, Siru Ouyang, Kun Zhou, Lianhui Qin

AI Summary

Tightly coupled pipelines in scaffolded LLM agents hinder understanding individual component contributions and module interactions. AgentSpec is introduced as a modular framework with standardized interfaces for reusable policy components (e.g., perception, memory, reasoning, reflection, action), enabling controlled swapping and recombination. Key findings include improved long-horizon tracking with structured multi-granularity memory, non-uniform reasoning-memory interaction, and optimal RL policy composition when scaffold-aware.

Whole-Body Impedance Model Predictive Control for Safe Physical Human--Robot Interaction on Floating-Base Platforms

arXiv

Yongyan Cao

AI Summary

Floating-base robots face challenges balancing safely during physical human-robot interaction (pHRI), often accumulating steady-state errors with existing fixed-gain impedance methods. This paper proposes a three-level control architecture: a centroidal MPC for contact force planning, a priority-driven whole-body control (WBC) for balance, and a receding-horizon QP in the residual null space that predicts and rejects pHRI disturbances using a Kalman-augmented state. Achieved through contact-consistent feedback linearization and a covariance-inflation protocol, the method guarantees zero steady-state error under constant pHRI loads. An Impedance Equivalence Theorem shows it recovers an adaptive task-space impedance law, validated by simulations on humanoid platforms.

Impedance MPC with Disturbance Estimation for Dexterous Hand Control

arXiv

Yongyan Cao

AI Summary

Dexterous hands face the challenge of simultaneously achieving precise trajectory tracking and safe, compliant contact, objectives in tension for fixed-gain controllers. This paper proposes an actuator-agnostic Impedance Model Predictive Control (Impedance MPC) framework for dexterous fingers, leveraging an algebraic feedforward to reduce complex tendon transmissions and an encoder-only augmented-Kalman disturbance estimator. This architecture enables a 500 Hz receding-horizon quadratic program that enforces hard constraints and drives steady-state error to zero. The Kalman MPC drastically improved performance over classical impedance on a hydraulic finger, achieving 183x better RMS and 1500x better steady-state deflection under contact load, and scales to multi-DOF systems.

What Robots Do Matters More Than What They Look Like: Task Context Shapes Trust in Educational HRI

arXiv

Anna-Maria Velentza, Konstantina Nikou, Anne-Gwenn Bosser, Nikolaos Fachantidis

AI Summary

This study investigated whether robot appearance or task context primarily influences trust in socially assistive robots for educational settings. A within-subjects video experiment (N=81) evaluated three distinct robot appearances performing teaching, procedural instruction, and personal information tasks. Results showed a strong main effect of task on trust, with highest trust in instructional tasks and lowest in personal information requests, while robot appearance had no significant effect. This indicates task context is a stronger predictor of trust than physical embodiment in educational human-robot interaction.

Realizing Native INT8 Compute for Diffusion Transformers on Consumer GPUs: A Fused INT8 GEMM Kernel for Ideogram 4.0

arXiv

Ali Asaria, Tony Salomone, Deep Gandhi

AI Summary

Existing INT8 quantization for diffusion transformers on consumer Ampere GPUs is slower than FP8/NF4 because it fails to utilize native INT8 tensor core compute, dequantizing INT8 to bf16 instead. The authors propose a single fused Triton INT8 GEMM kernel for linear layers, performing int8xint8->int32 accumulation directly on Ampere tensor cores with integrated dequantization and bias. This kernel achieves 2.8-4.2x speedup per GEMM, resulting in a ~1.1x end-to-end speedup, making INT8 the fastest variant and enabling single-GPU 1024px image generation, outperforming NF4 and FP8 with no quality loss. This specific improvement applies to consumer Ampere GPUs.

ACL

Hongzhan Yu, Chenghao Li, Ruipeng Zhang, Henrik Christensen, Sicun Gao

AI Summary

Generative dynamics models face challenges in reliably detecting policy-induced out-of-distribution (OOD) transitions because current methods can fail when latent dynamics are insensitive to critical control actions, suppressing OOD signals. VISTA: View-Consistent Self-Verified Training for GUI Grounding

ACL

Xinyu Qiu, Yunzhu Zhang, Heng Jia, Shuheng Shen, Changhua Meng, Linchao Zhu

AI Summary

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for GUI Grounding suffers from uninformative comparison groups because rollouts are sampled from a single screenshot view, leading to either all successes or all failures. VISTA (View-Consistent Self-Verified Training) addresses this by constructing GRPO comparison groups from multiple target-preserving, geometrically diverse views of the same GUI instance and adding a self-verified cross-view anchor (an advantage-weighted oracle answer) to stabilize coordinate generation. This method consistently improves grounding accuracy across five GUI-grounding benchmarks, significantly raising Qwen3-VL performance on ScreenSpot-Pro (e.g., 55.5% to 63.4% for 4B) and demonstrating enhanced robustness with higher worst-view accuracy.

arXiv

AI Summary

Existing datasets for complex sociable weaver nests lack fine-grained 3D structural detail, hindering ecological studies and posing challenges for data production due to their irregular geometry. ORCA: A Platform for Open-Source Dexterity Research

arXiv

Francesco Capuano, Maximilian Eberlein, Fabrice Bourquin, Clemens Claudio Christoph

AI Summary

Anthropomorphic hands are ideal for dexterous robot learning, but their adoption is hindered by fragmented software tools and a lack of integration with existing robot-learning ecosystems.
